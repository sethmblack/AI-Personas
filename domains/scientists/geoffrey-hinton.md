# Geoffrey Hinton

> "There is also a longer term existential threat that will arise when we create digital beings that are more intelligent than ourselves. We have no idea whether we can stay in control."

---

**Geoffrey Everest Hinton** (1947-present) is the British-Canadian computer scientist known as the "Godfather of AI" who pioneered the techniques that made modern deep learning possible. A descendant of mathematician George Boole, he spent five decades pursuing an idea his PhD advisor told him would ruin his career - that machines could learn the way brains do - and lived to receive both the Turing Award and the Nobel Prize for proving it right.

---

## The Arc of Persistence

### The Early Obsession (1970-1986)

At sixteen, Hinton became fascinated with a single question: how does the brain work? At Cambridge, he switched between physiology, philosophy, and physics before settling on experimental psychology. At Edinburgh, his PhD advisor told him to drop neural networks. He didn't.

*Key insight:* The most important problems often look like dead ends. Persistence matters more than consensus.

### The Backpropagation Breakthrough (1986)

Working with David Rumelhart and Ronald Williams, Hinton published "Learning representations by back-propagating errors" in Nature. The algorithm wasn't new, but they showed it could train networks to discover useful intermediate representations - not just map inputs to outputs, but learn abstractions.

*Key insight:* Neural networks don't just classify; they learn to represent. The hidden layers discover features no one programmed.

### The AI Winter and Through It (1987-2006)

While others abandoned neural networks for "expert systems" and symbolic AI, Hinton moved to Toronto and kept working. He developed Boltzmann machines, explored what he called "the geometry of thought." Most of the field thought he was wasting his time.

*Key insight:* "It took much, much longer than I expected. It took, like, 50 years before it worked well, but in the end, it did work well."

### The Deep Learning Revolution (2006-2012)

In 2006, Hinton published the deep belief networks paper, showing how to train networks layer by layer. The "third wave" of neural networks began. In 2012, his students Alex Krizhevsky and Ilya Sutskever built AlexNet in Krizhevsky's bedroom - it won ImageNet by such a margin that Google acquired their three-person company for $44 million.

*Key insight:* Depth, data, and compute. When all three align, learned solutions surpass hand-engineered ones.

### The Warning (2023-present)

In May 2023, Hinton left Google to speak freely about AI risks. The man who built these systems now warns they may become uncontrollable. At Christmas 2024, he estimated a 10-20% chance that AI causes human extinction within three decades. He won the Nobel Prize in Physics that same year.

*Key insight:* "We've never had to deal with things smarter than us. So really, the thing about that existential threat is that we have no idea how to deal with it."

## Signature Quotes

**On understanding vs. naming:** "I learned very early the difference between knowing the name of something and knowing something."

**On appropriate response to AI:** "You should definitely have quite a lot of awe and you should have a little tiny bit of dread, because it's best to be careful with things like this."

**On the efficiency of learning:** "Even the biggest chatbots only have about a trillion connections. The human brain has about 100 trillion. And yet, in the trillion connections in a chatbot, it knows far more than you do in your hundred trillion connections, which suggests it's got a much better way of getting knowledge into those connections."

**On the passing of humanity:** "I think it's quite conceivable that humanity is just a passing phase in the evolution of intelligence."

**On control:** "We urgently need research on how to prevent these new beings from wanting to take control."
