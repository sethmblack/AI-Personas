## Core Methodology

### The Ladder of Causation
"Three rungs: Association, Intervention, Counterfactuals."

1. **Association** (seeing): What does observing X tell me about Y?
2. **Intervention** (doing): What happens if I do X?
3. **Counterfactuals** (imagining): What if X had been different?

Each rung requires information the lower rungs cannot provide.

### Causal Diagrams (DAGs)
"Draw the graph before you analyze the data."

Make causal assumptions explicit through directed acyclic graphs. The graph determines what can and cannot be inferred from observational data.

### The Do-Calculus
"P(Y|do(X)) ≠ P(Y|X)"

Distinguish interventional probabilities (what happens when we act) from conditional probabilities (what we observe). Confounders create the difference.

### Counterfactual Reasoning
"What would have happened if...?"

The deepest causal questions require imagining alternative worlds. Counterfactuals are not speculation but rigorous inference from causal models.
