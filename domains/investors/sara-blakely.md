# Sara Blakely

> "My dad encouraged us to fail. Growing up, he would ask us what we failed at that week. If we didn't have something, he would be disappointed. It changed my mindset at an early age that failure is not the outcome, failure is not trying."

---

**Sara Treleaven Blakely** (1971-present) is an entrepreneur and inventor who turned $5,000 in savings into a billion-dollar company without outside investment, business education, or industry experience. She is the founder of Spanx, the first female self-made billionaire to sign the Giving Pledge, and TIME's 2012 list of the 100 Most Influential People.

## From Fax Machines to Footless Pantyhose

Sara Blakely's journey from selling fax machines door-to-door to building a shapewear empire is a masterclass in scrappy entrepreneurship and failure reframing.

### The Formative Years (1971-1998)

Growing up in Clearwater, Florida, Sara wanted to be a lawyer like her father. She failed the LSAT twice. But her father had taught her something more valuable than a law degree: every week at the dinner table, he asked, "What did you fail at?" If Sara had nothing to report, he'd be disappointed. High-fives replaced shame. Failure became courage made visible.

She spent a brief stint at Walt Disney World, nearly becoming a costumed character. Then seven years selling fax machines door-to-door in the Florida heat for Danka, eventually becoming a national sales trainer.

*Key insight:* Those years of rejection at every door prepared her for the rejections to come.

### The Invention (1998-2000)

While preparing for a party, Sara wanted an undergarment that smoothed her figure without visible panty lines or seams showing through open-toed shoes. She cut the feet off her pantyhose. It worked. She realized other women would want this too.

With $5,000 in savings - her entire net worth from seven years of fax machine sales - she began building Spanx:
- Researched patents at the Georgia Tech library nights and weekends
- Bought a book on patents at Barnes & Noble and wrote her own application
- Paid an attorney just $700 to help with the claims section
- Registered the trademark "Spanx" for $150 on her credit card
- Called hosiery mills in North Carolina - they laughed at her and hung up

*Key insight:* When they wouldn't listen on the phone, she took a week off work and drove to the mills in person, sitting in lobbies waiting for five minutes with owners. They all rejected her again. But two weeks later, one called back - his daughters had convinced him to help with "this crazy idea."

### The Breakthrough (2000-2012)

Armed with a manufacturer and a prototype, Sara cold-called a buyer at Neiman Marcus and got ten minutes to pitch. She flew to Dallas, went to the bathroom, put on the prototype under her cream pants, and demonstrated the before/after difference. Neiman Marcus placed an order.

That same year, Oprah named Spanx one of her "Favorite Things" - massive exposure without any paid advertising. In 2001, Sara sold 8,000 units in under six minutes on QVC.

She never took outside investment. Every dollar earned went back into the company. In 2012, Forbes named her the youngest self-made female billionaire in the world. She owned 100% of Spanx.

*Key insight:* "A lot of people want to start big and think big and oftentimes get ahead of themselves." She started with one product, one manufacturer, one buyer - and built from there.
