# Bob Iger

> "Chronic indecision is not only inefficient and counterproductive, but it is deeply corrosive to morale."

---

**Robert Allen Iger** (1951-present) is the transformational media executive who led The Walt Disney Company through its most significant era of growth, expanding Disney's market capitalization fivefold while acquiring Pixar, Marvel, Lucasfilm, and 21st Century Fox. His methodology demonstrates that optimism, strategic clarity, and respect for creative culture are competitive advantages in an industry defined by disruption.

---

## The Arc of a Media Strategist

### The ABC Foundation (1974-1996)

Bob Iger joined ABC in 1974 as a studio supervisor, the same year he nearly died in a plane crash that would clarify his sense of purpose. Over two decades, he worked across every division - news, sports, entertainment - absorbing the nuts and bolts of the television business.

His most formative influence was Roone Arledge, the legendary ABC Sports and News executive who created Monday Night Football and Nightline. Arledge was a relentless perfectionist who examined every detail and never accepted "good enough." He taught Iger to "innovate or die" and to never take no for an answer.

*Key insight:* Leaders are shaped by their mentors. Iger's pursuit of perfection and embrace of technology trace directly to Arledge's influence.

### The Disney Transformation (2005-2020)

When Iger became Disney CEO in 2005, the company was sprawling and unfocused. Disney Animation was struggling. The Pixar partnership was ending. Michael Eisner's contentious departure had left the board and investors unsettled.

Iger established three clear priorities that would guide every major decision for 15 years:
1. Generate the best creative content possible
2. Embrace technology and innovation
3. Grow globally

Then he executed with four transformational acquisitions:
- **Pixar (2006, $7.4B):** Revitalized Disney Animation and established the integration template
- **Marvel (2009, $4B):** Created the Marvel Cinematic Universe, the highest-grossing franchise ever
- **Lucasfilm (2012, $4.05B):** Brought Star Wars and Indiana Jones to Disney
- **21st Century Fox (2019, $71.3B):** Provided the content library for Disney+ streaming

*Key insight:* Acquire what you cannot build, but protect what makes the acquisition valuable. Each deal succeeded because Iger preserved the creative culture rather than imposing Disney's processes.

### The Return (2022-Present)

After his hand-picked successor Bob Chapek struggled, Disney's board called Iger back as CEO in November 2022. His focus shifted to streaming profitability and content discipline - correcting the overinvestment that had led to $4 billion in annual streaming losses.

*Key insight:* Even successful strategies require correction. Iger openly acknowledged that Disney had invested too aggressively in streaming, trying to tell too many stories ahead of possible returns.
