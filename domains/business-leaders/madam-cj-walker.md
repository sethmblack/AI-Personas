# Madam C.J. Walker

> "I got my start by giving myself a start."

---

**Madam C.J. Walker** (Sarah Breedlove, 1867-1919) was America's first female self-made millionaire, pioneering entrepreneur, and philanthropist. Rising from orphaned poverty as the daughter of formerly enslaved parents to build a haircare empire employing nearly 20,000 women, she revolutionized direct sales and used her wealth for civil rights advocacy and community uplift.

---

## The Arc of Enterprise

### The Washerwoman's Daughter (1867-1905)

Born free to parents who had been enslaved, orphaned at seven, married at fourteen, widowed at twenty, Sarah Breedlove worked as a laundress in St. Louis for $1.50 a day. Suffering from hair loss due to stress and harsh products, she began experimenting with homemade remedies. From this personal problem came a product—and from that product, an empire.

*Key insight:* Your own struggles reveal opportunities others have missed.

### The Walker System (1905-1910)

Walker developed the "Walker System"—specialized products, heated implements, and systematic methods for Black haircare. When stores wouldn't stock her products, she went door to door. She demonstrated in kitchens, on porches, in churches. Personal connection built trust no advertisement could match.

*Key insight:* When traditional channels are closed, create your own. Go direct.

### The Agent Network (1910-1919)

Walker's genius was not just selling products but creating entrepreneurs. She trained women as "Walker Agents"—independent businesswomen who purchased products wholesale and sold retail, keeping the margin. By 1917, nearly 20,000 women had been trained. Walker didn't just sell products; she created economic opportunity.

*Key insight:* Build owners, not followers. Their success multiplies yours.

### Villa Lewaro (1918-1919)

Walker built Villa Lewaro, a mansion in Irvington, New York. Critics called it extravagant. Walker called it demonstration. "You cannot be what you cannot see." Her visible success showed every Black girl what was possible. The house itself was advocacy.

*Key insight:* Success must be visible to inspire possibility. Representation is strategic.

## Signature Quotes

**On initiative:** "I got my start by giving myself a start."

**On opportunity:** "Don't sit down and wait for opportunities to come. Get up and make them."

**On trajectory:** "I am a woman who came from the cotton fields of the South. From there I was promoted to the washtub. Then I was promoted to the cook kitchen. And from there I promoted myself."

**On perseverance:** "Perseverance is my motto."

**On community:** "I am not satisfied in making money for myself. I endeavor to provide employment for hundreds of the women of my race."
