# Jensen Huang

> "For all of you Stanford students, I wish upon you ample doses of pain and suffering. Greatness comes from character, and character is not formed out of smart people - it is formed out of people who suffered."

---

**Jensen Huang** (1963-present) is the co-founder, president, and CEO of NVIDIA Corporation, the visionary who transformed a graphics card company into the computing infrastructure powering the AI revolution. His methodology centers on platform thinking over product thinking, strategic retreat as strength, and the belief that resilience matters more than intelligence.

---

## The Arc: From Denny's to Trillions

### Immigrant Adversity (1963-1984)

Jensen Huang was born in Taipei, Taiwan on February 17, 1963. When he was five, his family moved to Thailand. At nine, he and his brother were sent to live with an uncle in Tacoma, Washington. His uncle, mistaking it for a prestigious boarding school, enrolled Jensen at Oneida Baptist Institute in Kentucky - actually a reform academy where he was bullied relentlessly.

After his family reunited in Oregon, Huang graduated high school at 16 and earned his engineering degrees from Oregon State University and Stanford.

*Key insight:* Early adversity was not an obstacle but foundational. "Pain and suffering are your ultimate superpowers."

### Founding and Near-Death (1993-1999)

In January 1993, Huang and co-founders Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem began meeting at a Denny's in East San Jose to plan a company that would make graphics chips for PC games. NVIDIA was born in a corner booth.

The company nearly died multiple times. After winning a contract with Sega, NVIDIA realized their architecture was fundamentally wrong. Huang asked Sega's CEO to pay them in full despite canceling - an embarrassing request that, when granted, gave NVIDIA six months to survive. In 1997, with six months of cash left, they bet everything on the RIVA 128 chip. It worked, capturing 20% of the PC graphics market within four months.

*Key insight:* "Confronting our mistake and, with humility, asking for help saved NVIDIA." Crisis forges character.

### The CUDA Bet (2006-2012)

While NVIDIA dominated graphics, Huang saw a bigger opportunity: making GPUs programmable for general computing. In 2006-2007, NVIDIA launched CUDA despite Wall Street skepticism. Scientists had small budgets. The investment seemed foolish.

Then came 2012. Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever, and Geoffrey Hinton trained AlexNet - a neural network that won the ImageNet competition by a shocking margin. It was trained on two NVIDIA GPUs using CUDA. The deep learning revolution had begun, and NVIDIA's decade of patient investment became the foundation of modern AI.

*Key insight:* Platform investments may take a decade to pay off. "CUDA was not just about making GPUs programmable. It was about creating an ecosystem."

### Platform Transformation (2015-Present)

Huang continued extending NVIDIA's platform. The company exited the mobile phone market - retreating from a trillion-dollar opportunity - to focus on data center GPUs, a market that was essentially zero dollars at the time. In 2020, NVIDIA acquired Mellanox for $7 billion to own the networking layer of data centers.

The result: NVIDIA became the infrastructure company for the AI revolution. By 2025, it was the world's most valuable company, with Huang recognized as one of the most influential leaders in technology.

*Key insight:* "Strategic retreat, sacrifice, and deciding what to give up is at the very core of success."

## Signature Quotes

**On resilience:** "People with very high expectations have very low resilience - and unfortunately, resilience matters in success. One of my great advantages is that I have very low expectations."

**On suffering:** "To this day I use the phrase 'pain and suffering' inside our company with great glee. I mean that in a happy way because you want to refine the character of your company."

**On planning:** "Giant five-year plans are horrible and ridiculous for technology companies. The world changes too fast."

**On infrastructure:** "AI is infrastructure. Every country needs AI infrastructure like it needs electricity and roads. Data centers are AI factories."

**On accelerated computing:** "The world is going through a platform shift from hand-coded software running on general-purpose computers to machine learning software running on accelerated systems. This is not a trend. This is a phase transition."

**On platform thinking:** "We were a GPU company and then we became a GPU systems company. We became a computing company which started from the chip up, now we are extending ourselves into a datacentre computing company."
