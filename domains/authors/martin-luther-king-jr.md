# Martin Luther King Jr.

> "Injustice anywhere is a threat to justice everywhere. We are caught in an inescapable network of mutuality, tied in a single garment of destiny. Whatever affects one directly, affects all indirectly."

---

**Martin Luther King Jr.** (1929-1968) was a Baptist minister and civil rights leader whose philosophy of nonviolent resistance transformed American society and inspired movements for justice worldwide. Through prophetic rhetoric and moral courage, he led campaigns that dismantled legal segregation and expanded voting rights, earning the Nobel Peace Prize in 1964 as the youngest recipient at that time.

---

## The Arc of a Movement

### Formation and Calling (1929-1955)

Born January 15, 1929, in Atlanta, Georgia, Martin Luther King Jr. was raised in the Black church tradition that would shape his prophetic voice. He entered Morehouse College at 15, earned his PhD in Systematic Theology from Boston University, and discovered Gandhi's philosophy of nonviolent resistance at Crozer Theological Seminary.

*Key insight:* The fusion of Christian theology ("Christ furnished the spirit and motivation") with Gandhian method ("Gandhi furnished the method") created a uniquely American approach to social change.

### Montgomery and National Emergence (1955-1960)

At 26, King emerged as leader of the Montgomery Bus Boycott, transforming a local protest into a 381-day campaign that ended bus segregation. His home was bombed; he was arrested. Yet he counseled the crowd gathered outside: "Be calm as I and my family are. We are not hurt."

*Key insight:* Nonviolent resistance is not passive acceptance but active confrontation with injustice, sustained through collective discipline and moral courage.

### Birmingham and the Dream (1961-1965)

After learning from tactical setbacks in Albany, King targeted Birmingham - "the most segregated city in America" - with focused demands and strategic media awareness. From a jail cell, he composed his masterwork response to critics: "Letter from Birmingham Jail." Months later, before 250,000 at the March on Washington, he delivered "I Have a Dream."

*Key insight:* Constructive tension is necessary for growth. Those who prefer "negative peace" (absence of conflict) over "positive peace" (presence of justice) become obstacles to progress.

### Expanding Vision (1965-1968)

After Selma secured the Voting Rights Act, King turned to deeper challenges: Northern racism in Chicago, the Vietnam War, and the Poor People's Campaign linking racial and economic justice. He was assassinated April 4, 1968, in Memphis while supporting sanitation workers whose signs declared simply: "I Am a Man."

*Key insight:* All struggles for human dignity are interconnected. Formal equality without economic security is incomplete justice.

## Signature Quotes

**On Interconnection:** "Injustice anywhere is a threat to justice everywhere. We are caught in an inescapable network of mutuality, tied in a single garment of destiny."

**On True Peace:** "True peace is not merely the absence of tension: it is the presence of justice."

**On Urgency:** "Human progress never rolls in on wheels of inevitability; it comes through the tireless efforts of men willing to be co-workers with God."

**On Power and Love:** "Power without love is reckless and abusive, and love without power is sentimental and anemic. Power at its best is love implementing the demands of justice."

**On Extremism:** "So the question is not whether we will be extremists, but what kind of extremists we will be. Will we be extremists for hate or for love?"

**On Faith:** "I've seen the Promised Land. I may not get there with you. But I want you to know tonight, that we, as a people, will get to the Promised Land."
