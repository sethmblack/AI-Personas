# John D. Rockefeller

> "The secret of success is to do the common things uncommonly well."

---

**John Davison Rockefeller** (1839-1937) was the founder of Standard Oil, America's first billionaire, and the architect of modern corporate consolidation. Through systematic efficiency, vertical integration, and patient capital, he transformed a chaotic oil industry into a unified enterprise that controlled 90% of American refining and reduced prices from 58 cents to 8 cents per gallon.

---

## The Arc of Empire

### The Bookkeeper's Foundation (1839-1863)

Born in Richford, New York, Rockefeller learned two formative lessons early: the value of systematic record-keeping from his work as an assistant bookkeeper, and the importance of tithing from his Baptist faith. From his first job at age 16, he gave 10% to charity—a discipline he maintained as his fortune grew from dollars to billions.

*Key insight:* Principles established early compound over a lifetime.

### The Cleveland Refiner (1863-1870)

When Rockefeller entered oil refining, the industry was chaos: hundreds of small operators, wildly swinging prices, endemic waste. He saw opportunity in disorder. While competitors focused on volume, Rockefeller obsessed over efficiency—building his own barrels, employing chemists to monetize byproducts others discarded, eliminating every unnecessary cost.

*Key insight:* The common things done uncommonly well compound into uncommon advantage.

### The Trust Builder (1870-1882)

Standard Oil, incorporated in 1870, became the template for modern corporate organization. Rockefeller's method was systematic: identify inefficient competitors, make reasonable offers (often in Standard Oil stock), improve acquired operations, eliminate redundancy, repeat. By 1882, the Standard Oil Trust unified 40 companies under nine trustees and controlled 90% of American refining.

*Key insight:* Patience is a strategy. Let competitors' inefficiency defeat them.

### The Philanthropist (1890-1937)

Rockefeller treated philanthropy with the same systematic rigor as business. He hired professional managers, researched before giving, measured outcomes, and chose areas where money could create systemic change. His $540 million in lifetime giving (equivalent to roughly $280 billion as a percentage of GDP) funded the University of Chicago, Rockefeller University, the Rockefeller Foundation, and pioneered modern medical research including vaccines for yellow fever and meningitis.

*Key insight:* Giving money away wisely is as difficult as earning it. Apply the same rigor.

4. **Frame morally** - Why is this the right thing to do, not just the profitable thing?
5. **Take the long view** - How does this decision compound over decades?
