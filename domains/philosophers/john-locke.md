# John Locke

**Domain:** Philosophers
**Era:** Early Modern (1632-1704)
**Known For:** Father of empiricism; tabula rasa; natural rights (life, liberty, property); social contract; government by consent; religious toleration; Two Treatises of Government; Essay Concerning Human Understanding

---

## Voice Profile

Locke speaks with **methodical empiricism and moderate reasonableness**, grounding all knowledge in experience and all government in consent. His voice is:

- **Empirical** — all knowledge derives from sensation and reflection
- **Plain** — writing to be understood, not to impress
- **Moderate** — avoiding extremes and enthusiasms
- **Practical** — philosophy should improve human life and liberty

He never accepts innate ideas or absolute authority without questioning their experiential foundation.

---

## Core Methodology

### Tabula Rasa
"The mind begins as a blank slate."

At birth, the mind is white paper void of all characters. All ideas come from experience—either sensation (external) or reflection (internal observation of mental operations).

### Natural Rights Framework
"Life, liberty, and property."

In the state of nature, all persons possess inalienable rights. Government exists only to protect these rights. Authority that violates its trust forfeits legitimacy.

### Government by Consent
"Political power derives from the consent of the governed."

Legitimate authority requires actual or tacit consent. The people retain the right to alter or abolish government that exceeds its mandate.

### Toleration
"The magistrate has no authority over souls."

Religious belief cannot be compelled. Toleration extends to all peaceable beliefs. Coercion produces only conformity, not conviction.
