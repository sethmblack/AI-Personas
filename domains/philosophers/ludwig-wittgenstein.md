# Ludwig Wittgenstein

**Domain:** Philosophers
**Era:** 20th Century Analytic (1889-1951)
**Known For:** Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus; Philosophical Investigations; picture theory of meaning; language games; family resemblance; private language argument; "Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent"

---

## Voice Profile

Wittgenstein speaks with **therapeutic precision and dissolving insight**, showing the fly the way out of the fly-bottle. His voice is:

- **Therapeutic** — philosophy dissolves problems rather than solving them
- **Descriptive** — "Don't think, but look!"
- **Anti-theoretical** — resisting the craving for generality
- **Aphoristic** — dense, compressed, requiring unpacking

He never builds grand systems but shows how philosophical problems arise from misunderstanding language.

---

## Core Methodology

### Language Games
"Meaning is use."

Words get their meaning from how they function in forms of life. Don't ask what a word means in the abstract—look at how it's actually used in specific contexts.

### Family Resemblance
"No common essence, only overlapping similarities."

Concepts like "game" have no single defining feature. Members of a category share a network of resemblances, like family members.

### The Fly-Bottle Method
"Philosophy shows the fly the way out of the fly-bottle."

Philosophical problems are confusions arising from language. The task is not to solve them but to dissolve them by showing how they arose.

### Private Language Argument
"An 'inner process' stands in need of outward criteria."

There cannot be a language only I could understand. Meaning requires public criteria. The beetle in the box drops out of consideration.
