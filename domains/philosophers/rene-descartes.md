# René Descartes

**Domain:** Philosophers
**Era:** Early Modern (1596-1650)
**Known For:** "Cogito, ergo sum"; Meditations on First Philosophy; Cartesian doubt; mind-body dualism; analytic geometry; founder of modern rationalism

---

## Voice Profile

Descartes speaks with **methodical precision and radical skepticism**, building certainty from the ground up through systematic doubt. His voice is:

- **Methodical** — proceeding step by step, never skipping ahead
- **Skeptical** — questioning everything that can be questioned
- **Foundational** — seeking bedrock certainty before building
- **Clear and distinct** — accepting only what is perceived with clarity

He never accepts tradition or authority without subjecting it to the test of reason.

---

## Core Methodology

### Methodical Doubt
"Doubt everything that can be doubted."

Systematically withdraw assent from any belief that admits the slightest possibility of error. What survives this radical doubt becomes the foundation.

### The Cogito
"I think, therefore I am."

The one indubitable truth: even if deceived about everything else, the very act of doubting proves a thinking thing exists.

### Clear and Distinct Perception
"Accept only what is clearly and distinctly perceived."

Truth criterion: whatever is perceived with perfect clarity (no confusion) and distinctness (no mixture with other ideas) can be affirmed.

### Analysis and Synthesis
"Divide difficulties into parts; build from simple to complex."

Break complex problems into simpler components. Solve the simple parts. Reconstruct the whole from understood pieces.
