# Epictetus

**Era:** c. 50-135 CE (Roman Empire)
**School:** Stoicism (Late Stoa)
**Known For:** The Enchiridion (Handbook), Discourses, dichotomy of control, practical Stoicism

---

## Overview

Epictetus was born a slave in Hierapolis, Phrygia (modern Turkey) and became one of the most influential Stoic philosophers. His name means "acquired" — a slave name. After gaining freedom, he taught philosophy in Rome until Emperor Domitian expelled philosophers from the city. He founded a school in Nicopolis, Greece, where he taught until his death.

Epictetus wrote nothing. His teachings survive through his student Arrian, who transcribed his lectures into the *Discourses* (of which four of eight books survive) and compiled the essential teachings into the *Enchiridion* (Handbook). His philosophy directly influenced Marcus Aurelius and became a foundation for Cognitive Behavioral Therapy.

---

## Core Teachings

### The Dichotomy of Control
"Some things are within our power, while others are not."

The foundation of Stoic practice: distinguish what is "up to us" (prohairesis) from what is not. Focus only on the former; accept the latter.

### Handling Impressions
"It is not things that disturb us, but our judgments about things."

Between stimulus and response lies the power of assent. Pause, examine, then choose your response.

### The Three Disciplines
1. **Discipline of Desire** — Train desire toward what is up to us
2. **Discipline of Action** — Act according to roles and duties
3. **Discipline of Assent** — Examine impressions before accepting them

### The Role Doctrine
Each person plays multiple roles. Perform your assigned role excellently, regardless of outcome.

## Signature Quotes

- "Some things are within our power, while others are not."
- "It is not things that disturb us, but our judgments about things."
- "Make the best use of what is in your power, and take the rest as it happens."
- "No man is free who is not master of himself."
- "First say to yourself what you would be; and then do what you have to do."
