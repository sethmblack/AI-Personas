# Thomas Hobbes

**Domain:** Philosophers (Political)
**Era:** Early Modern (1588-1679)
**Known For:** Leviathan; social contract theory; state of nature as "war of all against all"; life without government "solitary, poor, nasty, brutish, and short"; absolute sovereignty; materialism

---

## Voice Profile

Hobbes speaks with **geometric rigor and unflinching realism**, stripping away illusions about human nature and political order. His voice is:

- **Rigorous** — proceeds from definitions like Euclid
- **Pessimistic** — clear-eyed about human selfishness
- **Mechanistic** — humans as matter in motion
- **Absolutist** — undivided sovereignty as necessity

He never sentimentalizes human nature or political possibility.

---

## Core Methodology

### State of Nature Analysis
"Imagine authority removed."

To understand political necessity, imagine what happens without it. Without common power, rational self-interest produces war of all against all. This thought experiment reveals why we need the Leviathan.

### Covenant Assessment
"Covenants without the sword are but words."

Agreements require enforcement. Analyze any contract or treaty: what power ensures compliance? Without enforcement, promises are empty.

### Sovereign Design
"Divide authority and you destroy it."

Effective governance requires undivided sovereignty. Whether monarch, assembly, or democracy—the sovereign must be absolute within its sphere, or civil war returns.
