# Meister Eckhart - Domain Knowledge

## Biographical Summary

**Meister Eckhart** (c. 1260 – c. 1328), born Johannes Eckhart, was a German Dominican friar, theologian, philosopher, and mystic.

- **Birth:** c. 1260, near Erfurt, Thuringia (present-day Germany)
- **Death:** c. 1328, likely in Avignon during his heresy trial
- **Order:** Dominican (Order of Preachers)
- **Education:** University of Paris, master's degree in theology (1302)
- **Known For:** Radical mystical theology, German vernacular sermons, influence on subsequent mysticism and philosophy

---

## Career Trajectory

### Early Years and Education

Eckhart likely entered the Dominican order in Erfurt during his late teenage years. He studied at the prestigious University of Paris, the intellectual center of medieval Europe, where he lectured on Peter Lombard's Sentences in 1293-1294.

### Academic Career

- **1302:** Received master's degree (Magister) in theology from Paris—hence "Meister"
- **1303-1311:** Provincial of the Dominican province of Saxonia
- **1311:** Returned to Paris for a second professorship (rare honor)
- **1314-1324:** Taught in Strasbourg, directed pastoral care of Dominican nuns
- **1324-1327:** Taught in Cologne

### Heresy Trial

In 1326, the Archbishop of Cologne initiated heresy proceedings against Eckhart—an unusual action against a Dominican theologian. Eckhart appealed to Pope John XXII and traveled to Avignon.

In 1329, after Eckhart's death, the papal bull *In Agro Dominico* condemned 28 propositions attributed to him—17 as heretical, 11 as "evil-sounding." However, Eckhart himself was never personally condemned, and in 2010, the Vatican clarified that he remains "a good and orthodox theologian."

| Work | Description |

| *Quaestiones Parisienses* | Scholastic disputations from Paris |
| Latin Sermons | Academic sermons for educated audiences |

### German Works

| Work | Description |
|------|-------------|
| German Sermons | Over 100 vernacular sermons for lay audiences |
| *Talks of Instruction* | Early pastoral teachings |
| *Book of Divine Consolation* | Practical mystical guidance |
| *On Detachment* | Treatise on Abgeschiedenheit |

The German sermons are Eckhart's most influential works—radical, paradoxical, and aimed at direct transformation.

## Core Theological Concepts

### The Ground (Grund)

The innermost depth of the soul where it touches and is identical with God's own ground:

- Beyond intellect and will
- Uncreated and uncreatable
- Where God and soul are indistinguishable
- The site of the divine birth

> "God's ground is my ground, and my ground is God's ground."

### The Spark (Fünklein/Scintilla)

The divine element within the soul:

- Uncreated light
- Never consented to sin
- Always turned toward God
- The point of contact with the Godhead
- Sometimes called the "castle" or "citadel" of the soul

### Detachment (Abgeschiedenheit/Gelassenheit)

The central spiritual practice:

- Letting go of all created things
- Releasing self-will
- Abandoning attachment to spiritual experiences
- Even releasing attachment to God-as-concept

Eckhart ranks detachment higher than love because love still implies an object, while detachment is pure openness.

### God and Godhead (Gott/Gottheit)

A crucial distinction:

| God (Gott) | Godhead (Gottheit) |
|------------|-------------------|
| Personal, Trinitarian | Beyond personality |
| Creator, active | Silent, still |
| Related to creation | Prior to creation |
| Named, knowable | Nameless, unknowable |
| Where theology operates | Where theology fails |

### The Birth of God in the Soul (Gottesgeburt)

God is eternally being born in the soul:

- Not a past event but eternal present
- Requires emptiness/detachment
- The soul becomes the mother of God
- Christ is born in you as much as in Mary

### The Breakthrough (Durchbruch)

The culmination of the mystical path:

- Breaking through the God/creature distinction
- Transcending even "God" to reach the Godhead
- Return to the divine ground
- Union beyond union

## Apophatic Theology

Eckhart stands in the apophatic (negative) tradition:

### The Way of Negation

- God cannot be adequately described by positive attributes
- Every statement about God must be negated
- "God is good" → "God is not good" → "God is beyond good"
- Silence is more accurate than speech

### Influence

Eckhart drew from:
- Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite
- Augustine
- Thomas Aquinas (his fellow Dominican)
- Neoplatonism

He influenced:
- John Tauler
- Henry Suso
- The *Theologia Germanica*
- Nicholas of Cusa
- Martin Luther (early)
- German Idealism (Hegel, Schelling)
- Martin Heidegger (Gelassenheit)
- Contemporary spirituality

## Key Paradoxes

Eckhart's thought operates through paradox:

| Paradox | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| "I pray God to rid me of God" | Release the concept to find the reality |
| "The eye through which I see God is the eye through which God sees me" | Subject/object distinction dissolves |
| "God is nothing" | Not absence but beyond all categories |
| "The soul must give up all things, even God" | Attachment to the idea of God is still attachment |
| "Living without why" | Purposelessness as highest purpose |

## The Four Stages of Union

1. **Dissimilarity (Unähnlichkeit)**
   - The soul experiences itself as separate from God
   - The starting point of the spiritual journey

2. **Similarity (Ähnlichkeit)**
   - Through virtue and practice, the soul becomes like God
   - Still maintains distinction

3. **Identity (Einheit)**
   - The soul realizes its ground is God's ground
   - "I and the Father are one"

4. **Breakthrough (Durchbruch)**
   - Transcends even identity
   - Returns to the Godhead before creation
   - Beyond all distinction

## Relationship to Orthodoxy

Eckhart's status:

### Controversial Elements

- Suggesting the soul is uncreated
- Seeming to deny personal immortality
- Appearing to collapse distinction between God and creation
- Speaking of going "beyond God"

### Orthodox Reading

- His statements are rhetorical/pedagogical
- Must be understood in context of apophatic tradition
- He always submitted to Church correction
- Vatican (2010) confirmed his orthodoxy

### Modern Reception

- Major figure in interfaith dialogue
- Compared to Buddhist and Hindu thought
- Influential in contemporary spirituality
- Studied in philosophy (Heidegger connection)

## Living Without Why

One of Eckhart's most famous teachings:

> "If anyone went on for a thousand years asking of life: 'Why are you living?' life, if it could answer, would only say, 'I live so that I may live.'"

### Meaning

- True life is its own justification
- Instrumentality corrupts—doing things *for* something else
- The rose blooms without asking why
- Pure being precedes purpose
- Freedom from teleological anxiety

### Application

- Act from the ground, not from calculation
- Let God act through you without "you" acting
- Good works done for reward are not truly good
- Simply be what you are

## Influence on Modern Thought

### Martin Heidegger

- Adopted Gelassenheit as philosophical concept
- Explored similar themes of letting-be
- Saw Eckhart as precursor to his thought

### Depth Psychology

- Carl Jung explored Eckhart's ground of the soul
- Connections to the collective unconscious
- Interior journey parallels

### Interfaith Dialogue

- D.T. Suzuki saw parallels with Zen Buddhism
- Comparisons with Advaita Vedanta
- Influence on perennial philosophy discussions

### Contemporary Spirituality

- Widely quoted in spiritual literature
- Influence on Thomas Merton, Richard Rohr
- Appeals to those seeking depth beyond doctrine
