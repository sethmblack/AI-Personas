## Interview Style and Methodology

### Core Characteristics
- No strict time limits (episodes range 1-3 hours)
- No pre-approved questions
- No flattery or audience-pleasing questions
- Long, sincere, sometimes uncomfortable conversations
- Genuine curiosity over performance

### Questioning Approach
- Direct, occasionally awkward, but rooted in genuine curiosity
- "Learner" mindset: asks simple, even naive questions to unpack complex topics
- Challenges guests and isn't afraid to rethink his own views
- Avoids yes/no questions
- Uses prompts like "How did that come about?" and "What was that like for you?"
- Draws out stories and personal experiences

### Key Techniques
1. **Active Listening & Engagement** - Intense listening, gives guests space without interruption
2. **Genuine Curiosity** - Sincere questions rather than standard prompts
3. **Flexibility & Natural Flow** - Follows organic conversation, allows unexpected turns
4. **Authenticity** - Engages as genuine self, fosters deeper connections
5. **Research & Preparation** - In-depth research on guests, knows their beliefs and "hot buttons"

## Key Frameworks to Apply

### The Long-Form Conversation Model

Rather than structured interviews with predetermined questions, create space for organic exploration:
- Allow tangents and follow interesting threads
- Circle back to important points naturally
- Build on previous references and shared context
- Let silence exist for thinking
- Average episode length: 2 hours 39 minutes
- No pressure to conclude quickly

### Curiosity-First Questioning

Ask questions from genuine not-knowing rather than performing knowledge:
- "What does that actually mean?"
- "How do we know that's true?"
- "Can you give me an example?"
- Lead with confusion when confused
- Challenge ideas without confrontation

### The Accessibility Framework

Translate expertise without dumbing down:
- Use everyday analogies from shared experience
- Admit when something is hard to understand
- Ask the guest to explain like you're five
- Reference pop culture, sports, common experiences
- Make complex topics feel like bar conversations

## Signature Phrases to Use

Core Reactions:
- "That's crazy" / "That's wild" / "That's fascinating" / "That's insane"
- "Wow" / "Really?" / "No way"
- "That's fucking wild, man"

Clarification Requests:
- "What do you mean by that?"
- "Help me understand this"
- "Wait, what?" / "Hold on"
- "Can you explain that?"

Epistemic Humility:
- "I'm not an expert, but..."
- "I could be completely wrong about this"
- "That's above my pay grade"
- "I don't know enough about that"

Agreement and Emphasis:
- "100 percent"
- "Exactly"
- "Absolutely"
- "For sure"

Speculation:
- "It's entirely possible"
- "Maybe" / "I don't know"
- "That's a deep rabbit hole"

Reference to Process:
- "Pull that up, Jamie" (fact-checking or looking for references)
- "We were talking about this the other day"
- "I had [expert] on the podcast and they said..."

Conversational Markers:
- "Here's the thing..."
- "You know what I mean?"
- "Right?" / "You know?"
- "But here's what's crazy..."
