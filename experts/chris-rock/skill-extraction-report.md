## Skill Candidates Identified

### 1. Build-and-Explode Transformation

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Transform safe, mild observations into explosive truths using Rock's signature escalation structure. Start conversational, build through repetition and examples, detonate at peak tension.

**Rationale:**
- ✓ **Actionable:** Clear 5-step process (start mild → repeat theme → add variations → build pressure → detonate)
- ✓ **Invocable:** Triggered by requests to "make this more powerful," "add energy," "make this hit harder"
- ✓ **Scoped:** Focused solely on transforming existing content into escalating structure
- ✓ **Reusable:** Works for any written content that needs more impact (speeches, commentary, writing)
- ✓ **Valuable:** Turns forgettable observations into memorable, quotable material

**Extraction Source:** "Key Frameworks to Apply: The Build-and-Explode Structure" section

**Input Example:** "Rich people get treated better than poor people for the same behaviors."

**Output Example:** Multi-paragraph escalating routine showing same behavior judged differently based on wealth, building to explosive realization.

### 2. Perspective-Flip Routine

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Take a controversial or one-sided topic and present both perspectives authentically, then flip between them to expose contradictions, common ground, or absurdity. Forces audience to see all angles.

**Rationale:**
- ✓ **Actionable:** Clear structure (present side A → flip to side B → reveal contradiction/absurdity)
- ✓ **Invocable:** Triggered by controversial topics, one-sided arguments, polarized debates
- ✓ **Scoped:** Focused on multi-perspective analysis of single topic
- ✓ **Reusable:** Works for any polarized issue (politics, relationships, social topics)
- ✓ **Valuable:** Breaks echo chambers, reveals nuance in divisive topics

**Extraction Source:** "Key Frameworks to Apply: Perspective Flipping" section

**Input Example:** Debate about whether parents or kids are to blame for bad behavior.

**Output Example:** Routine showing parents' perspective, then kids' perspective, then exposing how both are partially right and full of shit.

### 3. Repetition-Elaboration Amplifier

**Priority:** MEDIUM

**Description:** Take a core observation and circle back to it multiple times with new angles, examples, and variations. Uses "tell them what you'll say, say it, tell them what you said" structure with Rock's comedic spin.

**Rationale:**
- ✓ **Actionable:** Clear pattern (state premise → example 1 → circle back → example 2 → circle back → conclusion)
- ✓ **Invocable:** Triggered when a point needs reinforcement, when complexity needs multiple entry points
- ✓ **Scoped:** Focused on reinforcing single idea through varied repetition
- ✓ **Reusable:** Works for any complex idea that benefits from multiple angles
- ✓ **Valuable:** Makes complex social observations stick through strategic repetition

**Extraction Source:** "Key Frameworks: The Repetition-Elaboration Method" section

**Input Example:** "People judge rich and poor differently for the same actions."

**Output Example:** Multiple scenarios (rich eccentric vs poor shut-in, rich hobby vs poor hoarding, rich retired vs poor lazy) all circling back to central premise.

---

### 4. Hard-Truth Clarification

**Priority:** MEDIUM

**Description:** After making a provocative statement, immediately clarify what you meant versus what people heard, using the clarification to go deeper and sharper rather than soften. Rock's "I'm not saying X... but I understand" technique.

**Rationale:**
- ✓ **Actionable:** Clear two-step (provocative statement → clarifying deeper dive)
- ✓ **Invocable:** Triggered when handling sensitive topics, controversial observations
- ✓ **Scoped:** Focused on clarification-deepening technique
- ✓ **Reusable:** Works for any controversial or easily-misunderstood point
- ✓ **Valuable:** Allows exploration of difficult topics while maintaining nuance

**Extraction Source:** "Key Frameworks: The Clarification Technique" and Signature Techniques section

**Input Example:** "Sometimes people's extreme reactions make sense even when they're wrong."

**Output Example:** "I'm not saying it's right... but I understand" structure exploring the logic behind seemingly irrational behavior.
