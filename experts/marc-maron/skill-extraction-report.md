## Extraction Criteria

Each skill candidate must meet ALL 5 criteria:
- **Actionable** - Clear, repeatable steps
- **Invocable** - Could be triggered by request
- **Scoped** - One responsibility, clear boundaries
- **Reusable** - Applies across contexts
- **Valuable** - Saves significant effort

## Skill Candidates

### 1. Confessional Opening

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Establish intimacy and safety in conversation by leading with personal vulnerability—admitting your own struggles, fears, or failures before asking others to share theirs.

**Trigger Conditions:**
- "How do I open up to someone?"
- "How do I get someone to trust me?"
- "How do I start a difficult conversation?"
- When building rapport before deeper discussion

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Clear steps: identify personal connection, lead with admission, calibrate disclosure level |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered by need to establish intimacy or trust |
| Scoped | Yes | Only handles opening; does not structure full conversation |
| Reusable | Yes | Applies to interviews, therapy, sales, management, relationships |
| Valuable | Yes | Transforms conversation dynamics immediately |

**Output:** Personal disclosure statement calibrated to context that models the vulnerability being requested

### 2. Neurotic Spiral Mapping

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Track and articulate the path of anxious, obsessive, or catastrophic thinking—mapping the worry from trigger through escalation to exhaustion or release.

**Trigger Conditions:**
- "I can't stop worrying about..."
- "My mind keeps going to..."
- "I know this is crazy but I keep thinking..."
- When someone is caught in repetitive anxious thought

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Clear process: identify trigger, trace escalation, find the loop, locate the release |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered by anxiety spirals or obsessive worry |
| Scoped | Yes | Maps the spiral; does not resolve underlying issues |
| Reusable | Yes | Works for any anxiety-driven thought pattern |
| Valuable | Yes | Externalizes internal chaos; makes the invisible visible |

**Output:** Articulated map of the worry spiral with identified trigger, escalation points, and natural release valve

### 3. Wound Interview

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Draw out authentic, unguarded responses by asking about failure, pain, and struggle rather than success and achievement—going to the wound instead of the highlight reel.

**Trigger Conditions:**
- "How do I get someone to really talk?"
- "How do I get past the surface?"
- "They're giving me the PR version"
- When conversation stays shallow despite depth being needed

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Clear technique: identify likely wound, model vulnerability, ask specifically, allow silence |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered when conversation needs depth |
| Scoped | Yes | Specifically for drawing out authentic responses; not general interviewing |
| Reusable | Yes | Works in journalism, management, therapy, research, relationships |
| Valuable | Yes | Bypasses performance to reach genuine material |

**Output:** Specific wound-seeking questions tailored to the person and context

### 4. Recovery Frame Analysis

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Examine patterns of compulsive behavior, avoidance, and coping through the lens of addiction and recovery—identifying what someone is running from and what they're using to run.

**Trigger Conditions:**
- "Why do I keep doing this?"
- "I know it's bad for me but..."
- "I can't stop..."
- When behavior patterns suggest compulsion or avoidance

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Clear framework: identify behavior, find the engine, name the avoidance, examine substitution patterns |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered by compulsive or self-defeating behavior patterns |
| Scoped | Yes | Applies recovery lens; does not provide treatment or therapy |
| Reusable | Yes | Works for any compulsive behavior, not just substance use |
| Valuable | Yes | Provides explanatory framework for self-defeating patterns |

**Output:** Analysis of behavior pattern through recovery framework with identified avoidance and underlying engine

### 5. Uncomfortable Follow-Up Generator

**Priority:** MEDIUM

**Description:** Generate specific follow-up questions that push past surface answers to reach the real material—the question the first answer was designed to avoid.

**Trigger Conditions:**
- When someone gives a polished or rehearsed answer
- "That sounds like a prepared response"
- "I want to go deeper"
- After receiving PR-speak or deflection

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Identify what's being avoided, formulate question that goes there directly |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered by surface-level responses |
| Scoped | Yes | Only generates follow-up; does not structure full interview |
| Reusable | Yes | Works in any interview or conversation context |
| Valuable | Partial | Useful but somewhat depends on interviewer skill |

**Output:** 2-3 specific follow-up questions designed to reach what the surface answer concealed

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|

## Notes

- Maron's methodology is more intuitive than systematic, making skill extraction require some formalization
- The confessional opening is foundational—other skills depend on the trust it creates
- Recovery frame analysis extends beyond addiction to any compulsive or avoidant behavior
- Comedy transmutation requires comedic sensibility that may not be fully procedural
- Skills can chain: confessional opening → wound interview → recovery frame analysis
- Neurotic spiral mapping is particularly useful for people who want to understand their own anxiety patterns
- The "uncomfortable follow-up" is embedded in Maron's interview style but can be extracted as standalone technique
