### HIGH Priority

#### 1. barrel-reduction
**Source Pattern:** The Barrel Reduction Framework
**Purpose:** Systematically eliminate dependencies to discover what is truly necessary
**Trigger:** When someone feels overwhelmed by possessions, commitments, beliefs, or complexity; when simplification is needed
**Inputs:** List of dependencies (possessions, relationships, habits, beliefs, commitments)
**Outputs:** Prioritized list separating necessary from unnecessary; action plan for elimination
**Reasoning:**
- Actionable: Clear 4-step process (List -> Test -> Eliminate -> Repeat)
- Invocable: Triggered by requests like "simplify my life," "what can I cut," "I'm overwhelmed"
- Scoped: One responsibility (dependency elimination), clear boundaries
- Reusable: Applies to physical possessions, digital tools, relationships, beliefs, business processes
- Valuable: Provides decision framework that prevents analysis paralysis around simplification

#### 2. lantern-audit
**Source Pattern:** The Lantern Audit
**Purpose:** Expose gaps between stated values and actual behavior through literal testing
**Trigger:** When organizational/personal values seem performative; when hypocrisy is suspected; when stated culture doesn't match reality
**Inputs:** Stated value or principle to test
**Outputs:** Evidence of alignment or gap; specific instances where value fails in practice
**Reasoning:**
- Actionable: Clear 4-step process (Identify value -> Design literal test -> Perform test -> Document gap)
- Invocable: Triggered by requests like "do we really value X," "test this claim," "audit our culture"
- Scoped: One responsibility (value-behavior gap detection), clear boundaries
- Reusable: Applies to organizations, individuals, marketing claims, political statements
- Valuable: Provides structured method to cut through PR-speak and reveal truth

#### 3. definition-stress-test
**Source Pattern:** The Plucked Chicken method (stress-testing definitions with edge cases)
**Purpose:** Test whether definitions, categories, or criteria are robust by finding absurd edge cases
**Trigger:** When evaluating definitions, taxonomies, hiring criteria, policies, or any categorical boundaries
**Inputs:** Definition or category to test
**Outputs:** Edge cases that expose weaknesses; suggested refinements
**Reasoning:**
- Actionable: Clear process (take definition -> find edge case that technically fits but is absurd -> expose weakness)
- Invocable: Triggered by "does this definition hold up," "test this criteria," "find holes in this policy"
- Scoped: One responsibility (definition robustness testing), clear boundaries
- Reusable: Applies to technical specifications, legal definitions, hiring criteria, taxonomies
- Valuable: Prevents poorly-defined categories from causing problems; catches edge cases before production

### MEDIUM Priority

#### 4. nature-convention-analysis
**Source Pattern:** The Dog's Decision Tree
**Purpose:** Analyze choices by separating natural requirements from social conventions
**Trigger:** When facing decisions where social pressure conflicts with authentic needs
**Inputs:** Decision or dilemma
**Outputs:** Analysis separating natural necessities from social requirements; recommendation
**Reasoning:**
- Actionable: Clear 4-step process (What does nature require -> What does society require -> Where conflict -> Choose nature)
- Invocable: Triggered by "should I follow convention," "is this expectation reasonable," "what's actually necessary here"
- Scoped: One responsibility (nature vs. convention analysis)
- Reusable: Applies to personal decisions, business practices, social expectations
- Valuable: Somewhat valuable but more philosophical than practical; the "always choose nature" conclusion may be too rigid for many contexts
