# Jamie Dimon

> "You can't be prepared after the fact. You've got to be prepared before the fact."

---

**James "Jamie" Dimon** (1956-present) is the chairman and CEO of JPMorgan Chase, the largest bank in the United States by assets. He built the concept of the "fortress balance sheet" into an operating philosophy that enabled JPMorgan to not just survive the 2008 financial crisis but to emerge stronger, acquiring Bear Stearns and Washington Mutual while competitors collapsed or required bailouts.

---

## The Banking Executive's Arc

### The Weill Years (1982-1998)

Fresh from Harvard Business School, Dimon became Sandy Weill's right hand at American Express, then followed him through a series of acquisitions that built Travelers Group and eventually merged it with Citicorp to create Citigroup. For sixteen years, Dimon learned banking through consolidation - how to integrate operations, cut costs, and build scale.

*Key insight:* Acquisitions succeed or fail on integration. The deal is just the beginning; execution determines value.

### The Exile and Return (1998-2004)

In 1998, Dimon was abruptly fired from Citigroup after a falling out with Weill. He spent eighteen months on the sidelines before accepting the CEO role at Bank One, a struggling Chicago-based bank. He turned it around through operational discipline, talent upgrades, and honest assessment of problems.

*Key insight:* When you get knocked down, use the time to reflect and prepare. Dimon emerged from exile with a clearer philosophy and stronger resolve.

### The Crisis Test (2008)

When the financial system collapsed, Dimon's JPMorgan had a fortress balance sheet - capital and liquidity far exceeding regulatory requirements. This strength enabled action when others were paralyzed. In March 2008, JPMorgan acquired Bear Stearns for $10 per share (down from $170 a year earlier). In September, they bought Washington Mutual for $1.9 billion after the largest bank failure in U.S. history.

*Key insight:* "We were not buying a house - we were buying a house on fire." Crisis acquisitions require massive margins for error and strength to absorb unknown liabilities.

### The Long Game (2008-Present)

The crisis acquisitions ultimately cost nearly $19 billion in legal settlements - far more than the purchase prices. Dimon has said he would not repeat the Bear Stearns deal. Yet JPMorgan emerged as the dominant American bank, with market capitalization exceeding its rivals. The fortress philosophy proved its worth.

*Key insight:* Building strength during good times is the price of acting decisively during bad times. The fortress must exist before the siege begins.
