# Paul Tillich: Expertise and Knowledge Base

## Biographical Overview

**Full Name:** Paul Johannes Tillich
**Born:** August 20, 1886, Starzeddel, Province of Brandenburg, Germany
**Died:** October 22, 1965, Chicago, Illinois, USA
**Nationality:** German-American (emigrated 1933)
**Known For:** Systematic Theology, Method of Correlation, "Ground of Being," Ultimate Concern, The Courage to Be, Religious Socialism, Theology of Culture

---

## Intellectual Biography

### Early Formation (1886-1914)

- Born in Starzeddel, a small village in Brandenburg, Prussia
- Father Johannes Tillich was a conservative Lutheran pastor
- Mother Mathilde Durselen came from the more liberal Rhineland
- Childhood in Schonfliess fostered "a strong sense of historical continuity" and deep religious commitment
- Early fascination with philosophy, particularly German Idealism
- Studied at University of Berlin, Tubingen, and Halle (1905-1912)
- PhD in philosophy from University of Breslau (1911) - dissertation on Schelling
- Licentiate in theology from University of Halle (1912)
- Ordained as Lutheran minister (1912)

### World War I and Aftermath (1914-1924)

- Served as military chaplain in German army (1914-1918)
- Four years at the front profoundly shattered his idealism
- Experienced spiritual crisis - "the traditional approach to God was no longer possible"
- After the war, became active in religious socialism movement
- Published "Basic Principles of Religious Socialism" (1923) - foundational text
- Developed concept of "kairos" - the fulfilled moment of historical possibility

### German Academic Period (1924-1933)

- Teaching positions at Berlin, Marburg, Dresden, Leipzig, and Frankfurt universities
- Participated eagerly in discussion groups seeking new understanding of human situation
- Published over 100 essays, articles, and reviews (1919-1933)
- Wrote *The Socialist Decision* (1933) - which he considered his best work
- Dismissed from Frankfurt position by Nazis in 1933 for political views
- First non-Jewish academic dismissed by Nazi regime

### American Period (1933-1965)

- Emigrated to United States in October 1933 at age 47
- Began teaching at Union Theological Seminary, New York (February 1934)
- Struggled initially with English language
- Became naturalized American citizen
- Union Theological Seminary (1934-1955) - rose to full professor
- Harvard University (1955-1962) - University Professor
- University of Chicago (1962-1965) - John Nuveen Professor of Theology
- Died October 22, 1965, ten days after suffering a heart attack

### The Systematic Theology Structure

**Volume I: Reason and Revelation; Being and God**
- Method of correlation introduced
- Ontological approach to God as Being-itself
- Critique of natural theology and supernaturalism

**Volume II: Existence and The Christ**
- Analysis of estrangement and existential predicament
- Christ as the New Being overcoming estrangement
- Christology as answer to human existence questions

**Volume III: Life and the Spirit; History and Kingdom of God**
- Analysis of life's ambiguities
- The Spirit as presence of the divine
- Philosophy of history and eschatology

## Key Concepts Glossary

| Concept | Definition |
|---------|------------|
| Ground of Being (Being-itself) | God is not a being but the power and ground of all being |
| Ultimate Concern | Faith as being grasped by what concerns us ultimately |
| Method of Correlation | Theology correlates existential questions with revelatory answers |
| Protestant Principle | Prophetic judgment against elevating finite to ultimate status |
| Catholic Substance | Affirmation of sacramental presence and concrete religious embodiment |
| Kairos | The "right time" - fulfilled moment when eternal breaks into temporal |
| Theonomy | Autonomy and heteronomy reconciled through divine depth |
| The New Being | Overcoming of estrangement manifested in Christ |
| Estrangement | Separation from ground of being, others, and self |
| Courage to Be | Self-affirmation of being despite threat of nonbeing |
| God above God | Ground of being beyond theistic God who can be doubted |
| Symbol | Participates in the reality to which it points (vs. mere sign) |
| Demonic | Elevation of finite to claim of ultimate, unconditional status |
| Depth of Reason | Transcendent ground that precedes reason and manifests through it |

## The Three Forms of Anxiety

| Type | Relative Form | Absolute Form | Domain |
|------|--------------|---------------|--------|
| Ontic | Fate | Death | Existence itself |
| Moral | Guilt | Condemnation | Moral self-affirmation |
| Spiritual | Emptiness | Meaninglessness | Participation in meaning |

### Anxiety vs. Fear

- **Fear** has a definite object that can be faced, attacked, or endured
- **Anxiety** has no object - it is directed toward nothingness, toward the threat of nonbeing itself
- Existential anxiety is not neurotic but belongs to the structure of finite existence
- Neurotic anxiety is the attempt to avoid existential anxiety

## The Method of Correlation

### Structure

1. **Philosophy analyzes** the human situation and formulates questions implied in existence
2. **Theology demonstrates** that the symbols of Christian message are answers to these questions
3. **Questions shape the form** of theological answers (though not their content)
4. **Content comes from revelation** - cannot be derived from questions alone

### Five Correlations in Systematic Theology

| Human Question | Theological Answer |
|----------------|-------------------|
| Reason and its limits | Revelation |
| Being and finitude | God as Being-itself |
| Existence and estrangement | Christ as New Being |
| Life and its ambiguities | Spirit as divine presence |
| History and its meaning | Kingdom of God |

## Historical Context

### Weimar Republic and Religious Socialism

- Active participant in Germany's religious socialism movement
- Deeply conversant with Marxist theory and politics
- Sought synthesis of Christian eschatology and socialist critique
- "Basic Principles of Religious Socialism" (1923) - foundational text
- *The Socialist Decision* (1933) - argued necessity of socialism vs. fascism

### Opposition to Nazism

- First non-Jewish academic dismissed from position by Nazis (1933)
- *The Socialist Decision* was banned and burned
- Emigration to America not exile of choice but necessity

### American Influence

- Made existentialist theology accessible to English-speaking world
- *The Courage to Be* and *Dynamics of Faith* reached large public audience
- Influenced Martin Luther King Jr. (who studied with Tillich at Boston University)
- Paved way for dialogue between theology and depth psychology

## Philosophical Influences

### German Idealism

- **Schelling** - major influence; dissertation and lifelong engagement
- **Hegel** - dialectical method, though critically appropriated
- Concept of reconciliation of opposites

### Existentialism

- **Kierkegaard** - existence as decision, anxiety, faith
- **Heidegger** - ontological analysis, being and nonbeing, finitude
- **Nietzsche** - death of God, courage, will to power

### Religious Thought

- **Luther** - justification by grace through faith; "Protestant principle"
- **Meister Eckhart** - mystical depth, God beyond God
- **Nicholas of Cusa** - coincidentia oppositorum

## Scholarly Assessment

### Contributions

- Made existentialist theology accessible to wider audience
- Bridged philosophy and theology systematically
- Provided framework for engaging secular culture theologically
- Influential theory of religious symbols
- Analysis of anxiety and courage remains psychologically relevant

### Critiques

- Some consider theology too philosophical, not biblical enough
- "Ground of Being" language criticized as impersonal
- Method of correlation accused of subordinating revelation to philosophy
- Personal life controversies emerged posthumously (affairs, complex sexuality)
- Some evangelicals reject ontological approach to God

### Legacy Position

- One of most influential Protestant theologians of 20th century
- Work continues to influence philosophy of religion, depth psychology
- "Secularity and church" dialogue owes much to his thought
- Remains important resource for those at boundary of faith and doubt
