## Career Timeline

### Early Years and Education (1962-1984)
- Born in Berwyn, Illinois, second of seven children
- Grew up in Naperville, Illinois, suburb of Chicago
- Father was an alcoholic; difficult family circumstances shaped his comedy as escape
- Attended Naperville North High School, graduated at age 16
- Attended College of DuPage in Glen Ellyn, Illinois (one year)
- Transferred to Marquette University in Milwaukee, Wisconsin
- Transferred again to Southern Illinois University in Carbondale
- Created late-night radio comedy show "The Prime Time Special" at WIDB (midnight to 4 AM)
- Left three credits short of graduating to pursue comedy in Chicago
- Completed credits at Columbia College Chicago
- Received bachelor's degree in communications from SIU in 1984

### Chicago Improv Training (1984-1987)
- First studied with Del Close, legendary improv guru
- Attended the Players Workshop where he met Robert Smigel
- Performed at ImprovOlympic alongside Chris Farley and Tim Meadows
- Multiple stints at Second City (was "fired five times" by his own joking count)
- Meeting Del Close convinced him a career in arts was feasible
- Odenkirk recalled Close was "the first time I'd heard an old person excited about what they were doing next"
- Learned to apply improv philosophy to written sketch comedy

### SNL and Early Writing Career (1987-1995)
- Hired as writer for Saturday Night Live in 1987
- Won Emmy Award for writing on SNL in 1989
- Briefly shared an office with Ben Stiller at SNL
- During summer hiatus from SNL, wrote and acted in Second City Mainstage show "Flag Burning Permitted in Lobby Only"
- Created "Matt Foley: Motivational Speaker" character for Chris Farley at Second City
- Matt Foley debuted on SNL May 8, 1993 (after Odenkirk had left)
- Robert Smigel helped adapt Matt Foley for television, adding the coffee table fall ending
- Matt Foley appeared in eight SNL sketches; feature film was planned before Farley's death in 1997
- Cast of The Ben Stiller Show (1992-1993) alongside Janeane Garofalo and Andy Dick
- Created and starred in memorable sketch "Manson Lassie" on Ben Stiller Show
- Won Emmy for Outstanding Writing for The Ben Stiller Show in 1993 despite show's cancellation

### Mr. Show Era (1995-1998)
- Co-created Mr. Show with Bob and David with David Cross
- Four seasons on HBO (November 3, 1995 - December 28, 1998)
- Show originated from live comedy performances at Los Angeles comedy clubs
- Manager Bernie Brillstein helped sell show to HBO
- HBO's Carolyn Strauss saw their live show, brought it to president Chris Albrecht
- Pioneered interconnected sketch format with surprising transitions
- Cross and Odenkirk introduced episodes as semi-fictionalized versions of themselves
- Mixed on-stage sketches (live audience) with pre-taped segments
- Drew performers and writers from alternative comedy scene
- Served as career springboard for many up-and-coming comedians
- Cult following despite modest ratings
- Netflix revival "W/ Bob & David" (2015) - four episodes

### Tim and Eric Collaboration (2000s-2010s)
- Tim Heidecker and Eric Wareheim mailed tape of sketches to comedians they admired
- Odenkirk responded with enthusiasm, agreed to collaborate
- Executive produced "Tom Goes to the Mayor" - sold it to Cartoon Network
- Creative consultant on "Tim and Eric Awesome Show, Great Job!"
- Appeared as "Pep Pep" and other characters throughout the series
- Mentored Heidecker and Wareheim's careers
- Helped establish Adult Swim's distinctive aesthetic

### Breaking Bad and Dramatic Breakthrough (2009-2013)
- Cast as Saul Goodman in Breaking Bad
- Initially guest actor for only four episodes
- Vince Gilligan and Peter Gould were impressed by performance
- Joined main cast in Season 3, remained through Series finale (Season 5)
- Character became fan favorite
- Odenkirk speculated character's popularity came from being "the program's least hypocritical figure" who "is good at his job"
- Demonstrated dramatic range beneath comedy persona

### Better Call Saul and Peak Recognition (2015-2022)
- Lead role as Jimmy McGill/Saul Goodman
- Six seasons depicting Saul's origins and moral deterioration
- Post-Breaking Bad storyline as "Gene Takavic"
- Received six nominations for Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actor in a Drama Series
- Ties record for most nominations in category without a win
- Proved comedy actors can lead prestige drama

### Action Star Reinvention and Health Crisis (2021-Present)
- Starred in Nobody (2021) as action lead Hutch Mansell
- Directed by Ilya Naishuller, screenplay by John Wick creator Derek Kolstad
- Trained extensively for stunts and fight choreography
- July 27, 2021: Suffered "widow-maker" heart attack on set of Better Call Saul final season
- Training for Nobody helped him survive: "I have almost no scarring on my heart"
- Working out enlarged veins around heart, allowing more blood flow during CPR
- Made full recovery, completed final season of Better Call Saul
- Published memoir "Comedy Comedy Comedy Drama" (January 18, 2022, Random House)
- Memoir became New York Times bestseller
- Published children's book "Zilot & Other Important Rhymes" (2023) with daughter Erin's illustrations
- Son Nate Odenkirk is comedic writer featured in The New Yorker

## Personal Philosophy and Quotes

### On Comedy as Truth

"Comedy is about being honest. It can be a brutal honesty, but it's some way of telling a truth about people."

"All people are sad clowns. That is the key to comedy - and it's a buffer against reality."

### On the Creative Process

"I'm hypersensitive to negativity and duplicity, and I want to push it away by writing comedy. Maybe that hypersensitivity comes across and allows me to play dastardly, multi-layered people."

"One trick for surviving Hollywood's beatdown is to keep making new things in spite of every 'no.' To somehow stay in touch with the joy that brought you into the game."

### On Comedy vs. Drama

"Drama is more focused and it reveals itself to you, whereas comedy is just right there, when you first read it."

"Comedy is my first love, it's natural for me to get earnest and honest. I have comedy friends who congenitally can't do that, but I can."

### On Satirical Philosophy

"Laughter and discomfort should exist side by side."

## Core Methodology

### The Commitment Principle
Total sincerity in absurd premises creates comedy through the gap between delivery and content. The performer never acknowledges the absurdity. The audience laughs because the character takes it seriously.

### Structural Experimentation
Traditional sketch format understood well enough to be subverted. Mr. Show pioneered interconnected sketches where one led into the next through surprising transitions, similar to Monty Python's Flying Circus but with American sensibility.

### Character Psychology
Even broad comic characters have genuine internal logic. Understanding what a character truly wants makes every choice funnier and more sustainable. Jimmy McGill wants legitimacy and respect; Matt Foley wants to save these kids from his own fate.

### Earned Weirdness
Absurdism that follows from premises taken to logical extremes, not random chaos for its own sake. Each strange element must serve the piece. The alternative was never anti-comedy; it was anti-lazy-comedy.

### The Second Draft Principle
First ideas are obvious ideas. Real comedy writing happens when you push past initial concepts to find what surprises you. Write past where you think you are done.

### Collaborative Friction
The best work comes from creative partners who challenge each other. Different perspectives create stronger material, but collaboration requires ego management.

## Key Frameworks

### Sketch Comedy Architecture
1. **Premise establishment** - What is the world of this sketch?
2. **Point of view** - Whose experience grounds the comedy?
3. **Escalation pattern** - How does it build?
4. **Surprise destination** - Where does it go that we did not expect?
5. **Commitment level** - How sincere is the performance?

### Character Development for Comedy
1. **Genuine want** - What does this person actually need?
2. **Contradiction** - What about them works against their goal?
3. **Dignity** - How do they see themselves?
4. **Hidden depth** - What pain or history drives them?
5. **Sustainability** - Can this character support multiple scenes or a series?

### Alternative Comedy Principles
- Reject lazy jokes, not jokes themselves
- Surprise yourself before trying to surprise audience
- Smart can also be funny; weird can also be smart
- Subvert expectations but not randomly
- Trust audience intelligence
- Commercial failure does not invalidate artistic success

### The Transition Method (Mr. Show Innovation)
- One sketch leads directly into the next through unexpected connection
- A prop, phrase, or character bridges the gap
- Creates cumulative meaning across an episode
- Rewards attentive viewing
- Influenced Adult Swim aesthetic and subsequent sketch comedy
