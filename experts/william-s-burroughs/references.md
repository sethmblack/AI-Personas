# William S. Burroughs - References

This document provides verified sources for the methodologies, concepts, and quotes referenced in the William S. Burroughs expert persona.

## Primary Works

### Major Novels

- **Junkie** (1953). Ace Books. Originally published under the pen name William Lee as *Junkie: Confessions of an Unredeemed Drug Addict*. Republished as *Junky: The Definitive Text of "Junk"* (2003), edited by Oliver Harris.

- **Naked Lunch** (1959). Olympia Press (Paris); Grove Press (US, 1962). Subject of one of the last major literary censorship cases in the United States. Restored Text edition edited by Barry Miles and James Grauerholz (2003).

- **Queer** (written 1951-1953, published 1985). Viking Press. 25th Anniversary Edition (2010), edited by Oliver Harris.

### The Nova Trilogy (Cut-Up Trilogy)

- **The Soft Machine** (1961). Olympia Press. Multiple revised editions published subsequently.

- **The Ticket That Exploded** (1962). Olympia Press. Revised edition 1967.

- **Nova Express** (1964). Grove Press.

### The Red Night Trilogy

- **Cities of the Red Night** (1981). Holt, Rinehart and Winston.

- **The Place of Dead Roads** (1983). Holt, Rinehart and Winston.

- **The Western Lands** (1987). Viking Press.

### Other Significant Works

- **The Yage Letters** (1963). City Lights Books. Correspondence with Allen Ginsberg. Redux edition (2006), edited by Oliver Harris.

- **The Wild Boys: A Book of the Dead** (1971). Grove Press.

- **Exterminator!** (1973). Viking Press.

- **Port of Saints** (1973). Covent Garden Press; revised edition 1980.

- **The Cat Inside** (1992). Viking Press.

- **My Education: A Book of Dreams** (1995). Viking Press.

- **And the Hippos Were Boiled in Their Tanks** (2008). Grove Press. Collaborative novel with Jack Kerouac, written 1945.

## Cut-Up Theory and Practice

### Collaborative Works with Brion Gysin

- **Minutes to Go** (1960). Two Cities Editions (Paris). With Brion Gysin, Sinclair Beiles, and Gregory Corso. First published cut-up experiments.

- **The Exterminator** (1960). Auerhahn Press (San Francisco). With Brion Gysin.

- **The Third Mind** (1978). Viking Press. French edition 1977 (Editions Flammarion). Historical collection of cut-up experiments from 1960-1978 and manifesto on the cut-up's significance. Articulates the concept that creative collaboration generates a "third consciousness" transcending individual capabilities.

### Theoretical Writings

- **The Electronic Revolution** (1970). Expanded Media Editions (West Germany); second edition 1971 (Cambridge, England) with French translation by Henri Chopin. Extends cut-up technique into blueprint for psychological and political disruption via electronic media; advances theory of language as virus and word as viral mechanism.

- **The Job: Interviews with William S. Burroughs** (1969/1970). Daniel Odier, interviewer. Editions Pierre Belfond (Paris, 1969); Grove Press (US, 1970); Jonathan Cape (UK, 1970). Revised and enlarged edition (1974) includes "Playback from Eden to Watergate" and "Electronic Revolution." Contains Burroughs' explanations of abstract ideas from his novels and thoughts on Scientology, power, and control.

- **The Adding Machine: Collected Essays** (1985). John Calder (UK); Grove Press (US, 1986). Essays covering thirty years of writing on literature, the cut-up method, coincidence, control, and Burroughs' influences including Hemingway, Fitzgerald, Conrad, Kerouac, and Ginsberg.

### Films with Antony Balch

- **Towers Open Fire** (1963). Short film applying cut-up technique to visual media.

- **The Cut-Ups** (1966). Experimental film demonstrating cut-up method applied to film.

- **Bill and Tony** (1972). Short experimental film.

## Biographies

- **Morgan, Ted. *Literary Outlaw: The Life and Times of William S. Burroughs*** (1988). Henry Holt and Company. The most comprehensive biography, covering Burroughs' life from his St. Louis upbringing through his travels, the accidental shooting of his wife Joan Vollmer, his relationships with Kerouac, Ginsberg, and Gysin, and his literary innovations.

- **Miles, Barry. *William Burroughs: El Hombre Invisible*** (1992). Virgin Books. Concise biography providing accessible introduction to Burroughs' life and work.

- **Miles, Barry. *The Beat Hotel: Ginsberg, Burroughs, and Corso in Paris, 1958-1963*** (2000). Grove Press. Documents the Paris period when Burroughs and Gysin developed the cut-up technique at the legendary Beat Hotel (9 rue Git-le-Coeur).

- **Harris, Oliver. *William Burroughs and the Secret of Fascination*** (2003). Southern Illinois University Press. Critical biography examining Burroughs' compositional methods and the making of his major works.

### Anthologies and Readers

- **Grauerholz, James and Ira Silverberg, eds. *Word Virus: The William S. Burroughs Reader*** (1998). Grove Press. Introduction by Ann Douglas. First comprehensive compilation covering all phases of Burroughs' career, with biographical and textual background essays by Grauerholz, Burroughs' longtime friend and literary executor.

## Critical Studies

### Book-Length Studies

- **Skerl, Jennie. *William S. Burroughs*** (1985). Twayne Publishers (Twayne's United States Authors Series). First major critical study arguing Burroughs' work should be understood within hipsterism as a world-view; helped establish scholarly legitimacy of post-*Naked Lunch* works.

- **Lydenberg, Robin. *Word Cultures: Radical Theory and Practice in William S. Burroughs' Fiction*** (1987). University of Illinois Press. Pioneering study demonstrating that ideas characteristic of post-structuralism and deconstruction were developed independently by Burroughs. Includes chapters on *Naked Lunch*, the Nova Trilogy, and the negative poetics of the cut-up method.

- **Skerl, Jennie and Robin Lydenberg, eds. *William S. Burroughs at the Front: Critical Reception, 1959-1989*** (1991). Southern Illinois University Press. Twenty-five critical essays reflecting historical reception, including contributions from Mary McCarthy, Marshall McLuhan, Cary Nelson, Tony Tanner, and Ihab Hassan.

- **Murphy, Timothy S., ed. *Retaking the Universe: William S. Burroughs in the Age of Globalization*** (2004). Pluto Press. Landmark collection marking new era in Burroughs criticism with ambitious scope of analysis.

- **Robinson, Edward S. *Shift Linguals: Cut-Up Narratives from William S. Burroughs to the Present*** (2011). Rodopi (Postmodern Studies 46). First sustained analysis of cut-ups as narrative form, exploring works of Burroughs, Gysin, Kathy Acker, and John Giorno within frameworks of postmodernism and the avant-garde.

- **Miller, Gerald Alva Jr. *Understanding William S. Burroughs*** (2014). University of South Carolina Press. Argues Burroughs ushered in the era of both postmodern fiction and poststructural philosophy.

- **Harris, Oliver and Ian MacFadyen, eds. *William S. Burroughs Cutting Up the Century*** (2019). Indiana University Press. Essays on vocal performance, collaboration, imperial decadence, and transcendence in Burroughs' work.

### Key Critical Essays and Perspectives

- **Jameson, Fredric.** Places Burroughs among leading exponents of postmodern literature alongside Thomas Pynchon and Ishmael Reed.

- **Mottram, Eric. *William Burroughs: The Algebra of Need*** (1971). Intrepid Press. One of the earliest book-length critical studies.

- **Vernon, John.** Examines schizophrenic nature of reality in Burroughs' work, emphasizing dichotomy between mechanical and organic structures.

### Online Resources

- **RealityStudio** (realitystudio.org). Comprehensive scholarly resource for Burroughs studies, featuring texts, interviews, bibliographies, and critical essays.

## Documentaries

- **Burroughs: The Movie** (1983). Directed by Howard Brookner. The first documentary made with Burroughs' full participation. Shot by Tom DiCillo with sound by Jim Jarmusch. Features Allen Ginsberg, Brion Gysin, Francis Bacon, Herbert Huncke, Patti Smith, Terry Southern, and Lauren Hutton. Premiered at New York Film Festival 1983. Restored and released by Criterion Collection (2015), Edition #789.

- **Commissioner of Sewers** (1991). Directed by Klaus Maeck. Hour-long interview with Burroughs expounding on American culture, art, and morals. Features extensive interview with German writer Jurgen Ploog discussing cut-ups, language as weapon, word as virus, dreams, and time-space travel. Includes footage from Burroughs' last European reading in Berlin (May 9, 1986) and clips from *Decoder* and *Drugstore Cowboy*.

- **William S. Burroughs: A Man Within** (2010). Directed by Yony Leyser. Features never-before-seen footage and interviews with John Waters, Genesis P-Orridge, Laurie Anderson, Peter Weller, David Cronenberg, Iggy Pop, Gus Van Sant, Sonic Youth, Anne Waldman, Amiri Baraka, and Jello Biafra. Narration by Peter Weller, soundtrack by Sonic Youth. Broadcast on PBS Independent Lens.

- **Arena: Burroughs** (1983). BBC Arena documentary. UK television premiere.

## Key Quotes - Source Verification

### "Language is a virus from outer space."
This phrase appears throughout Burroughs' work and interviews. Elaborated in *The Ticket That Exploded* (1962) and *The Electronic Revolution* (1970), where Burroughs develops the theory that the word is a parasitic organism that restructured human consciousness.

### "When you cut into the present the future leaks out."
From cut-up experiments and interviews. Burroughs suggested cut-ups may be effective as divination, as documented in accounts of his collaboration with Brion Gysin and in *The Third Mind* (1978).

### "The face of evil is always the face of total need."
From the introduction to *Naked Lunch* (1959), articulating the "algebra of need" concept central to Burroughs' analysis of addiction and control.

### "A paranoid is someone who knows a little of what's going on."
Widely attributed to Burroughs from interviews and essays, reflecting his characteristic blend of dark humor and conspiracy awareness.

## Related Figures

- **Brion Gysin** (1916-1986). Painter, writer, and collaborator who accidentally discovered the cut-up technique at the Beat Hotel in 1957 and introduced it to Burroughs.

- **Allen Ginsberg** (1926-1997). Fellow Beat Generation writer and lifelong friend; collaborated on *The Yage Letters*.

- **Jack Kerouac** (1922-1969). Beat Generation novelist who called Burroughs "the greatest satirical writer since Jonathan Swift"; collaborated on *And the Hippos Were Boiled in Their Tanks*.

- **James Grauerholz**. Burroughs' longtime friend, manager, and literary executor; co-editor of posthumous editions and *Word Virus*.
