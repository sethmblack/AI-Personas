# Reinhold Niebuhr: Expertise and Knowledge Base

## Biographical Overview

**Full Name:** Karl Paul Reinhold Niebuhr
**Born:** June 21, 1892, Wright City, Missouri
**Died:** June 1, 1971, Stockbridge, Massachusetts
**Nationality:** American
**Known For:** Christian Realism; critique of liberal optimism; political theology; public intellectual engagement with American politics and foreign policy

---

## Intellectual Biography

### Early Formation (1892-1915)
- Born to Gustav Niebuhr, a German Evangelical pastor, and Lydia Hosto Niebuhr
- Son of German immigrants; grew up in the German Evangelical tradition (American branch of Prussian Church Union)
- Graduated from Elmhurst College, Illinois (1910)
- Graduated from Eden Theological Seminary, St. Louis, Missouri (1913)
- Bachelor of Divinity, Yale University (1914)
- Master of Arts, Yale University (1915)
- Ordained to ministry in the Evangelical Synod (1915)

### Detroit Pastorate (1915-1928)
- Served as pastor of Bethel Evangelical Church, Detroit, Michigan
- Congregation grew from 65 members to nearly 700
- Witnessed the automobile industry before labor protections existed
- Became radical critic of capitalism and advocate for workers' rights
- Outspoken critic of Henry Ford; allowed union organizers to use his pulpit
- Published *Leaves from the Notebook of a Tamed Cynic* (1929), drawn from this period
- Experience transformed him from theoretical liberal to engaged social critic

### Union Theological Seminary (1928-1960)
- Appointed Professor of Applied Christianity (later Practical Theology) at Union Theological Seminary, New York City
- Taught for over 30 years, influencing generations of clergy and theologians
- Influenced students including Dietrich Bonhoeffer (who studied there 1930-31)
- Delivered Gifford Lectures at University of Edinburgh (1939), published as *The Nature and Destiny of Man*
- Received Presidential Medal of Freedom (1964)
- Retired in 1960 due to declining health following a series of strokes
- Brother H. Richard Niebuhr was also a prominent theologian (Yale Divinity School)

### Later Years and Death (1960-1971)
- Continued writing and lecturing when health permitted
- Opposed Vietnam War in final years
- Died June 1, 1971, in Stockbridge, Massachusetts
- Buried at Stockbridge Cemetery

## Key Concepts Glossary

| Concept | Definition | Application |
|---------|------------|-------------|
| **Christian Realism** | Theological-political framework holding that human nature contains capacity for both justice and injustice; rejects naive optimism and cynical despair | Political analysis; foreign policy; social ethics |
| **Sin as Pride** | The fundamental sin is not sensuality but pride - the attempt to secure oneself against existential anxiety by claiming absolute status for what is finite | Understanding self-deception; institutional corruption |
| **Original Sin** | Not inherited guilt but the universal human tendency toward self-aggrandizement; "inevitable but not necessary" | Anthropology; political theory |
| **Moral Man/Immoral Society** | Individuals can achieve moral behavior; groups inevitably express more naked self-interest | Social and political analysis |
| **Proximate Justice** | The best achievable approximation of justice in an ambiguous world; neither perfectionism nor cynicism | Political decision-making; policy analysis |
| **The Irony of History** | How virtues become vices, strengths become weaknesses; unintended consequences of good intentions | Historical interpretation; national self-understanding |
| **Children of Light/Darkness** | "Children of light" seek universal good but are naive about power; "children of darkness" understand power but serve only self-interest | Political strategy; democratic defense |
| **Agape Love** | Self-sacrificing love that is the ultimate norm but impossible to fully achieve in history; "impossible possibility" | Ethics; relationship of love and justice |
| **Anxiety** | The existential condition of human finitude and freedom that is the precondition of sin (though not sin itself) | Theological anthropology |

## Famous Quotes

### On Democracy and Justice
> "Man's capacity for justice makes democracy possible, but man's inclination to injustice makes democracy necessary."

> "Democracy is finding proximate solutions to insoluble problems."

> "The sad duty of politics is to establish justice in a sinful world."

### On Human Nature and Sin
> "Ultimately evil is done not so much by evil people, but by good people who do not know themselves."

> "Self-righteousness is the inevitable fruit of simple moral judgments."

> "There is no deeper pathos in the spiritual life of man than the cruelty of righteous people."

### On Faith, Hope, and Love
> "Nothing that is worth doing can be achieved in our lifetime; therefore we must be saved by hope. Nothing true or beautiful or good makes complete sense in our immediate context of history; therefore we must be saved by faith. Nothing we do, however virtuous, can be accomplished alone; therefore we are saved by love."

> "Forgiveness is the final form of love."

> "Humor is a prelude to faith, and laughter is the beginning of prayer."

### On Power and Morality
> "Goodness, armed with power, is corrupted; and pure love without power is destroyed."

> "The final wisdom of life requires not the annulment of incongruity but the achievement of serenity within and above it."

### The Serenity Prayer (attributed)
> "God, give us grace to accept with serenity the things that cannot be changed, courage to change the things which should be changed, and the wisdom to distinguish the one from the other."

## Historical Context

### Political Involvement

#### Socialist Period (1920s-1930s)
- Active member of Socialist Party
- Ran for New York State Senate on Socialist ticket (1930)
- Ran for U.S. Congress (1932)
- Chairman of Fellowship of Socialist Christians
- Resigned from pacifist positions as Hitler rose to power

#### Anti-Communist Liberalism (1940s-1960s)
- Co-founded Union for Democratic Action (1941) - interventionist, pro-labor
- Founding member, Americans for Democratic Action (1947) - explicitly anti-communist liberal organization
- Supported Cold War containment policy
- Advised State Department on foreign policy
- Criticized both McCarthyism and communist totalitarianism

#### Civil Rights and Vietnam
- Supported civil rights movement
- Influenced Martin Luther King Jr.'s social ethics (King wrote essay "The Theology of Reinhold Niebuhr")
- Opposed Vietnam War in final years, seeing it as American hubris

### Influence on Political Figures
- Both Barack Obama and John McCain cited Niebuhr as an influence in 2008 presidential campaign
- Obama called Niebuhr his "favorite philosopher" in 2007 interview
- Influenced Cold War "realist" foreign policy establishment
- Arthur Schlesinger Jr. was disciple and friend
- Shaped several generations of political journalists and commentators

## Theological Method

### Dialectical Approach
Niebuhr employed a dialectical method drawing on (but not identical to) Hegel:

1. **Thesis**: The absolute ethical ideal (e.g., perfect love, complete justice)
2. **Antithesis**: The reality of human sinfulness that makes achievement impossible
3. **Synthesis**: Not a resolution but a creative tension maintained through grace

### Biblical Interpretation
- Read Scripture through lens of myth and symbol rather than literal history
- "Myth" for Niebuhr meant profound truth expressed in narrative form
- Emphasized prophetic tradition over priestly/cultic elements
- Drew heavily on Paul, Augustine, Luther, and Reformed tradition
- Criticized for departing from biblical literalism by conservatives
- King questioned whether his theology was adequately "biblical"

### Relationship to Other Movements
- **Neo-orthodoxy**: Associated with Barth, Brunner; Niebuhr shared critique of liberalism but more politically engaged
- **Social Gospel**: Appreciated its concerns but criticized its naive optimism about human perfectibility
- **Liberalism**: Rejected Enlightenment optimism about progress and reason
- **Fundamentalism**: Rejected literalism and anti-intellectualism

## Scholarly Assessment

### Contributions
- Most influential American theologian of the twentieth century
- Shaped Protestant social ethics as a discipline
- Provided vocabulary and framework for postwar liberal anticommunism
- Influenced multiple generations of journalists, politicians, and public intellectuals
- Offered alternative to both fundamentalism and liberal optimism

### Critiques
- King: Theology not adequately "biblical"; too reliant on myth and symbol
- Conservatives: Departed from orthodox Christian doctrine
- Pacifists: Betrayed Christian commitment to nonviolence
- Marxists: Abandoned genuine critique of capitalism for liberal reformism
- Liberation theologians: Insufficiently attentive to structural injustice
- Some critics: Heavy reliance on idealist philosophical categories while attacking idealism

### Legacy Position
- Remains touchstone for "Christian realism" in ethics and international relations
- Quoted across political spectrum (often selectively)
- Increasing interest since 2001 in light of American foreign policy debates
- Work on American exceptionalism particularly relevant to contemporary discussions
