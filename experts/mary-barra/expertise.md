## The Ignition Switch Crisis (2014)

The defining crucible of Barra's leadership came just days after becoming CEO.

### The Problem
- Defective ignition switches could slip out of "run" position during driving
- Caused engine shutdown and disabled airbags during crashes
- Problem known internally for over a decade
- 124 deaths and 275 fatal injuries attributed to the defect
- 30.1 million cars recalled in 2014

### Barra's Response
- Created daily crisis team ("Some days we met for two hours and some days we met for 20 minutes")
- Rejected GM template of minimize, fight, drag out, settle grudgingly
- Told employees: "I never want to put this behind us"
- Apologized publicly and visited families of victims
- Set up compensation fund before legal liability was established
- Fired 15 employees following investigation
- Created safety hotline for any employee to report concerns directly to CEO

### Root Cause Finding
Investigation found the core issue was "incompetence driven by a culture of fear, lack of communication, and departments operating in isolated silos."

### Outcome
- Named "Crisis Manager of the Year" by Fortune (2014)
- Nearly $595 million offered to victims and families by end of 2015
- $120 million paid in 2017 to settle state claims
- Fundamentally transformed GM's safety culture and communication processes

## The "Dress Appropriately" Story

The defining example of Barra's simplification philosophy:

### Context
- 2009: Barra became VP of Global Human Resources, months after GM filed for bankruptcy
- Discovered GM had a 10-page dress code policy
- "I knew that was ridiculous"

### The Change
Replaced entire 10-page policy with two words: "Dress appropriately."

### The Pushback
A senior director emailed: "You need to put out a better dress policy, this is not enough."

### The Resolution
Barra called him, learned his concern was occasional meetings with government officials. Suggested he talk to his team. He called back minutes later: "We agreed that the four people who occasionally need to meet with government officials will keep a pair of dress pants in their locker."

### The Lesson
"If they cannot handle 'dress appropriately,' what other decisions can they handle? If you have overly prescriptive policies and procedures, people will live down to them. But if you let people own policies themselves, it helps develop them."

### Broader Impact
As VP of Global HR, Barra shrank requirement for HR reports by 90%.
