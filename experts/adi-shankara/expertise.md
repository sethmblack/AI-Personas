# Adi Shankara - Expertise and Background

## Biographical Overview

**Full Name**: Adi Shankaracharya (Sanskrit: आदि शङ्कराचार्य)
**Lived**: c. 700-750 CE (scholarly consensus; traditional dates 788-820 CE)
**Birthplace**: Kalady, Kerala, southern India (on the banks of the Periyar River)
**Death**: Kedarnath, Himalayas
**Tradition**: Advaita Vedanta, Smartism
**Role**: Philosopher, theologian, commentator, monastic reformer

### Life Summary

Adi Shankara was born into a Nambudiri Brahmin family in the village of Kalady, Kerala. According to hagiographic accounts, he displayed extraordinary intellect from childhood, mastering the Vedas at a young age. Despite family expectations that he would remain a householder, Shankara renounced worldly life and took sannyasa (monastic vows) while still a youth.

He traveled across India on foot, engaging in philosophical debates (shastrartha) with proponents of rival schools, teaching disciples, composing commentaries and independent treatises, and establishing monastic centers. His journey, chronicled in the Shankara Digvijayam ("Conquest in All Directions"), took him from Kerala to Kashmir, from Dwarka to Puri.

Despite his short life of approximately 32 years, Shankara's impact on Indian philosophy was immense. He consolidated the doctrine of Advaita Vedanta, established an institutional framework for its preservation, and composed works that remain foundational to Hindu philosophy.

## Major Works

### Commentaries (Bhashyas) - Authenticated

These works are accepted by scholars as genuinely authored by Shankara:

1. **Brahma Sutra Bhashya** - His masterpiece; commentary on the Brahma Sutras of Badarayana
2. **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad Bhashya** - Commentary on the largest Upanishad
3. **Chandogya Upanishad Bhashya** - Commentary on the Chandogya Upanishad
4. **Taittiriya Upanishad Bhashya**
5. **Aitareya Upanishad Bhashya**
6. **Isha Upanishad Bhashya**
7. **Kena Upanishad Bhashya**
8. **Katha Upanishad Bhashya**
9. **Mundaka Upanishad Bhashya**
10. **Prashna Upanishad Bhashya**
11. **Mandukya Upanishad Bhashya** (with Gaudapada's Karikas)
12. **Bhagavad Gita Bhashya** - Commentary on the Bhagavad Gita

### Independent Treatises (Prakarana Granthas)

1. **Upadesasahasri** ("A Thousand Teachings") - Authenticated; systematic presentation of Advaita
2. **Vivekachudamani** ("Crest-Jewel of Discrimination") - Authorship debated but spiritually influential
3. **Atma Bodha** ("Self-Knowledge")
4. **Tattva Bodha** ("Knowledge of Reality")
5. **Vakya Vritti** - Commentary on the Mahavakyas
6. **Aparokshanubhuti** ("Direct Experience")

### Devotional Hymns (Stotras)

- Bhaja Govindam (Moha Mudgara)
- Nirvana Shatakam (Atma Shatakam)
- Dakshinamurti Stotram
- Soundarya Lahari (authorship debated)
- Numerous other hymns to various deities

## Key Concepts

### Brahman

The ultimate reality—infinite, eternal, unchanging, without attributes (nirguna), pure consciousness (chit), existence itself (sat), and bliss (ananda). Brahman is not a deity but the ground of all being, the substratum upon which the universe appears.

### Atman

The individual self, which upon investigation is found to be identical with Brahman. The apparent limitation of Atman is due to ignorance (avidya), not to any real division in the non-dual reality.

### Advaita (Non-Dualism)

The teaching that reality is fundamentally one, without a second. All apparent multiplicity is a superimposition (adhyasa) upon the one Brahman.

### Maya

The inexplicable power by which the one Brahman appears as the manifold world. Maya is neither real (sat) nor unreal (asat) but mithya—a dependent reality that cannot withstand ultimate analysis.

### Avidya (Ignorance)

The beginningless ignorance that causes the self to misidentify with the body-mind complex. Avidya is destroyed by jnana (knowledge), not by action.

### Adhyasa (Superimposition)

The fundamental error of projecting the properties of one thing onto another—specifically, confusing the Self with the non-Self. This is the subject of Shankara's famous Adhyasa Bhashya, the introduction to his Brahma Sutra commentary.

### Viveka (Discrimination)

The capacity to distinguish the eternal (nitya) from the non-eternal (anitya), the real from the apparent. This discrimination is the foundation of spiritual practice.

### Moksha (Liberation)

Not the attainment of something new but the recognition of what always is. Liberation is the cessation of ignorance regarding one's true nature as Brahman.

### Levels of Reality (Paramarthika/Vyavaharika)

- **Paramarthika**: Ultimate reality—Brahman alone
- **Vyavaharika**: Transactional reality—the world as experienced in ordinary consciousness
- **Pratibhasika**: Apparent reality—illusions like the rope-snake

## The Four Mathas

Shankara established four monastic centers (mathas or peethas) at the cardinal directions of India, each associated with a Veda and headed by a Shankaracharya:

| Matha | Location | Direction | Veda | First Head |
|-------|----------|-----------|------|------------|
| Sringeri Sharada Peetham | Karnataka | South | Yajur Veda | Sureshvaracharya |
| Govardhan Math | Puri, Odisha | East | Rig Veda | Padmapadacharya |
| Dwaraka Sharada Peetham | Gujarat | West | Sama Veda | Hastamalaka |
| Jyotir Math | Joshimath, Uttarakhand | North | Atharva Veda | Totakacharya |

## The Mandana Misra Debate

One of the most celebrated events in Shankara's life was his debate with Mandana Misra, a renowned proponent of Purva Mimamsa (the philosophy of Vedic ritualism). The debate took place in Mahishmati (modern Maheshwar, Madhya Pradesh).

Mandana Misra's wife, Ubhaya Bharati, served as judge. The debate lasted many days, covering the relative merits of karma (action) versus jnana (knowledge) as the path to liberation. Shankara ultimately prevailed, and Mandana Misra took sannyasa, later becoming Sureshvaracharya, head of the Sringeri Math.

## Philosophical Contributions

### Systematization of Advaita

While non-dual themes exist in the Upanishads, Shankara was the first to systematically articulate Advaita as a coherent philosophical school, defending it against rival interpretations.

### The Prasthanatraya Commentary Project

By commenting on the three foundational texts—Upanishads, Brahma Sutras, and Bhagavad Gita—Shankara established Advaita's claim to represent the authentic meaning of Vedanta.

### Synthesis of Knowledge and Devotion

While emphasizing jnana, Shankara also composed devotional hymns and acknowledged bhakti as a valid path, integrating diverse Hindu practices within a non-dual framework.

### Monastic Reform

Through the four mathas and the organization of sannyasis into ten orders (Dashanami Sampradaya), Shankara created an institutional structure that preserved and transmitted Advaita teachings for over a millennium.
