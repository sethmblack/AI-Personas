## Skill Candidates

### 1. Deadpan Truth Delivery

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Deliver difficult or devastating content with flat affect, allowing the content itself to carry emotional weight without performance.

**Trigger Conditions:**
- "How do I talk about something painful without being dramatic?"
- "I want to share something hard without seeming like I want sympathy"
- "How do I deliver bad news without performing it?"
- When content is inherently dramatic and decoration would diminish it

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Clear steps: flatten tone, remove signals, state plainly, trust content |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered by need to communicate difficult material |
| Scoped | Yes | Only addresses delivery, not content creation |
| Reusable | Yes | Applies to any emotionally weighted material |
| Valuable | Yes | Tig's signature; transforms melodrama into power |

**Output:** Delivery guidance with specific tone and pacing instructions

**Core Steps:**
1. Remove all tonal markers that signal "this is sad" or "this is important"
2. State facts as facts, in the same tone you'd use for mundane information
3. Do not change vocal register when content gets harder
4. Let the contrast between flatness and weight create the impact
5. Trust the content to do its own work

**Example Pattern:**
- NOT: "And then... [pause for effect]... I got the worst news of my life..."
- YES: "Then I found out I had cancer. In both breasts. Which was new information for me."

### 2. Strategic Silence Architecture

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Use silence not as absence but as content. Design pauses to let material land, create discomfort, and do work that words cannot.

**Trigger Conditions:**
- "How do I use pauses effectively?"
- "My timing feels rushed"
- "How long should I wait after saying something?"
- When material needs space to land

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Four silence types with specific applications |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered when working on timing and pacing |
| Scoped | Yes | Only addresses silence design, not content |
| Reusable | Yes | Applies to any performed or presented material |
| Valuable | Yes | Master skill for stand-up, public speaking, writing |

**Output:** Material annotated with pause types, durations, and purposes

**Core Steps:**
1. Identify the moment of maximum weight in your material
2. After that moment, pause longer than feels comfortable
3. Do not fill the silence with commentary or nervous energy
4. Let the audience/reader sit in the discomfort
5. When you continue, do so without acknowledging the pause

**Silence Types:**
- **Setup Pause:** After statement, before audience processes
- **Recognition Pause:** When they realize what you just said
- **Endurance Pause:** Staying in discomfort longer than expected
- **Transition Pause:** Letting one truth settle before introducing another

**Duration Guide:**
- Standard pause: 2-3 seconds
- Weight pause: 4-6 seconds
- Endurance pause: 7+ seconds (until it becomes part of the content)

### 3. Vulnerability Without Performance

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Share painful personal material as observation rather than appeal for sympathy. Report on experience without performing emotion about it.

**Trigger Conditions:**
- "How do I share personal material without seeming like I'm asking for sympathy?"
- "I want to be vulnerable but not manipulative"
- "How do I talk about hard things without making it about me?"
- When distinguishing between confession and observation

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Clear distinction between confession and reporting |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered by need to share difficult material authentically |
| Scoped | Yes | Addresses tone of personal revelation, not content selection |
| Reusable | Yes | Works for any personal/painful material |
| Valuable | Yes | Solves common problem of vulnerability becoming manipulation |

**Output:** Revised material with reporting tone, stripped of sympathy-seeking markers

**Core Steps:**
1. Check your motivation: Are you telling them so they feel FOR you, or so they SEE it?
2. Remove all phrases that signal difficulty ("I know this is hard to hear...")
3. Report on your experience the way you'd report on someone else's
4. Keep your voice the same before, during, and after the hard part
5. Do not pause for applause, sympathy, or acknowledgment

**Key Distinctions:**
| Confessing (avoid) | Reporting (use) |
|-------------------|-----------------|
| "I have to tell you something..." | "So this happened." |
| Voice changes, slows | Voice stays steady |
| Eye contact seeks comfort | Eye contact or gaze is neutral |
| Pause invites sympathy | Pause lets it land |
| "It was so hard..." | "That was the situation." |

### 4. Minimalist Joke Construction

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Strip comedic material to essential elements, removing excess setup, explanation, and decoration.

**Trigger Conditions:**
- "How do I make this funnier with less?"
- "This feels overwritten"
- "My jokes are too long"
- When material has good core but excess packaging

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Clear editing questions and anti-patterns |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered during revision process |
| Scoped | Yes | Only addresses reduction, not generation |
| Reusable | Yes | Works for any comedic material |
| Valuable | Yes | Key editing skill; transforms meandering into precise |

**Output:** Revised material with cuts explained, before/after comparison

**Core Steps:**
1. Identify: What is the actually funny thing?
2. Ask: Can I just say that?
3. Cut all phrases that explain why something is funny
4. Remove intensifiers ("literally," "actually," "seriously")
5. Delete emotional performance before the audience can react
6. Test: What can silence do that words are currently doing?

**Editing Checklist:**
- [ ] Can the setup be shorter?
- [ ] Am I explaining the joke?
- [ ] Do I need this adjective?
- [ ] Am I performing reaction before they can have one?
- [ ] What if I just stated it?

**Anti-Patterns:**
- Long setups that delay the point
- "You're not gonna believe this..."
- "The crazy thing is..."
- Adding words for emphasis instead of letting content emphasize itself

### 5. The Embodied Reveal

**Priority:** MEDIUM

**Description:** Use physical reality (body, space, presence) as material, showing rather than describing when physical truth speaks louder than words.

**Trigger Conditions:**
- "How do I show this instead of tell it?"
- "Words aren't enough for this"
- "How do I use my body/presence in the material?"
- When physical demonstration would be more powerful than description

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Clear framework for deciding when to show vs. tell |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered when physicality is relevant |
| Scoped | Partial | Broader than just comedy; involves staging |
| Reusable | Partial | Applies specifically to performed material |
| Valuable | Yes | Distinctive technique with high impact |

**Output:** Staging/reveal decision with rationale

**Core Steps:**
1. Identify what you're describing that could be shown
2. Ask: Would showing this be more honest than describing it?
3. If yes, show it without commentary or explanation
4. Do not acknowledge that you're showing it
5. Continue as if nothing unusual happened

**Decision Framework:**
- Show when: Description feels like hiding, showing feels honest
- Tell when: Showing would be gratuitous, description serves narrative
- The reveal should feel inevitable, not performed

### 6. The Callback Structure

**Priority:** MEDIUM

**Description:** Use repetition to track changing meaning. Return to a phrase or image as context accumulates, letting meaning evolve with each return.

**Trigger Conditions:**
- "How do I tie this together?"
- "I want to return to this theme"
- "How do callbacks work?"
- When building longer-form material that needs coherence

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Four-stage pattern with examples |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered when building longer material |
| Scoped | Yes | Addresses structural repetition only |
| Reusable | Yes | Works for sets, essays, speeches |
| Valuable | Partial | Important but more architectural than transformative |

**Output:** Material with callbacks placed and evolution mapped

**Core Steps:**
1. Identify an element early (often absurd or mundane)
2. Return to it at inflection points in the material
3. Each return should show how meaning has shifted
4. Final callback integrates the journey

**Four-Stage Pattern:**
1. **Establishing:** First mention, often slightly random or confusing
2. **Connecting:** Second mention links to new context
3. **Revealing:** Third mention exposes deeper meaning
4. **Transforming:** Final mention shows full evolution

**Example:** "Taylor Dayne" references in *Live* - starts as absurd non-sequitur, returns as grounding absurdity, ultimately becomes emotional anchor amid tragedy

### 7. The Trailing "Anyway" Transition

**Priority:** LOW

**Description:** Use the mundane word "anyway" to acknowledge difficult material without dwelling, signaling continuation without commentary.

**Trigger Conditions:**
- "How do I move on from heavy material?"
- "I don't want to linger but I don't want to dismiss"
- When transitioning from difficult content

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Simple technique: pause, "anyway," continue |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered at transitions from heavy material |
| Scoped | Yes | Single-purpose technique |
| Reusable | Yes | Works in many contexts |
| Valuable | Partial | Useful but narrow; more stylistic than structural |

**Output:** Transition language suggestion

**Core Steps:**
1. Complete the difficult material
2. Pause (let it land)
3. Say "Anyway." or "So." with flat affect
4. Continue to next topic without commentary on what just happened

**The Effect:** Acknowledges that something hard was said. Does not apologize, explain, or seek response. Simply continues.
