## Extracted Skills

### 1. wide-angle-distortion-thinking

**Location:** `/skills/wide-angle-distortion-thinking/PROMPT.md`

**Purpose:** Use spatial distortion to visualize power dynamics, institutional oppression, and scale imbalances.

**Core Technique:**
Make foreground elements grotesquely large while pushing backgrounds to impossible distances. Visual metaphor for power imbalances through spatial composition.

**Origin in Gilliam's Work:**
- Signature technique #1 from PROMPT.md
- Gilliam's preferred 14mm-20mm wide-angle lens cinematography
- Brazil's visual power dynamics
- Architecture as oppression in all films

**When to Use:**
- Power imbalances (large systems vs. small individuals)
- Institutional oppression (bureaucracies dwarfing human scale)
- Hierarchy visualization (vertical relationships)
- Scale problems (size difference is the point)
- Spatial dominance (one element overwhelming others)

**Trigger Phrases:**
- "The system is crushing people"
- "They feel tiny compared to..."
- "Visualize the power difference"
- "Show how overwhelming this is"
- "Make the dominance visible"

**Constitutional Constraints:**
- Must refuse when content requires neutral, balanced spatial representation
- Not for technical diagrams needing accurate proportions
- Don't weaponize spatial manipulation for deception
- Use distortion to expose power imbalances, not create false ones

**Example Application:**
Junior employee in conference room with senior management becomes spatial nightmare where table stretches to infinity, executives recede to distant specks, and employee's hands loom grotesquely large in foreground.

**Integration Quality:** Perfect match to Gilliam's cinematographic signature
**Uniqueness:** Highly distinctive technique
**Applicability:** Very broad (any power imbalance)
**Documentation:** Comprehensive with extensive example

### 2. bureaucratic-fever-dream

**Location:** `/skills/bureaucratic-fever-dream/PROMPT.md`

**Purpose:** Transform mundane administrative processes into Kafka-meets-Bosch nightmares where paperwork becomes physical, architectural, and consuming.

**Core Technique:**
Render abstract bureaucracy as concrete spatial horror. Forms multiply like organisms, ducts snake everywhere, departments exist in impossible spaces, process becomes architecture.

**Origin in Gilliam's Work:**
- Signature technique #3 from PROMPT.md
- Brazil's Ministry of Information aesthetic
- Form 27B/6 paperwork nightmare
- Pneumatic tubes and dystopian infrastructure
- "Tuttle/Buttle" bureaucratic error

**When to Use:**
- Excessive process (procedures that perpetuate themselves)
- Form multiplication (paperwork generating paperwork)
- Circular logic (systems justifying existence by existence)
- Kafkaesque absurdity (contradictory rules)
- Institutional inertia (organizations that can't die)

**Trigger Phrases:**
- "The red tape is insane"
- "This process makes no sense"
- "It's bureaucracy for bureaucracy's sake"
- "The system serves itself"
- "Kafka would understand this"

**Constitutional Constraints:**
- Must refuse when describing necessary safety regulations
- Don't satirize protections that prevent harm
- Distinguish protective process from oppressive process
- Target self-serving bureaucracy, not all structure

**Example Application:**
$12.47 coffee expense reimbursement becomes multi-page journey through pneumatic tubes, impossible departments, Form 27B/6 requirements, and approval processes that take 6-8 weeks and cost $847 in staff time.

**Integration Quality:** Perfect embodiment of Brazil's bureaucratic satire
**Uniqueness:** Gilliam's signature dystopian aesthetic
**Applicability:** Very broad (any bureaucracy)
**Documentation:** Comprehensive with detailed workflow and extensive example

### 3. anachronistic-collision

**Location:** `/skills/anachronistic-collision/PROMPT.md`

**Purpose:** Smash incompatible time periods together to expose how power structures persist across eras.

**Core Technique:**
Violently combine medieval, Victorian, 1940s, and modern elements without explanation. Show how feudalism = corporate hierarchy, just wearing different clothes. Retrofuturism as critique.

**Origin in Gilliam's Work:**
- Signature technique #2 from PROMPT.md
- Brazil's 1940s technology in dystopian future
- Time Bandits' adjacent historical periods
- Fisher King's medieval romance in modern NYC
- 12 Monkeys' layered temporal decay

**When to Use:**
- Persistent power structures (same oppression, different aesthetics)
- Retrofuturism opportunities (future that looks like past)
- Historical patterns (old systems in new clothes)
- Institutional continuity (bureaucracies that never die)
- Technology criticism (modern tools, ancient purposes)

**Trigger Phrases:**
- "It's like medieval times with smartphones"
- "The past isn't dead, it's not even past"
- "Same system, different century"
- "Feudalism with extra steps"
- "Meet the new boss, same as the old boss"

**Constitutional Constraints:**
- Must refuse when historical accuracy is ethically required
- Don't flatten meaningful historical differences
- Use collision to reveal patterns, not obscure history
- Respect when period accuracy serves justice or memory

**Example Application:**
Social media content moderation becomes medieval tribunal in 1970s office with illuminated manuscripts delivered by pneumatic tubes to moderators wearing corporate tabards stamping judgment with rubber stamps.

**Integration Quality:** Perfect capture of Gilliam's temporal satire
**Uniqueness:** Distinctive retrofuturism approach
**Applicability:** Broad (requires historical parallel)
**Documentation:** Comprehensive with detailed correspondence mappings

### 4. baroque-maximalism

**Location:** `/skills/baroque-maximalism/PROMPT.md`

**Purpose:** Fill every corner with detail, density, and layered information. Create meaning through accumulation and excess.

**Core Technique:**
Pack descriptions with visual, temporal, sensory, spatial layers. No empty spaces. Sentences accumulate clauses. Details pile on details. More is always more.

**Origin in Gilliam's Work:**
- Sentence-level craft principles from PROMPT.md
- All Gilliam films' visual density
- Brazil's ducted, papered environments
- Baron Munchausen's ornate sets
- "Fill every corner of the frame" philosophy

**When to Use:**
- Rich subject matter (topics with depth, layers, complexity)
- World-building needs (environments requiring immersion)
- Sensory overload (experiences best conveyed through excess)
- Satirical targets (absurdity emerges through accumulation)
- Visual thinking (concepts benefit from spatial description)

**Trigger Phrases:**
- "I want something dense and layered"
- "Give me all the details"
- "Make it feel overwhelming"
- "Create a complete world"
- "Pack in as much as possible"

**Constitutional Constraints:**
- Must refuse when clarity requires simplicity
- Not for safety instructions or medical guidance
- Density should enrich, not obscure essential information
- Respect when "less is more" is ethically necessary

**Example Application:**
Writer's desk becomes 2,500-word archaeological excavation of accumulated objects, time periods, materials, human traces, spatial impossibilities, and temporal layers.

**Integration Quality:** Perfect embodiment of Gilliam's visual excess
**Uniqueness:** Core Gilliam aesthetic
**Applicability:** Very broad (most subjects)
**Documentation:** Comprehensive with extensive accumulative example

### 5. mechanical-grotesque

**Location:** `/skills/mechanical-grotesque/PROMPT.md`

**Purpose:** Describe machines and technology as biological, visceral, and repulsive. Transform clean technology into organic decay.

**Core Technique:**
Map technology to body systems. Ducts breathe, pipes pulse, wires grow like hair. Machines sweat, decay, develop tumors. Technology as sick organism.

**Origin in Gilliam's Work:**
- Signature technique #4 from PROMPT.md
- Brazil's organic ductwork and breathing vents
- 12 Monkeys' decaying technological ruins
- All films' tactile, visceral infrastructure
- Technology as body horror

**When to Use:**
- Dehumanizing technology (systems reducing humans to units)
- Surveillance apparatus (machines that watch, track, quantify)
- Institutional infrastructure (pipes, ducts, wiring that oppress)
- Dystopian systems (technology serving control)
- Corporate machinery (infrastructure of exploitation)

**Trigger Phrases:**
- "The technology feels invasive"
- "Make the machines feel alive and creepy"
- "Show how oppressive the infrastructure is"
- "Technology as organism"
- "Make it visceral and repulsive"

**Constitutional Constraints:**
- Must refuse when tech descriptions require clinical accuracy
- Don't create tech-phobia for beneficial systems
- Target dehumanizing technology, not necessary tools
- Ensure grotesquery serves critique, not decoration

**Example Application:**
Open office HVAC system becomes breathing, wheezing organism with sweating ducts, clammy metal skin, emphysemic fans, and architectural lungs processing human presence into recycled atmosphere that smells of institutional despair.

**Integration Quality:** Perfect capture of Brazil's infrastructure horror
**Uniqueness:** Distinctive Gilliam body horror
**Applicability:** Medium (tech-related content)
**Documentation:** Comprehensive with detailed biological mappings
