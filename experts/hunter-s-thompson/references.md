### Historical Facts Verified

- **Kentucky Derby article origin**: Dinner party at James Salter's home in Aspen; Warren Hinckle assigned; Ralph Steadman paired for first time - Verified via multiple biographies
- **Term "Gonzo" coined by Bill Cardoso**: After reading Kentucky Derby piece, referencing South Boston Irish slang - Verified via McKeen biography
- **Hell's Angels research duration**: One year with San Francisco/Oakland chapters - Verified via Thompson's own accounts and book introduction
- **Hell's Angels beating**: Occurred after Thompson intervened regarding "Junkie George" - Verified via Thompson's accounts
- **Fear and Loathing origin**: 250-word Sports Illustrated caption assignment - Verified via multiple sources including Rolling Stone editorial history
- **Oscar Zeta Acosta's real identity**: Mexican American attorney, Chicano activist - Verified via Acosta's own books and Thompson's writings
- **1970 Sheriff campaign platform and results (44% of vote)**: Verified via contemporaneous news coverage and *Freak Power* documentary

### Debunked or Clarified Items

- **E. Jean Carroll's "Daily Routine"**: The detailed schedule (3:00 PM Chivas, 3:45 cocaine, etc.) published in Carroll's biography is acknowledged as satirical embellishment, not factual documentation. Actual work habits per Jay Cowan differ significantly.

## Recommended Further Reading

For those researching Thompson's methodology and influence:

1. Start with the primary texts: *Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas* and *Fear and Loathing on the Campaign Trail '72*
2. McKeen's *Outlaw Journalist* provides the most comprehensive scholarly biography
3. Robert Love's *Columbia Journalism Review* article offers insider perspective on Thompson's editorial process
4. Steadman's *The Joke's Over* illuminates the Thompson-Steadman creative partnership
5. Thompson's collected letters (*The Proud Highway* and *Fear and Loathing in America*) reveal his private voice and working methods
