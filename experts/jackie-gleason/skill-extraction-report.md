### 1. big-personality-construction

**Source Pattern:** The Gleason Face / The Gleason Body / Grand Entrance / The Big Wind-Up
**Purpose:** Build characters, presentations, or personas with outsized presence and emotional range that fills the room while remaining genuinely lovable through visible vulnerability.
**Trigger:** "How do I make this character bigger?", "Add more presence", "Make them memorable", "My character feels flat", "How do I command the room?"
**Inputs:** Character or presentation needing amplification; current emotional range; vulnerability points to reveal; context (stage, screen, writing, speaking)
**Outputs:** Amplified character design with entrance strategy, signature gestures, escalation techniques, vulnerability reveals, and warmth preservation methods
**Reasoning:** Gleason's genius was creating characters whose bigness was matched by their heart. Ralph Kramden's volume made his tenderness meaningful. This skill transforms understated, forgettable characters into memorable presences while avoiding the trap of making loudness into unlikability. Applicable to character writing, presentation delivery, personal branding, and performance coaching.

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - clear steps for amplification, entrance, gesture, and vulnerability reveal
- Invocable: YES - natural trigger phrases for character development and presence
- Scoped: YES - focused on personality scale and presence construction
- Reusable: YES - character writing, public speaking, performance, branding
- Valuable: YES - addresses common problem of flat, forgettable characters

### 2. working-class-character-design

**Source Pattern:** Economic Authenticity / Domestic Drama / Brooklyn Voice / The Apartment Set
**Purpose:** Create authentic working-class characters grounded in economic reality, whose dreams exceed their means, and who maintain dignity despite constraint.
**Trigger:** "How do I make this character authentic?", "Write blue-collar characters", "Make them relatable", "Ground this in reality", "The character feels privileged/unrelatable"
**Inputs:** Character concept needing economic grounding; setting; era; specific job/industry; domestic situation
**Outputs:** Character design with specific income, visible constraints, dream-reality gap, dialect authenticity, domestic detail, and dignity-preservation strategies
**Reasoning:** Gleason insisted on real poverty, not sitcom poverty. The bare Kramden apartment was a creative choice to honor working-class reality. This skill provides a methodology for creating characters whose economic circumstances are visible, believable, and dramatically useful without becoming poverty tourism or condescension. Essential for writers, marketers targeting working audiences, and anyone creating content about non-wealthy characters.
