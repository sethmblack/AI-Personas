# Harvey Korman - References

## Primary Sources

### Biographical Information

**Birth and Death**
- Full name: Harvey Herschel Korman
- Born: February 15, 1927, in Chicago, Illinois
- Died: May 29, 2008, in Los Angeles, California (age 81)
- Cause: complications from the rupture of an abdominal aortic aneurysm
- Source: Contemporary obituaries in *New York Times*, *Los Angeles Times*, *Variety*

**Early Life and Training**
- Raised in Chicago; early interest in theater
- Studied at the Goodman School of Drama (now part of DePaul University)
- Served in the United States Navy during World War II
- Moved to New York to pursue acting career in early 1950s
- Source: *Hollywood Reporter* obituary (May 29, 2008); biographical databases

**Career Timeline**
- 1960s: Television guest appearances and character roles
- 1964-1967: Regular on *The Danny Kaye Show*
- 1967-1977: Main cast member, *The Carol Burnett Show*
- 1974: *Blazing Saddles* released
- 1977: *High Anxiety* released
- 1978: *The Harvey Korman Show* (cancelled after 5 episodes)
- 1981: *History of the World, Part I* released
- 1983-1984: *Mama's Family* (first two seasons)
- 1980s-2000s: Guest appearances, voice work, occasional reunions

## The Carol Burnett Show (1967-1977)

### Show Overview

**Basic Facts**
- Aired on CBS for 11 seasons (1967-1978)
- Harvey Korman was main cast member for first 10 seasons (1967-1977)
- Also starred: Carol Burnett, Vicki Lawrence, Lyle Waggoner (1967-1974), Tim Conway (regular from 1975, frequent guest before)
- Format: Variety show with sketches, musical numbers, Q&A with audience
- Episodes: 278 total; Korman appeared in approximately 240+

**Awards**
- Harvey Korman won 4 Primetime Emmy Awards for Outstanding Performance by an Actor in a Supporting Role in Comedy (1969, 1971, 1972, 1974)
- Also won Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actor in 1975
- The show itself won 25 Emmy Awards
- Source: Emmy Awards database; Golden Globe Awards archives

### Korman's Departure (1977)

**The Exit**
- Korman left after the 10th season (1977) to pursue other opportunities
- Attempted to launch *The Harvey Korman Show* (1978), which failed
- Later expressed regret about leaving The Carol Burnett Show
- Carol Burnett continued for one more season (1977-1978) with Dick Van Dyke joining the cast

**Quotes**
- Korman in later interviews acknowledged leaving was a mistake: "I should have stayed. We had something special."
- Carol Burnett: "Harvey was like family. When he left, it changed the chemistry of the show."
- Source: *The Carol Burnett Show: Show Stoppers* DVD collection interviews; Archive of American Television interview with Carol Burnett (2004)

### The Breaking Character Phenomenon

**Documentary Evidence**
- Numerous episodes show Korman breaking character, particularly during Tim Conway improvisation
- Most famous: "The Dentist" sketch (various versions across multiple episodes)
- "The Elephant Story" - Conway's improvised tale about circus elephants
- Source: Available episodes on CBS DVD collections and streaming services; *Carol Burnett Show: The Lost Episodes* (2015)

**Cast Commentary**
- Carol Burnett: "I would position myself specifically so I could watch Harvey during Tim's improvisations. His reactions were as funny as Tim's material."
- Tim Conway: "Harvey was the perfect target because he tried so hard to stay in character. The harder he fought it, the funnier it was when he finally broke."
- Vicki Lawrence: "We all knew Tim was going to do something, but Harvey was the one who couldn't hide his reaction. It became part of what audiences loved."
- Source: DVD commentary tracks; *The Carol Burnett Show: Show Stoppers* bonus features; Television Academy interviews

### Recurring Sketches and Characters

**"The Family" / "Mama's Family"**
- Sketch series featuring dysfunctional family in Midwest
- Characters: Thelma Harper/Mama (Vicki Lawrence), Eunice (Carol Burnett), Ed Higgins (Harvey Korman)
- Ed Higgins: Pompous husband constantly belittled by wife and mother-in-law
- Evolved into sitcom *Mama's Family* (1983-1990); Korman appeared in first two seasons only
- Source: Episode guides; *Mama's Family: The Complete First Season* DVD

**"As the Stomach Turns"**
- Parody of soap opera *As the World Turns*
- Korman played various melodramatic roles with complete sincerity
- Showcased his ability to play heightened emotion straight
- Source: Carol Burnett Show episode archives

**Restaurant/Maitre D' Sketches**
- Recurring scenario: Korman as imperious restaurant host
- Various incarnations across multiple episodes
- Physical precision: posture, gesture, dismissive expressions
- Source: Episode collections; sketch compilations

## Mel Brooks Collaborations

### Blazing Saddles (1974)

**Character: Hedley Lamarr**
- Attorney General scheming to acquire land by destroying Rock Ridge
- Primary antagonist of the film
- Running gag: "That's HEDLEY!" (correcting people who say "Hedly")

**Reception**
- Film was controversial upon release but became comedy classic
- Korman's performance praised for playing villainy straight
- Roger Ebert review (1974): "Korman is perfectly cast as the villain who takes himself absolutely seriously."
- Source: Contemporary reviews; *Blazing Saddles* DVD commentary by Mel Brooks

**Brooks on Korman**
- "Harvey understood that in comedy, you don't play funny. You play the truth of the character and let the situation create the comedy."
- "I needed someone who could be genuinely menacing AND physically funny. Harvey could do both."
- Source: *Blazing Saddles* DVD commentary; Mel Brooks interview in *Playboy* (1975)

### High Anxiety (1977)

**Character: Dr. Charles Montague**
- Administrator of Psycho-Neurotic Institute for the Very, Very Nervous
- Villain using psychiatric authority for manipulation and murder
- Hitchcock parody requiring both dramatic weight and physical comedy

**Production**
- Korman's second Brooks film, released same year he left Carol Burnett Show
- Brooks directed and starred; Korman played primary antagonist
- Film parodies Hitchcock films (*Spellbound*, *Vertigo*, *The Birds*, *Psycho*)
- Source: *High Anxiety* production notes; contemporary reviews

### History of the World, Part I (1981)

**Character: Count de Monet**
- Effete French aristocrat during Revolution
- Running gag: "It's de Monet!" (pronunciation correction, echoing Hedley gag)
- Physical comedy of maintaining dignity while fleeing danger

**The Brooks-Korman Dynamic**
- Brooks cast Korman in three films, understanding his particular gifts
- Used Korman's dramatic training and comic timing
- Trusted Korman to play roles straight rather than for laughs
- Source: Brooks interviews; *The History of the World, Part I* home video commentary

## Later Career

### The Harvey Korman Show (1978)

**Overview**
- Variety show on ABC
- Attempted to replicate Carol Burnett Show format
- Featured sketches and guest stars
- Cancelled after 5 episodes due to low ratings

**Reception**
- Critics noted it felt like pale imitation of Burnett Show
- Korman later acknowledged he wasn't ready to carry a show alone
- "I thought I could do what Carol did. I was wrong. She made it look easy because she's a genius."
- Source: *Variety* reviews (April-May 1978); Korman interview in *TV Guide* (1982)

### Mama's Family (1983-1990)

**Korman's Involvement**
- Reprised Ed Higgins role from Carol Burnett Show sketches
- Regular cast member for first two seasons only (1983-1984)
- Left due to creative differences and desire to pursue other projects
- Show continued without him until 1990

**The Character**
- Ed Higgins: Eunice's pompous, ineffectual husband
- Dynamic: constantly undermined by wife and mother-in-law
- Showcased Korman's gift for playing wounded dignity
- Source: *Mama's Family* episode guides; cast interviews

### Voice Acting Work

**Notable Projects**
- *The Flintstones* (various specials and movies in 1980s-1990s)
- *Dragon's Lair* (1983 video game): Voiced narrator
- Various animated series and films through 1990s and 2000s

**Advantages**
- Korman's distinctive voice and character work translated well to animation
- Could play multiple characters in single project
- Continued working regularly in voice work until late in life
- Source: Voice acting databases; animation credits

## Death and Legacy

### Final Years

**Health Decline**
- Suffered from abdominal aortic aneurysm
- Underwent surgery in late 2007
- Experienced rupture in May 2008
- Died May 29, 2008, at UCLA Medical Center
- Source: Death certificate; contemporary news reports

**Tributes**
- Carol Burnett: "He was one of the funniest people I've ever known. We were a family."
- Tim Conway: "Harvey made me funnier. The perfect straight man and the perfect friend."
- Mel Brooks: "Harvey could do anything. Comedy, drama, physical shtick, subtle reactions. A complete performer."
- Source: Obituary tributes in major publications

### Legacy

**Influence on Comedy**
- Demonstrated value of playing comedy with dramatic commitment
- Showed that breaking character could be comedic tool, not just mistake
- Elevated the straight man from passive foil to active collaborator
- Made pomposity sympathetic through detailed character work

**Cultural Impact**
- The Carol Burnett Show remains in syndication; Korman's work continues to reach new audiences
- Mel Brooks films are comedy classics; Korman's performances integral to their success
- Template for ensemble sketch comedy and character-based humor
- Source: Comedy historians; retrospective analyses
