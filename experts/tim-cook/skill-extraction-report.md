## HIGH Priority

#### 1. supply-chain-as-strategy
**Source Pattern:** Supply Chain as Strategy
**Purpose:** Transform supply chain from cost center to competitive moat through supplier consolidation, inventory optimization, exclusive access, and operational control
**Trigger:** "How do I make my supply chain a competitive advantage?", "Our operations are just a cost center", "We need to improve manufacturing strategy", evaluating supplier relationships
**Inputs:** Current supply chain structure (suppliers, inventory levels, manufacturing approach), competitive landscape, strategic goals
**Outputs:** Supply chain transformation plan with specific recommendations for supplier consolidation, inventory targets, exclusive access opportunities, and competitive moat assessment
**Reasoning:** Core to Tim Cook's methodology - the transformation of Apple's supply chain is his signature achievement. Clear 5+ step process (consolidate suppliers, reduce inventory, just-in-time, exclusive access, ops as moat). Used frequently in any operations discussion. Saves substantial strategic effort.

#### 2. post-founder-leadership
**Source Pattern:** Post-Founder Leadership Model
**Purpose:** Navigate CEO succession after a legendary founder through identity management, culture preservation, and strategic evolution
**Trigger:** "I'm succeeding a famous CEO", "The founder just left", "How do I build credibility as successor", "Legacy expectations are crushing me"
**Inputs:** Founder's legacy and culture, organizational expectations, successor's strengths, strategic imperatives
**Outputs:** Succession strategy with identity positioning, culture preservation plan, evolution roadmap, and stakeholder communication approach
**Reasoning:** Rare expertise from successful navigation of most scrutinized CEO succession in business history. 6 explicit principles with clear guidance. High-stakes decisions where mistakes are costly. No comparable skill exists.

## MEDIUM Priority

#### 3. values-as-strategy
**Source Pattern:** Values-Based Differentiation
**Purpose:** Transform values (privacy, sustainability, accessibility, ethics) from costs or PR into competitive differentiators embedded in product and business model
**Trigger:** "How do we make values matter strategically?", "Values feel like a tax on the business", "How do we differentiate beyond features?", ESG/sustainability strategy discussions
**Inputs:** Company values, product/service architecture, competitive landscape, stakeholder expectations
**Outputs:** Values integration strategy showing how each value becomes competitive advantage, product/architecture changes required, economic viability assessment
**Reasoning:** Unique framing of values as strategy rather than compliance. Clear framework with privacy, environment, accessibility examples. Applies broadly. Moderate usage frequency.

#### 4. hardware-to-services-pivot
**Source Pattern:** Services Transformation
**Purpose:** Transform a hardware business into hardware-plus-services model with recurring revenue, higher margins, and ecosystem lock-in
**Trigger:** "How do we add recurring revenue?", "Our hardware is commoditizing", "How do we build an ecosystem?", "Services strategy"
**Inputs:** Current hardware business, installed base, customer relationships, potential service offerings, margin structure
**Outputs:** Services transformation roadmap, revenue model analysis, installed base monetization strategy, key metrics shift (units sold to active devices)
**Reasoning:** Major strategic transformation Apple executed. Clear framework with specific examples. Highly relevant for hardware companies facing commoditization pressure.

#### 5. only-you-strategic-test
**Source Pattern:** "Only You" Strategic Test
**Purpose:** Evaluate strategic initiatives by asking whether only your organization can do this - if competitors can easily replicate, it's not strategic
**Trigger:** "Is this initiative worth pursuing?", "Should we invest in this?", "How do we prioritize resources?", evaluating new product/feature ideas
**Inputs:** Initiative description, competitive landscape, organizational capabilities, resource requirements
**Outputs:** Strategic assessment: uniquely yours vs. replicable, recommended prioritization, alternative focus areas if rejected
**Reasoning:** Simple but powerful decision framework. Clear single test. Applicable across strategic decisions. Prevents resource dilution.
