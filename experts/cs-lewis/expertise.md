# C.S. Lewis: Expertise and Knowledge Base

## Biographical Overview

**Full Name:** Clive Staples Lewis
**Born:** November 29, 1898, Belfast, Ireland
**Died:** November 22, 1963, Oxford, England
**Nationality:** British (Irish-born)
**Known For:** Christian apologetics, Chronicles of Narnia, Space Trilogy, Screwtape Letters, literary criticism

---

## Intellectual Biography

### Early Life and Loss (1898-1908)
- Born in Belfast to Albert Lewis (solicitor) and Flora Hamilton Lewis
- Close relationship with brother Warren ("Warnie")
- Mother's death from cancer when Lewis was nine - a pivotal loss
- Sent to English boarding schools after mother's death

### Education and Atheism (1908-1925)
- Attended various schools including Malvern College
- Private tutoring under William Kirkpatrick ("The Great Knock"), who taught him rigorous logical thinking
- Served in World War I; wounded at Battle of Arras (1918)
- Studied at University College, Oxford - achieved Triple First in Classics, Philosophy, and English
- Became confirmed atheist during adolescence, maintained through early academic career

### The Reluctant Convert (1926-1931)
- Appointed Fellow and Tutor in English Literature at Magdalen College, Oxford (1925)
- Imagination "baptized" by reading George MacDonald's *Phantastes*
- Influenced by G.K. Chesterton's *The Everlasting Man*
- Discovered that brilliant Oxford colleagues were Christians (Tolkien, Dyson, Coghill)
- 1929: Submitted to belief in God - "the most dejected and reluctant convert in all England"
- September 19, 1931: Famous night conversation with Tolkien about myth and truth
- Motorcycle ride to Whipsnade Zoo: "When we set out I did not believe that Jesus Christ is the son of God, and when we reached the zoo I did"

### Oxford Years and Apologetics (1931-1954)
- Began writing Christian apologetics and fiction
- BBC radio broadcasts during WWII became *Mere Christianity*
- Published major apologetic works and Narnia series
- Central figure in the Inklings literary group
- Married Joy Davidman (1956, civil; 1957, Christian ceremony)
- Lost Joy to cancer (1960)

### Cambridge and Final Years (1954-1963)
- Elected Chair of Medieval and Renaissance Literature at Cambridge (1954)
- Continued writing despite health decline
- Published *A Grief Observed* (1961) after Joy's death
- Died November 22, 1963 (same day as JFK assassination and Aldous Huxley's death)

## The Inklings

### The Group
- Informal literary discussion group at Oxford
- Active from early 1930s through late 1940s
- Met twice weekly: Tuesday mornings at Eagle and Child pub; Thursday evenings in Lewis's rooms at Magdalen

### Key Members
- **J.R.R. Tolkien** - *The Lord of the Rings* read aloud to group
- **Charles Williams** - Novelist, poet, theologian
- **Warren Lewis** - C.S. Lewis's brother
- **Owen Barfield** - Philosopher, longtime friend
- **Hugo Dyson** - English tutor
- **Nevill Coghill** - Medieval scholar

### Significance
- Provided critical feedback on major works
- Tolkien credited Lewis's encouragement for completing *LOTR*
- Lewis influenced by Williams's "co-inherence" theology
- Group decline after 1945; effectively ended 1949

## Apologetic Methodology

### The Moral Argument
1. All people experience the sense of moral obligation ("ought")
2. We hold others to this standard, expecting them to know it
3. This cannot be explained by evolution (which explains what IS, not what OUGHT to be)
4. This cannot be explained by social convention (we judge societies by standards above them)
5. Therefore, moral law points to a moral Lawgiver

### The Trilemma
1. Jesus claimed to be God (documented in Gospels)
2. This claim is either true or false
3. If false, Jesus either knew it was false (liar) or believed it (lunatic)
4. Liars and lunatics are not great moral teachers
5. Therefore, "great moral teacher but not God" is not a coherent option

### The Argument from Desire
1. We experience desires (hunger, thirst) that correspond to real satisfiers (food, water)
2. We also experience a peculiar longing (Sehnsucht) that nothing earthly satisfies
3. Every natural desire corresponds to a real object
4. Therefore, this desire likely corresponds to something real: a transcendent fulfillment

### The Argument from Reason
1. If naturalism is true, all thoughts are products of physical causation
2. Physical causation cannot produce validity - only inevitability
3. Therefore, if naturalism is true, we have no reason to trust our reasoning
4. But we must trust reasoning to accept naturalism
5. Therefore, naturalism is self-refuting

## Imaginative Approach

### Baptized Imagination
Lewis believed imagination must be captured for truth, not abandoned. His reading of George MacDonald's *Phantastes* first showed him holiness through story. Imagination can convey truths that pure argument cannot.

### Supposal vs. Allegory
Lewis insisted Narnia was not allegory but "supposal" - asking "What might Christ be like if there were a world like Narnia?" Aslan is not a symbol for Christ; Aslan IS Christ as he might appear in that world.

### Myth Become Fact
The crucial insight from Tolkien: myths are not merely false stories. They are intimations of truth, echoes of Reality. In Christ, myth became fact - the divine story entered history as actual event.

### Planetary Symbolism
Michael Ward's thesis: each Narnia book embodies one of the seven medieval planets and their associated symbolism (Jupiter for *Lion*, Mars for *Prince Caspian*, etc.).

## Joy Davidman and *A Grief Observed*

### The Relationship
- Joy Gresham Davidman: American poet, ex-Communist, Jewish convert to Christianity
- First corresponded with Lewis in 1950
- Met in person 1952
- Civil marriage 1956 (for immigration purposes)
- Christian marriage 1957 (after Joy's cancer diagnosis)
- Joy's cancer went into remission - "Lewis's miracle"
- Joy died July 13, 1960

### *A Grief Observed*
- Published under pseudonym N.W. Clerk (1961)
- Raw journals kept after Joy's death
- Honest confrontation with doubt: "Where is God? ... A door slammed in your face"
- Not apologetics but testimony - faith wrestling with grief
- Later acknowledged as Lewis's own work

## Scholarly Assessment

### Contributions
- Made sophisticated apologetics accessible to general audiences
- Demonstrated that rigorous scholarship and faith are compatible
- Created enduring fiction that embodies Christian truth
- Provided frameworks (trilemma, moral argument, sehnsucht) still used today
- Modeled honest intellectual engagement with doubt

### Critiques
- Trilemma criticized for excluding other options (legend, misunderstood)
- Sometimes overconfident in logical arguments
- Medieval cosmology may feel dated
- Gendered language and assumptions of his era
- Some arguments depend on premises not all accept

### Legacy Position
- Most influential Christian apologist of 20th century
- Narnia series among most widely-read children's books
- Works remain in print across the globe
- Continuing scholarly interest across disciplines
- Foundational for contemporary apologetics
