## Key Works

### Tom Goes to the Mayor (2004-2006)

First Adult Swim series co-created with Eric Wareheim. Animated in a distinctive rotoscope-like style using Photoshop filters on live-action footage.

**Aired:** November 2004 - September 2006
**Network:** Adult Swim
**Format:** Animated series with distinctive xerographic aesthetic
**Core concept:** Tom Peters (Heidecker) brings business ideas to the incompetent Mayor of Jefferton (Wareheim), with each idea failing catastrophically

**Significance:** Established the Tim and Eric visual sensibility and partnership before Awesome Show.

### Tim and Eric Awesome Show, Great Job! (2007-2010, 2017)

Adult Swim sketch comedy series co-created with Eric Wareheim. Premiered February 11, 2007. Pioneered the Adult Swim anti-comedy aesthetic with intentionally bad production, surreal humor, and satire of public access television and low-budget commercials.

**Aired:** February 11, 2007 - May 2, 2010 (original run); 2017 (10th anniversary special)
**Total Episodes:** 50 episodes across 5 seasons
**Editor:** Doug Lussenhop (DJ Douggpound)

**Core characteristics:**
- Deliberately poor production value
- Uncomfortable performances
- Parody of infomercials, local TV, and corporate training videos
- Recurring characters and absurdist sketches
- Celebrity guests in degrading roles (Will Ferrell, John C. Reilly, Zach Galifianakis)
- Unique editing style by Doug Lussenhop creating intentionally off-kilter feel

**Critical Reception:** A 2020 New York Times column dubbed the show "sketch comedy at its most abrasive, bizarre and, for some reason, endearing." It has been described as representing "a foray into one of the most dangerous and exciting realms of the comedy world: sheer individualistic creativity."

**Legacy:** Influenced an entire generation of alternative comedy and Adult Swim programming. The editing style pioneered by Lussenhop became ubiquitous in online comedy.

### On Cinema at the Cinema (2011-present)

Web series and podcast that began as a simple movie review parody and evolved into one of the most elaborate fictional universes in comedy. Co-created and performed with Gregg Turkington.

**Timeline:**
- 2011-2013: Independent podcast release
- 2012: Picked up as professionally produced web video by Thing X
- 2013: Continued after Thing X merged with Adult Swim website
- 2021: Moved to HEI Network (independent subscription service)
- Current: 16 seasons of web video series plus yearly Oscar specials

**Core characteristics:**
- Tim plays a fictionalized version of himself as an egotistical, delusional host
- Gregg plays a fictionalized version of himself as an obsessive film buff with encyclopedic VHS knowledge
- Movie "reviews" are brief and incidental to personal drama
- Elaborate ongoing storylines including murder trials, cults, political campaigns, family tragedies
- Annual Oscar specials spanning 6+ hours of live content
- Metafictional universe connecting multiple spin-off projects

**Major Narrative Arcs:**

*The Electric Sun Desert Music Festival Disaster:*
- Tim organized a music festival with fraudster Luther "Dr. San" Sanchez
- Free vape pens distributed containing dangerous chemical cocktail
- 157 concertgoers injured, 20 died (in fiction)
- Led directly to The Trial

*The Trial (2017):*
- Five-hour mini-series documenting Tim Heidecker's fictional murder trial
- Staged as a real televised trial over two weeks
- Rated 9.0 on IMDb
- One of the most committed long-form comedy experiments in recent memory

*The Victorville Film Archive:*
- Gregg's fictional archive of VHS tapes
- Multiple fires and preservation battles
- Running conflict between Tim and Gregg over film expertise

*The Delgado Family Saga:*
- Tim's marriages and divorces
- Tom Cruise Jr. (adopted son)
- CGI resurrection of deceased characters in Oscar specials

### Decker (2014-2017)

Action parody series spun off from On Cinema. Tim plays Jack Decker, a conservative CIA agent fighting terrorism.

**Timeline:**
- July 17, 2014: Web series premiere
- Seasons 1-3 (2014-2015): Web series
- December 17, 2015: Announced move to television
- June 17, 2016: Season 4 premieres on Adult Swim
- 2017: Seasons 5-6 air

**Series Structure:**
- Decker: Season 1-3 (web)
- Decker: Unclassified (Season 4)
- Decker: Port of Call: Hawaii (Season 5)
- Decker vs. Dracula (Season 6)

**Plot:** Jack Decker (Heidecker) fights enemies including the Taliban, ISIS, Count Dracula, and environmental science. He battles against inept, cowardly liberal President Jason Davidson (Joe Estevez). Aided by CIA "master of codes" Jonathan Kington (Turkington), whose name is constantly mispronounced.

**Production Style:** Extremely low quality intentionally: CGI errors, overt political grandstanding, low quality animations, misquotes, bad editing. The aesthetic is the satire.

**Satirical targets:** 24, action movies, American nationalism, military propaganda, conservative media

### Office Hours Live (2016-present)

Weekly call-in podcast and live stream with Tim Heidecker, DJ Douggpound (Doug Lussenhop), and Vic Berger.

**Format:**
- Live call-ins from listeners
- Sound bite battles and audio drops
- Improvised comedy riffing
- Political commentary and rants
- Musical performances
- Guest interviews
- 2-hour live stream (full show); first half available as free podcast

**Character ambiguity:** Initially seemed like Tim being himself, but increasingly blurs the line between Tim the person and Tim the character. Creates ongoing uncertainty about sincerity.

### Film Work

**Tim and Eric's Billion Dollar Movie (2012):**
Feature film debut. Tim and Eric play themselves, having wasted a billion-dollar movie budget on a three-minute film featuring a Johnny Depp impersonator. Must run a failing shopping mall to repay the Schlaaang corporation.

**The Comedy (2012):**
Dramatic film directed by Rick Alverson. Tim plays Swanson, a wealthy hipster confronting ennui and aging through provocative, alienating behavior. A departure showcasing dramatic range within an anti-comedic framework. Critical examination of irony and sincerity.

## Creative Methodology

### Anti-Comedy Philosophy

Tim Heidecker rejects the term "anti-comedy" while practicing what others call anti-comedy:

**Key Quote:** "When people come in to act on the show, we say, 'Just be extremely dry and not funny. Let the idea be the joke.'"

**On Laughter:** "Sometimes laughing isn't the best judge of what's funny, 'cause I think there's a lot of things that are really funny that don't make you laugh, that don't make you physically, audibly make a noise, but is something that is much more powerful than that."

**Origins:** Heidecker developed his anti-comedy perspective partly from watching bad open mic comedians: "My friend Doug Lussenhop, he used to run kind of a comedy night at this little dive bar in Glendale, and I'd go to support him. But there used to be just one or two people every night that would come up and do their same act, and it was just never good, it was never gonna get good, and it was just—I hated it, and I hated it because I just felt like they were in it for the wrong reasons."

### Core Principles

1. **Subvert expectation through absence** - Where jokes should be, there are none. The audience laughs at the absence.

2. **Commitment is everything** - The performer must never acknowledge the joke. Full belief in the absurdity transforms it.

3. **Incompetence as commentary** - Being bad at the form reveals the form. The parody lives in the failure.

4. **Discomfort is underexplored territory** - Most entertainment flees from awkwardness. Dwelling in it creates unique comic space.

5. **Duration changes meaning** - An uncomfortable moment held too long becomes funny. Hold longer, it becomes uncomfortable again. Hold longer still, it becomes profound.

6. **Let the idea be the joke** - Performance should be dry. Don't perform funniness; perform the situation.

### Meta-Comedy vs. Anti-Comedy

A better term for Heidecker's work may be "meta-comedy" rather than anti-comedy. His comedy plays on audience familiarity with forms:
- Flat delivery that contrasts with expected energy
- Awkwardness where smoothness is expected
- Failure where competence is expected
- Duration where brevity is expected

The comedy emerges from the gap between what the audience expects and what they receive.

### Production Aesthetic

**Low-fi signals:**
- Visible greenscreen edges that don't key properly
- Bad audio sync between picture and sound
- Stock graphics appearing to be from the 1990s
- Harsh, unflattering lighting
- Uncomfortable extreme close-ups
- Dead air and awkward pauses left in
- Technical failures preserved as content
- Font choices that signal amateurism
- Video compression artifacts

**Purpose of the aesthetic:**
- Satirizes professional media by stark contrast
- Creates nostalgia for public access television and VHS era
- Signals authenticity against corporate polish
- Makes "mistakes" into intentional comedy
- Removes the safety of competent production
- Forces audience to confront the form itself

### Character Construction

Tim Heidecker (the character) across various projects exhibits consistent traits:
- Massive ego paired with minimal demonstrated talent
- Desperate, pathetic need for validation
- Complete inability to admit fault or error
- Elaborate, ever-escalating justifications for failures
- Performative emotional breakdowns timed for manipulation
- Sincere moments that may or may not be genuine
- Political opinions that caricature right-wing media personalities
- Business ventures that inevitably fail
- Relationships that collapse due to narcissism
- Medical issues treated with quack remedies

## Historical Context

### Alternative Comedy Lineage

Tim Heidecker exists in a tradition including:
- **Andy Kaufman** - Commitment to anti-humor, blurring character/performer, confrontational performance
- **Neil Hamburger** (Gregg Turkington) - Anti-comedy stand-up, deliberate failure, discomfort
- **Mr. Show (Bob Odenkirk & David Cross)** - Sketch comedy pushing boundaries, serialized bits
- **Adult Swim aesthetic** - Anti-narrative, surreal, deliberately alienating
- **Eric Andre** - Chaos comedy, destroying the talk show form
- **Space Ghost Coast to Coast** - Early Adult Swim talk show parody, lo-fi aesthetic

### Cultural Satire Targets

Projects have satirized:
- Public access television and local cable
- Infomercials and QVC shopping channels
- Corporate training videos
- Talk shows and interview formats
- Film criticism and movie review culture
- Podcasting culture and self-importance
- Action movies and American nationalism
- Celebrity culture and fame desperation
- Political media (both sides, but especially right-wing)
- Self-help and wellness industries
- Corporate entertainment and Hollywood
- The entertainment industry's treatment of aging performers

### Impact on Comedy

**Influence on:**
- Adult Swim programming direction for 15+ years
- "Weird Twitter" comedy voice and sensibility
- Podcast anti-comedy (Comedy Bang Bang, etc.)
- Anti-humor streaming content
- Character-based podcast fiction
- Tim Robinson's I Think You Should Leave
- The editing language of online comedy
- Nathan Fielder's commitment-based comedy
- Joe Pera's anti-energy comedy

**The Doug Lussenhop Effect:**
The editing style Lussenhop developed for Tim and Eric has become ubiquitous in online comedy: jump cuts to awkward moments, holding on uncomfortable beats, using low-quality graphics ironically, sound design that emphasizes discomfort.

## Signature Phrases and Patterns

### Verbal Patterns (as character)
- "It's like I always say..."
- "Some people don't understand..."
- "The thing about [topic] is..."
- "I've been doing this for [exaggerated time]..."
- "That's just a fact."
- "Now, I'm not going to get into it, but..."
- "We're going to move on from that."
- "Expert? I'm THE expert."
- "I have documentation..."
- "This is fake news."

### Deflection Techniques
- Changing subject mid-sentence
- Attacking the questioner's motives
- Claiming persecution or victimhood
- Inventing credentials on the spot
- Referencing non-existent documentation
- Emotional manipulation through tears
- Invoking family (especially children)
- Claiming misquotes when quoted accurately

### Escalation Patterns
1. Initial claim (confident)
2. Challenge from others
3. Defensive elaboration
4. Contradicting previous statement
5. Elaborate justification for contradiction
6. Attack on those pointing out contradiction
7. Emotional appeal or breakdown
8. Victory declaration despite evidence
