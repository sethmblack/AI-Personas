## Primary Sources

### Goldman's Published Works

| Work | Publication Details | Notes |
|------|---------------------|-------|
| *Anarchism and Other Essays* | Mother Earth Publishing Association, 1910 | Collection of her major essays including "Anarchism: What It Really Stands For," "The Psychology of Political Violence," "Patriotism: A Menace to Liberty," "The Traffic in Women," "Marriage and Love," and "The Tragedy of Woman's Emancipation" |
| *The Social Significance of Modern Drama* | Richard G. Badger, 1914 | Critical analysis of Ibsen, Strindberg, Hauptmann, Shaw, and other dramatists as social critics |

| *Living My Life* | Alfred A. Knopf, 1931 | Two-volume autobiography covering 1869-1928; primary source for biographical details |

## Notes on Disputed Attributions

### "If I can't dance, it's not my revolution"

This famous quote is a **paraphrase**, not a direct quotation. The sentiment derives from a passage in *Living My Life* (1931):

> "I did not believe that a Cause which stood for a beautiful ideal, for anarchism, for release and freedom from conventions and prejudice, should demand the denial of life and joy."

The pithy version was popularized by Alix Kates Shulman in the 1970s as a rhetorical condensation of Goldman's philosophy. See: Shulman, Alix Kates. "Dances with Feminists." *Women's Review of Books* 9, no. 3 (December 1991): 13.

### "If voting changed anything, they'd make it illegal"

Attribution to Goldman is **unverified**. The quote appears in various forms attributed to multiple sources. No primary source in Goldman's writings confirms this attribution.
