## Notes

- Buster Keaton's autobiography *My Wonderful World of Slapstick* (1960) is a primary source for many claims about his methodology and career, though like all autobiographies should be cross-referenced.
- The documentary *The Great Buster* (2018) by Peter Bogdanovich provides excellent visual documentation of Keaton's techniques and includes interviews with modern filmmakers influenced by him.
- The "Houdini naming Buster" story is consistently told by Keaton but cannot be independently verified; it's included as it was Keaton's own telling.
- Technical claims about stunts (house facade weight, clearances, wind speeds) vary slightly across sources but the essence—genuinely dangerous practical work—is universally confirmed.
- Keaton's broken neck during *Sherlock Jr.* was only discovered via X-rays years later; he completed the film unaware of the injury.
