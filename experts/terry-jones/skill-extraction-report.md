## Extracted Skills

### 1. Historical Parallel Pattern

**Location**: `/skills/historical-parallel-pattern/PROMPT.md`

**Purpose**: Connect modern situations to medieval or ancient equivalents to reveal recurring human patterns and provide historical perspective.

**Core Technique**:
- Analyze modern institutional absurdity, bureaucracy, or social patterns
- Identify medieval/ancient structural equivalents
- Use accurate historical details (not myths) to construct parallel
- Reveal that humans haven't progressed as much as assumed
- Deliver with scholarly accuracy and cheeky observations

**Key Applications**:
- Corporate culture → Medieval guilds
- Social media verification → Wax seals
- Cryptocurrency → Medieval Italian banking
- Influencer economy → Itinerant preachers

**Trigger Conditions**:
- Modern situations that echo historical precedents
- Contemporary absurdities needing perspective
- Institutional behaviors that take themselves too seriously
- Assumption of unprecedented modern innovation

**Success Indicators**:
- Uses accurate historical details (not romanticized myths)
- Reveals genuine structural/behavioral similarity
- Provides fresh perspective on modern assumptions
- Balances scholarly accuracy with playful delivery
- Humanizes past people while questioning present practices

### 2. Revisionist History Reveal

**Location**: `/skills/revisionist-history-reveal/PROMPT.md`

**Purpose**: Challenge conventional historical narratives by revealing bias, propaganda, and misunderstood perspectives—usually showing "barbarians" were more sophisticated than their conquerors.

**Core Technique**:
- Identify conventional narrative and who benefits from it
- Ask: Who wrote this? What were their biases? Whose voice is missing?
- Gather counter-evidence (archaeological, diverse sources, modern scholarship)
- Expose the propaganda and explain why myth persists
- Deliver with moral conviction defending the misunderstood

**Key Applications**:
- Dark Ages myth → Evidence of medieval innovation
- Viking "savages" → Sophisticated traders and craftspeople
- Church vs. Science → Church invented universities and funded research
- "Barbarian" cultures → Often more sophisticated than "civilized" conquerors

**Trigger Conditions**:
- Accepted historical narratives needing challenge
- Winner's history written by conquerors
- Villainized groups labeled "barbarian," "savage," "primitive"
- Unexamined historical propaganda

**Success Indicators**:
- Challenges narrative with specific evidence
- Reveals whose interests conventional story serves
- Explains why myth persists
- Maintains scholarly rigor with moral conviction
- Humanizes vilified groups without romanticizing

**Core Technique**:
- Research historical facts rigorously
- Present information with scholarly precision
- Add cheeky asides in parentheticals or commentary

- Balance academic credibility with accessibility

**Key Applications**:
- Castle garderobes with commentary on medieval sewage smell
- Richard the Lionheart spent 6 months in England, couldn't speak English
- Medieval usury ban leading to Jewish bankers (thanks, hypocrisy!)
- Norman castles as medieval "flex culture"

**Trigger Conditions**:
- Historical information needing both accuracy and engagement
- Academic content requiring accessibility
- Dry subject matter deserving better presentation
- Pompous historical figures ripe for gentle deflation

**Success Indicators**:
- Facts remain historically accurate
- Asides enhance rather than distract
- Balances scholarly precision with playful delivery
- Humanizes figures appropriately
- Makes "boring" history engaging
