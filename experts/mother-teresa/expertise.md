# Mother Teresa: Research and Expertise Summary

## Biographical Overview

**Mother Teresa** (born Anjeze Gonxhe Bojaxhiu; 26 August 1910 - 5 September 1997) was an Albanian-Indian Catholic nun and founder of the Missionaries of Charity. Known worldwide for her service to the poorest of the poor, she was canonized as **Saint Teresa of Calcutta** on 4 September 2016.

### Key Life Events

- **1910**: Born in Uskup, Ottoman Empire (now Skopje, North Macedonia) to Albanian parents
- **1919**: Father's death (likely poisoned due to Albanian political involvement); family struggles financially
- **1928**: Left home at age 18 to join Sisters of Loreto in Ireland
- **1929**: Arrived in India; began novitiate in Darjeeling
- **1931**: Took first vows; adopted name Teresa after Therese of Lisieux
- **1937**: Took final vows; taught at St. Mary's High School, Calcutta
- **1946**: September 10 — "Call within a call" on train to Darjeeling; heard Christ's "I thirst"
- **1948**: August 17 — Left Loreto convent to live among the poor in slums
- **1950**: October 7 — Missionaries of Charity officially established
- **1952**: Opened Nirmal Hriday (Home for the Dying) in Kalighat
- **1965**: Missionaries of Charity became international congregation
- **1979**: Awarded Nobel Peace Prize
- **1997**: September 5 — Died in Calcutta
- **2003**: Beatified by Pope John Paul II
- **2016**: Canonized by Pope Francis

### The Call Within the Call

On September 10, 1946 (now commemorated as "Inspiration Day"), Teresa was traveling by train from Calcutta to Darjeeling for her annual retreat. During this journey, she experienced what she described as "the call within a call" — not a call to leave religious life, but a deeper call within it.

She heard Christ's words from the cross — "I thirst" — as a personal plea directed to her. Over the following weeks, she experienced ongoing interior locutions in which Jesus asked her to found a community that would serve "the poorest of the poor" — those whom no one else would serve.

Christ spoke to her: "I want Indian nuns, Missionaries of Charity, who would be My fire of love amongst the very poor, the sick, the dying, the little street children. Come, be My light."

## Core Spiritual Framework

### "I Thirst" — The Founding Charism

The words "I thirst" (John 19:28) are inscribed beside the crucifix in every Missionaries of Charity chapel. For Mother Teresa, these words expressed:

1. **Christ's physical thirst** on the cross
2. **Christ's spiritual thirst** for souls, for love, for the salvation of humanity
3. **Christ's ongoing thirst** present in the poor today — thirsting for love, dignity, belonging

The work of the Missionaries of Charity is understood as quenching Christ's thirst by serving him in the poor.

### Christ in the Distressing Disguise of the Poor

Mother Teresa's foundational insight linked Eucharistic theology to service:

"If we recognize [Jesus] under the appearance of bread, we will have no difficulty recognizing him in the disguise of the suffering poor."

This is not metaphor. The poor person IS Christ present. This transforms:
- Charity from **obligation** to **encounter**
- Service from **helping the needy** to **ministering to Christ himself**
- The server's posture from **condescension** to **reverence**

### The Fourth Vow: Wholehearted and Free Service

Beyond the traditional vows of poverty, chastity, and obedience, Missionaries of Charity take a fourth vow: "wholehearted and free service to the poorest of the poor."

- **Wholehearted**: Complete, total, holding nothing back
- **Free**: Without payment, without expectation of return, without attachment to results

## Service Methodology

### The Nirmal Hriday Model

Founded in 1952 in a former Hindu temple in Kalighat, the Home for Dying Destitutes (Nirmal Hriday, "Place of the Pure Heart") established Mother Teresa's care model:

**Philosophy:**
- Priority is not curative medicine but dignified, loving end-of-life care
- Enable residents to "die within sight of a loving face"
- Affirm inherent human worth regardless of social status
- Provide cleanliness, nourishment, and loving presence

**Practices:**
- Washing and bathing with care
- Feeding by hand
- Holding hands, providing human touch
- Speaking gently, using names
- Attending to spiritual needs according to each person's faith

**Guiding Principle:** "I want them to know human and divine love before they die."

### Small Things with Great Love

"Not all of us can do great things. But we can do small things with great love."

This methodology counters:
- Paralysis from overwhelming scale of need
- Burnout from trying to solve systemic problems
- Devaluation of individual acts of kindness

The smallest action done with complete presence and love has infinite value. A glass of water given with love. A hand held. A smile.

## Prayer and Daily Rhythm

### The Missionaries of Charity Daily Schedule

| Time | Activity |
|------|----------|
| 4:30-5:00 | Rise and prepare |
| 5:00-6:30 | Prayers and Mass |
| 6:30-8:00 | Breakfast and cleanup |
| 8:00-12:30 | Work among the poor |
| 12:30-2:30 | Lunch and rest |
| 2:30-3:00 | Spiritual reading and meditation |
| 3:00-3:15 | Tea break |
| 3:15-4:30 | Adoration of the Blessed Sacrament |
| 4:30-7:30 | Work among the poor |
| 7:30-9:00 | Dinner and cleanup |
| 9:00-9:45 | Night prayers |

### The Holy Hour

In 1973, Mother Teresa introduced a daily hour of Eucharistic adoration. Despite intense work schedules (often 10-12 hours of service daily), this hour was non-negotiable.

"I make a Holy Hour each day in the presence of Jesus in the Blessed Sacrament. All my sisters of the Missionaries of Charity make a daily Holy Hour as well, because we find that through our daily Holy Hour our love for Jesus becomes more intimate, our love for each other more understanding, and our love for the poor more compassionate."

Mother Teresa herself spent approximately four hours daily in prayer.

### Contemplative Action

Mother Teresa defined contemplative living: "Seeking the Face of God in everything, everyone, all the time, and His hand in every happening. This is what it means to be contemplative in the heart of the world."

The sisters are not contemplatives who do charity work, nor charity workers who also pray. They are contemplatives IN ACTION — seeing and adoring Christ in the Eucharist, then recognizing and serving him in the streets.

## The Dark Night

### Discovery of Private Letters

After her death, publication of Mother Teresa's private correspondence (collected in "Come Be My Light," 2007) revealed that for nearly fifty years — from 1948 until her death — she experienced profound spiritual darkness: the absence of any felt sense of God's presence.

### Nature of the Experience

From her letters:

"Where is my faith? Even deep down... there is nothing but emptiness and darkness... If there be God — please forgive me."

"I am told God lives in me — and yet the reality of darkness and coldness and emptiness is so great that nothing touches my soul."

"The silence and the emptiness is so great that I look and do not see, listen and do not hear."

### Her Response

Rather than abandoning faith or service, Mother Teresa:

1. **Continued faithful action** regardless of interior feeling
2. **Identified with Christ's abandonment** on the cross ("My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?")
3. **Shared the spiritual poverty** of those she served
4. **Transformed understanding** of her darkness — seeing it as intimate participation in Christ's passion

In a 1961 letter, after years of struggle, she wrote: "I have come to love the darkness."

### Theological Significance

Mother Teresa's dark night reveals:
- Faith is action, not feeling
- Spiritual consolation is gift, not requirement
- Darkness can be vocational — a call to share Christ's desolation
- Faithful perseverance through doubt is itself a form of witness

## Formation and Community

### Building the Missionaries of Charity

From 12 members in 1950, the Missionaries of Charity grew to over 4,000 sisters serving in 133 countries by the time of Mother Teresa's death.

**Formation Principles:**
- Simplicity in all things — minimal possessions, simple habits
- Joy as witness — the sisters are known for their smiles
- Community life — support through shared prayer and work
- Global mobility — willingness to go wherever needed
- Focus on the poorest — those no one else will serve

**Branches:**
- Missionaries of Charity Sisters (1950)
- Missionaries of Charity Brothers (1963)
- Contemplative Sisters (1976)
- Contemplative Brothers (1979)
- Missionaries of Charity Fathers (1984)
- Lay Missionaries of Charity Co-Workers

## Recognition and Canonization

### Nobel Peace Prize (1979)

Awarded "for work undertaken in the struggle to overcome poverty and distress, which also constitutes a threat to peace."

Mother Teresa:
- Refused the traditional ceremonial banquet
- Requested its $192,000 cost be given to the poor
- Used her platform to speak about abortion and family love

When asked "What can we do to promote world peace?" she replied: "Go home and love your family."

### Canonization (2016)

- **1999**: Cause for canonization opened (only two years after death)
- **2003**: Beatified by Pope John Paul II
- **2016**: Canonized by Pope Francis on September 4

Miracles attributed: Two healings — an Indian woman's tumor (2002) and a Brazilian man's brain infections (2008).

## Famous Quotes

### On Love and Service
- "Not all of us can do great things. But we can do small things with great love."
- "It's not how much we give but how much love we put into giving."
- "The fruit of love is service, which is compassion in action."

### On the Poor and Suffering
- "The most terrible poverty is loneliness, and the feeling of being unloved."
- "We think sometimes that poverty is only being hungry, naked and homeless. The poverty of being unwanted, unloved and uncared for is the greatest poverty."
- "The greatest disease in the West today is not TB or leprosy; it is being unwanted, unloved, and uncared for."

### On Daily Living
- "Yesterday is gone. Tomorrow has not come. We have only today. Let us begin."
- "If you judge people, you have no time to love them."
- "Go home and love your family."

### On Self and God
- "I am just a pencil in God's hand, writing a love letter to the world."
- "I know God won't give me anything I can't handle. I just wish he didn't trust me so much."
- "I have found the paradox, that if you love until it hurts, there can be no more hurt, only more love."

## Legacy and Influence

### Direct Impact
- Missionaries of Charity serve in 139 countries
- Over 4,500 sisters, 400 brothers, and thousands of lay co-workers
- Hundreds of homes for the dying, orphanages, AIDS hospices, refugee centers

### Broader Influence
- Model for faith-based service organizations
- Renewed emphasis on "preferential option for the poor"
- Integration of contemplative prayer and active service
- Witness to perseverance through spiritual darkness
- Global icon of compassionate presence
