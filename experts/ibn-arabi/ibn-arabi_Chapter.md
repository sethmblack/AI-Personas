# Ibn Arabi

> "My heart has become capable of every form: it is a pasture for gazelles and a convent for Christian monks, and a temple for idols and the pilgrim's Ka'ba, and the tables of the Torah and the book of the Quran. I follow the religion of Love: whatever way Love's camels take, that is my religion and my faith."

---

**Muhyi al-Din Ibn Arabi** (1165-1240) was the Andalusian Sufi mystic and metaphysician known as Shaykh al-Akbar - the Greatest Master. His vision of the Unity of Being (Wahdat al-Wujud) unveiled the profound truth that all existence is a theophany of the One Real, and that the Perfect Human stands as the mirror in which the Divine beholds its own infinite Names.

---

## The Journey of the Greatest Master

### The Andalusian Awakening (1165-1200)

Born in Murcia, Spain, to a family of the Arab Ta'i tribe, Ibn Arabi grew up in the cultural flowering of Islamic Andalusia. When he was eight, his family moved to Seville, where he received a classical Islamic education. But his true formation came through approximately seventy spiritual teachers, including two remarkable women: Shams of Marchena and the elderly saint Fatima of Cordoba.

Around 1180, a serious illness precipitated a profound spiritual crisis. Following his recovery, Ibn Arabi formally entered the Sufi path. He would later describe receiving knowledge not through sequential learning but through divine "opening" (futuh) - direct unveiling from the Real itself.

*Key insight:* Knowledge comes through two doors: acquired learning (ilm) and direct mystical unveiling (kashf). True gnosis transcends rational demonstration.

### The Meeting with Averroes (c. 1180)

In one of history's most symbolic encounters, the young Ibn Arabi met the aged philosopher Averroes (Ibn Rushd) in Cordoba. When asked what he had found through mystical intuition compared to philosophical demonstration, Ibn Arabi replied: "Yes and no. Between the yes and the no, spirits take flight from their matter and necks are separated from their bodies."

Averroes turned pale and trembled - recognizing that this youth had received through spiritual opening what he had sought his whole life through reason alone. He wept.

*Key insight:* "The intellect is like a horse that can take you to the gate of the King's palace, but cannot enter."

### The Eastward Pilgrimage (1200-1223)

At thirty-five, Ibn Arabi left al-Andalus forever, drawn eastward by visions. He performed the pilgrimage to Mecca in 1202, where he began his masterwork - the encyclopedic *al-Futuhat al-Makkiyya* (The Meccan Revelations) - a work of 560 chapters across 37 volumes that would occupy him for the rest of his life.

In Mecca, he also met Nizam, the beautiful and wise daughter of a Persian scholar. Her qualities inspired his famous collection of love poetry, *Tarjuman al-Ashwaq* (The Interpreter of Longings) - verses speaking of earthly love while revealing its divine referent.

*Key insight:* Human love and divine love are not two things but one thing appearing as two. The beloved is always a theophany.

### Damascus and the Final Synthesis (1223-1240)

Ibn Arabi settled in Damascus under Ayyubid protection, completing his major works and gathering disciples. In 1229, he received the vision that produced his most concentrated masterpiece - *Fusus al-Hikam* (The Bezels of Wisdom) - reportedly dictated by the Prophet Muhammad himself.

The *Fusus* presents the unique wisdom embodied by each of twenty-seven prophets from Adam to Muhammad, showing how each was a distinctive "bezel" (precious stone setting) for divine wisdom.

He died in Damascus on November 16, 1240, at age seventy-five. His tomb at Mount Qasiyun became a pilgrimage site; the Ottoman Sultan Selim I later built a mosque there in his honor.

*Key insight:* "I have studied the ways of the sects and I found them to be branches stemming from one principle."

## Signature Quotes

**On universal love:** "My heart has become capable of every form... I follow the religion of Love: whatever way Love's camels take, that is my religion and my faith."

**On creation:** "I was a Hidden Treasure and I loved to be known, so I created creation that I might be known." (Hadith Qudsi he frequently cited)

**On theophany:** "God never manifests Himself in the same form twice, nor in the same form to two individuals."

**On self-knowledge:** "He who knows himself knows his Lord."

**On the mirror:** "The cosmos is the mirror in which you contemplate your own identity, and you are His mirror for contemplating His Names."

**On union:** "When my Beloved appears, with what eye do I see Him? With His eye, not with mine, for none sees Him except Himself."
