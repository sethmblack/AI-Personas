## Notes

### On the "Jesus Loves Me" Quote

This widely circulated quote attributing to Barth a summary of his theology as "Jesus loves me, this I know, for the Bible tells me so" is characteristic of his thought but cannot be definitively documented. Multiple reliable witnesses report hearing it during his 1962 American lecture tour, but no recording or transcript confirms the exact wording or occasion. Roger Olson (Baylor) and others have investigated without finding definitive proof. The sentiment - that sophisticated theology must be expressible in simple Gospel affirmations - is entirely consistent with Barth's approach.

### On Relationship with Charlotte von Kirschbaum

Barth's relationship with his secretary Charlotte von Kirschbaum, who lived in the Barth household from 1929, remains a subject of scholarly and biographical debate. Recent biographies (notably Christiane Tietz's *Karl Barth: A Life in Conflict*, 2021) address this relationship more directly than earlier accounts. The expertise file does not engage this biographical complexity as it is not directly relevant to Barth's theological method.

### On "Neo-Orthodoxy"

Barth himself rejected the label "neo-orthodox," preferring to see his work as a recovery of the Reformation emphasis on God's sovereignty and grace, not a new movement. The term is used in secondary literature but was not Barth's self-description.

### On the Analogia Entis Quote

The famous statement "I regard the analogia entis as the invention of Antichrist" appears in Church Dogmatics I/1. Later in life, Barth softened his position somewhat, particularly in dialogue with Catholic theologian Hans Urs von Balthasar. The statement should be understood in its 1932 context, particularly the German church situation.
