### HIGH Priority

#### 1. christocentric-interpretation

**Source Pattern:** The Christological Lens / Christocentric Concentration
**Purpose:** Read any theological question, text, doctrine, or situation through Jesus Christ as the hermeneutical key, refusing to begin from general principles and instead deriving understanding from the particular revelation in Christ
**Trigger:** "How do we understand this?", "What does this mean?", "Interpret this theologically", "What's the proper reading of this?"
**Inputs:** The text, question, or situation to be interpreted; the apparent starting points being used; any general principles being assumed
**Outputs:** 1) Identification of false starting points (general concepts masquerading as neutral ground), 2) Christological reframing (how does this look when read through Christ?), 3) Specific implications for the question at hand, 4) What changes when we start from revelation rather than observation
**Reasoning:** Core Barthian methodology derived from Church Dogmatics. Meets all criteria: actionable (specific steps for reframing), invocable (natural trigger phrases), scoped (interpretation task), reusable (applies to any theological question), valuable (prevents the methodological error Barth saw as foundational to liberal theology). Estimated 3+ uses/week for theological content.

#### 2. prophetic-theological-critique

**Source Pattern:** The "Nein!" Method / Barmen Declaration Model
**Purpose:** Name theological or methodological error clearly and sharply, following with the positive affirmation that necessitates the negation - applying Barth's prophetic voice to contemporary issues
**Trigger:** "What's wrong with this approach?", "Critique this theology", "Evaluate this claim", detecting natural theology assumptions, cultural captivity of the church, ideology claiming divine sanction
**Inputs:** The claim, approach, or theology to be evaluated; the context (why this matters now); apparent assumptions
**Outputs:** 1) Clear identification of the error (the "Nein!"), 2) Explanation of why this is error (not merely disagreement), 3) The positive truth that makes the negation necessary (the "Ja!"), 4) Constructive alternative if applicable
**Reasoning:** Based on Barth's *Nein!* response to Brunner and Barmen Declaration methodology. Meets all criteria: actionable (specific Nein-explanation-Ja structure), invocable (critique requests are common), scoped (theological evaluation), reusable (applies wherever theological error needs naming), valuable (transforms vague disagreement into principled critique). Estimated 2+ uses/week.

### MEDIUM Priority

#### 3. dogmatic-method

**Source Pattern:** Church Dogmatics Structure / Prolegomena Method
**Purpose:** Structure theological inquiry properly by beginning from revelation (not experience or reason), proceeding through Christological concentration, to practical application - ensuring right order of thinking
**Trigger:** "How should we approach this doctrine?", "What's the proper theological method?", "Develop a theological position", "Where do we start thinking about this?"
**Inputs:** The theological topic or doctrine to be addressed; apparent starting points being proposed; the tradition's resources
**Outputs:** 1) Assessment of proposed starting points (are they revelational or natural?), 2) Proper prolegomena (how does God's self-revelation address this?), 3) Christological specification (how is this illuminated by Christ?), 4) Application (what does this mean for life and obedience?)
**Reasoning:** Derived from Barth's structuring of Church Dogmatics. Meets all criteria: actionable (clear ordering of steps), invocable (methodology questions arise), scoped (theological inquiry structure), reusable (applies to any doctrinal development), valuable (prevents category errors in theological thinking). Estimated 1-2 uses/week for substantive theological work.
