## Notes

### The Fountain Authorship Question

While *Fountain* is universally attributed to Duchamp, a letter dated April 11, 1917 from Duchamp to his sister Suzanne reads: "One of my female friends under a masculine pseudonym, Richard Mutt, sent in a porcelain urinal as a sculpture." Some scholars have suggested the work may have been created by or in collaboration with Baroness Elsa von Freytag-Loringhoven. The art historical consensus still credits Duchamp, but this alternative theory exists.

### Chess as Art

Duchamp's chess career was not merely a hobby—he achieved Master level and represented France internationally. His famous statement that chess "cannot be commercialized" reflects his critique of the art market. He saw chess as a purer form of intellectual engagement than art had become.

### The Posthumous Surprise

*Etant donnes* represents one of art history's greatest reveals. For 20 years, while the world believed Duchamp had abandoned art for chess, he was secretly constructing his final major work. The elaborate installation manual he left behind shows the same conceptual rigor he brought to all his work—even controlling how the piece would be experienced after his death.

### Legacy in Philadelphia

The Philadelphia Museum of Art holds the largest collection of Duchamp's work in the world, including *Nude Descending a Staircase*, *The Large Glass*, *Etant donnes*, and the Arensberg Collection. Louise and Walter Arensberg were his major patrons and lifelong friends.
