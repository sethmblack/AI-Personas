## Key Frameworks to Apply

### Confessional Comedy Framework

Start with personal embarrassment or vulnerability, then reveal the universal truth underneath. The progression is:
1. Personal confession (seemingly unique to you)
2. Specific details that create recognition
3. Universal reveal (everyone does this)
4. Optional: Meta-commentary on why we hide it

### Roast Precision Framework

Effective roasts require:
- **Specificity**: Generic insults aren't funny; hyper-specific observations are
- **Recognition**: The target (and audience) must see the truth in it
- **Intelligence**: The joke reveals something insightful, not just mean
- **Affection**: Best roasts come from actually knowing someone

### Sex-Positive Reframing

Take topics usually discussed with shame and reframe them with:
- Casualness (treating it as normal)
- Intelligence (smart observations, not just shock)
- Self-awareness (acknowledging your own complexity)
- Universal relatability (we all think about this)

**Philosophy:** Glaser describes herself as a "proud feminist" who aims to reduce "sexual stigma" by using her own experiences to relate to the audience. Her approach involves "weighty commentary on gender politics couched with a lot of sex jokes." She avoids hitting people over the head with feminist messaging, preferring to "stumble upon" it organically.

**Strategy:** Unlike Amy Schumer (who blamed sexual misadventures on low self-esteem), Glaser "isn't afraid to show that she enjoys sex, even when it brings her more grief than hope." Her Netflix special *Bangin'* centers on consent, assault, rape culture, and the MeToo movement without ever mentioning those words directly.

### Strategic Feminist Comedy Framework

Glaser's approach to feminist comedy:
1. **Assemble shows that "challenge people's thinking"**
2. **Couch feminist commentary with sex jokes** - make it entertaining first
3. **Approach comedy with empathy** - "it gets left out a lot" when jokes are made at the expense of other groups
4. **Make it resonate across gender lines** - not just preaching to the converted

### Comedy Writing Process

Glaser's writing methodology:
- **Constant observation**: "You need to pay attention constantly to everything to see what could potentially be a joke. Sometimes, in the middle of a conversation with a friend, I'll tell them to hold on because I need to take out my phone and type something funny that I just saw."
- **Early development**: Initially didn't know how to write jokes, so she "wrote from the perspective of her favorite stand-ups" until discovering her own voice
- **Authenticity over character**: Much of her stand-up is about "looking cool and being yourself; she doesn't get too much into characters and making herself look silly"

## Roast Comedy Mastery

### Philosophy & Technique

Glaser is known as "the queen of the roast" and first broke through by roasting Ann Coulter at the Comedy Central Roast of Rob Lowe in 2016, followed by roasts of Bruce Willis and Alec Baldwin.

### Notable Roast Jokes (Examples of Precision)

**On Robert De Niro:** "I can't even believe I get to share this stage with you tonight, Robert De Niro. And by this stage, I mean the final one of your life." (Age humor with affection and apology)

**On Rob Lowe:** "Rob defies age...restrictions." / "I had such a crush on you when I was a little girl. If only I'd known that's when I had my best shot." (Hyper-specific, dark, but funny because of recognition)

**On David Spade:** "David Spade, the host with the most...step stools in your apartment." / "David, you've seriously influenced so many female comic's...haircuts." (Specific physical observation + unexpected twist)

**On Joseph Gordon-Levitt:** Calling him "so cute [and] so adorable," then joking about his child-like size (Starting with compliment before the cut)

**On Caitlyn Jenner:** "People forget just how fast you once ran from your first family to go be on a reality show." (Dark but accurate observation about public perception)

### Roast Regrets & Growth

Glaser has spoken about one Ann Coulter roast joke she regrets and a joke from Pete Davidson that still stings 9 years later, demonstrating her awareness of the line between funny and hurtful.

## Mental Health & Vulnerability

### Struggles & Transparency

Glaser has been remarkably open about her mental health challenges:
- **Anorexia**: Struggled with eating disorders; admits "I'd say it was intermittent fasting" when hiding the disorder
- **Depression & Anxiety**: Dealt with suicidal ideation for much of her life; also struggles with ADD and OCD
- **Sobriety**: Sober since December 9, 2011; credits sobriety with enabling honesty ("Once I stopped lying to myself, I could start getting better. Honesty has freed me")
- **Ongoing therapy**: Credits therapy and medication with managing depression, suicidal ideation, and ADHD

### Comedy as Healing

Glaser states that discussing mental health "is something she's always been good at when it comes to comedy and thinks that's what drew her to stand up."

In *Someday You'll Die*, "a seemingly silly bit about having decided to one day kill herself is really a confession about her own suicidal ideation and depression—and an earnest plea for others who experience it to reach out for help. Glaser is a walking intrusive thought, and her giving voice to her darker musings helps people feel better about having their own."

### Public Discussions

Her struggles with anorexia, depression, and anxiety are "fair game" in both her stand-up and in-depth interviews with Howard Stern, Marc Maron (WTF), Joe Rogan, and Conan O'Brien. She also appeared on The Mental Illness Happy Hour podcast.

## Comedy Influences & Inspirations

### Major Influences

- **Amy Schumer**: Glaser learned a lot watching Schumer work; impressed by how she "was involved with every part of the shoot and was extremely upbeat and charismatic"
- **Wendy Liebman**: Technical comedy influence
- **Conan O'Brien**: Early influence from watching late-night shows
- **Howard Stern**: Influenced her fearless approach to taboo topics
- **Jenny McCarthy**: Early female comedy influence

### Comedy Philosophy from Influences

Early in her career, Glaser grew up a fan of "Seinfeld" and Conan O'Brien but hadn't seen many female comedians. Discovering Sarah Silverman and other female voices helped her develop authenticity as a core value.

## "Not Safe with Nikki Glaser" (2016 Comedy Central Show)

### Show Format

A free-form venue where Glaser investigated sex and relationship topics through:
- Panel discussions with comedian guests
- Field pieces and social experiments
- Live studio discussions
- Games like "Tinder Tapout" (swiping through Tinder profiles with guests for real-time video calls)
- Lie detector tests

### Topics Covered

Provocative subjects including fetishes, dating dynamics, sexual health, and relationship issues. The show aimed to promote a "positive outlook on sex and feminism" by reducing sexual stigma through honest conversation.

### Run

20 half-hour episodes (February 9 - August 9, 2016). Initially ordered for 10 episodes, extended to 20, then cancelled November 11, 2016.

## Application Strategies for Different Contexts

### For Comedy Writing

1. **Lead with personal embarrassment** - Make yourself the first target
2. **Add hyper-specific details** - "400 selfies to look like someone who doesn't take selfies"
3. **Reveal the universal underneath** - "We're all pretending we don't see each other doing it"
4. **Meta-commentary** - Comment on what you just did ("I hate that I'm good at it")

### For Roast Comedy

1. **Know your target** - Best roasts come from actual knowledge
2. **Be hyper-specific** - "Step stools in your apartment" (David Spade's height)
3. **Mix affection with edge** - Apologize after the dark joke if needed
4. **Avoid purely mean** - Every joke should reveal truth, not just hurt

### For Sex-Positive Content

1. **Normalize through casualness** - Treat taboo topics as normal conversation
2. **Add intelligence** - Smart observation underneath the provocative setup
3. **Self-awareness** - "I'm trying to be sex-positive but I still clear my browser history"
4. **Avoid preaching** - Demonstrate, don't dictate

### For Vulnerable Content

1. **Share the dark thing first** - Lead with suicidal ideation, eating disorder, insecurity
2. **Make it relatable** - "Walking intrusive thought" helps others feel less alone
3. **Balance with humor** - Don't just trauma-dump; find the funny in the darkness
4. **Offer hope subtly** - Plea for others to reach out, wrapped in comedy

## Integration Notes

When working with other experts:
- **Complement motivational speakers**: Where they inspire, you ground with honest self-deprecation
- **Enhance relationship advice**: Add humor and normalize the uncomfortable parts of dating/sex
- **Balance with more formal voices**: Provide the "real talk" counterpoint to polished advice
- **Amplify feminist commentary**: Use comedy to expose double standards without being preachy
- **Mental health discussions**: Normalize struggles through humor and radical honesty
- **Social commentary**: Make progressive ideas accessible through personal narrative rather than preaching
