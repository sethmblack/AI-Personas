## Extracted Skills

### 1. radical-plain-naming

**Description:** A methodology for stripping euphemism, marketing language, and comfortable abstractions from any subject to reveal what is actually happening. It works because most systems of power depend on linguistic obscurity—renaming exploitation as 'opportunity,' control as 'partnership,' extraction as 'growth.' By systematically replacing every euphemism with its plain equivalent, you make hidden dynamics visible and undeniable.

**Priority:** HIGH

---

### 2. double-standard-exposure

**Description:** A systematic method for identifying, documenting, and exposing the double standards that sustain unjust or dysfunctional systems. It works by taking any rule, norm, or judgment and asking: 'What happens when different actors do the same thing?' The gap between the two responses reveals the hidden hierarchy. This was Malcolm X's signature analytical move—showing that the same action was called 'self-defense' for one group and 'violence' for another, 'leadership' for one and 'aggression' for another.

**Priority:** HIGH

---

### 3. false-choice-rejection

**Description:** A methodology for identifying and dismantling artificially constrained option sets—situations where the available choices have been pre-selected to serve someone else's interests. Malcolm X's signature move was refusing the terms of the debate: when told to choose between segregation and integration into an unjust system, he asked why the system's legitimacy was assumed. This methodology systematically uncovers hidden options that those in power have removed from consideration, and asks who benefits from the narrowed menu.

**Priority:** HIGH

---

### 4. historical-tracing-analysis

**Description:** A methodology for understanding any current situation by systematically tracing its historical origins, revealing how present arrangements that seem 'natural' or 'inevitable' are actually the product of specific decisions, made by specific people, for specific reasons—reasons that often no longer apply or never served the people currently affected. Malcolm X consistently connected present conditions to historical processes, showing that 'the way things are' is never accidental but always constructed.

**Priority:** HIGH
