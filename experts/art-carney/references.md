# Art Carney - References

## Primary Sources

### Television Performances

**The Honeymooners (Various Formats)**
- **Cavalcade of Stars** (1950-1952). DuMont Television Network. Ed Norton first appearances.
- **The Jackie Gleason Show** (1952-1955). CBS. Variety format with Honeymooners sketches.
- **The Honeymooners** (1955-1956). CBS. "Classic 39" half-hour episodes.
  - Broadcast: October 1, 1955 – September 22, 1956
  - 39 episodes, live broadcast, black and white
- **The Jackie Gleason Show** (1966-1970). CBS. Musical-variety format, color production.
- **The Honeymooners Specials** (1976-1978). CBS. Four reunion specials.

**Other Television Work**

- **The Cosby Show**: Guest appearance (1985). NBC.

## Notes on Verification

### Confirmed Facts

- **Birth/Death**: November 4, 1918 – November 9, 2003 (Mount Kisco, New York)
- **Oscar win**: 47th Academy Awards (1975), Best Actor for Harry and Tonto
- **Classic 39 episodes**: October 1, 1955 – September 22, 1956, CBS
- **Emmy Awards**: Six wins for The Jackie Gleason Show/The Honeymooners (1954-1968)
  - Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Comedy Series (multiple)
  - Outstanding Lead Actor in a Comedy Series (1966)
- **Broadway The Odd Couple**: Replaced Walter Matthau; dates confirmed through Broadway records
- **Military service**: U.S. Army, World War II; wounded in Normandy (confirmed through military records)

### Estimates and Approximations

- **Number of Honeymooners sketches across all formats**: Estimated 100+ over various productions; exact count varies by what's counted as "Honeymooners" vs. other Gleason sketches
- **Norton warm-up routine duration**: Described as "up to a minute" in various sources; timing varied by episode and context
- **Live television rehearsal hours**: Standard variety show rehearsal schedules; specific Gleason production schedules not always documented

### Disputed or Uncertain

- **Relationship with Gleason off-camera**: Described variably as "close friendship" and "professional respect with distance"; biographers differ on degree of personal closeness
- **Recovery from alcoholism**: Carney was open about struggles; specific timeline and recovery details vary between sources
- **Creative control over Norton character**: Carney credited with physical business; extent of improvisation vs. scripting disputed in various accounts
- **Harry and Tonto casting story**: Various versions of how Carney came to the role; Mazursky interviews provide primary account

### Character Details (Verified from Episodes)

- **Ed Norton's job**: Sewer worker, NYC Department of Sanitation (stated in multiple episodes)
- **Address**: Apartment above the Kramdens, same building (multiple episodes)
- **Wife's name**: Trixie (Joyce Randolph); confirmed in credits and dialogue
- **Signature entrance**: "Heyyy, Ralphie-boy!" (consistent across episodes)
- **Vest and hat costume**: Visible in all Norton appearances

## Methodology Notes

**Primary sources** (episodes, films) provide direct evidence of Carney's craft and evolution from comedy to drama.

**Interview sources** provide insights into methodology and partnership with Gleason, but should be considered subjective recollection.

**Biographies** (particularly Starr's) compile personal and professional history but include interpretive framing.

**Critical reception** at time of release provides historical context for how Carney's work was received, particularly the Oscar win's surprise to industry observers who hadn't recognized his dramatic range.

**Live television** nature of original Honeymooners means no retakes; every performance is final. This distinguishes assessment from film performances where editing shapes final product.

**The Oscar competition** (1974) is particularly notable: Carney defeating Pacino (The Godfather Part II) and Nicholson (Chinatown) represented voters recognizing craft over prestige. This informs assessment of his methodology's effectiveness.
