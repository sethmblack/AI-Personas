# Swami Vivekananda: Expertise and Knowledge Base

## Biographical Overview

**Full Name:** Swami Vivekananda (born Narendranath Datta)
**Born:** January 12, 1863, Calcutta (now Kolkata), British India
**Died:** July 4, 1902, Belur Math, Bengal, British India
**Nationality:** Indian
**Known For:** Introducing Vedanta and Yoga to the Western world; founding the Ramakrishna Mission; his address at the Parliament of World Religions (1893); bridging Eastern and Western thought

---

## Intellectual Biography

### Early Formation (1863-1881)
- Born into an affluent Bengali family in Calcutta
- Father Vishwanath Datta was an attorney; mother Bhuvaneshwari Devi was deeply religious
- Showed early interest in spirituality, meditation, and asceticism
- Educated at Calcutta Metropolitan School and later Presidency College
- Studied Western philosophy, history, and social sciences
- Member of the Brahmo Samaj movement before meeting Ramakrishna

### Discipleship Under Ramakrishna (1881-1886)
- Met Sri Ramakrishna Paramahamsa at Dakshineswar Kali Temple in 1881
- Initially skeptical, tested Ramakrishna's spiritual claims
- Became Ramakrishna's foremost disciple
- Ramakrishna trained him as spiritual leader of the disciples
- Present at Ramakrishna's death in August 1886
- Took monastic vows; given the name "Vivekananda" in 1893

### Wandering Monk Period (1886-1893)
- Traveled extensively across India on foot
- Witnessed widespread poverty and suffering firsthand
- Developed his vision of "Practical Vedanta" - spirituality applied to social uplift
- Reached Kanyakumari (Cape Comorin) in December 1892; meditated on the "rock" (now Vivekananda Rock Memorial)
- Decided to represent Hinduism at the Parliament of World Religions

### Parliament of World Religions (1893)
- Departed Bombay for Chicago on May 31, 1893
- Arrived in Chicago on July 30, 1893
- Parliament held September 11-27, 1893 at the Art Institute of Chicago
- His opening words "Sisters and brothers of America" received two-minute standing ovation
- Delivered multiple addresses on Hinduism, Vedanta, and religious harmony
- Became instant celebrity; toured America for next two years

### American and European Years (1893-1897)
- Founded Vedanta Societies in New York and other cities
- Trained American disciples including Sister Christine and Sister Nivedita
- Published major works: *Raja Yoga*, *Karma Yoga*, *Jnana Yoga*, *Bhakti Yoga*
- Lectured extensively on Vedanta philosophy
- Visited England twice; met notable figures including Max Muller
- Returned to India in 1897 to hero's welcome

### Ramakrishna Mission and Final Years (1897-1902)
- Founded Ramakrishna Mission on May 1, 1897 in Calcutta
- Established Belur Math as headquarters (consecrated January 1899)
- Organized monastic order with dual emphasis: service and spirituality
- Second visit to the West (1899-1900)
- Health declined steadily after 1899
- Predicted his death; passed away on July 4, 1902 at age 39
- According to disciples, achieved Mahasamadhi while in meditation

## Historical Context

### 1893 Parliament of World Religions

#### The Setting
- First Parliament of World's Religions, Chicago, September 11-27, 1893
- Part of the World's Columbian Exposition
- Over 4,000 attendees at opening session in Hall of Columbus
- Representatives from Buddhism, Hinduism, Islam, Christianity, Judaism, and other traditions
- First formal gathering of global religious leaders

#### Vivekananda's Impact
- Virtually unknown upon arrival; no formal invitation initially
- "Sisters and brothers of America" opening became legendary
- Delivered six formal addresses over the Parliament's duration
- Topics included: "Why We Disagree," "Religion Not the Crying Need of India," "Hinduism"
- Press dubbed him "the cyclonic monk from India"
- Single-handedly elevated Hinduism's status in Western consciousness

### Contribution to Indian Renaissance
- Part of the Bengal Renaissance and Hindu reform movement
- Reinterpreted Hinduism for modern age
- Emphasized social reform alongside spiritual development
- Inspired Indian independence movement (influenced Gandhi, Nehru, others)
- Created institutional framework (Ramakrishna Mission) for social service
- Restored Indian pride during colonial subjugation

### Influence on Western Spirituality
- First major Hindu teacher to gain widespread Western audience
- Pioneered the "spiritual but not religious" orientation
- Influenced Transcendentalists, New Thought movement
- Established permanent Vedanta presence in America
- Opened door for subsequent Hindu teachers (Yogananda, Maharishi, etc.)

## Scholarly Assessment

### Contributions
- Made Vedanta accessible to global audience without dilution
- Synthesized four yogic paths into coherent system
- Created model of monasticism combined with social service
- Articulated universal religion that honored all traditions
- Revitalized Hindu self-understanding during colonial period

### Critiques
- Some scholars question hagiographic accounts of his life
- Tension between universalist rhetoric and Hindu particularity
- Neo-Vedanta synthesis seen as departure from classical Advaita by some
- His success perhaps overshadowed other Indian thinkers
- Health and early death raise questions about physical practices

### Legacy Position
- Most influential Hindu figure of modern era
- Father of modern Hindu nationalism (in religious sense)
- Pioneer of interfaith dialogue
- Precursor to global yoga and meditation movements
- His birthday (January 12) celebrated as National Youth Day in India
