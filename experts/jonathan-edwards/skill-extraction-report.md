### HIGH Priority

#### 1. religious-affections-discernment

**Source Pattern:** The Religious Affections (1746) - Twelve Signs of Truly Gracious Affections
**Purpose:** Distinguish true spiritual affections from false using Edwards's comprehensive framework of twelve positive signs that identify genuine religious experience
**Trigger:** "Is this real?", "How do I know my faith is genuine?", "Authentic vs. false religion", "Is my experience from God?", "Discerning true from false conversion"
**Inputs:** Description of religious experience, emotional response, or claimed spiritual transformation to evaluate; context of how it arose and what effects it has produced
**Outputs:** Structured assessment examining: 1) Whether the experience passes or fails each of the twelve signs, 2) Specific evidence for spiritual vs. merely natural origin, 3) The decisive test of persevering practice, 4) Guidance for the person experiencing it
**Reasoning:** Edwards's most rigorous contribution - a systematic framework for evaluating religious experience that remains unsurpassed. Highly actionable (twelve specific tests), invocable (common pastoral question), scoped (religious experience evaluation), reusable (applies to any claimed spiritual experience), valuable (addresses perennial problem of religious self-deception). Estimated 2+ uses/week for pastoral/spiritual direction contexts.

#### 2. aesthetic-theology-analysis

**Source Pattern:** Edwards's aesthetic theology from True Virtue and the spiritual sense framework
**Purpose:** Analyze any subject through the lens of divine beauty and excellency, revealing how it relates to the ultimate beauty that is God's holiness
**Trigger:** "What makes this beautiful?", "Why does this matter ultimately?", "What is the excellence here?", "Help me see the deeper significance", "Beauty assessment"
**Inputs:** Description of the subject, situation, or question to analyze through aesthetic-theological lens
**Outputs:** Analysis identifying: 1) The beauty or ugliness present (not merely good/bad but beautiful/ugly), 2) How this relates to Being in general and the greatest Being, 3) Whether the beauty is spiritual or merely natural, 4) What a "spiritual sense" would perceive that natural perception misses, 5) The connection to God's excellency
**Reasoning:** Unique to Edwards - the integration of aesthetics and theology. Provides a framework most lack for seeing beauty as objective and theological. Actionable (specific categories to apply), invocable (questions of meaning and significance), scoped (aesthetic-theological analysis), reusable (applies to any subject), valuable (transforms perception). Estimated 1-2 uses/week.

#### 3. revival-assessment-framework

**Source Pattern:** The Distinguishing Marks of a Work of the Spirit of God (1741)
**Purpose:** Evaluate any religious movement, awakening, or spiritual enthusiasm to determine whether it bears marks of genuine divine work or mere human/demonic counterfeiting
**Trigger:** "Is this movement genuine?", "Revival evaluation", "Spiritual awakening assessment", "Is this from God?", "Religious enthusiasm analysis"
**Inputs:** Description of the religious movement, awakening, or spiritual phenomenon; observed characteristics, fruits, and excesses
**Outputs:** Assessment examining: 1) Presence or absence of the five positive marks (esteem of Jesus, opposition to Satan's kingdom, regard for Scripture, leading into truth, producing love), 2) Whether negative features actually disprove divine origin (the nine neutral signs), 3) Overall assessment of predominant character, 4) Recommendations for engagement or caution
**Reasoning:** Edwards wrote this framework during the Great Awakening precisely to enable such assessments. Highly actionable (specific marks to check), invocable (common question when religious enthusiasm arises), scoped (movement evaluation), reusable (applies to any religious awakening claim), valuable (prevents both credulous acceptance and cynical rejection). Estimated 1 use/week in religious/spiritual contexts.

### MEDIUM Priority

#### 4. compatibilist-will-analysis

**Source Pattern:** Freedom of the Will (1754)
**Purpose:** Analyze any question of responsibility, blame, or moral agency using Edwards's compatibilist framework that maintains determinism while preserving moral responsibility
**Trigger:** "Is this their fault?", "Could they have done otherwise?", "Free will and responsibility", "Why blame if determined?"
**Inputs:** Description of action or choice; question about responsibility; context of competing views on freedom
**Outputs:** Analysis distinguishing: 1) Natural vs. moral ability/inability, 2) Natural vs. moral necessity, 3) Why determination by motive does not remove responsibility, 4) The incoherence of libertarian alternatives
**Reasoning:** Philosophically rigorous framework for age-old questions. Actionable (clear distinctions to apply), invocable (questions of blame and responsibility), scoped (will and moral responsibility), reusable (applies to any moral agency question). However, more specialized and philosophical than the top three. Estimated <1 use/week.
