## Key Frameworks

### The Religious Affections (1746)

Edwards's magnum opus on the nature of true religion. The central thesis: "True religion, in great part, consists in holy affections."

#### Definition of Affections

The affections are not mere emotions but the will in vigorous exercise. They are "the more vigorous and sensible exercises of the inclination and will of the soul."

**Key distinction:**
- **Passions:** Involuntary, overwhelming emotions that overpower reason
- **Affections:** Vigorous inclinations that engage the whole soul (mind, heart, will)

#### Twelve Signs That Neither Prove Nor Disprove True Affections

1. Affections are raised very high
2. They have great effects on the body
3. They cause fluent, fervent speech about religion
4. They are not produced by the person's own efforts
5. They come with texts of Scripture impressed on the mind
6. They display an appearance of love
7. They are many and varied
8. Comforts and joys follow in a certain order
9. They cause much external religious activity
10. They cause much praise of God
11. They produce great confidence of a good estate
12. The person with them appears very convincing to others

**The key insight:** High religious experience, dramatic effects, even apparent love and confidence - none of these prove authenticity. The false can perfectly mimic all of them.

#### Twelve Signs of Truly Gracious Affections

1. **Spiritual origin** - They arise from divine influence, not natural causes
2. **Object is divine excellence** - They are drawn to God for His own beauty, not merely His benefits
3. **Founded on moral excellency** - They love the holiness of God, not merely His power or knowledge
4. **Accompanied by spiritual understanding** - There is light, not just heat
5. **Deep conviction of reality** - Spiritual truths are known to be true
6. **Evangelical humiliation** - Self is seen as lowly before God's holiness
7. **Nature transformed** - There is a change in the fundamental disposition
8. **Christlike spirit** - Meekness, quietness, forgiveness, mercy
9. **Tender heart** - Softness toward God and spiritual things
10. **Beautiful symmetry** - All graces present in proportion
11. **Increase spiritual appetite** - Not satisfied but hungering for more
12. **Christian practice** - They issue in persevering holy action

**The key insight:** The single greatest sign is the last - persevering practice. "Gracious affections have their exercise and fruit in Christian practice."

### Freedom of the Will (1754)

Edwards's philosophical masterwork defending Calvinist determinism against Arminian libertarianism.

#### Central Argument

The will is always determined by the strongest motive (what appears most agreeable to the mind at the moment of choice). There is no "liberty of indifference" - no power to choose contrary to one's inclinations.

**Key terms:**
- **Natural ability:** Physical capacity to perform an action
- **Moral ability:** Inclination or willingness to perform it
- **Natural necessity:** Compulsion from outside forces
- **Moral necessity:** Determination by one's own motives and inclinations

**The key insight:** Moral necessity does not undermine responsibility. We are responsible for choices that flow from our own character, even though those choices are determined.

#### Against Arminianism

Edwards argues that libertarian free will (the power of "contrary choice") is:
1. **Logically incoherent** - If the will is self-determining, what determines it to determine itself? Infinite regress.
2. **Morally insignificant** - A truly random choice would not reflect the person's character
3. **Theologically dangerous** - It would make salvation dependent on unpredictable human decisions

**The key insight:** True freedom is not freedom from determination but freedom to act according to one's nature. The holy will of Christ was free precisely because it could not sin.

### The End for Which God Created the World (1755)

Edwards's treatise on God's purpose in creation, published posthumously with *True Virtue*.

#### Central Thesis

God created the world for His own glory. This glory is communicated when:
1. **Emanation:** God's fullness flows outward in creation
2. **Remanation:** Creatures know, love, and rejoice in God, returning His glory to Him

**Key insight:** God's glory and creature happiness are not competing ends. The creature's highest happiness IS glorifying and enjoying God.

#### The Diffusive Nature of Goodness

God's glory is like a fountain that naturally overflows. Creation is not for God's benefit (He needs nothing) but is the natural expression of infinite goodness that tends to communicate itself.

> "It is a thing infinitely good in itself that God's glory should be known by a glorious society of created beings."

### True Virtue (1755)

Edwards's ethical treatise, also published posthumously.

#### Definition of True Virtue

True virtue is "benevolence to Being in general" - love toward all existence, especially toward the greatest Being (God).

**Key distinctions:**
- **True virtue:** Disinterested benevolence toward Being in general
- **Natural virtues:** Love limited to particular beings (family, nation) based on natural affection
- **Self-love:** Not truly virtuous even when it produces beneficial actions

**The key insight:** Most apparent virtue is merely enlightened self-interest or natural affection. True virtue requires loving Being itself, which only regeneration makes possible.

#### Beauty and Virtue

Edwards links virtue to beauty. True virtue IS spiritual beauty - it has the same structure as natural beauty (consent of being to being, agreement, harmony) applied to moral beings.

> "Virtue is the beauty of the qualities and exercises of the heart."

### The Sinners Sermon (1741)

"Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God" - delivered July 8, 1741 at Enfield, Connecticut.

#### Rhetorical Strategy

- **Vivid imagery:** God's wrath as bent bow, sinners as spiders over flame
- **Present tense urgency:** "There is nothing between you and hell but the air"
- **Philosophical precision:** Careful argument about God's lack of obligation to the sinner
- **Affective appeal:** Not merely to convince but to awaken

#### Key Passages

> "The bow of God's wrath is bent, and the arrow made ready on the string, and justice bends the arrow at your heart, and strains the bow, and it is nothing but the mere pleasure of God, and that of an angry God, without any promise or obligation at all, that keeps the arrow one moment from being made drunk with your blood."

> "O sinner! Consider the fearful danger you are in: it is a great furnace of wrath, a wide and bottomless pit, full of the fire of wrath, that you are held over in the hand of that God, whose wrath is provoked and incensed as much against you, as against many of the damned in hell."

**The key insight:** Edwards sought not to terrify for terror's sake but to awaken the unregenerate sense of danger. The sermon ends with an appeal to flee to Christ.

### The Distinguishing Marks of a Work of the Spirit of God (1741)

Originally delivered at Yale's commencement, this treatise assesses the Great Awakening.

#### Nine Negative Signs (Not Evidence Against a Work)

A movement is not proven false merely because it features:
1. Things unusual and unprecedented
2. Bodily effects (crying out, falling down)
3. Much talk about religion
4. Strong impressions on imagination
5. Example influencing others
6. Imprudence and errors
7. Errors in judgment and Satan's delusions
8. Some falling into gross errors or sins
9. Ministers promoting it who press too much on terrors

#### Five Positive Signs (Evidence of Genuine Work)

From 1 John 4, Edwards identifies marks that can only come from the Holy Spirit:
1. **Raises esteem of Jesus** - Confirms Christ came in the flesh
2. **Operates against Satan's kingdom** - Turns from sin
3. **Produces greater regard for Scripture** - Love of God's Word
4. **Leads into truth** - Conviction of reality of divine things
5. **Produces love** - Love to God and man

**The key insight:** Judge a movement by its fruits, not its excesses. The presence of errors and enthusiasms does not disprove divine origin if the positive marks are present.

## The Spiritual Sense

Central to Edwards's entire theology is the concept of a spiritual sense - a supernatural perceptual capacity given in regeneration.

### Nature of the New Sense

The spiritual sense is:
- **A new simple idea** - Like a sense of sweetness, not derivable from other ideas
- **Perceptual, not intellectual** - Knowing about divine beauty vs. perceiving it
- **Supernatural in origin** - Given only by the Holy Spirit
- **The basis of all true religion** - Without it, no amount of doctrine produces genuine faith

### The Honey Analogy

> "There is a difference between having an opinion, that God is holy and gracious, and having a sense of the loveliness and beauty of that holiness and grace. There is a difference between having a rational judgment that honey is sweet, and having a sense of its sweetness."

**The key insight:** Natural people can have extensive theological knowledge yet entirely lack true religion because they have never perceived the beauty of what they know about.

## Major Works

### A Faithful Narrative of the Surprising Work of God (1737)

Account of the 1734-35 Northampton revival. First published in London, it influenced British evangelicalism and John Wesley.

### The Distinguishing Marks of a Work of the Spirit of God (1741)

Defense and assessment of the Great Awakening. Originally a Yale commencement address.

### Some Thoughts Concerning the Present Revival (1743)

Fuller defense of the Awakening while acknowledging excesses.

### A Treatise Concerning Religious Affections (1746)

Edwards's masterwork on the psychology of religious experience. Twelve signs of truly gracious affections.

### An Humble Inquiry (1749)

Argues against the "Halfway Covenant" - that only converted persons should be admitted to communion.

### Life of David Brainerd (1749)

Biography of the missionary to Native Americans. Extraordinarily influential in promoting missions.

### Freedom of the Will (1754)

Full title: "A Careful and Strict Inquiry into the Modern Prevailing Notions of that Freedom of Will Which Is Supposed to be Essential to Moral Agency, Virtue and Vice, Reward and Punishment, Praise and Blame."

### The Great Christian Doctrine of Original Sin Defended (1758)

Defense of the Reformed doctrine of original sin against John Taylor.

### Two Dissertations (1765, posthumous)

Containing "Concerning the End for Which God Created the World" and "The Nature of True Virtue."

## Integration Notes

When working with other experts:

- **With John Calvin:** Deep alignment on sovereignty and predestination; Edwards extends and philosophically develops Reformed theology
- **With Augustine:** Shared emphasis on grace, affections, and sovereignty; Edwards is consciously Augustinian
- **With John Locke:** Edwards appropriates Lockean epistemology (simple ideas, empiricism) for theological purposes
- **With George Whitefield:** Partners in the Great Awakening; Edwards provided theological foundation for Whitefield's evangelism
- **With Blaise Pascal:** Compare the "heart" that knows God; both integrate reason and affection
- **With Friedrich Schleiermacher:** Contrast experience-centered approaches; Edwards grounds experience in objective divine excellence

## The Great Awakening Context

### Timeline of Revival

- **1734-35:** Local awakening at Northampton under Edwards
- **1739-40:** George Whitefield arrives from England
- **1740-42:** Height of the Great Awakening across colonies
- **1743-44:** Decline begins; excesses multiply
- **1750s:** Post-Awakening consolidation and reflection

### Edwards's Role

Edwards served as:
1. **Practitioner** - Led revival at Northampton
2. **Chronicler** - Wrote "Faithful Narrative" describing the awakening
3. **Theologian** - Provided theological framework for understanding revival
4. **Assessor** - Developed criteria for distinguishing true from false religion
5. **Defender** - Argued against critics ("Old Lights") who opposed the Awakening
6. **Critic** - Warned against excesses while defending the movement's core

### The New Divinity

Edwards's disciples developed his theology into the "New Divinity" or "Edwardseanism":
- Samuel Hopkins
- Joseph Bellamy
- Jonathan Edwards Jr.

Key emphases: disinterested benevolence, governmental theory of atonement, immediate repentance.

## Practical Applications

### For Assessing Religious Experience

Edwards provides unmatched tools for distinguishing authentic from counterfeit spiritual experience:
1. Apply the twelve signs of true affections
2. Look for the primacy of divine beauty (not self-interest)
3. Check for persevering practice
4. Examine whether humility increases

### For Understanding Motivation

Edwards's analysis of the will clarifies:
1. We always act on our strongest motive
2. True change requires change in what we find most agreeable
3. Mere knowledge does not produce action; affection does
4. The spiritual sense transforms what appears beautiful

### For Integrating Head and Heart

Edwards shows that:
1. True religion is neither cold orthodoxy nor empty emotion
2. The affections ARE the will in vigorous exercise
3. Light and heat belong together
4. Spiritual understanding includes perceiving beauty, not just assenting to truth

### For Understanding Beauty and Excellence

Edwards's aesthetic theology provides:
1. Framework for seeing beauty as objective, not merely subjective
2. Connection between beauty and moral categories
3. Understanding of why excellence attracts and ugliness repels
4. Grounding of aesthetics in theology
