## HIGH Priority

### 1. tenx-thinking

**Source Pattern:** The 10x Framework
**Purpose:** Challenge incremental thinking by reframing problems to require 10x improvement, forcing abandonment of existing assumptions and discovery of revolutionary approaches.
**Trigger:** "Is this thinking big enough?" or "We're optimizing our current approach" or "How do we improve this?" or "What's wrong with our strategy?"
**Inputs:** Current approach or plan, existing constraints, target metrics
**Outputs:** Reframed problem statement, identified assumptions to question, 10x target definition, recommended approach pivots
**Reasoning:** Clear 3-step process (identify current approach, challenge assumptions, define 10x target). Used frequently when evaluating any strategic initiative. Saves significant time by preventing teams from optimizing dead-end approaches. Core to Page's methodology.

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Clear steps: analyze current, question assumptions, reframe at 10x
- Invocable: YES - "How do we make this 10x better?" "Is this ambitious enough?"
- Scoped: YES - Focused on reframing ambition level, not full strategy
- Reusable: YES - Applies to products, processes, organizations, any initiative
- Valuable: YES - Prevents wasted effort on incremental improvements
- **Decision: CANDIDATE**

### 2. toothbrush-test

**Source Pattern:** The Toothbrush Test
**Purpose:** Evaluate any product, feature, or acquisition by testing whether it will be used daily by billions and genuinely improves lives, prioritizing usefulness over financial metrics.
**Trigger:** "Should we build this?" or "Should we acquire this company?" or "How do we prioritize features?" or "Is this product worth pursuing?"
**Inputs:** Product/feature/acquisition description, target user base, usage frequency expectations, life improvement claim
**Outputs:** Pass/fail assessment, usage frequency analysis, scale potential evaluation, recommendation with rationale
**Reasoning:** Binary evaluation framework with clear criteria (daily use + improves life). Applicable to product decisions, M&A evaluation, and feature prioritization. Documented success with Android, YouTube acquisitions. Prevents building clever but inessential products.

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Two-part test with clear pass/fail criteria
- Invocable: YES - "Does this pass the toothbrush test?" "Should we acquire this?"
- Scoped: YES - Evaluates single product/feature/acquisition
- Reusable: YES - Works for any product decision context
- Valuable: YES - Prevents investment in low-utility projects
- **Decision: CANDIDATE**

---

### 3. moonshot-evaluator

**Source Pattern:** Moonshot Criteria
**Purpose:** Determine whether a proposed project qualifies as a true moonshot by testing against three required components: huge problem, radical solution, enabling technology.
**Trigger:** "Is this a moonshot?" or "Does this deserve big investment?" or "Is this ambitious or just dressed-up incrementalism?" or "Should we fund this as a moonshot?"
**Inputs:** Project proposal, problem statement, proposed solution, technology assessment
**Outputs:** Three-component evaluation (huge problem: yes/no, radical solution: yes/no, enabling technology: yes/no), moonshot determination, investment recommendation
**Reasoning:** Clear three-part evaluation framework. Distinguishes genuine moonshots from ambitious-sounding incremental work. Essential for allocating resources to high-potential projects. Each component has observable criteria.

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Three binary evaluations with clear criteria
- Invocable: YES - "Is this a real moonshot?" "Does this qualify for moonshot resources?"
- Scoped: YES - Evaluates single project against three criteria
- Reusable: YES - Applies to any ambitious project proposal
- Valuable: YES - Prevents misallocation of moonshot resources
- **Decision: CANDIDATE**

## MEDIUM Priority

### 4. alphabet-structure-assessment

**Source Pattern:** The Alphabet Principle
**Purpose:** Evaluate whether a business unit or project has grown different enough from the core to warrant structural independence with its own CEO and culture.
**Trigger:** "Should this be its own company?" or "Is our org structure slowing us down?" or "This project doesn't fit our culture" or "How should we organize these diverse initiatives?"
**Inputs:** Business unit description, relationship to core business, resource requirements, culture needs, leadership assessment
**Outputs:** Independence recommendation, structural options, transition considerations, CEO requirements
**Reasoning:** Multi-factor assessment for organizational design decisions. Based on documented Alphabet restructuring rationale. Less frequently invoked than 10x or toothbrush test, but high value when relevant.

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Assessment framework with clear factors to evaluate
- Invocable: YES - "Should we spin this off?" "Does this need independence?"
- Scoped: YES - Evaluates single business unit's structural fit
- Reusable: YES - Applies to any diversifying organization
- Valuable: YES - Prevents bureaucracy from killing entrepreneurial units
- **Decision: CANDIDATE**

### 5. asymmetric-bet-sizing

**Source Pattern:** Bet Sizing for Asymmetric Returns
**Purpose:** Evaluate portfolio of initiatives using moonshot math: fund projects with low probability but massive potential returns, accepting that most will fail while optimizing for total value.
**Trigger:** "How should we allocate our innovation budget?" or "Should we fund this risky project?" or "Our portfolio is too conservative" or "How do we think about failure?"
**Inputs:** Project portfolio, probability estimates, potential return magnitudes, risk tolerance parameters
**Outputs:** Portfolio allocation recommendations, risk acceptance framework, expected value analysis, failure normalization guidance
**Reasoning:** Counter-intuitive framework that reframes how to evaluate risky investments. Based on documented Google/Alphabet approach to Other Bets. Requires portfolio-level thinking rather than individual project evaluation.

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Framework for evaluating projects by expected value
- Invocable: YES - "Is our portfolio balanced right?" "Should we take this bet?"
- Scoped: YES - Focused on portfolio-level investment decisions
- Reusable: YES - Applies to any innovation portfolio context
- Valuable: YES - Prevents over-conservatism in R&D investment
- **Decision: CANDIDATE**

### 6. assumption-auditor

**Source Pattern:** Healthy Disregard for the Impossible
**Purpose:** Systematically identify and challenge assumptions in any plan or constraint, distinguishing between genuine limitations (physics) and artificial ones (convention, policy, fear).
**Trigger:** "This can't be done" or "What assumptions are we making?" or "Why does it have to work this way?" or "We're stuck on this problem"
**Inputs:** Current plan or constraint statement, stated limitations, historical context
**Outputs:** Categorized assumptions (physics vs convention vs fear), challenged constraints, alternative framings, investigation recommendations
**Reasoning:** Foundational skill for 10x thinking - you cannot achieve 10x without first identifying what assumptions are blocking you. Frequently applicable in problem-solving contexts. Explicit methodology documented in Page's speeches.

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Categorize each constraint, challenge non-physics limitations
- Invocable: YES - "Why can't we do this?" "What's really impossible here?"
- Scoped: YES - Focused on assumption identification and categorization
- Reusable: YES - Applies to any stuck situation or planning context
- Valuable: YES - Unlocks solutions hidden by false assumptions
- **Decision: CANDIDATE**
