## Evaluation Methodology

Each pattern evaluated against 5 criteria:
1. **Actionable** - Has clear, repeatable steps
2. **Invocable** - Could be triggered by request
3. **Scoped** - One responsibility, clear boundaries
4. **Reusable** - Applies across multiple contexts
5. **Valuable** - Saves significant effort or ensures consistency

Decision: ALL 5 criteria must be YES for CANDIDATE status.

## HIGH Priority

### 1. eleven-star-experience

**Source Pattern:** The 11-Star Experience Framework
**Purpose:** Design exceptional customer experiences by imagining progressively extraordinary versions and working backward to find the feasible sweet spot between delightful and practical.
**Trigger:** "Design an exceptional experience" or "How do we exceed customer expectations?" or "What would make customers tell everyone about this?" or "How do we go beyond 5-star service?"
**Inputs:** Current experience to improve, customer context, touchpoint being designed, constraints (feasibility, cost, time)
**Outputs:** Experience design ranging from 5-star baseline through 11-star fantasy, recommended sweet spot, specific improvements to implement
**Reasoning:** Clear 11-step methodology (each star level is a design exercise), used frequently when designing any customer touchpoint, saves 45+ minutes of ideation by structuring the brainstorm. Highly reusable across product, service, and experience design contexts. No existing skill covers this framework.

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Clear progression from 5 to 11 stars with specific examples
- Invocable: YES - "Design an 11-star experience for..."
- Scoped: YES - Experience design only, does not extend to implementation
- Reusable: YES - Applies to any customer touchpoint, any industry
- Valuable: YES - Saves significant ideation time, ensures teams think beyond adequate

**Decision: CANDIDATE**

### 2. founder-mode-assessment

**Source Pattern:** Founder Mode vs Manager Mode
**Purpose:** Diagnose whether a leader or organization is operating in founder mode (deep product engagement) or manager mode (delegation-heavy) and provide guidance for appropriate engagement level.
**Trigger:** "Am I too hands-off?" or "Should I be more involved?" or "Is our CEO too disconnected from product?" or "How do I stay in the details without micromanaging?" or "Founder mode audit"
**Inputs:** Current leadership structure, CEO/founder involvement level, product decision-making process, team feedback on leadership engagement
**Outputs:** Assessment of current mode (founder vs manager), gap analysis, specific recommendations for engagement level, partnership model guidance
**Reasoning:** Addresses major leadership challenge identified by Chesky and documented by Paul Graham. Clear diagnostic criteria (skip-level meetings, product review involvement, chief editor behavior). High value for startups and scaled companies reassessing leadership structure. Distinct from existing `founder-ceo-partnership` and `post-founder-leadership` skills which focus on different aspects.

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Specific diagnostic questions and engagement behaviors
- Invocable: YES - "Assess my leadership engagement level"
- Scoped: YES - Leadership engagement assessment only
- Reusable: YES - Applies to any CEO/founder at any stage
- Valuable: YES - Prevents major leadership mistakes, saves costly reorganizations

**Decision: CANDIDATE**

### 3. crisis-communication-design

**Source Pattern:** COVID-19 Crisis Leadership - The Layoff Letter Philosophy
**Purpose:** Design compassionate, human crisis communications (especially layoffs) that maintain dignity, provide support, and preserve organizational trust.
**Trigger:** "How do I communicate layoffs?" or "Writing difficult company announcement" or "Crisis communication with humanity" or "Layoff letter review"
**Inputs:** Crisis context, affected population, support being offered, company values, speaker/CEO voice
**Outputs:** Structured crisis communication with empathy framework, specific support commitments, human (not corporate) tone, follow-up action plan
**Reasoning:** Chesky's 3,374-word layoff letter became a model for humane crisis communication. Clear methodology: speak from heart, be vulnerable, provide generous support, actively help affected people. High-stakes situations where poor communication causes lasting damage. No existing skill covers this specific crisis communication approach.

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Clear principles: cut deep (avoid rounds), speak from heart, provide support, help placement
- Invocable: YES - "Help me write a layoff communication"
- Scoped: YES - Crisis communication only, not crisis management broadly
- Reusable: YES - Applies to any difficult organizational communication
- Valuable: YES - Prevents reputation damage, maintains trust, high-stakes decisions

**Decision: CANDIDATE**

### 5. do-things-that-dont-scale

**Source Pattern:** Do Things That Do Not Scale
**Purpose:** Identify opportunities for handcrafted, unscalable customer engagement to build deep understanding before scaling.
**Trigger:** "We need to scale faster" or "How do we achieve product-market fit?" or "Should we automate this?" or "We're losing touch with customers"
**Inputs:** Current customer engagement approach, scale pressure, product-market fit status, team capacity for manual work
**Outputs:** Recommendations for unscalable activities, priority customer segments for handcrafted engagement, timeline for eventual scaling
**Reasoning:** Counterintuitive but highly valuable framework. Clear methodology: fly to customers, knock on doors, handwrite notes. However, this advice is widely known via Paul Graham's essays.

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Specific unscalable activities listed
- Invocable: YES - "How do we do things that don't scale?"
- Scoped: YES - Early-stage customer engagement
- Reusable: YES - Applies to any startup or new product
- Valuable: YES - Prevents premature scaling, builds product-market fit

**Decision: CANDIDATE - But lower priority due to concept being widely documented**

### 6. chief-editor-leadership

**Source Pattern:** The Chief Editor Model
**Purpose:** Implement the chief editor leadership model where CEOs review all work as editors rather than autocrats - engaged without micromanaging.
**Trigger:** "How do I stay involved without micromanaging?" or "Partnership leadership model" or "Chief editor approach"
**Inputs:** Current review cadence, team size, project types, CEO/leader time availability
**Outputs:** Review cadence design, partnership meeting structure, criteria for when to engage vs delegate
**Reasoning:** Distinctive Chesky/Jobs approach to leadership. Clear distinction from micromanagement. However, narrower scope than founder-mode-assessment (which encompasses this).

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Specific review cadence, partnership approach
- Invocable: YES - "How do I be a chief editor?"
- Scoped: YES - Leadership review process
- Reusable: YES - Applies to any detail-oriented leader
- Valuable: PARTIAL - Valuable but narrower than founder-mode, which includes this
