## Key Companies

### Airbnb (2008-present)

Co-founded with Joe Gebbia (met at RISD) and Nathan Blecharczyk. Transformed from air mattresses during a design conference into the world's largest accommodation platform.

**The Origin Story:**

October 2007: Chesky moved to San Francisco to live with RISD classmate Joe Gebbia. Neither could afford rent. During the Industrial Designers Society of America conference, when hotel rooms were scarce, they opened their house to short-term renters as a bed and breakfast, providing air mattresses for guests and Pop-Tarts for breakfast.

**Major milestones:**
- 2007: Hosted first guests on air mattresses during IDSAC conference
- 2008: Created "Obama O's" and "Cap'n McCain's" cereal to fund company ($30,000 in sales)
- 2009: Joined Y Combinator, received $20,000 seed funding
- 2009: Sequoia Capital invested $585,000
- 2011: Moved to "Belong Anywhere" mission
- 2012: Project Snow White storyboarding initiative
- 2014: Published "Don't Fuck Up the Culture" letter
- 2016: Named Glassdoor's #1 Best Place to Work
- 2020: Lost 80% of business in 8 weeks during COVID
- 2020: Led company through COVID crisis, conducted historic layoff of 1,900 employees
- 2020: Successful IPO (December), valued at $47 billion
- 2024: Paul Graham writes "Founder Mode" essay inspired by Chesky talk at Y Combinator

**Current Scale:**
- Over 4 million hosts
- More than 1.5 billion guests welcomed
- Presence in over 220 countries and regions

## The Obama O's Story: Be a Cereal Entrepreneur

In 2008, with $20,000 in debt each and investors rejecting them, the founders turned to entrepreneurial creativity.

**The idea:** "We're Air Bed & Breakfast. The air beds aren't working out, maybe we could sell breakfast."

**The execution:**
1. Purchased 1,000 boxes of commercial cereals
2. Designed custom packaging: "Obama O's" and "Cap'n McCain's"
3. Hand-folded boxes and sealed them with hot glue gun
4. Sold as limited-edition collector items at $40/box
5. Celebrities like Perez Hilton and Katy Perry purchased

**Results:**
- Sold out of Obama O's (not Cap'n McCain's)
- Netted approximately $30,000
- Demonstrated scrappy entrepreneurship to Paul Graham
- Led to Y Combinator acceptance

This became Airbnb core value: "Be a cereal entrepreneur" - do whatever it takes to survive.

### The 11-Star Experience Framework

Developed as a method to design exceptional customer experiences. First discussed publicly in Reid Hoffman's "Masters of Scale" podcast (May 2017).

**The methodology:**
Start with what a 5-star experience looks like. Then extrapolate to 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 stars. The point is that 10 or 11-star experiences are not feasible, but there is a sweet spot between delightful and achievable.

**The scale as Chesky describes it:**

| Star Level | Experience |
|------------|------------|
| 1-star | It was terrible |
| 3-star | It was okay |
| 5-star | Standard good service - you get to the apartment, host lets you in |
| 6-star | Host welcomes you, offers snacks, shows you around |
| 7-star | Host picks you up at airport |
| 8-star | Host drives you in a limousine |
| 9-star | Private jet picks you up |
| 10-star | The Beatles check-in - 5,000 fans greeting you at the airport with cards welcoming you to the country, press conference in front yard |
| 11-star | Complete fantasy - impossible but illuminating |

**The key insight:**
"If I say 'what can I do to make this better?' they'll say something small. If I say 'Reid, what would it take for me to design something that you would literally tell every single person you've ever encountered?' - you start to ask these questions and it really helps you think through this problem."

### Snow White Storyboarding

Inspired by Walt Disney's production of Snow White, which was planned entirely in storyboards - the first feature-length animated film.

**Origin:** Chesky read a biography of Walt Disney during Christmas break. As Joe Gebbia recalls: "Brian comes back from Christmas break with inspiration from Walt Disney and how he storyboarded everything from Snow White, and we kind of all are sitting around the conference table inspired, but wondering, what do we do next?"

**The process:**
1. Identify the journey (guest, host, employee, etc.)
2. Create frame-by-frame storyboard of entire experience
3. Make specific decisions at each frame (who is this person, what do they feel?)
4. Identify friction points and opportunities for delight

**Why it works:** Forces specificity. You cannot stay abstract when drawing frames.

Chesky explains: "When you have to storyboard something, the more realistic it is, the more decisions you have to make. Like are these hosts men or women? Are they young, are they old? Where do they live? The city or the countryside? Why are they hosting? Are they nervous? It's not that they show up to the house. They show up to the house, how many bags do they have? How are they feeling? Are they tired? At that point you start designing for stuff for a very particular use case."

**Implementation:**
- Airbnb hired Pixar animator Nick Sung to produce final copies
- Three core storyboards created: guest process, host process, hiring process
- Each journey captured in approximately 15 frames
- Storyboards now hang prominently in Airbnb headquarters
- Guide marketing, advertising, customer service, and product decisions

**Impact:** The storyboard initiative catalyzed the most aggressive period of growth in Airbnb's history.

### Do Things That Do Not Scale

Paul Graham's advice to Airbnb founders became a core operating principle.

**The origin:** At Y Combinator in 2009, Paul Graham told them: "In order to scale, you have to do things that don't scale."

**How Airbnb applied this:**
- Founders personally flew to New York to meet hosts (commuting from Mountain View for Y Combinator dinners, then flying to NYC)
- Knocked on doors: "Hello. Hey, this is Brian, Joe, we're founders and we just want to meet you."
- Took photos of listings themselves (posing as professional photographers)
- Sent handwritten notes to hosts
- Created personal relationships with first thousands of users

**Key insights:**
- "The biggest leaps you ever get is when you're small."
- "I miss those times. Yes, it's exciting to have traction, to have a company that's huge scale, but the biggest leaps you ever get is when you're small."
- "Nothing creates more long-term value than the hand-crafted work you do in the early days: the time you spend in real time with your customers, really hearing what they need, want, and love."

### Founder Mode vs Manager Mode

Concept Chesky discussed at Y Combinator in September 2024, which inspired Paul Graham's viral essay.

**Context:** The talk was intended to be off the record and last only 30 minutes, but stretched to two hours. According to Paul Graham, many founders present said it was the best talk they had ever witnessed. Jared Friedman of Y Combinator described it as "the startup version of the Potsdam Conference - a moment in time when the best minds came together and invented a new doctrine."

**The problem with manager mode:**
- Hire good people and give them room
- Delegate to layers of management
- Trust the org chart
- Results: founders getting "gaslit" from both sides - by people telling them to run companies like managers, and by employees when they do

**Founder mode principles:**
- Stay deeply involved in product details
- Be the chief product person
- Be the "chief editor" of the company
- Understand your product thoroughly enough to make decisions at every level
- Not micromanaging, but deep engagement

**The paradox:** "There's a paradox where being in the details sounds like micromanagement; it sounds like it's slowing teams down. But when you're in the details, you can actually help make decisions faster."

**The Jobs-Ive model:** When Chesky asked Jony Ive about working with Steve Jobs, who was famously in the details, Ive said he never felt micromanaged. "Because he was partnered with me." Jobs and Ive had lunch every day, Jobs saw everything Ive was working on, but never told him what to do. Instead, they talked through solutions together.

**Chesky's formulation:** "If I'm in the details with somebody, am I making them better or am I disempowering them? And I hope that when people feel like I'm involved in projects, they feel like I'm helping them push to think bigger."

**On autonomy:** "If you want to be autonomous, start your own company." When an executive asked "Is this your job or mine?" Chesky responded: "It should never be either/or. If I'm not in the details with you, I can't help you. And if I can't help you, I'm not adding any value. It's a partnership."

### The Chief Editor Model

Chesky operates as the "chief editor" of Airbnb, reviewing all work before it ships.

**Key principles:**
- "I review all the work. Now, before it ships, I am the chief editor of the company."
- Leaders act like chief editors, meticulously reviewing projects without stifling creativity
- Not telling people what to do, but working through problems with them
- "Great leadership is presence, not absence."

**On the difference from micromanagement:**
"A lot of people hear this [and] say, 'Wait, isn't that like micromanaging?' There is a difference. You can be in the details of people without telling them what to do, working through problems with them."

### Functional Organization and Product Management Evolution

In 2020, facing crisis, Chesky reorganized Airbnb following Steve Jobs's Apple playbook.

**The pre-crisis state:**
- 10 different divisions with subdivisions
- "Very much run by product managers"
- More people added = more projects = less app change = costs up
- Traditional PM function disconnected from product marketing

**The Jobs-inspired changes:**
1. Eliminated divisional structure, moved to functional organization
2. "Everyone's gonna work on everything together. There are no longer swim lanes."
3. One roadmap, centralized with CEO
4. Reduced ongoing initiatives dramatically
5. Release to users twice a year (80% of work in these releases)

**Product management transformation:**
- Eliminated "classic product management function"
- Combined product management with product marketing
- Made the PM/marketing team much smaller
- Elevated design to lead product decisions
- "You cannot develop product unless you know how to talk about the products and sell those products"
- "Apple didn't have it either" (traditional PM function)

**Chesky's clarification:** "Make no mistake, product managers are critical, they just shouldn't be doing the job of a designer."

**The architect analogy:** "Who's the product manager of a building? The architect."

## Crisis Leadership: COVID-19 Response

In early 2020, Airbnb lost 80% of its business in eight weeks. In January 2020, 80% of Airbnb's business in China had already collapsed.

### The Layoff

On May 5, 2020, Chesky laid off 1,900 employees (25% of workforce).

**What made it different:**
- Chesky personally reviewed every single layoff
- Wrote 3,374-word letter to employees
- Spoke from the heart, not corporate templates
- Provided generous severance: 14+ weeks pay, 12 months health insurance (COBRA in US), 4 months mental health support through KonTerra
- Created alumni directory for laid-off employees to publish their information
- Hundreds of thousands of recruiters and people visited those profiles
- Personally called CEOs of other companies asking them to hire Airbnb alumni

**The layoff letter philosophy:**
"I basically looked at a bunch of people who'd done layoff letters, and I noticed something about the way companies lay people off. It's pretty inhumane. It seems like a human being didn't write it. It feels like an AI prompt or something. AI is more compassionate than most of these layoff letters, and I think what ends up happening is that the CEOs get very risk averse. They're not vulnerable, they're afraid to say the wrong thing. They don't actually speak from the heart."

**The principle:** "Cut deep" - do layoffs the right way by cutting deep enough to avoid doing rounds.

### The Pivot

Recognized that travel would change. Pivoted to:
- Local stays (within driving distance)
- Longer-term reservations
- Scaled back Transportation, Studios, Hotels, and Lux
- Focused on core host community

### Crisis Psychology

"The hardest thing to manage in a crisis is your own psychology. People look in your eyes, and if you think you're screwed, they see it in your eyes. You need to be optimistic. But it can't be optimism that's delusional... The optimistic mentality is the mentality you need to be creative."

"I think the psychology of the leader becomes the psychology of the organization."

The company survived, recovered, and went public in December 2020.

## Culture Philosophy

### "Don't Fuck Up the Culture"

Famous letter sent to entire company, later published publicly.

**Core message:**
"The thing that will endure for 100 years, the way it has for most 100 year companies, is the culture. The culture is what creates the foundation for all future innovation. If you break the culture, you break the machine that creates your products."

### Culture Principles

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Values before hiring | Define values before first hire - Airbnb was one of the only companies in Silicon Valley to do this |
| Hire for values | Equal weight on expertise and values |
| Zero tolerance | Acting against values is fireable regardless of skills or seniority |
| Culture reduces process | Strong culture means less need for corporate bureaucracy |
| Problems are temporary | Culture is forever |

**The key insight:** "The stronger the culture, the less corporate process a company needs. When the culture is strong, you can trust everyone to do the right thing. People can be independent and autonomous. They can be entrepreneurial. And if we have a company that is entrepreneurial in spirit, we will be able to take our next '(wo)man on the moon' leap."

### Airbnb Core Values

- **Champion the mission** - Make people feel like they belong anywhere
- **Be a host** - Hospitality mindset in everything
- **Simplify** - Reduce complexity
- **Every frame matters** - Pay attention to every detail (derived from storyboarding)
- **Be a cereal entrepreneur** - Do whatever it takes (reference to Obama O's)
- **Embrace the adventure** - Take risks, explore

## Leadership Philosophy

### Product Obsession

"Build something 100 people love, not something 1 million people kind of like."

"The best marketing is investing in the user experience. And then your users will market for you."

"I am obsessive about not just doing what we've done before. The goal of our company is to constantly reach beyond our comfort zone."

### 10x Thinking

Chesky sets goals that feel too big - not because they are meant to be hit every time, but because they force first-principle thinking. Big goals change how teams think, not just what they do.

### The Jony Ive Advice

During the pandemic, Chesky sought advice from Jony Ive, who suggested: "You don't manage people. You manage people through the work."

This led Chesky to eliminate layers of management, remove managers who were not deeply involved in the work, and select highly skilled "experts" to lead key functions.

- **With Jony Ive:** Ive directly advised Chesky. Compare "manage through the work" principle to Jobs-Ive partnership model.
