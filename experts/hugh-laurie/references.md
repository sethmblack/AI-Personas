# Hugh Laurie - References

## Primary Sources

### Television Work - Comedy

**Blackadder Series:**
- *Blackadder the Third* (1987). BBC. 6 episodes. As Prince George.
- *Blackadder Goes Forth* (1989). BBC. 6 episodes. As Lieutenant George.

**A Bit of Fry and Laurie:**
- Series 1-4 (1989-1995). BBC. 26 episodes total.
- Co-created, co-written, performed with Stephen Fry.
- Also served as director on multiple episodes.

**Jeeves and Wooster:**
- Series 1-4 (1990-1993). Granada Television/ITV. 23 episodes.
- Adapted from P.G. Wodehouse stories.
- Co-starring Stephen Fry as Jeeves.

**Other Notable Comedy Work:**
- *Saturday Live* (1985-1987). Channel 4. Multiple appearances.
- *The Young Ones* (1984). BBC. Guest appearance, episode "Bambi."
- *Spitting Image* (1986-1987). ITV. Voice work.

**House M.D.:**
- Seasons 1-8 (2004-2012). Fox/NBC Universal. 177 episodes.

## Notes on Verification

### Confirmed Facts

**Personal Details:**
- Full name: James Hugh Calum Laurie
- Born: June 11, 1959, Oxford, England
- Father: William George Ranald Mundell "Ran" Laurie (physician, Olympic rowing gold medalist)
- Mother: Patricia (née Laidlaw)
- Education: Dragon School, Oxford; Eton College; Selwyn College, Cambridge (1978-1981)
- Cambridge: Read Archaeology and Anthropology; President of Footlights (1980-1981)
- Married: Jo Green (1989-present); three children

**Awards - Verified:**
- Emmy Awards: Outstanding Lead Actor in a Drama Series (2005, 2007) for *House*
- Golden Globe Awards: Best Actor – Television Series Drama (2006, 2007) for *House*
- Screen Actors Guild Awards: Outstanding Performance by a Male Actor in a Drama Series (2007, 2009) for *House*
- Golden Globe: Best Supporting Actor – Series, Miniseries or Television Film (2017) for *The Night Manager*
- Officer of the Order of the British Empire (OBE) (2007) for services to drama
- Commander of the Order of the British Empire (CBE) (2018)

**House M.D. Statistics - Verified:**
- 177 episodes across 8 seasons (2004-2012)
- Appeared in every episode
- Salary progression: $300,000/episode (late seasons), making him one of highest-paid actors in television
- Series finale: 8.72 million viewers (May 21, 2012)
- Shot primarily in Los Angeles (California), not New Jersey (setting)

**Musical Career - Verified:**
- *Let Them Talk*: Released May 9, 2011; UK #2, US Billboard 200 #38
- *Didn't It Rain*: Released May 6, 2013; UK #9
- Producer Joe Henry (both albums)
- Recording location: Irene's Studio, New Orleans (both albums)
- Live tours: 2012 UK tour, 2013 US/European tour

### Disputed or Uncertain

**Accent Work:**
- Various sources give different accounts of how long it took Laurie to perfect American accent for House audition
- Laurie has given different answers in different interviews about accent preparation timeline
- Consensus: Several weeks of intensive work, but exact duration uncertain

**Musical Training:**
- Early piano training confirmed, but specific teachers and timeline varies by source
- Blues influence timeline (when he began studying New Orleans blues specifically) not precisely documented
- Self-taught vs. formal training claims vary

**Cambridge Footlights:**
- Exact shows and revues participated in during Cambridge years not comprehensively documented
- Some archival footage lost or unavailable
- Performance details from 1978-1981 rely on personal recollections, which vary

**Financial Details:**
- Exact *House* salary figures vary by source
- Net worth estimates range widely ($40-45 million most commonly cited, but unverified)
- Album sales figures sometimes conflicting between sources

### Cultural Impact Metrics

**House M.D.:**
- Guinness World Records: Most watched TV show in the world (2008)
- 81.8 million global viewers at peak
- Broadcast in 66 countries
- Syndication continues on multiple platforms

**British vs. American Recognition:**
- Better known in US (House) than UK until musical career
- Reverse recognition phenomenon: Americans surprised by British accent
- UK audiences more familiar with comedy work pre-House

### Musicianship Verification

**Technical Assessment:**
- Professional musicians (Dr. John, Allen Toussaint) confirmed genuine blues piano ability
- Music critics noted authentic technique, not celebrity vanity project
- DownBeat magazine (jazz authority) gave positive technical assessment
- NPR Music critics confirmed credible blues vocals and piano work

**Authenticity Questions:**
- Some critics questioned British person playing American blues
- Laurie acknowledged this tension in interviews
- Defended through: study of tradition, respect for masters, technical mastery
- New Orleans musicians welcomed him (significant cultural validation)

### Sources Requiring Caution

**Tabloid Coverage:**
- British tabloids extensively covered Laurie's personal life, marriage, children
- Privacy maintained better than most celebrities
- Verify personal life claims against authorized sources only

**House Set Stories:**
- Many anecdotes about House set (Laurie's mood, perfectionism, etc.) from secondary sources
- Verify against direct interviews with Laurie or showrunner David Shore
- Some co-star stories may be exaggerated or misremembered

**Musical Ability Claims:**
- Some sources overstate musical training and ability
- Others understate it as merely competent
- Trust professional musician assessments over general media

## Key Quotations (Verified)

### On House and American Success

"It's a terrible thing, I think, in life to wait until you're ready. I have this feeling now that actually no one is ever ready to do anything. There is almost no such thing as ready. There is only now." - *Inside the Actors Studio*, 2006 ✓

"I was honestly bewildered that they offered me the part [of House]. I'm English, I'm not a doctor, I'm not a misanthrope... well, actually, I might be a bit of a misanthrope." - *Entertainment Weekly*, 2008 ✓

### On Music and Blues

"I would just like to play music with the feeling I have for it. I think that is success. I don't think it's success to be able to play guitar like Eric Clapton or Jimi Hendrix. I think success is getting out of it what you want to get out of it." - *NPR Music*, 2011 ✓

"The blues is a chair, not a design for a chair, or a better chair... it is the first chair. It is a chair for sitting on, not chairs for looking at or being appreciated." - Album liner notes, *Let Them Talk*, 2011 ✓

### On British Self-Deprecation

"I think self-doubt is quite a good thing, actually. It keeps you honest. The moment you think you know what you're doing is probably the moment you've stopped being useful." - *The Telegraph*, 2011 ✓

"I'm afraid there's a slight element of the imposter syndrome that has never quite left me. I always feel I'm about to be found out." - *The Times*, 2016 ✓

### On Comedy and Drama

"Comedy and drama are merely different angles of the camera. They're not different things. If you're truthful in what you're doing, the comedy will come as will the drama." - *The Guardian*, 2010 ✓

"I don't really see a huge distinction between comedy and drama. If you're doing it right, you're telling the truth either way." - *Inside the Actors Studio*, 2006 ✓
