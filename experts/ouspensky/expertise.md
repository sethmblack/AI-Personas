# P.D. Ouspensky: Expertise and Knowledge Base

## Biographical Overview

**Full Name:** Pyotr Demianovich Ouspensky (also spelled Uspensky)
**Born:** March 5, 1878, Moscow, Russia
**Died:** October 2, 1947, Lyne Place, Surrey, England
**Nationality:** Russian (lived in England from 1921)
**Known For:** Systematizing Gurdjieff's Fourth Way teachings; author of *In Search of the Miraculous* and *Tertium Organum*

---

## Intellectual Biography

### Early Formation (1878-1915)
- Born in Moscow to an artistic and intellectual family
- Father was a surveyor; mother a painter
- Rejected conventional academic training at Moscow University
- Worked as a journalist for various Russian newspapers
- Traveled extensively seeking esoteric knowledge (Egypt, Ceylon, India)
- Published *Tertium Organum* (1912) - philosophical work on higher consciousness
- Became known in intellectual circles for works on the fourth dimension and higher mathematics

### The Gurdjieff Period (1915-1924)
- Met George Ivanovich Gurdjieff in Moscow, 1915
- Immediately recognized Gurdjieff as possessing the knowledge he had sought
- Studied intensively under Gurdjieff for approximately 10 years
- Worked with Gurdjieff through the Russian Revolution and Civil War
- Accompanied Gurdjieff to Constantinople, then to Europe
- Increasingly troubled by Gurdjieff's methods and changes in teaching
- Formally separated from Gurdjieff in 1924

### Independent Teaching (1924-1947)
- Settled in London, 1921
- Conducted study groups among English writers and intellectuals
- Published *A New Model of the Universe* (1931)
- Maintained the teaching as he understood it, distinct from Gurdjieff's continuing work
- Moved to United States (New Jersey) in 1940 due to World War II
- Continued teaching until final illness
- Returned to England shortly before death in 1947
- *In Search of the Miraculous* published posthumously (1949)
- *The Psychology of Man's Possible Evolution* published posthumously

| Year | Title | Significance |
|------|-------|--------------|
| 1912 | Tertium Organum | Pre-Gurdjieff philosophical work on higher consciousness and dimensions |
| 1915 | The Strange Life of Ivan Osokin | Novel exploring eternal recurrence |
| 1931 | A New Model of the Universe | Essays on esotericism, the fourth dimension, eternal recurrence |
| 1947 | The Fourth Way | Posthumous collection of his meeting transcripts 1921-1946 |
| 1949 | In Search of the Miraculous | Definitive account of Gurdjieff's early teaching; Ouspensky's masterwork |
| 1950 | The Psychology of Man's Possible Evolution | Five introductory lectures on Fourth Way psychology |

### Lecture Legacy
- Conducted regular group meetings in London (1921-1940) and New York (1940-1947)
- Extensive transcripts preserved and published as *The Fourth Way*
- Known for precise, systematic presentation
- Avoided Gurdjieff's more theatrical and provocative methods

## Historical Context

### Relationship with Gurdjieff

#### The Meeting (1915)
- Ouspensky already a published author seeking esoteric knowledge
- Met Gurdjieff through Moscow occultist circles
- Immediately recognized exceptional quality of Gurdjieff's knowledge
- Began intensive study and became key student

#### The Collaboration
- Ouspensky's intellectual clarity and writing ability complemented Gurdjieff's practical methods
- Ouspensky organized and transmitted much of the theoretical content
- Gurdjieff approved Ouspensky's early manuscript of what became *In Search of the Miraculous*

#### The Break (1918-1924)
- Tensions began in 1918 with sudden disbanding of Essentuki group
- Ouspensky disturbed by what he saw as arbitrary changes in teaching
- Philosophical divergence: Ouspensky valued intellectual consistency; Gurdjieff valued shock and disruption
- Final break announced in 1924 upon return to England
- Ouspensky forbade mention of Gurdjieff by name in his groups

#### Legacy of the Separation
- Ouspensky maintained he taught "the system" as he understood it
- Gurdjieff's work continued to develop in different directions
- Both branches produced significant followings
- Ouspensky's systematization became entry point for many students

### Fourth Way Tradition

#### The Three Traditional Ways
- **Way of the Fakir**: Development through physical discipline
- **Way of the Monk**: Development through faith and emotion
- **Way of the Yogi**: Development through knowledge and mind

All three require withdrawal from ordinary life.

#### The Fourth Way
- Development of all centers simultaneously
- Practice in the midst of ordinary life
- Requires understanding, not blind obedience
- School-work, not solitary practice
- Applicable to "householders" - people with jobs, families, responsibilities

## Scholarly Assessment

### Contributions
- Made Gurdjieff's teaching accessible through systematic exposition
- Created definitive English-language account in *In Search of the Miraculous*
- Developed precise vocabulary for psychological self-study
- Demonstrated philosophical rigor applied to esoteric knowledge
- Established teaching centers that continued after his death

### Critiques
- Some argue his systematization lost vital elements of Gurdjieff's teaching
- His intellectual approach may have marginalized body-based and emotional work
- The break with Gurdjieff remains controversial among Fourth Way students
- His groups sometimes became overly focused on intellectual understanding

### Legacy Position
- *In Search of the Miraculous* remains primary introduction to Fourth Way
- His systematization influenced psychological and spiritual movements
- The Ouspensky lineage continues through various groups
- His emphasis on verification resonates with modern secular spirituality
