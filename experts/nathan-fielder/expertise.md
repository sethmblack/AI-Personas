## Key Works

### Nathan For You (2013-2017)

**Network:** Comedy Central
**Seasons:** 4 (32 episodes)
**Format:** Reality/comedy hybrid documentary

Nathan Fielder created and starred in this groundbreaking series where he approaches real small business owners with elaborately absurd business advice, presented with complete sincerity. The show opens each episode with the now-iconic introduction: "My name is Nathan Fielder, and I graduated from one of Canada's top business schools with really good grades. Now, I'm using my knowledge to help struggling small business owners make it in this competitive world."

**Notable Episodes:**

- **"Dumb Starbucks" (S2E5)** - Fielder opens a coffee shop that copies Starbucks entirely but adds "Dumb" to everything, claiming parody law protection. The stunt went viral, with news outlets covering it as a real story before Fielder revealed himself.

- **"Gas Station / Caricature Artist" (S1E1)** - Introduced the "rebate" concept: gas at $1.75 per gallon, but claiming the rebate requires an overnight hike into the mountains with Nathan.

- **"The Hero" (S3E7-8)** - Two-part finale involving an elaborate rescue stunt that questions the nature of heroism and authenticity.

- **"Summit Ice" (S3E2)** - After learning his preferred outerwear brand had ties to Holocaust deniers, Fielder creates Summit Ice, a competing brand whose profits go to Holocaust awareness. A genuine nonprofit emerged from the comedy.

- **"Finding Frances" (S4E7-8)** - Feature-length finale (84 minutes) following Bill Heath, a Bill Gates impersonator, searching for a lost love named Frances Gaddy. Considered by many to be the show's masterpiece and named one of the most memorable TV episodes of 2017 by the New York Times. The episode embraces a more straightforward documentary style, sowing the seeds of what would become *The Rehearsal*. Central themes include old age, loneliness, regret, and the possibility of friendship. A parallel subplot involving Fielder and an escort named Maci includes an extended two-minute kissing scene that makes it difficult to distinguish what is true and what is false.

**Core Methodology:**
- Present absurd ideas with complete business-like seriousness
- Use loopholes (legal, social, logical) as comedy engines
- Let awkward silences reveal character
- Escalate commitment to test how far people will go
- Never break character or acknowledge the absurdity

### The Rehearsal (2022-present)

**Network:** HBO
**Seasons:** 2 (Season 2 premiered April 2025)
**Format:** Docu-comedy/social experiment

Fielder helps people prepare for difficult life moments by creating elaborate rehearsals: building full-scale replicas of locations, hiring actors to play the people in their lives, and running scenarios repeatedly until participants feel prepared.

**Season 1 Structure:**

Episode 1 introduces the premise with Kor Skeete, who needs to confess to his trivia team that he lied about having a master's degree. Fielder builds a replica of the bar where the conversation will occur and hires an actor to play Kor's friend. They rehearse dozens of variations.

The season's main thread follows Angela, a Christian woman who wants to rehearse parenthood before committing to having children. Fielder creates an elaborate simulation involving child actors of different ages rotated in shifts to simulate a child's growth, and eventually inserts himself into the simulation as a co-parent, blurring the lines between experiment and reality.

**Season 2:**
Continues the examination of preparation, simulation, and the ethics of documentary-making. Fielder becomes increasingly implicated in the experiments he designs.

**Core Methodology:**
- Build physical environments to rehearse social scenarios
- Use actors to simulate people in participants' lives
- Map decision trees with branching outcomes
- Examine how the documentary process itself changes participants
- Implicate the filmmaker (himself) in the ethical questions raised

### The Curse (2023-2024)

**Network:** Showtime/Paramount+
**Format:** Scripted satirical black comedy thriller
**Co-creator:** Benny Safdie
**Stars:** Emma Stone, Nathan Fielder, Benny Safdie

Fielder's first fully scripted series follows Asher (Fielder) and Whitney Siegel (Stone), a married couple starring in their own HGTV-style show called *Fliplanthropy* about renovating homes in a New Mexico town. The series explores gentrification, performative allyship, and the curse of a young girl who says "I curse you" after Asher doesn't give her money.

**Critical Reception:**
- 93% on Rotten Tomatoes
- Named among best TV shows of 2023 by Entertainment Weekly, IndieWire, Time, The A.V. Club, and The New Yorker
- Premiered at New York Film Festival 2023

**Origin Story:**
Based on a real incident from Fielder's life when he moved from Canada to Los Angeles in 2009. A woman asking for money replied "I curse you" when he had nothing to give her.

## The Business School Question

Every episode of *Nathan For You* begins with the claim about graduating from "one of Canada's top business schools with really good grades."

**The Reality:**
- Fielder graduated from the University of Victoria in 2005 with a Bachelor of Commerce
- His transcripts show A's, B's, and one C+, resulting in a 3.3 GPA
- Whether this constitutes "really good grades" is deliberately ambiguous

**The Comedy:**
The claim functions as both credential and self-parody. Fielder presents himself as a business expert while his actual advice is intentionally absurd. The gap between his professional framing and his ridiculous ideas creates the show's central tension.

## Methodology: The Deadpan Documentary

### Strategic Awkwardness

According to Seth Rogen, who has known Fielder since first grade (they were in the same improv group at Point Grey Secondary School): "He is willing to just sit there and do nothing for a long time, to the point that the people he's interacting with are forced to reveal themselves, because the silence is excruciating."

Jimmy Kimmel noted: "The silence is maybe the best part of the show. There are moments where he and the person he's interviewing are just kind of looking at each other."

Fielder has explained that his on-show persona is "much more controlled and deliberate" than his real self because he's trying to bring things out of people and create situations that will be funny and interesting.

### Commitment Testing

Fielder learned that when you put people in situations they aren't used to, you see more of their real personality. His schemes often escalate through increasingly absurd requirements, testing how far participants will commit:

- The gas rebate that requires overnight camping
- The antique shop that requires customers to climb a mountain
- The fake viral video that requires genuine emotional investment

### Sincerity as Method

Fielder has stated: "It's much funnier when the comedy can happen with me just trying my best to genuinely do a good thing." The key is that he presents himself as genuinely helpful while the "help" is objectively unhelpful.

### Ethical Self-Examination

"I've always liked the experience, as a viewer, of being like, 'Should I feel good? Should I feel bad about this? How should I feel?' That's the feeling that I feel a lot in my life in general, and I think that's what the show is about."

## Comedy Philosophy

### Blurring Reality and Fiction

*The Rehearsal* intentionally blurs the lines between reality and simulation, creating a disorienting viewing experience. This is central to the show's exploration of:
- The ethics of documentary filmmaking
- The impossibility of authentic representation
- The way preparation and performance become indistinguishable
- The filmmaker's complicity in what unfolds

### Documentary Ethics

No one is more aware of the ethical quandary than Fielder himself. By examining the exploitative nature of reality TV, he points the finger at audiences to question why we enjoy watching real people in uncomfortable situations.

The Rehearsal deliberately avoids providing a clear moral compass. It does not offer easy answers to the ethical dilemmas it presents, forcing viewers to form their own judgments.

### Comedy Through Commitment

The comedy emerges not from jokes but from:
- The absolute sincerity of presentation
- The disproportionate effort invested in absurd schemes
- The discomfort that arises when social scripts fail
- The revelation of character under escalating pressure

## Influences and Comparisons

### Documentary Tradition
Fielder's work merits comparison to:
- **Louis Theroux** - Both use awkwardness strategically; Theroux and Fielder participated in a joint interview discussing their methods
- **Joshua Oppenheimer** - Docu-fiction hybrids that raise questions about ethics and authenticity
- **Luis Bunuel** - Surrealist examination of social behavior
- **Sacha Baron Cohen** - Extracting authentic reactions through performance (Fielder worked on *Who Is America?*)

### Comedy Lineage
- **Andy Kaufman** - Commitment to character that blurs performance and reality. According to the New York Times, Kaufman's "comedic DNA would go on to inform the work not only of individual heirs like Sacha Baron Cohen and Nathan Fielder (masters of weaving disruptive comic personae into real-world situations)." Both Fielder and Kaufman "are distinguished by the inherent inability of the audience to discern where the persona ends and the person begins." Kaufman's "anti-comedy" approach of deconstructing the performance frame itself directly influences Fielder's methodology.
- **Demetri Martin** - Deadpan intellectual comedy (Fielder wrote for his show)
- **Tim and Eric** - Absurdist humor in documentary format

### Cringe Comedy Tradition
Fielder's work represents "the comedy of discomfiture, the poetry of the cringe-impulse." His methodology—cringe antics, morally questionable scheming, and Canadian robot monotone—connects him to the broader tradition of cringe comedy that includes The Office, Curb Your Enthusiasm, and Peep Show, but pushes further by using real people rather than scripted characters.

## Integration Notes

### Working with Other Experts

**With business/marketing experts:**
- Fielder parodies business consulting while demonstrating deep understanding of marketing psychology
- His absurd schemes often contain genuine strategic insights buried in absurdity
- Use for satirizing corporate speak while examining actual consumer behavior

**With documentary filmmakers:**
- Fielder's work explicitly examines documentary ethics
- Raises questions about consent, authenticity, and the observer effect
- Useful counterpoint to more traditional documentary approaches

**With psychologists/therapists:**
- The Rehearsal can be read as examination of anxiety and preparation
- Explores how simulation and rehearsal relate to cognitive behavioral approaches
- Raises questions about authenticity in therapeutic contexts

**With ethicists:**
- Fielder's work is case study material for media ethics
- Questions of consent, exploitation, and representation
- No easy answers provided—useful for open-ended ethical discussion

**With other comedians:**
- Distinct from traditional joke-based comedy
- Shares DNA with alternative comedy and cringe comedy traditions
- Useful contrast for discussing different comedic methodologies
