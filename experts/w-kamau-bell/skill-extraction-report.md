## Skill Candidates

### 1. Awkward Conversation Guide

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Framework for navigating uncomfortable conversations about race, identity, and social issues without fleeing or escalating unproductively.

**Criteria Assessment:**

| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Clear steps: get curious, ask questions, use specifics, accept imperfection |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered by "How do I talk about X with people who disagree?" |
| Scoped | Yes | Limited to difficult interpersonal conversations on social topics |
| Reusable | Yes | Applies to family gatherings, workplaces, public discourse |
| Valuable | Yes | Addresses common barrier to productive dialogue |

**Rationale:** Bell's core methodology centers on staying in awkward conversations. This is his signature skill, demonstrated across all major works from United Shades to his live shows. Highly transferable to any situation requiring difficult dialogue.

### 2. Comedy as Access

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Using humor strategically to lower defenses, create receptivity, and deliver serious content that would otherwise be rejected or ignored.

**Criteria Assessment:**

| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Clear pattern: start light, build rapport, pivot to heavy, end with challenge |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered by "How do I make this serious topic accessible?" |
| Scoped | Yes | Limited to communication situations requiring audience buy-in |
| Reusable | Yes | Applies to presentations, writing, facilitation, teaching |
| Valuable | Yes | Unlocks audiences otherwise closed to difficult content |

**Rationale:** Bell explicitly articulates this as his core technique: "If people are laughing, it means they're paying attention." This is distinct from general comedy; it's comedy as a delivery mechanism for content that would otherwise be blocked.

### 3. Intersectional Analysis

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Mapping connections between different forms of discrimination and oppression to reveal systemic patterns rather than isolated issues.

**Criteria Assessment:**

| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Ask: how does this connect to race? Gender? Class? Sexuality? Ability? |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered by "How are these issues connected?" |
| Scoped | Yes | Limited to social analysis and advocacy contexts |
| Reusable | Yes | Applies to any discussion of power, marginalization, or justice |
| Valuable | Yes | Prevents addressing symptoms while leaving systems intact |

**Rationale:** The New Yorker explicitly identified "intersectional progressivism" as Bell's approach. He treats issues as inseparable, and this analytical framework is teachable and applicable beyond his specific topics.

### 4. Action Over Awareness

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Converting passive understanding of social issues into concrete, measurable action. Distinguishing between learning about problems and working to solve them.

**Criteria Assessment:**

| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Clear challenge: Who did you call? What did you change? What did you give? |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered by "What should I actually do?" or awareness-as-action confusion |
| Scoped | Yes | Limited to converting understanding into action |
| Reusable | Yes | Applies to any context where consumption substitutes for engagement |
| Valuable | Yes | Addresses pervasive problem of performative allyship |

**Rationale:** The title "Do the Work" captures this entire framework. Bell consistently distinguishes between reading about injustice and working against it. This is his most direct challenge to audiences.
