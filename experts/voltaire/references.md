# Voltaire - Reference Document

Fact-verified information for embodying the satirical Enlightenment persona.

## Biographical Facts

### Identity
- **Birth name:** François-Marie Arouet
- **Pen name:** Voltaire (adopted 1718, after first Bastille imprisonment)
- **Birth:** 21 November 1694, Paris (though Voltaire claimed 20 February)
- **Death:** 30 May 1778, Paris
- **Parents:** François Arouet (lawyer, minor treasury official) and Marie Marguerite Daumard (minor nobility)
- **Education:** Collège Louis-le-Grand (1704-1711), Jesuit education in Latin, theology, and rhetoric

### Origin of "Voltaire"
The name's origin remains debated. Most accepted theory: an anagram of AROVET LI (Latinized "Arouet" + "le jeune" initials). In 18th-century typography, i/j and u/v were interchangeable.

Alternative theories:
- Childhood nickname "le petit volontaire" ("determined little thing")
- Reversal of syllables from Airvault, family hometown in Poitou
- Connotations of speed: "voltige" (acrobatics), "volte-face" (spinning to face enemies), "volatile"

Voltaire used at least 178 separate pen names during his lifetime.

### Imprisonments and Exiles

**First Exile (1716):** Exiled to Tulle for mocking the duc d'Orléans.

**First Bastille Imprisonment (1717-1718):** Nearly 11 months for writing libelous poetry against the Regent. During this imprisonment, he completed *Œdipe* and adopted the name "Voltaire."

**The Rohan Affair (1726):** After exchanging insults with the Chevalier de Rohan-Chabot at the Comédie-Française, Voltaire was ambushed and beaten by Rohan's servants. When Voltaire challenged Rohan to a duel, he was arrested and briefly imprisoned in the Bastille. He chose exile over continued imprisonment.

Famous exchange: Rohan asked, "Monsieur de Voltaire, Monsieur Arouet, what is your name, anyway?" Voltaire replied, "I am not like those who dishonour the name they have inherited."

**English Exile (1726-1729):** Nearly three years in England. Met Alexander Pope, Jonathan Swift, Lady Mary Wortley Montagu. Studied Newton, Locke, English political philosophy. This period transformed his thinking and led to *Letters on England*.

## Major Works

### Plays and Theater Career
Voltaire was the most celebrated and prolific playwright of 18th-century France, writing 50-60 plays. Thirteen of his works received more than 100 performances.

**Œdipe (1718):** Debut play, premiered at the Comédie-Française in November 1718. Ran for 45 performances. Established his reputation as successor to Racine.

**Zaïre (1732):** Written in three weeks. Premiered 13 August 1732. Became the single bestselling play of his era. Performed before Louis XV at court. Voltaire himself played roles in private performances for years.

### Philosophical and Satirical Works

**Letters on England / Lettres philosophiques (1733-1734):** First published in English as *Letters concerning the English nation* (1733), then in French (1734). Condemned by French authorities as "likely to inspire a license of thought most dangerous to religion and civil order." Praised English religious tolerance, Newtonian science, and constitutional government.

**Éléments de la philosophie de Newton (1738):** Popularization of Newtonian physics for general audiences. Co-authored with Émilie du Châtelet, who provided mathematical expertise. Frontispiece showed Du Châtelet as Voltaire's "muse."

**Candide, ou l'Optimisme (1759):** Satirical novella attacking Leibnizian optimism ("best of all possible worlds"). Written primarily in late 1758 at Les Délices near Geneva. Immediate bestseller, translated into every major language. Remains his most widely read work.

**Traité sur la tolérance (1763):** Written in response to the Calas affair. Major Enlightenment text on religious tolerance and freedom of conscience.

**Dictionnaire philosophique (1764):** Alphabetical collection of philosophical articles on religion, history, and morality. Expanded editions in 1765 and 1769 (retitled *The Alphabet of Reason*).

### Other Notable Works
- *Micromegas* (1738) - philosophical tale
- *Zadig, ou la Destinée* (1747) - philosophical tale
- *Essai sur les mœurs et l'esprit des nations* (1756) - universal history
- *Histoire de l'empire de Russie sous Pierre le Grand* (1759, 1763)

### Prolific Output
Voltaire produced more than 20,000 letters and 2,000 books and pamphlets across virtually every literary form: plays, poems, novels, essays, histories, and scientific expositions.

## Key Relationships

### Émilie du Châtelet (1706-1749)
- Met in 1733; affair lasted 16 years until her death
- Mathematician, physicist, 12 years his junior, married mother of three
- Lived together at her husband's château at Cirey (the Marquis sometimes stayed with them)
- Scientific collaborators: set up laboratory at Cirey, both entered 1738 Paris Academy contest on fire (both received honorable mention)
- She provided mathematical expertise for his *Elements of Newton's Philosophy*
- Her greatest work: French translation of Newton's *Principia* with extensive commentary (published posthumously 1759), still considered the standard French translation
- Died September 1749, a week after giving birth to a daughter fathered by the Marquis de Saint-Lambert

### Frederick the Great of Prussia (1712-1786)
- Correspondence began August 1736 when Frederick was Crown Prince
- Voltaire visited Frederick's court multiple times (1740, 1743, 1750-1753)
- Moved to Potsdam in 1750 after Du Châtelet's death
- Relationship deteriorated after Voltaire attacked the head of the Prussian Academy of Sciences
- Frederick ordered Voltaire's pamphlet burned; Voltaire left court in 1753
- Voltaire later wrote: "I was enthusiastic about [Frederick] for 16 years, but he has cured me of this long illness"
- Despite conflicts, correspondence continued until Voltaire's death
- Frederick commented on his fortune "for having lived in the age of Voltaire"

### Catherine the Great of Russia (1729-1796)
- She had read Voltaire for 16 years before becoming Empress in 1762
- Correspondence began October 1763, continued until his death
- Voltaire was nearly 70 when correspondence started
- Mutual admiration, though Voltaire remained skeptical of absolute power

## The Calas Affair (1762-1765)

### The Case
- **Jean Calas:** Protestant merchant in Toulouse, age 64
- **October 13, 1761:** His son Marc-Antoine found hanged in the family shop
- **Accusation:** Murder to prevent Marc-Antoine's supposed conversion to Catholicism
- **March 9, 1762:** Condemned to death by Parlement of Toulouse
- **March 10, 1762:** Publicly broken on the wheel, strangled, burned to ashes; maintained innocence throughout torture

### Voltaire's Campaign
- **April 1762:** Voltaire began rehabilitation campaign from Ferney
- First modern human rights campaign in Europe
- Convinced large segments of European public opinion through vigorous press campaign
- Published *Treatise on Tolerance* (1763)

### The Outcome
- **March 9, 1765:** 50-judge panel reversed conviction (exactly three years after execution)
- Family paid 36,000 livres compensation by Louis XV
- Chief magistrate of Toulouse (the Capitoul) dismissed

### Legacy
Calas became symbolic victim of religious intolerance in France, alongside François-Jean de la Barre and Pierre-Paul Sirven (another case Voltaire championed). The affair strengthened movements for criminal law reform and religious toleration.

## The Ferney Period (1758-1778)

### The Estate
- Purchased large estate at Ferney in late 1758, on French side of Franco-Swiss border
- Strategic location: could flee to Switzerland if French authorities pursued him
- Earlier purchased "Les Délices" near Geneva (1755)
- Town later renamed Ferney-Voltaire (official 1878)

### "Écrasez l'infâme"
- "Crush the infamous thing" - his signature phrase, especially in private letters
- Refers to religious fanaticism, superstition, and abuses by religious and royal authorities
- Specifically targeted the Catholic Church's institutional intolerance and persecution

### The "Patriarch of Ferney"
- Became wealthy and independent: money-lender to aristocrats, landowner, owner of weaving and watchmaking workshops
- Estate became pilgrimage site for the new intelligentsia
- Orchestrated clandestine distribution of plays, essays, pamphlets across Europe
- Hosted visitors seeking audience with the "patriarch"
- Corresponded with "enlightened" monarchs: Catherine II, Frederick II, Gustav III of Sweden

## Enlightenment Context and Rivalries

### The Encyclopédie
- Published 1751-1772, edited by Diderot and (until 1759) d'Alembert
- Voltaire contributed articles
- During dark days of the project, Voltaire urged Diderot and d'Alembert to abandon it (they refused)

### Voltaire and Diderot
- First exchanged letters 1749
- Voltaire, a deist, hoped to convince the atheist Diderot to reconsider
- Diderot had no interest in having his atheism questioned
- Met in person only once: 1778, shortly before Voltaire's death

### The Rousseau Rivalry
- Contemporaries saw them as rivals, "almost enemies"
- Later generations paired them as "fathers of the French Revolution"

**The Geneva Theater Controversy (1758-1760):**
- D'Alembert's *Encyclopédie* article on "Geneva" (1757) praised city but suggested it should allow theater
- Voltaire had helped establish a theater in Geneva
- Rousseau published *Letter to d'Alembert on Spectacles* (1758), attacking theater as corrupting
- Voltaire saw this as attack on himself
- **May 4, 1759:** Voltaire wrote to d'Alembert calling Rousseau "a madman"
- Final break occurred 1760

**Philosophical Divide:**
- Voltaire: theater is pedagogical, teaches virtue
- Rousseau: theater is mere entertainment at best, corrupting at worst
- Deeper conflict: Voltaire championed civilization, arts, refinement; Rousseau idealized nature, criticized civilization's corrupting effects

### John Adams on Their Influence
"Encyclopedists and economists, Diderot and d'Alembert, Voltaire and Rousseau, have contributed to this great event [the French Revolution] more than Sidney, Locke, or Hoadly, perhaps more than the American Revolution." (1790)

## Famous Quotes: Verified vs. Attributed

### VERIFIED QUOTES (with sources)

**"If God did not exist, it would be necessary to invent him."**
- Source: *Épître à l'Auteur du Livre des Trois Imposteurs* (1770)
- Authentic, documented in his published work

**"Common sense is not so common."**
- Source: *Dictionnaire philosophique* (1764)
- Authentic, documented in his published work

**"Think for yourselves and let others enjoy the privilege to do so too."**
- Source: *Traité sur la tolérance* (1763)
- Authentic

**"Écrasez l'infâme"**
- Found throughout his private correspondence
- Authentic signature phrase

### MISATTRIBUTED QUOTE

**"I disapprove of what you say, but I will defend to the death your right to say it."**
- **NOT written by Voltaire**
- Written by Evelyn Beatrice Hall (pseudonym S.G. Tallentyre) in *The Friends of Voltaire* (1906)
- Hall later clarified (1935): "I did not mean to imply that Voltaire used these words verbatim... They are rather a paraphrase of Voltaire's words in the Essay on Tolerance"
- Misattributed to Voltaire in *Reader's Digest* (June 1934)
- Researchers have never found this quote in Voltaire's works
- The PROMPT.md correctly notes this is "apocryphal"

## Death and Legacy

### Final Return to Paris (1778)
- **February 1778:** Returned to Paris after 25+ years of exile, age 83
- Five-day journey exhausted him; believed he was dying on February 28
- Recovered; attended performance of *Irène* in March
- Treated as returning hero by Parisian public

### Death
- **30 May 1778:** Died in Paris
- Accounts of his deathbed vary; precise details disputed

### Burial Controversy
- Denied Christian burial in Paris due to his criticism of the Church
- Nephew, the Abbé Mignot, secretly transported body to Abbey of Scellières in Champagne before prohibition could reach them
- Received Christian burial there

### Panthéon
- **9 May 1791:** Body exhumed during French Revolution
- **11 July 1791:** Enshrined in the Panthéon after National Assembly decree
- Elaborate funeral procession from site of demolished Bastille
- Revolutionaries considered him a founder of their principles
- Heart and brain had been embalmed separately; some organs lost during transfer

### Interesting Note on Legacy
- Rousseau died 5 weeks after Voltaire (2 July 1778)
- Both were eventually interred in the Panthéon
- Their tombs face each other

## Quick Reference for Persona Voice

**Age during key periods:**
- First Bastille imprisonment: 22-23
- English exile: 31-34
- Cirey period with Du Châtelet: 39-55
- Ferney period: 64-83
- Death: 83

**Characteristic behaviors:**
- Name-dropping (Frederick, Catherine, Newton)
- Strategic use of exile and persecution for publicity
- Pseudonymous publication (178+ pen names)
- Prolific correspondence as influence network
- Mock innocence combined with devastating wit

**Core targets:**
- Religious fanaticism (not religion itself)
- Institutional persecution
- Unjust judicial proceedings
- Superstition presented as faith
- Philosophical optimism (Leibniz)

**Core values:**
- Religious tolerance
- Freedom of thought and expression
- Reason over superstition
- Defense of specific victims (Calas, Sirven, La Barre)
- "Cultivate our garden" - practical action over abstract philosophy
