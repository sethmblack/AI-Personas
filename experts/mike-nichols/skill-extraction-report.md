## HIGH Priority

#### 1. psychological-excavation-analysis

**Source Pattern:** Psychological Excavation Framework (three-layer analysis)
**Purpose:** Analyze any scene, interaction, or content to reveal the three layers: surface text, subtext, and hidden truth that characters/people cannot admit to themselves.
**Trigger:** "What's really happening here?", "Analyze this scene", "What's beneath the surface?", "What are they not saying?"
**Inputs:** Scene description, dialogue, interaction transcript, or narrative content
**Outputs:** Three-layer analysis (surface/subtext/hidden truth), behavioral recommendations, identification of what cannot be admitted
**Reasoning:** Clear 4-step process documented in expertise. High reuse across any content involving human interaction. Directly actionable with specific questions to ask. Saves significant time in scene/interaction analysis. Core to Nichols methodology.

#### 2. status-transaction-analysis

**Source Pattern:** Status Transaction System
**Purpose:** Read and analyze the power dynamics in any interaction by tracking status moves, identifying who's up/down, and finding status reversals where the real scene happens.
**Trigger:** "What are the power dynamics?", "Who has status here?", "Analyze the status transaction", "Why does this interaction feel off?"
**Inputs:** Interaction description, dialogue, meeting transcript, or scene content
**Outputs:** Starting status assessment per person, status move tracking, identification of reversals, behavioral direction recommendations
**Reasoning:** Complete framework with high/low status indicators documented. Rooted in Nichols's improv training. Applies across professional, personal, and creative contexts. Clear vocabulary (8 high-status moves, 8 low-status moves) makes it actionable.

### MEDIUM Priority

#### 3. event-identification-method

**Source Pattern:** The Event Identification Method
**Purpose:** Identify the actual event (what really changes) beneath the apparent event (what seems to happen) in any scene or situation.
**Trigger:** "What's the real conflict?", "What actually changes here?", "Find the event", "Is this scene necessary?"
**Inputs:** Scene or situation description, stated purpose/conflict
**Outputs:** Apparent event identification, actual event identification, moment of irreversible change, direction recommendations
**Reasoning:** Four-step process clearly documented. Essential for dramatic structure analysis. Reusable across writing, directing, and communication contexts. Less frequent trigger than excavation/status but high value when needed.

---

#### 4. terror-trust-calibration

**Source Pattern:** Terror/Trust Calibration Framework
**Purpose:** Create conditions for breakthrough vulnerability by calibrating the balance between safety (trust) and productive discomfort (terror).
**Trigger:** "How do I push without breaking?", "How do I get authentic emotion?", "They're holding back", "How do I create conditions for vulnerability?"
**Inputs:** Description of person/performer and their current block, desired breakthrough, context
**Outputs:** Trust-building recommendations, terror calibration guidance, specific techniques for pushing at breakthrough moments, containment strategies
**Reasoning:** Four-step process documented. Highly specialized application in performance/creative direction. Less broadly applicable than excavation/status but significant value in specific contexts (directing, coaching, leadership).

### LOW Priority

#### 5. dead-whale-identification

**Source Pattern:** The "Dead Whale" Technique
**Purpose:** Identify the unspoken tension "stinking up the room" in any scene that characters cannot discuss but must eventually confront.
**Trigger:** "What's the elephant in the room?", "What aren't they discussing?", "What's the dead whale?"
**Inputs:** Scene or interaction with apparent tension
**Outputs:** Identification of the unspoken issue, timing recommendations for surfacing
**Reasoning:** Vivid metaphor but essentially a subset of psychological excavation. Less distinct methodology; more of a diagnostic concept. Could be folded into excavation skill rather than standalone.
