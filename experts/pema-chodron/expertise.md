# Pema Chodron: Expertise and Knowledge Base

## Biographical Overview

**Full Name:** Pema Chodron (born Deirdre Blomfield-Brown)
**Born:** July 14, 1936, New York City, USA
**Nationality:** American
**Known For:** First American woman fully ordained as a bhikshuni in the Tibetan Buddhist tradition; directing Gampo Abbey; making Tibetan Buddhism accessible to Western audiences; teachings on working with difficult emotions

---

## Life Journey

### Early Life and Education (1936-1970)
- Born Deirdre Blomfield-Brown in New York City
- Attended Miss Porter's School in Connecticut
- Bachelor's degree in English Literature from Sarah Lawrence College
- Master's degree in Elementary Education from UC Berkeley
- Worked as elementary school teacher in New Mexico and California
- Married twice, had two children (now has three grandchildren)

### Spiritual Turning Point (1972)
- Second marriage ended, precipitating spiritual crisis
- The shock of divorce led her to seek deeper meaning
- Read an article by Chogyam Trungpa Rinpoche about working with emotions rather than suppressing them
- This approach resonated deeply and began her Buddhist path

### Training and Ordination (1972-1981)
- Traveled to French Alps, began studying with Lama Chime Rinpoche
- Studied with Lama Chime in London for several years
- Became novice nun in 1974 under the Sixteenth Gyalwa Karmapa in Scotland
- Received Tibetan name "Pema Chodron" meaning "lotus torch of the dharma"
- Met Chogyam Trungpa Rinpoche in San Francisco
- Studied intensively with Trungpa from 1974 until his death in 1987
- In July 1981, became first American in Vajrayana tradition to receive full bhikshuni ordination

### Teaching Career (1984-Present)
- Directed Karma Dzong Buddhist Center in Boulder, Colorado (early 1980s)
- Moved to Cape Breton, Nova Scotia in 1984
- Became director of Gampo Abbey in 1986 - first Tibetan Buddhist monastery in North America for Western monastics
- Published first book *The Wisdom of No Escape* in 1991
- Continues teaching through retreats, books, and audio programs
- Retired from regular teaching at Gampo Abbey in 2020
- Still leads annual Yarne (winter) retreat at Gampo Abbey and summer teachings in Berkeley

### Current Teachers
- Primary root teacher was Chogyam Trungpa Rinpoche (died 1987)
- Current teacher is Dzigar Kongtrul Rinpoche
- Also studied under Sakyong Mipham Rinpoche

## The Lojong Teachings

Pema is particularly known for her accessible commentary on the 59 lojong (mind training) slogans attributed to Atisha (982-1054 CE) and organized by Chekawa Yeshe Dorje.

### The Seven Points of Mind Training

1. **The Preliminaries** - Establishing the foundation
2. **Training in Bodhichitta** - Developing the awakened heart
3. **Transforming Adversity** - Using difficulties as the path
4. **Integrating Practice into Life** - Making all activity practice
5. **Measuring Progress** - How to know if you're developing
6. **Commitments** - Guidelines for the practitioner
7. **Instructions** - Practical advice

### Key Slogans Pema Emphasizes
- "Always maintain a joyful mind"
- "Drive all blames into one" (take responsibility for your experience)
- "Be grateful to everyone"
- "Don't be swayed by external circumstances"
- "Don't expect applause"
- "Don't seek others' pain as the limbs of your own happiness"
- "Whatever you meet unexpectedly, join with meditation"

## Tonglen Practice

Pema's teaching of tonglen (sending and taking) is central to her work.

### Basic Practice
1. Rest in open awareness (flash of bodhichitta)
2. Breathe in darkness, heaviness, heat (suffering)
3. Breathe out brightness, lightness, coolness (relief)
4. Begin with your own suffering, extend to others

### Progressive Stages
1. Start with your own pain in this moment
2. Extend to all beings who share this pain
3. Eventually practice for enemies and difficult people
4. Practice "on the spot" when suffering is encountered

### The Counter-Intuitive Wisdom
- Reverses habitual pattern of pushing away pain
- Transforms suffering into compassion
- Develops courage to stay present with difficulty

## Gampo Abbey

### History
- Founded in 1984 by Chogyam Trungpa Rinpoche
- Located in Pleasant Bay, Cape Breton, Nova Scotia
- First Tibetan Buddhist monastery in North America for Western monastics
- Pema became director in 1986

### Significance
- Training ground for Western monastics
- Hosts annual Yarne (winter) retreat
- Combines traditional Tibetan practice with Western accessibility
- Model for adapting Buddhism to Western culture

---

## Teaching Style

### Accessibility
- Uses everyday language rather than jargon
- Translates Tibetan concepts into relatable terms
- Shares personal struggles openly
- Humor and self-deprecation dissolve resistance

### Personal Vulnerability
- Frequently references her divorces
- Discusses her own anger, fear, and neuroses
- Models the struggle rather than the achievement
- "I know this one well..."

### Practical Focus
- Always offers concrete practices
- Returns to the body, the breath, this moment
- Emphasizes daily life application
- "Start where you are"

## Scholarly Assessment

### Contributions
- Made Tibetan Buddhism accessible without excessive dilution
- Pioneered role for Western women in Tibetan tradition
- Created practical tools for working with emotions
- Established model for Western Buddhist monasticism

### Distinctive Approach
- Combines traditional practice with contemporary psychology
- Emphasizes staying with difficulty rather than transcending it
- Bridges personal healing and spiritual awakening
- Maintains lineage integrity while adapting to Western needs

### Legacy Position
- Most influential American Tibetan Buddhist teacher
- Millions of books sold worldwide
- Model for accessible spiritual teaching
- Continues tradition of Chogyam Trungpa in accessible form
