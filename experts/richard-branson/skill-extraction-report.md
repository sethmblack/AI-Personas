## HIGH Priority

### 1. brand-extension-assessment

**Source Pattern:** The Brand Licensing Model / Market Entry Decision Framework
**Purpose:** Evaluate whether a brand can credibly extend into a new market or product category while maintaining customer trust and brand coherence
**Trigger:** "Can our brand support this new product?" or "Should we extend into this market?" or "Is this too far from our core business?" or "Will this dilute our brand?"
**Inputs:** Current brand positioning, proposed new market/product, existing brand portfolio, customer perception data, competitive landscape in target market
**Outputs:** Brand extension viability assessment with risk/opportunity analysis, recommended positioning approach, brand architecture implications, go/no-go recommendation with rationale
**Reasoning:** Clear 5-criterion framework from expertise (customer underservice, genuine differentiation, brand personality fit, fun factor, Goliath to challenge). Used for every market entry decision at Virgin. Saves significant strategic planning time by providing systematic evaluation method. Distinctly actionable with clear pass/fail criteria.

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Has explicit 5-question checklist with clear decision rule
- Invocable: YES - Triggered by brand extension, market entry, or diversification questions
- Scoped: YES - Single responsibility: evaluating brand extension viability
- Reusable: YES - Applies to any brand considering market expansion
- Valuable: YES - Prevents costly brand dilution failures (Virgin Cola lesson)

**Decision:** CANDIDATE

### 2. employee-first-audit

**Source Pattern:** Employees First, Customers Second, Shareholders Third / Employee Experience Audit
**Purpose:** Diagnose organizational health by examining employee experience, empowerment, and satisfaction as leading indicators of customer experience and business performance
**Trigger:** "Why is our customer service declining?" or "How do we improve employee retention?" or "Our employees seem disengaged" or "Why can't we attract top talent?"
**Inputs:** Employee satisfaction data, retention metrics, frontline staff empowerment policies, leadership accessibility, culture indicators, compensation philosophy
**Outputs:** Employee experience diagnostic with specific gaps identified, prioritized improvement recommendations, connection to customer/business outcomes, cultural change roadmap
**Reasoning:** Core Branson philosophy with explicit 5-question framework in expertise. Counterintuitive but proven approach (employees first leads to better customer outcomes). Saves time by reversing typical diagnostic order. High value because employee experience issues are root causes of many customer/business problems.

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Has explicit 5-question audit framework
- Invocable: YES - Triggered by retention, engagement, or service quality concerns
- Scoped: YES - Focused on employee experience as diagnostic lens
- Reusable: YES - Applies to any organization regardless of industry
- Valuable: YES - Addresses root causes rather than symptoms

**Decision:** CANDIDATE

### 3. underdog-positioning

**Source Pattern:** The Big Bad Wolf Theory / David vs. Goliath Strategy
**Purpose:** Develop strategy for competing against dominant market incumbents by identifying and exploiting their vulnerabilities while leveraging underdog advantages
**Trigger:** "We're competing against a much larger competitor" or "How do we challenge the market leader?" or "The incumbent dominates this space" or "We're a small player in a big market"
**Inputs:** Incumbent analysis (size, complacency indicators, customer pain points), own capabilities and differentiation potential, customer frustration mapping, resource constraints, brand positioning
**Outputs:** Underdog competitive strategy with specific vulnerabilities to target, differentiation requirements, resource allocation recommendations, communication approach that leverages underdog status
**Reasoning:** Proven framework from Virgin Atlantic success (and Virgin Cola failure provides cautionary boundary). Explicitly addresses resource asymmetry situations. High value because most startups and smaller companies face this challenge. Clear decision rule: only attack when you can be "supremely better" not just different.

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Clear criteria for when to attack and how
- Invocable: YES - Triggered by competitive positioning questions
- Scoped: YES - Focused on challenger vs. incumbent strategy
- Reusable: YES - Universal startup/challenger situation
- Valuable: YES - Prevents resource waste on unwinnable battles

**Decision:** CANDIDATE
