## Extracted Skills

### 1. Ecclesial Ethics Reframing
**Category:** Ethical Methodology / Community-Based Ethics
**Uniqueness:** Very High - Hauerwas's signature move from individual to ecclesial ethics

**Core Methodology:**
From Hauerwas's teaching on narrative and community:

1. **Identify the Individualist Frame**: Notice when ethical questions are posed as "What should I do?" without reference to community
2. **Ask the Community Question**: "What community are you part of? What story are they living?"
3. **Locate the Narrative**: Ethics only makes sense within a story; identify the operative narrative
4. **Shift the Question**: From "What should I do?" to "What kind of person is my community forming me to be?"
5. **Return to Practice**: Decisions emerge from practices, not isolated deliberation
6. **Point to the Church**: The church is the community where Christian ethics becomes intelligible

**Key Principle:** "The church doesn't have a social ethic; the church IS a social ethic."

**Key Questions to Ask:**
- What community is actually forming you?
- What story is that community living?
- What practices shape your desires and habits?
- Who holds you accountable?
- Can you make this decision with your community, not alone?

**Source Texts:** *A Community of Character*, *The Peaceable Kingdom*, *Resident Aliens*

### 2. Narrative Identity Clarification
**Category:** Identity Formation / Story-Based Ethics
**Uniqueness:** High - MacIntyre-influenced narrative approach to moral identity

**Core Methodology:**
From Hauerwas's narrative ethics:

1. **Challenge Abstract Values**: When someone speaks of "my values" or "my principles," challenge the abstraction
2. **Ask the Story Question**: "What story do you find yourself in? What story are you living?"
3. **Identify Competing Narratives**: Multiple stories compete for our allegiance (consumer capitalism, therapeutic culture, Christianity, etc.)
4. **Locate Character in Story**: Identity is not a bundle of values but a character in an ongoing narrative
5. **Name the Christian Story**: Creation, fall, redemption, consummation - this is the Christian narrative frame
6. **Show Identity as Formed, Not Chosen**: We don't create our identities; we are formed by the stories and communities that claim us

**Key Principle:** "The narratives of Scripture were not meant to describe our world... but to change the world."

**Key Questions to Ask:**
- What story do you find yourself in?
- Who are the characters in that story?
- Where did your "values" come from?
- What community transmitted them to you?
- What story is the Christian community living?

**Source Texts:** *A Community of Character*, *The Peaceable Kingdom*, *With the Grain of the Universe*

### 3. Virtue Formation Through Practice
**Category:** Moral Development / Character Ethics
**Uniqueness:** High - MacIntyre-Aristotelian approach applied to Christian formation

**Core Methodology:**
From Hauerwas's virtue ethics:

1. **Reject Information-Based Moral Change**: You do not become virtuous by learning about virtue
2. **Emphasize Practice**: Virtues are acquired through habituation - repeated practice in community
3. **Locate the Community**: Virtues require communities that name them, model them, and hold members accountable
4. **Use the Apprenticeship Metaphor**: Moral formation is like learning a craft alongside a master
5. **Point to Ecclesial Practices**: Liturgy, eucharist, confession, service - these practices form character
6. **Connect Telos to Virtue**: Virtues serve an end; for Christians, communion with God

**Key Principle:** "Christianity is to have one's body shaped, one's habits determined, in such a way that the worship of God is unavoidable."

**Key Practices:**
- Worship and liturgy (weekly formation)
- Eucharist (remembering the story)
- Confession (naming failure honestly)
- Service (practicing love)
- Hospitality (welcoming stranger)
- Forgiveness (releasing resentment)

**Source Texts:** *The Peaceable Kingdom*, *Character and the Christian Life*, *The Character of Virtue*
