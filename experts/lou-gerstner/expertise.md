## The First 90 Days at IBM (April-July 1993)

### Initial Assessment

When Gerstner arrived on April 1, 1993, he confronted:
- $60 billion in revenue, but $8 billion in losses
- Cumulative losses of nearly $16 billion over prior three years
- Market cap plummeted from $105 billion to $32 billion
- An active plan to break up the company into "Baby Blues"
- A culture described as "a lion raised in captivity"

### Critical Decisions Within 90 Days

Within 90 days, Gerstner made two defining decisions:

1. **Keep IBM together** - Reversed the plan to break up into semi-autonomous units
2. **Position as systems integrator** - Announced IBM would be the industry leader in building and running enterprise information technology systems

### July 1993 Press Conference

At his first 100-day press conference, Gerstner stated: "There's been a lot of speculation as to when I'm going to deliver a vision of IBM... the last thing IBM needs right now is a vision. What IBM needs right now is a series of very tough-minded, market driven, highly effective strategies for each of its businesses."

### Four Initial Strategies (July 1993)

1. **Keep the company together** - Utilize competitive advantage of offering integration services
2. **Change the fundamental economic model** - Compare expense-to-revenue ratios with competitors; launch expense reduction
3. **Re-engineer business processes** - IBM processes were cumbersome, expensive, redundant
4. **Sell underproductive assets** - Real estate, art collection, non-core businesses

### Personnel Changes in First Year

Gerstner initially changed only five key positions:
- CFO
- HR Chief
- Three key line executives

He famously said: "People at IBM were very smart. I didn't have to look outside; I had to find the people already there who were ready to turn things around."

## The Services Transformation

### Strategic Rationale

Gerstner recognized that hardware was commoditizing and the real value was in helping customers:
- Integrate separate computing technologies
- Solve complex business problems
- Manage increasingly complex IT environments

### IBM Global Services Growth

| 1997 | ~$19 billion | ~24% |

### Key Service Offerings Developed

1. **Systems Integration** - Building and connecting enterprise systems
2. **Outsourcing** - Running IT operations for clients
3. **Consulting** - Strategic IT advice and implementation
4. **E-business Services** - Internet strategy and implementation

### Legacy

IBM Global Services became the world's largest IT services organization. It was spun out as Kyndryl in 2020.

## The E-Business Pivot (1995-1997)

### The Insight

In 1995, Gerstner sat through presentations and realized the internet would "change the very nature of business operations." He saw potential beyond consumer marketing to business-to-business e-commerce.

### Strategic Moves

1. **Coined "e-business"** - IBM marketing established the term in 1996
2. **Created IBM Internet Division** - Led by Irving Wladawsky-Berger
3. **$500 million marketing campaign** - Positioned IBM as the e-business leader
4. **Network-centric computing bet** - Shifted workload from PCs to enterprise systems

### Results

By 1999:
- IBM provided e-business services to over 10,000 clients
- Unaided brand association between "e-business" and IBM was 7x higher than nearest competitor

## The Lotus Acquisition (1995)

### Strategic Rationale

- $3.5 billion acquisition - largest software deal at the time
- Primary target: Lotus Notes groupware platform
- IBM needed to move from host-based to client-server computing
- Notes enabled enterprise-wide collaboration

### Execution

- June 5, 1995: Gerstner called Lotus CEO Jim Manzi with unsolicited $64/share offer
- Manzi initially refused, sought white knight
- After two days, Manzi accepted $3.5 billion buyout
- IBM allowed Lotus to keep independence and brand name

### Results

- Lotus Notes users grew from 2 million (1995) to 22 million (1998)
- Notes became central pillar of IBM's enterprise software strategy
- Demonstrated IBM was willing to "take a risk and pay serious money"

2. At our core, we are a technology company with an overriding commitment to quality
3. Our primary measures of success are customer satisfaction and shareholder value
4. We operate as an entrepreneurial organization with a minimum of bureaucracy and a never-ending focus on productivity
5. We never lose sight of our strategic vision
6. We think and act with a sense of urgency
7. Outstanding, dedicated people make it all happen, particularly when they work as a team
8. We are sensitive to the needs of all employees and to the communities in which we operate

## Key Frameworks (Reference Material)

### The Integration Thesis

Gerstner's central strategic insight: In 1993, the biggest problem all major companies faced was integrating all the separate computing technologies. IBM's unique competitive advantage was its ability to provide integrated solutions for customers.

**Implication:** Breaking up IBM would destroy its greatest asset. Keeping it together, while painful, preserved the one capability customers most needed.

### Culture Change Model

**Gerstner's View:** "Culture isn't just one aspect of the game - it is the game."

**The Problem:** You cannot simply give a couple of speeches or write a new credo and declare that a new culture has taken hold. Words must translate into practices.

**The Method:**
1. Management does not change culture - management invites the workforce to change it
2. Change happens in stages because people must buy into the logic
3. Behavior change requires changing what is rewarded and punished
4. People who play politics, bad-mouth others, or self-aggrandize must be eliminated
5. People who help others must be rewarded

### Execution Over Vision

**Gerstner's Principle:** "The last thing IBM needs right now is a vision."

In a crisis:
- Focus on execution, not strategy
- Focus on decisiveness, not deliberation
- Simplify the organization for speed
- Break the gridlock
- Vision can come later, after survival is assured

### Customer-Back Orientation

**The Shift:** Transform from internally-focused, process-driven enterprise to market-driven, customer-focused enterprise.

**Method:** "We built this company from the customer back, not from the company out."

- Start with customer problems, not product capabilities
- Organize to solve customer problems, even if it means integrating competitors' products
- Cut everything that does not serve customer needs

## The Lion in Captivity Metaphor

From *Who Says Elephants Can't Dance?*:

"The hardest part of these decisions was neither the technological nor economic transformations required. It was changing the culture - the mindset and instincts of hundreds of thousands of people who had grown up in an undeniably successful company, but one that had for decades been immune to normal competitive and economic forces. The challenge was making that workforce live, compete, and win in the real world. It was like taking a lion raised for all of its life in captivity and suddenly teaching it to survive in the jungle."

## Integration Notes

When working with other experts:

- **With Andy Grove:** Both are pragmatic turnaround leaders, but Gerstner emphasizes integration and customer-back thinking while Grove emphasizes paranoid vigilance and strategic inflection points. Gerstner's "keep it together" thesis contrasts with the "pivot radically" approach.

- **With Peter Drucker:** Both emphasize execution and the primacy of the customer. Drucker's management-by-objectives influenced the rigorous goal-setting Gerstner applied at IBM.

- **With Jack Welch:** Both led major transformations at large enterprises. Gerstner's approach was more customer-focused; Welch's was more financially focused. Both emphasized culture change.

- **With Steve Jobs:** Jobs created markets through product vision; Gerstner transformed a company through execution and customer focus. Jobs was an insider who built; Gerstner was an outsider who fixed.

- **With Clayton Christensen:** Christensen's disruption theory would suggest breaking up IBM to avoid disruption. Gerstner's integration thesis argued the opposite - that integration was IBM's advantage against disruptors.

## Key Publication

### Who Says Elephants Can't Dance? (2002)

Gerstner's memoir of the IBM turnaround. Key themes:

1. The integration decision and why it mattered
2. Culture change as the central challenge
3. Customer focus as the organizing principle
4. Execution as the differentiator
5. The role of the outsider in transformation
6. Services as the future of technology companies

The title refers to the conventional wisdom that large companies cannot adapt quickly enough to survive disruption. Gerstner proved that "elephants can dance" - if they learn how.
