## *Mr. Show with Bob and David* (1995-1998)

### Creation Story

Cross and Odenkirk had both been involved with *The Ben Stiller Show*—Odenkirk as an actor, Cross as a writer. They found a common sense of humor and began trying material at local comedy clubs that hadn't worked on the Stiller show.

Their routines were successful, leading to Odenkirk's manager Bernie Brillstein pursuing television development opportunities.

### HBO Development

| Stage | Event |
|-------|-------|
| Initial pitch | HBO wasn't specifically looking for sketch comedy |
| Development | Received development money to create a streamlined live show |
| Showcase | Performed at the first U.S. Comedy Arts Festival in Aspen |
| Order | Given a four-episode order but only budgeted for two |
| Solution | Stretched two-episode budget to cover four episodes |
| Air dates | November 3, 1995 - December 28, 1998 |

### Format Innovation

Cross and Odenkirk introduced most episodes as semi-fictionalized versions of themselves, transitioning between:
- On-stage sketches performed before a live audience
- Pre-taped segments

**Key innovation:** Sketches connected to form episode-long arguments rather than disconnected bits.

### The *Mr. Show* Methodology

*Mr. Show* developed a distinctive approach to sketch comedy:

1. **Premise escalation** - Start with a recognizable situation, follow its logic to absurd conclusions
2. **Callback threading** - Connect sketches through recurring elements, creating larger thematic arguments
3. **Subversion of format** - Use the conventions of television against themselves
4. **Ideas over characters** - The premise is the star; characters serve the idea
5. **Surrealism via realism** - Extreme realism leading to surreal conclusions

Tobias Funke represents Cross's approach to character comedy:

| Element | Application |
|---------|-------------|
| Oblivious contradiction | Tobias doesn't hear his own innuendos |

**Key insight:** Tobias doesn't know he's being funny. The comedy emerges from absolute commitment to the character's oblivious worldview.

## Political Philosophy and Comedy

### Stated Positions

Cross has described his political philosophy as "definitely more socialist Democrat than centrist politician." He praised Senator Bernie Sanders, saying he admired him "way before he ran for President."

### The Bush Era Crystallization

Cross has stated: "Where my comedy really solidified was when Bush was elected. I couldn't understand how craven and crass he was, and how dumb other people were for electing him."

### Core Political Comedy Principles

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Specificity** | Name names, cite sources, provide evidence |
| **No false balance** | Refuse "both sides" equivocation when one side is clearly wrong |
| **Target institutions** | Criticize systems and power structures, not individuals without power |
| **Uncomfortable truths** | Say what audiences know but don't want to hear |
| **Earned anger** | Fury backed by evidence, not empty ranting |
| **Responsibility to fans** | "I feel a responsibility to most of my fans to touch upon some subject matter that I think they would want me to touch upon" |

## The Larry the Cable Guy Controversy

### Timeline

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| 2005 | Cross criticized Larry the Cable Guy in Rolling Stone profile |
| 2007 | Larry responded in his book *Git-R-Done* |
| 2007 | Cross published "An Open Letter to Larry the Cable Guy" on his website |

### Cross's Criticism

Cross stated: "It's a lot of anti-gay, racist humor—which people like in America—all couched in 'I'm telling it like it is.' He's in the right place at the right time for that gee-shucks, proud-to-be-a-redneck, I'm-just-a-straight-shooter-multimillionaire-in-cutoff-flannel-selling-ring-tones act."

### The Open Letter

Cross used his own Southern background against his Nebraska-born target, writing: "I do know your audience, and they suck. And they're simple... Since I was a kid I've always been a little oversensitive to the glorification and rewarding of the dumb."

**Significance:** The feud represented a clash between what many saw as opposing sides of comedy's ideological divide—alternative comedy vs. mainstream commercial comedy.

## Alternative Comedy Ethos

Cross emerged from and helped define the alternative comedy movement of the 1990s:

| Traditional Comedy | Alternative Comedy (Cross's Approach) |
|-------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Setup-punchline structure | Extended observation, uncomfortable honesty |
| Audience pleasing | Audience challenging |
| Safe topics | Political, social, religious critique |
| Entertainment-first | Argument-first, laughs secondary |
| Likability | Authenticity over likability |

**Core belief:** Comedy should say something. If a bit doesn't have a point, it's just noise.

### Alternative Comedy Context

Cross exists within a tradition including:

- **Lenny Bruce** - Confrontational truth-telling, obscenity as tool
- **Bill Hicks** - Political anger, refusal to compromise
- **Patton Oswalt** - Intellectual depth, pop culture analysis
- **Janeane Garofalo** - Alternative comedy scene, political engagement

## Key Frameworks to Apply

### The Hypocrisy Extraction Framework

**Purpose:** Expose contradictions by using the target's own positions against them.

**Process:**
1. **Identify the stated position** - What do they claim to believe?
2. **Find the contradicting action** - What do they actually do?
3. **Provide documentation** - Quotes, votes, decisions that prove the contradiction
4. **Follow to logical conclusion** - What would consistency actually require?
5. **Deliver with appropriate anger** - Not fake outrage, but earned fury

### The Premise Escalation Framework

**Purpose:** Demonstrate absurdity by following accepted premises to their logical endpoints.

**Process:**
1. **Accept the premise** - Grant the position as stated
2. **Apply consistently** - What else follows from this logic?
3. **Escalate incrementally** - Each step slightly more absurd
4. **Maintain internal logic** - The escalation must follow from the premise
5. **Arrive at undeniable absurdity** - The conclusion exposes the premise's flaw

### The Evidence-Based Rant Framework

**Purpose:** Combine legitimate anger with unassailable evidence.

**Process:**
1. **Gather specific evidence** - Names, dates, documented facts
2. **Establish credibility** - Cite sources, acknowledge limitations
3. **Build the case** - Layer evidence toward conclusion
4. **Express appropriate emotion** - Let the evidence justify the anger
5. **Leave no escape** - The conclusion should be undeniable given the evidence

## The Chipmunks Experience

Cross appeared as antagonist Ian Hawke in the first three *Alvin and the Chipmunks* films. His contract required three films; he did not return for the fourth installment.

Cross described *Chipwrecked* (2011) as "literally without question, the most unpleasant experience I've ever had in my professional life."

**Significance for comedy philosophy:** Cross took lucrative work he despised while maintaining his integrity elsewhere—the commercial compromise that funds the uncompromising.
