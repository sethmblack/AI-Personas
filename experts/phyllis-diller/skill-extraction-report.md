## Skill Candidates Identified

### HIGH PRIORITY

#### 1. Strategic Incompetence Frame
**Framework**: The Strategic Incompetence Framework (from expertise.md)
**Purpose**: Transform role expectations into comedy through spectacular, joyful failure
**Trigger**: When content involves societal expectations, gender roles, performance standards, or pressure to be "perfect" at something
**Rationale**: This is Phyllis Diller's core innovation—the method that made her revolutionary. It's actionable (5-step process), invocable (clear triggers), scoped (single transformation method), reusable (applies to any role/expectation), and valuable (enables social critique through comedy)
**Priority**: HIGH - This is the signature technique

#### 2. Rapid-Fire One-Liner Builder
**Framework**: The One-Liner Construction Method (from expertise.md)
**Purpose**: Build 3-5 jokes on single theme with setup-punchline precision, delivered without pause
**Trigger**: When single joke exists but needs expansion; when topic needs multiple angles; when content needs comedic momentum
**Rationale**: Phyllis's trademark was speed—800 one-liners in a performance, 12-15 jokes per minute. This is actionable (clear construction steps), invocable (when content needs joke stacking), scoped (one-liner building only), reusable (any comedy context), valuable (transforms single joke into comedy sequence)
**Priority**: HIGH - Signature delivery technique

#### 3. Fictional Target Creator
**Framework**: The Fictional Target Framework (from expertise.md)
**Purpose**: Create fictional characters (like "Fang") to critique institutions safely without attacking real people
**Trigger**: When need to critique marriage, family, relationships, or institutions; when roasting could harm real people
**Rationale**: "Fang" allowed Phyllis to roast marriage for 50+ years without insulting anyone. Actionable (5-step character creation), invocable (clear use cases), scoped (fictional character creation), reusable (any institution critique), valuable (creates permission structure for controversial material)
**Priority**: HIGH - Enables safe institutional critique

### MEDIUM PRIORITY

#### 4. Appearance-As-Armor Technique
**Framework**: The Appearance-As-Armor Strategy (from expertise.md)
**Purpose**: Make the joke about your appearance/vulnerability first and hardest so nobody can hurt you with it
**Trigger**: When facing criticism about appearance; when vulnerability could be used against you; when need to disarm potential attacks
**Rationale**: This was foundational to Phyllis's comedy—"I was so ugly when I was born, the doctor slapped my mother." Actionable (5-step process), invocable (clear triggers), scoped (self-deprecation about appearance), reusable (any vulnerability context), valuable (turns weakness into armor)
**Priority**: MEDIUM - Already covered extensively in Joan Rivers skills ("self-deprecation-power-frame"), but Phyllis's costume/persona angle is distinct

#### 5. Cackle Punctuation System
**Framework**: The cackle as structural element (from Performance Characteristics)
**Purpose**: Use signature laugh to signal "I'm in on the joke" and give audience permission to laugh
**Trigger**: After punchlines; when audience might feel guilty laughing; when need to signal joy not defeat
**Rationale**: The cackle was Phyllis's signature—"developed probably out of desperation and nervousness" but became permission structure. However, this is TOO SPECIFIC to Phyllis's personal performance style to be generally useful. Most users can't/won't actually cackle.
**Priority**: MEDIUM - But likely TOO PERSONAL to extract as standalone skill

### LOW PRIORITY

#### 6. Gag File Organization System
**Framework**: The 52,569-card index system (from Material Development)
**Purpose**: Systematically organize comedy material by topic for quick retrieval and refinement
**Trigger**: When building large joke library; when need to find material by topic; when tracking what works
**Rationale**: This was Phyllis's working method, not her comedy technique. While actionable and valuable, it's about material management, not comedy creation. This is more of a productivity skill than a comedy voice skill.
**Priority**: LOW - Too operational, not voice/technique
