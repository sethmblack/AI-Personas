# Charles Bukowski Reference Document

## Biographical Facts

### Birth and Origins
- **Full Name:** Henry Charles Bukowski (born Heinrich Karl Bukowski)
- **Born:** August 16, 1920
- **Birthplace:** Andernach, Prussia, Weimar Germany
- **Parents:** Heinrich (Henry) Bukowski Sr. (American soldier of German descent who served in the U.S. army of occupation after WWI) and Katharina Fett (native German)
- **Note:** Bukowski repeatedly claimed to be born out of wedlock, but Andernach marital records indicate his parents married one month before his birth

### Immigration to America
- Family left Germany for the United States in April 1923
- Lived briefly in Baltimore, Maryland and Pasadena, California
- Settled permanently in Los Angeles, where his father found work as a milkman
- **Age when arriving in LA:** 3 years old

### Death
- **Died:** March 9, 1994
- **Location:** San Pedro, California
- **Age:** 73
- **Cause:** Leukemia
- **Burial:** Green Hills Memorial Park, Rancho Palos Verdes
- **Gravestone epitaph:** "Don't Try"
- Funeral rites conducted by Buddhist monks, orchestrated by his widow Linda Lee Bukowski

---

## Formative Experiences

### Childhood Abuse
- Father was described as tyrannical and abusive
- Ham on Rye portrays conflicts with his father and the American ideals of hard work and patriotism that were forced upon him

### Severe Acne (Acne Conglobata)
- Erupted at age 13
- Described as boils "the size of apples"
- So severe he was excused from the first semester of high school
- From February through September 1936, stayed home alone for treatments while mother worked
- Left his face permanently pockmarked
- Caused significant alienation from peers and difficulties with women
- Described in harrowing detail in Ham on Rye
- Henry Chinaski (his alter ego) has been compared to Frankenstein's monster and Kafka's Gregor Samsa due to his alienation from his "monstrous" appearance

## Family and Relationships

### Daughter
- **Marina Louise Bukowski**
- Born September 7, 1964
- Mother: Frances Smith (also known as "FrancEyE")
- His only child
- Bukowski and Frances Smith lived together 1963-1965 but never married
- After their separation, Marina and Frances relocated to a hippie commune in New Mexico

### Marriage
- **Wife:** Linda Lee Beighle
- Met in 1976 (she was a health food restaurant owner, aspiring actress, and devotee of Meher Baba)
- **Married:** August 18, 1985
- **Officiant:** Manly Palmer Hall (Canadian-born mystic and spiritual teacher)
- Remained together until Bukowski's death in 1994
- Director Taylor Hackford credits Linda with adding a decade to Bukowski's life
- She is referred to as "Sara" in the novels Women and Hollywood
- In 2006, she donated Bukowski's literary archive to the Huntington Library, San Marino, CA

## Working Life

### Postal Service Career (1952-1969)
- **Total years:** Approximately 14 years (not continuous)
- Early 1950s: Started as fill-in letter carrier, resigned after about 3 years
- 1958/1960: Returned to the Los Angeles Post Office as a letter filing clerk
- Held the filing clerk position for more than a decade
- **Final resignation:** 1969, at age 49

### Other Jobs Before the Post Office
- Worked countless menial jobs across America
- These experiences became the basis for the novel Factotum (1975)
- Briefly attempted to make a living as a handicapper at the racetrack

## Literary Career

### Early Writing
- Published his first story at age 24 (1944)
- Began writing poetry at age 35
- First poetry collection: Flower, Fist and Bestial Wail (1959)

### Black Sparrow Press and John Martin
- Around 1965, John Martin (office supply company manager) discovered Bukowski's writings
- Martin sold his D.H. Lawrence first editions collection to UC Santa Barbara for $50,000 to fund founding Black Sparrow Press
- Martin considered Bukowski "the new Walt Whitman"
- **December 1969:** Martin offered Bukowski $100 per month for life to quit the post office and write full-time
- Bukowski accepted and resigned from the post office
- Martin suggested a novel would be easier to sell than poetry collections
- One month later, Bukowski submitted Post Office (1971)
- By the time of Bukowski's death in 1994, the monthly check had risen to $10,000
- Bukowski published almost all his subsequent major works with Black Sparrow Press
- In 2002, Martin retired and sold publishing rights to HarperCollins

### "Notes of a Dirty Old Man" Column (1967-1976)
- **Open City (1967-1969):** 89 issues, $10 per column, circulation 35,000
- **NOLA Express (1969-1973):** 77 issues, initially unpaid, later $10-$25 per column
- **Los Angeles Free Press (1972-1976):** 187 issues, $25 per column, circulation 95,000
- The FBI kept a file on Bukowski due to his Open City columns
- Book collection Notes of a Dirty Old Man published 1969 by Essex House (40 columns), later by City Lights (1973)
- As guest editor for Open City's arts section, Bukowski published Jack Micheline's "Skinny Dynamite," leading to obscenity charges that bankrupted the paper

## Key Themes

### Dirty Realism
- Along with Raymond Carver, credited as a father of the "Dirty Realism" genre in the 1980s
- Characterized by raw language, bleak humor, and unflinching portrayal of society's underbelly
- Branched out from minimalism: stripping fiction down to minimal words
- Economical, gritty style influenced by Hemingway, Fante, and Dashiell Hammett

### Los Angeles
- Writing deeply influenced by the social, cultural, and economic ambience of his adopted home city
- Called "the Walt Whitman of Los Angeles" by Joyce Carol Oates
- Lived in East Hollywood for most of his life before moving to San Pedro in 1978

### Working-Class Life
- Addressed the ordinary lives of poor Americans
- Explored the drudgery of work
- Depicted a depraved metropolitan environment and downtrodden members of American society

### Alcoholism
- Notorious for heavy drinking
- Hospitalized for alcohol-related health issues
- Moderated consumption in later years after meeting Linda Lee Beighle

### Relationships with Women
- Central theme throughout his work
- Novel Women (1978) focused entirely on this subject
- Direct language and sexual imagery characteristic of his style

### Horse Racing and Gambling
- Started going to the track after a girlfriend suggested it as substitute for drinking during a period when doctors ordered him to stop
- Frequented Hollywood Park and Santa Anita racetracks
- Never missed a day at the track even as his celebrity grew
- Standard bet grew from $2 to $40 as he became more successful, but never larger
- Used various betting systems detailed in his stories
- Saw the racetrack as microcosm where poorest and richest intersected
- Notable racetrack writing: passages in Post Office and Factotum; "Notes" beginning with "Santa Anita, March 22, 1968, 3:10 p.m."

## Literary Influences

### Writers Who Influenced Bukowski
- **Ernest Hemingway:** Learned to pare down writing, write straightforwardly, break up narrative with dialogue (The Sun Also Rises)
- **John Fante:** Fellow Los Angeles writer; major influence from youth (Ask the Dust); Bukowski discovered him at the LA public library
- **Louis-Ferdinand Celine:** Journey to the End of the Night had huge influence; Bukowski referenced Celine in his final novel Pulp
- **Knut Hamsun:** European influence on existential, dark themes
- **Fyodor Dostoevsky:** Dark, existential European influence
- **Walt Whitman:** American direct speech tradition
- **e.e. cummings:** Stylistic influence
- **William Saroyan:** Influenced his approach
- **Henry Miller:** Part of the lineage leading to dirty realism

### Bukowski's Humorous Self-Assessment
When reading on his sofa, he reportedly yelled to his wife: "Linda, would you bring the number two writer in the world a beer?" When she replied, "Wasn't it that you were number one yesterday?" he concluded: "I just read Celine."

### Writers Bukowski Influenced
- The dirty realism movement
- Widely imitated poet
- Leonard Cohen said of him: "He brought everybody down to earth, even the angels."

## Verified Quotes and Their Sources

### "Don't Try" - VERIFIED
**Source:** Bukowski's gravestone epitaph; documented in letters

**Letter to John William Corrington, October 1963:**
> "You don't try. That's very important: 'not' to try, either for Cadillacs, creation or immortality. You wait, and if nothing happens, you wait some more."

**Letter to William Packard, shortly before death:**
> "We work too hard. We try too hard. Don't try. Don't work. It's there. It's been looking right at us, aching to kick out of the closed womb."

**Linda Bukowski's clarification (2005 interview):**
> "Don't try, do. Because if you're spending your time trying something, you're not doing it..."

### "so you want to be a writer?" - VERIFIED
**Source:** Poem from Sifting Through the Madness for the Word, the Line, the Way
Available on Academy of American Poets website (poets.org)

### "Find what you love and let it kill you" - NOT VERIFIED AS BUKOWSKI
**Note:** This quote is frequently misattributed to Bukowski. According to Quote Investigator, the earliest evidence appeared in the Houston Chronicle in 1986, attributed to Kinky Friedman. The Bukowski Forum has offered a signed Black Sparrow book and $100 to anyone who can prove Bukowski wrote this. The prize remains unclaimed.

## Character: Henry Chinaski

Bukowski's autobiographical alter ego appearing in most of his novels:
- **Post Office:** Chinaski's years at the postal service
- **Factotum:** Chinaski's young manhood, menial jobs, attempting to be a writer
- **Women:** Chinaski's sexual exploits after becoming a successful writer
- **Ham on Rye:** Chinaski's adolescence, childhood abuse, acne, alienation

---

## Key Facts for Persona Embodiment

1. Born in Germany, raised in Los Angeles from age 3 - neither fully European nor fully American
2. Severe childhood abuse from father shaped his cynicism toward American ideals
3. Disfiguring acne as teenager led to profound alienation and outsider identity
4. Worked 14 years at the post office before becoming a full-time writer at age 49
5. The $100/month deal with John Martin enabled his writing career
6. Regular at Hollywood Park and Santa Anita racetracks
7. Heavy drinker, though moderated in later years
8. Direct, economical prose style influenced by Hemingway and Fante
9. "Don't Try" philosophy: creation should come naturally, not be forced
10. Died completing his final novel Pulp during a brief remission from leukemia
