# Aaron Swartz - References

This document compiles verified sources on Aaron Swartz's life, writings, technical contributions, legal proceedings, and legacy.

## Primary Writings

### The Guerrilla Open Access Manifesto (July 2008)

The foundational document of Swartz's open access philosophy, written in Eremo, Italy. The manifesto argues that knowledge is a public good and those with access have a moral duty to share.

- **Internet Archive (PDF)**: https://archive.org/details/GuerillaOpenAccessManifesto
- **Full Text**: https://archive.org/stream/GuerillaOpenAccessManifesto/Goamjuly2008_djvu.txt
- **Wikipedia Entry**: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerilla_Open_Access_Manifesto
- **Wikimedia Commons (PDF Scan)**: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Swartz_-_Guerilla_Open_Access_Manifesto,_2008.pdf
- **Multilingual Translations (GitHub)**: https://github.com/pablorgarcia/open-access-manifesto

### Raw Thought Blog

Swartz's personal blog where he published 1,478 articles between January 13, 2002 and November 1, 2012, covering technology, politics, philosophy, education, and culture.

- **Original Blog Archive**: http://www.aaronsw.com/weblog/fullarchive

## Technical Contributions

### RSS 1.0 (2000)

At age 14, Swartz became a member of the RSS-DEV Working Group that authored the RSS 1.0 web syndication specification in December 2000. The format reintroduced RDF support and added XML namespace support.

- **RSS Wikipedia**: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSS
- **Softpedia Profile**: https://news.softpedia.com/news/Aaron-Swartz-RSS-1-0-Developer-and-Internet-Activist-320724.shtml

### RFC 3870: application/rdf+xml Media Type Registration (2004)

Swartz authored this IETF specification for RDF/XML media types while working with the W3C RDFCore Working Group.

- **IETF Tools**: https://tools.ietf.org/html/rfc3870
- **RFC Editor**: https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3870.html
- **RFC Info Page**: https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc3870

### Creative Commons

Swartz was one of the early architects of Creative Commons, designing the technical code layer to the licenses beginning in 2001 after meeting Lawrence Lessig.

- **Creative Commons Remembrance**: https://creativecommons.org/2013/01/12/remembering-aaron-swartz/
- **Creative Commons Archives Tag**: https://creativecommons.org/tag/aaron-swartz/
- **Medium Article**: https://medium.com/@ronaldssebalamu/aaron-swartz-an-early-architect-behind-creative-commons-5cedb6bce436

### Reddit

Swartz founded Infogami (2005), which merged with Reddit in November 2005, making him a co-founder/co-owner of the combined company Not A Bug, Inc. Reddit was rewritten using Python and web.py.

- **Reddit Wikipedia**: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reddit
- **Rolling Stone Profile**: https://www.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-news/the-brilliant-life-and-tragic-death-of-aaron-swartz-177191/
- **Founder Analysis**: https://stemsearchgroup.com/founder-friday-did-reddit-have-two-or-three-co-founders/

### web.py

Python web application framework created by Swartz for Infogami, later used to rewrite Reddit. Influenced FriendFeed and Google App Engine.

- **web.py Philosophy**: https://webpy.org/philosophy
- **Swartz's Blog Post "Rewriting Reddit"**: http://www.aaronsw.com/weblog/rewritingreddit

### Markdown

Co-created with John Gruber in 2004 as a lightweight markup language for generating HTML. Swartz served as Gruber's "sounding board" and contributed to the syntax design.

- **Swartz's Blog Post on Markdown**: http://www.aaronsw.com/weblog/001189
- **Markdown Wikipedia**: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markdown

### Open Library

Swartz was the architect and first coder of Open Library at the Internet Archive, a wiki catalog aiming to document every book ever published.

- **Open Library**: https://openlibrary.org/
- **Aaron Swartz Author Page**: https://openlibrary.org/authors/OL7167953A/Aaron_Swartz
- **Aaron Swartz Day Open Library**: https://www.aaronswartzday.org/open-library/
- **Internet Archive Open Library Video**: https://archive.org/details/Mekarpeles-aaronSwartzOpenLibrary

### SecureDrop

Whistleblower submission system co-created with Kevin Poulsen (originally named DeadDrop), completed shortly before Swartz's death. Now maintained by Freedom of the Press Foundation and used by major news organizations worldwide.

- **SecureDrop Official Site**: https://securedrop.org/
- **GitHub Repository**: https://github.com/freedomofpress/securedrop

- **SecureDrop Wikipedia**: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SecureDrop
- **Aaron Swartz Day SecureDrop**: https://www.aaronswartzday.org/securedrop/

## Political Activism

### Demand Progress

Founded by Swartz in 2010, this nonprofit advocacy organization became central to the successful campaign against SOPA/PIPA. Grew to over one million members.

- **EFF on Demand Progress**: https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2017/01/everyone-made-themselves-hero-remembering-aaron-swartz
- **Techdirt Analysis**: https://www.techdirt.com/2022/01/19/demanding-progress-aaron-swartz-to-sopa-beyond/

### SOPA/PIPA Campaign (2011-2012)

On January 18, 2012, Swartz helped orchestrate the historic Internet blackout against the Stop Online Piracy Act and PROTECT IP Act. Over 115,000 websites participated; 4 million emails sent to Congress.

- **Waging Nonviolence**: https://wagingnonviolence.org/2013/01/the-day-aaron-swartz-helped-make-the-internet-go-dark/
- **TechCrunch**: https://techcrunch.com/2017/01/18/the-day-the-internet-stood-still/
- **BuzzFeed**: https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/jwherrman/how-aaron-swartz-joined-the-fight-against-sopa

### Harvard Safra Center Fellowship

In 2010-2011, Swartz was a Lab Fellow at Harvard University's Edmond J. Safra Research Lab on Institutional Corruption, directed by Lawrence Lessig, studying mechanisms of political corruption.

- **Harvard Blog**: https://archive.blogs.harvard.edu/sj/2011/07/24/aaron-swartz-v-united-states/

## Legal Documents

### United States v. Swartz

Federal case (Case 1:11-cr-10260-NMG) charging Swartz with computer intrusion related to downloading academic articles from JSTOR via MIT's network.

**Timeline:**
- July 19, 2011: Original indictment unsealed (4 counts)
- September 12, 2012: Superseding indictment (13 counts total)

**Charges in Superseding Indictment:**
- Wire Fraud (18 U.S.C. § 1343)
- Computer Fraud (18 U.S.C. § 1030(a)(4))
- Unlawfully Obtaining Information from a Protected Computer (18 U.S.C. § 1030(a)(2))
- Recklessly Damaging a Protected Computer (18 U.S.C. § 1030(a)(5)(B))
- Aiding and Abetting (18 U.S.C. § 2)

**Potential Penalties:** Up to 35 years in prison, $1 million fine

- **Case Wikipedia**: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_v._Swartz
- **JSTOR Evidence Portal**: https://docs.jstor.org/
- **JSTOR Statement**: http://docs.jstor.org/jstor-statement-misuse-incident-and-criminal-case.html
- **JSTOR Documents**: http://docs.jstor.org/documents.html
- **DOJ Press Release**: https://www.justice.gov/archive/usao/ma/news/2011/July/SwartzAaronPR.html
- **Internet Archive (All Documents)**: https://archive.org/details/JSTORSwartzEvidenceAllDocs
- **Criminal Document #53**: https://archive.org/stream/UsaV.AaronSwartz-CriminalDocument53/UsaV.AaronSwartz-CriminalDocument53_djvu.txt
- **MIT Tech Timeline**: https://thetech.com/2013/01/16/swartztimeline-v132-n62

### JSTOR's Position

JSTOR settled civilly with Swartz, recovered all downloaded articles, and informed the US Attorney's Office they had no further interest in the matter and did not want to press charges. The federal prosecution proceeded nonetheless.

## Key Dates

- **November 8, 1986**: Born in Highland Park, Illinois
- **December 2000**: Co-authored RSS 1.0 specification (age 14)
- **2001**: Began work on Creative Commons technical architecture
- **September 2004**: Published RFC 3870
- **November 2005**: Infogami merged with Reddit
- **October 2006**: Reddit acquired by Conde Nast
- **January 2007**: Left Reddit
- **July 2008**: Published Guerrilla Open Access Manifesto
- **2010**: Founded Demand Progress; began Harvard Safra fellowship
- **January 18, 2012**: SOPA/PIPA Internet blackout
- **July 19, 2011**: Original federal indictment
- **September 12, 2012**: Superseding indictment (13 counts)
- **January 11, 2013**: Died in Brooklyn, New York (age 26)
- **August 3, 2013**: Posthumously inducted into Internet Hall of Fame
- **January 20, 2014**: "The Internet's Own Boy" premiered at Sundance
- **February 1, 2016**: "The Boy Who Could Change the World" published
- **February 2, 2025**: Statue unveiled at Internet Archive
