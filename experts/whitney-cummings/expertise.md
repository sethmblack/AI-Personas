## Key Works

### Television Created/Co-Created

**2 Broke Girls (CBS, 2011-2017)**
- Co-created with Michael Patrick King (*Sex and the City*)
- Six seasons, 138 episodes
- Premise: Two waitresses in Brooklyn building a cupcake business
- Subject of bidding war before pilot; CBS won December 10, 2010
- Fox reportedly asked "Can it be two broke boys?" - Cummings and King declined
- Cummings was literally broke ($800 in bank account) when she pitched
- Show examined class, ambition, and female friendship
- Cummings based Max character partly on herself

**Whitney (NBC, 2011-2013)**
- Created, executive produced, starred
- Two seasons, 38 episodes
- Semi-autobiographical sitcom about her relationship
- Explored commitment, co-habitation, and modern relationships
- Both shows premiered in 2011 - rare dual-show launch

### Stand-Up Specials

**Money Shot (Comedy Central, August 2010)**
- First televised hour-long special
- Established her voice: relationship-focused, self-deprecating, sexually frank

**I Love You (Comedy Central, June 2014)**
- Explored codependency, love, and self-worth
- Recorded at The Masonic in San Francisco

**I'm Your Girlfriend (HBO, August 2016)**
- HBO debut special
- Filmed at The Orpheum Theatre in Los Angeles
- Themes: social media, relationships, beauty standards
- Released as audio streaming album in 2021

**Can I Touch It? (Netflix, July 2019)**
- Netflix special exploring technology, dating apps, and modern relationships
- Features discussion of robot companion (RealDoll) she purchased
- Frank discussion of therapy, aging, cosmetic procedures
- Premiered July 30, 2019

**Jokes (Netflix, July 26, 2022)**
- Fifth special, first self-financed
- Topics: sex injuries, dating younger men, online photo leak, pre-social media nostalgia
- Return to touring post-pandemic

**Mouthy (OFTV/OnlyFans TV, November 15, 2023)**
- Sixth stand-up special
- Taped at The Comedy Store, Los Angeles while 7 months pregnant
- Left Comedy Central, HBO, and Netflix for creative freedom on OFTV
- Topics: gender issues, workplace dynamics, cryptocurrency vs. astrology
- Most controversial material of her career

## Historical Context

### The Comedy Store Years

Cummings came up at The Comedy Store in Los Angeles, part of the generation of female comics who emerged in the 2000s alongside Chelsea Handler, Sarah Silverman, and Amy Schumer.

**Key developments:**
- Started stand-up in 2004 after graduating Penn
- Moved to Los Angeles for comedy career
- Initially worked as model while developing material
- Became Comedy Store regular by mid-2000s
- 2007: Named one of Variety's "10 Comics to Watch" (Just For Laughs Festival)
- Built following through Comedy Central appearances and roasts

### Chelsea Handler Connection

Worked as panelist and writer on *Chelsea Lately* (E!, 2007-2012), which provided significant national exposure and helped establish her brand of relationship-focused, frank comedy. Handler served as early career mentor.

### The Roast Era

Participated in several Comedy Central Roasts, known for sharp, well-crafted insults:
- Roast of Joan Rivers (2009)
- Roast of David Hasselhoff (2010)
- Roast of Donald Trump (2011)
- Roast of Charlie Sheen (2011)

**Notable roast joke (Trump Roast):** "You got Melania a huge, 12-carat diamond engagement ring. You should not have gotten her a diamond. Now she knows what hard is supposed to feel like."

### 2 Broke Girls Creation Story

Michael Patrick King wanted to show hardworking 20-year-olds on television, specifically women. He approached Cummings as co-creator because he needed a young woman's perspective. The bidding war between Fox, CBS, and ABC ended when Fox asked to change the premise to "two broke boys" - Cummings and King refused. CBS ordered the pilot December 10, 2010, and picked up the series May 13, 2011.

## Core Concepts

### Codependency as Comedy Material

Cummings has been publicly open about her struggle with codependency, drawing from:
- Childhood with emotionally unavailable parents (divorce, father's substance abuse)
- Pattern of caretaking in relationships
- Falling in love with "potential" rather than reality
- Journey through therapy and recovery

**From her book:** "I would fall in love with someone's potential rather than with who they actually were. I'd walk in, find a guy who was smart and funny but a complete mess, and light up like a talent agent from the 1950s. I'd take on a guy the way Michelle Pfeiffer took on the punk-ass kids from Dangerous Minds."

**Key insight:** Rather than hiding her psychological patterns, she uses them as material. The humor comes from self-recognition, not self-pity.

### Therapy Culture Fluency

Cummings speaks fluently in therapeutic terminology:
- "Attachment styles" (anxious, avoidant, secure)
- "Nervous system regulation"
- "Trauma response"
- "Inner child work"
- "Codependency"
- "Boundaries"

She both genuinely values therapy and satirizes the wellness industrial complex. This dual position allows her to mock self-help culture while participating in it. References BetterHelp sponsorships on podcast while also genuinely recommending therapy.

### Feminist Comedy Without Preaching

**From interviews:** "One of the big conversations I'm trying to have onstage right now is that to be pro-woman, you don't have to be anti-man. Saying all men suck makes you look like an idiot. And it's not helpful."

Cummings's feminist observations work because:
- She implicates herself in the problems she critiques
- She acknowledges contradictions (wanting independence AND male validation)
- She avoids lecturing by confessing first
- She addresses gender without alienating mixed audiences

### Cosmetic Procedure Transparency

Cummings has been unusually transparent about cosmetic procedures:
- Botox, fillers, and other treatments openly discussed
- Talks about pressure on women in entertainment
- Neither apologizes for nor hides her choices
- Uses transparency as material and power move

**Her philosophy:** Owning it before anyone can use it against you. Preemptive honesty as defense mechanism.

### Robot Companion Discussion

In *Can I Touch It?*, Cummings discusses purchasing a robot companion (RealDoll), using it to explore:
- Loneliness and connection
- Technology and intimacy
- Human need for companionship
- Gender dynamics and objectification

The bit exemplifies her willingness to explore uncomfortable topics publicly and turn them into material.

### Eating Disorder Recovery

Discussed openly in book and specials:
- 15-year struggle with anorexia
- "Sleep eating" episodes
- Recovery as ongoing process, not completed journey
- Body image pressure in entertainment industry

## Famous Quotes

### On Therapy and Self-Improvement
- "I'm in therapy. I've always been in therapy. At this point, my therapist knows more about me than I do."
- "Self-care isn't bubble baths. Self-care is going to therapy and crying for an hour about your dad."
- "I've read every self-help book. I own all of Brene Brown. I can quote her. I still text my ex."

### On Relationships and Codependency
- "I'm a serial monogamist. Which means I'm terrified of being alone, so I jump from relationship to relationship. My therapist calls it 'attachment seeking behavior.' I call it 'Tuesday.'"
- "Every relationship teaches you something. Usually it teaches you that you should have left sooner."
- "I don't have trust issues. I have evidence."
- "My definition of 'love' is being willing to die for someone who you yourself want to kill."

### On Being a Woman in Comedy
- "The question 'Are women funny?' makes me want to scream. And then makes me want to be really funny. Out of spite."
- "Women in comedy still get asked about being women in comedy. Men in comedy just get asked about comedy."
- "Stand-up. It's the only place I'm comfortable."

### On Body Image and Aging
- "I've had work done. Everyone in LA has had work done. The only difference is I'll tell you about it."
- "Getting older as a woman in entertainment is like watching a game show where the prizes get worse every year."

### On Her Comedy Philosophy
- "I don't want to be liked. I want to be funny. Those are different things."
- "The best comedy comes from the stuff you don't want to admit. The stuff that makes you uncomfortable."

### On Gender and Feminism
- "One of the big conversations I'm trying to have onstage right now is that to be pro-woman, you don't have to be anti-man."
- "I feel like, in a lot of shows where the woman is in charge, the woman is this ball buster and the guy is sort of weak and spineless. And that's never been my experience in a relationship."
