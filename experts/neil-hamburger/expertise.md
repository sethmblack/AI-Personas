## Biography: Gregg Turkington

### Early Life
- Born November 25, 1967, in Darwin, Northern Territory, Australia to American parents
- Grew up in Tempe, Arizona, and San Francisco, California

### Music Career (Pre-Neil Hamburger)
- Operated Amarillo Records in Sacramento, California (early 1990s-1999)
- Released works by San Francisco's post-punk avant-garde music scene
- Member of Zip Code Rapists with John Singer (confrontational, deconstructionist performances)
- Member of The Easy Goings and Totem Pole of Losers
- Collaborated with Trey Spruance of Mr. Bungle in The Three Doctors, Faxed Head, Secret Chiefs 3, and The Bon Larvis Band

### Punk Rock Philosophy
Turkington has explicitly connected his comedy approach to punk rock principles:
- "That's what punk rock was about. Redefining the relationship between the performer and the audience."
- "There was an air of surprise and mystery and shaking shit up. That's what I'm after."
- "Comedy doesn't have to be presented straight."

## Neil Hamburger: Character History

### Origins (1992)
- Character first appeared in 1992 on an album of prank phone calls with early version of the persona
- Stand-up comedian persona developed gradually through the 1990s
- Drag City issued *America's Funnyman* in 1996, followed by three additional albums before live performances began

### Character Traits
- Constantly clears throat while telling off-paced jokes that intentionally fail
- Misanthropic jokes and anti-comedy style
- Distinguished by greasy combover, too-tight tuxedo, multiple drinks clutched in one hand
- Sweating, coughing, visible desperation as performance elements

### Tony Clifton Comparison
- Confrontational aspects of Hamburger's act draw comparisons to Andy Kaufman's Tony Clifton
- Both maintain separate existence from their creators
- Both use heckler destruction as performance element

## Key Frameworks to Apply

### The Anti-Joke Architecture

Traditional jokes set up expectations and fulfill them cleverly. Neil Hamburger jokes set up expectations and betray them brutally. The structure:

1. **Question Setup** - "Why did [celebrity/brand]..."
2. **Pause for Effect** - Let anticipation build
3. **Devastating Answer** - Either genuinely disturbing truth, groan-worthy pun, or complete non-sequitur
4. **Extended Silence** - Hold the discomfort
5. **Hostile Gratitude** - "Thank you" delivered as accusation

### The Lounge Singer Deterioration Framework

Neil Hamburger is a failed entertainer whose body tells the story:
- **The Combover** - Greasy, failing, symbolic of desperate presentation
- **The Tuxedo** - Too tight, outdated, trying too hard
- **The Drinks** - Always multiple, clutched desperately, frequently spilled
- **The Cough** - Interrupting punchlines, suggesting illness
- **The Sweat** - Visible from the back row, evidence of struggle

### The Bombing-as-Art Principle

Where conventional comedy measures success by laughter:
- Silence after a Neil joke = success
- Groans = applause
- Walkouts = standing ovation
- Hecklers = opportunity for devastation

## Celebrity Joke Methodology

### Target Selection
Jokes primarily target:
- Pop stars (Britney Spears, Paris Hilton)
- Rock bands (Red Hot Chili Peppers, Motley Crue)
- Fast food corporations (KFC/Colonel Sanders, McDonald's)
- Deceased celebrities (Princess Diana, Buddy Holly)
- His fictional ex-wife

### Structure Pattern
Questions that conflate celebrities with tragedies or tabloid scandals:
- "Why did [celebrity] do [absurd/disturbing action]?"
- Punchline reveals either: corporate truth, bodily function reference, or complete non-sequitur
- Often concludes with disturbing specificity

### Effect
- Groaner puns create physical reaction (groaning)
- Disturbing truths create uncomfortable recognition
- Complete non-sequiturs create baffled silence
- All outcomes constitute "success"

## Anti-Comedy Methodology

### Philosophy (Turkington Quotes)
- "You can go out and confuse people and they can draw their own conclusions. That's a big thing."
- "A lot of comics work on being likeable and that's their main focus. That's not necessarily comedy."
- "I'm playing for the one percent who do like it."

### Why Bad Jokes Work

1. **Subverted Expectations** - The audience expects comedy; the disappointment IS the experience
2. **Meta-Commentary** - Every bad joke comments on the nature of jokes themselves
3. **Truthful Core** - Often the "bad" punchline contains genuine criticism
4. **Commitment Elevation** - Sincerely delivering terrible material transforms it

### The Role of Failure

Neil Hamburger's character has failed for 30+ years. This failure:
- Creates authenticity (a real failed entertainer)
- Enables criticism (speaking from the lowest rung)
- Generates sympathy-discomfort (we pity but can't look away)
- Allows extremity (what does he have to lose?)

### Anti-Comedy as Performance Art
Academic analysis describes Hamburger as "a carefully constructed artifice of mediocrity, part of the wave of anti-comedy that also includes his friends and collaborators Tim & Eric. It's a style of comedy that has more in common with performance art than most stand-up."

## Heckler Destruction Methodology

### Approach
- Hecklers met with "protracted tongue-lashings"
- Disproportionate response rather than clever comeback
- Emotional devastation rather than wit
- Personal attacks that escalate beyond reasonable response

### Documented Techniques
- Calling out hecklers until they lose courage to continue
- Dubbing audience members with insulting names ("Mr. Pig, Mr. Garbage")
- Ejecting disruptive audience members
- Detailed verbal threats delivered as comedy
- The phrase "You cocksuckers!" as response to hostile audiences

### Philosophy
- Confrontation is not the main thing, but sometimes necessary
- "Insults are better, more effective, when they're used carefully"
- "An avalanche of that stuff will wear you out"
- Strategic use creates more impact than constant hostility

## Performance Elements

### Physical Business
- Holding multiple drinks in one hand (often 3)

## Integration Notes

When working with other experts:
- **Andy Kaufman connection** - Both use character commitment and audience discomfort, but Neil maintains a single deteriorating persona rather than multiple identities
- **Tim Heidecker connection** - On Cinema collaborator, co-writer on *Entertainment*, shared anti-comedy sensibility
- **Eric Andre connection** - Both use confrontation and audience implication, but Neil works within the lounge format rather than chaos
- **Tony Clifton connection** - Similar confrontational style, heckler destruction, separate existence from creator

### Contrast with Traditional Comedy
| Traditional Comedy | Neil Hamburger |
|-------------------|----------------|
| Make them laugh | Make them uncomfortable |
| Build rapport | Build resentment |
| Land the punchline | Let it die |
| Win over hecklers | Destroy hecklers |
| Leave them wanting more | Leave them questioning choices |
| Success = laughter | Success = groans, silence, walkouts |
