## Biography

### Background
**Alan Roger Mulally** (born August 4, 1945, in Oakland, California) is an American aerospace engineer and manufacturing executive. He is best known for leading two of the most remarkable corporate turnarounds in American business history: Boeing Commercial Airplanes (1998-2006) and Ford Motor Company (2006-2014).

### Education
- B.S. in Aeronautical and Astronautical Engineering, University of Kansas (1967)
- M.S. in Aeronautical and Astronautical Engineering, University of Kansas (1969)
- M.S. in Management, MIT Sloan School of Management as Alfred P. Sloan Fellow (1982)

### Career Timeline

## The "Working Together" Management System

### Overview
The "Working Together" management system is Alan Mulally's signature contribution to organizational leadership. Originally developed at Boeing during the 777 program, it was refined and scaled at Ford to orchestrate one of the greatest corporate turnarounds in history.

### Five Core Elements

1. **Principles and Practices** - The foundational beliefs and behaviors
2. **Governance Process** - How the organization manages itself
3. **Leadership Team** - Includes leaders of all functional disciplines
4. **Creating Value Roadmap** - Vision, business environment, strategy, plan
5. **Business Plan Review (BPR)** - Weekly progress reviews with color-coded status

### Working Together Principles

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| People first, love 'em up | Include, appreciate, thank, celebrate. Earn hearts and minds through respect. |
| Everyone is included | All stakeholders matter equally: employees, customers, suppliers, investors, dealers |
| Compelling vision | A clear, inspiring picture of what we're creating together |
| Comprehensive strategy | Not just technical strategy but partnership, people, and financing strategies |
| Relentless implementation | Disciplined execution with continuous course correction |
| Clear performance goals | Measurable targets everyone can track |
| One plan | Single, aligned strategy eliminates politics and confusion |
| Facts and data | Decisions based on reality, not opinion or hierarchy |

## Business Plan Review (BPR)

### Structure
- **Frequency**: Weekly, same day, same time, same place
- **Attendance**: Mandatory for all senior leaders
- **Duration**: Limited (typically 4 hours)
- **Format**: Consistent structure, no surprises

### Color-Coded Status Reporting

| Color | Meaning | Response |
|-------|---------|----------|
| Green | On plan | Continue executing |
| Yellow | At risk, have a plan for success | Monitor closely, offer support |
| Red | Off plan, need help | Mobilize team resources, schedule SAR |

### BPR Rules
1. No side conversations
2. No humor at anyone's expense
3. Information is never used as a weapon
4. Report status honestly
5. Celebrate transparency, especially reds
6. Complex discussions move to Special Attention Reviews (SARs)

### The First Red Story
When Alan Mulally introduced BPR at Ford in 2006, Ford was losing $17 billion, yet every slide in the first meeting was green. It wasn't until Mark Fields turned a slide red (indicating a production problem) that the culture began to shift. Instead of punishment, Mulally applauded: "Mark, that's great visibility. Who can help?" The following week, the charts were "splattered with more red than a crime scene."

## One Ford Strategy

### Core Concept
"ONE FORD: One Team, One Plan, One Goal"

Before Mulally, Ford operated as regional fiefdoms with different products, strategies, and cultures. One Ford unified the global organization around:

1. **One Team** - Global collaboration, not regional competition
2. **One Plan** - Single strategy understood by all
3. **One Goal** - Profitable growth for all stakeholders

### Strategic Actions
- Sold Jaguar, Land Rover (to Tata Motors, 2008)
- Sold Aston Martin (2007)
- Sold Volvo Cars (to Geely, 2010)
- Reduced stake in Mazda
- Focused on Ford and Lincoln brands
- Unified global platforms and products

### Results
- Avoided bankruptcy without government bailout
- Only one of Big Three automakers to refuse bailout funds
- Returned to profitability by 2009
- Stock price recovered from $1.01 (2008) to sustained profitability

## The Boeing 777 Story: Origin of Working Together

### Context
In the fall of 1990, Boeing committed to build the 777, the most technically advanced and customer-focused aircraft ever built. Boeing's environment had become bureaucratic and department-focused. Frank Shrontz enlisted Alan Mulally to conduct an employee survey, which revealed that command and control leadership was no longer tolerated; employees wanted to be respected, valued, and to have a voice.

### Mulally's Innovation
Mulally created an entirely new management system for the Boeing 777 program. He established design/build teams with members from design, manufacturing, suppliers, and customer airlines. The 777 was the first Boeing airliner designed using digital software and the first to utilize fly-by-wire controls.

### Cultural Significance
The teams that worked on the 777 remember the time as "Boeing's Camelot." They were so dedicated to the culture that "Working Together" was painted on the nose of the first 777 aircraft.

### Recognition
- PBS created a five-part television special, "21st Century Jet: The Building of the 777"
- Named Design News Engineer of the Year (1996)
- AIAA Reed Aeronautics Award
- National Aeronautical Association's Collier Trophy (1995) for "greatest achievement in aeronautics and astronautics"

## Key Stories and Anecdotes

### The Executive Garage Story
On Mulally's first visit to Ford headquarters, he couldn't find a single Ford in the executive garage. He said: "Ford had become a house of brands. No one knew what the blue oval stood for anymore."

### The "Love the Dealers" Story
In Mulally's first speech to 4,000 Ford dealers and corporate employees, he explained why he came to the company and why he thought they could save Ford together. He told the dealers they were the most important stakeholders and that Ford would work with them as true partners. Then Mulally had the Ford employees stand and face the dealership owners and tell them out loud that they loved them.

### The Missing Executive Story
When a prominent member of Alan's leadership team didn't attend a BPR meeting, Alan immediately confronted him and asked "why?" This demonstrated that the weekly meeting cadence was non-negotiable regardless of seniority.

### The "All Green" Meeting Story
When Mulally introduced BPR at Ford, the company was hemorrhaging money, yet every single slide was green. "We were about to lose $17 billion, and apparently, everything was fine," Mulally recalled. In the past, admitting failure at Ford meant being shown the door, so leaders had learned to hide problems.

### Congressional Testimony for Competitors
Although Ford opted not to take any money from the federal government bailout, Mulally testified in Washington on behalf of Ford's competitors (GM and Chrysler). He did so because he viewed saving them as the right thing to do, knowing their demise would hurt the auto industry, the network of suppliers, and Ford itself.

## Detailed BPR Meeting Structure

### Weekly Business Plan Review Agenda
Every week the global leadership team meets to review:
1. **Business Environment** - Economy, energy, technology sectors, global labor, government relations, demographic trends
2. **Competitor Analysis** - What competitors are doing
3. **Customer Insights** - What's happening with customers
4. **Status Against Plan** - Color-coded reporting on every initiative
5. **Special Attention Items** - Items requiring deeper discussion (scheduled for SARs)

### BPR Meeting Rules
| Rule | Rationale |
|------|-----------|
| Same time, same place, weekly | Predictability creates reliability |
| Mandatory attendance | No one is more important than alignment |
| No side conversations | Respect for the process |
| No jokes at others' expense | Psychological safety |
| Status only, no debates | Efficiency; debates happen in SARs |
| Start on time, end on time | Respect for everyone's calendar |

## Working Together System: Complete Framework

### The Five Elements in Detail

**1. Principles and Practices**
The foundational beliefs that guide all behavior. These are non-negotiable and apply to everyone equally.

**2. Governance Process**
How the organization manages itself. This includes the cadence of reviews, decision-making authority, and escalation paths.

**3. Leadership Team**
Includes leaders of all functional disciplines. At Ford, this meant every business unit and every function was represented. No silos.

**4. Creating Value Roadmap**
Contains:
- Vision (where we're going)
- Business environment (what we face)
- Strategy (how we'll win)
- Plan (specific actions and timelines)

**5. Business Plan Review (BPR)**
Weekly progress reviews with color-coded status. Mulally describes this system as "an ever-spinning circle revisited weekly to ensure its effectiveness."

### Why It Works
The system succeeds because it creates complete transparency and psychological safety simultaneously. When people know that surfacing problems leads to help rather than punishment, they surface problems earlier. Earlier problem detection means easier problem solving.
