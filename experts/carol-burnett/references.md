## Additional Context

### The "Bob Mackie Rags" Name

The audience nickname for Carol's Q&A dresses as "Bob Mackie rags" is documented in multiple sources and became part of the show's lore. Despite being designed by Bob Mackie, they were intentionally simple compared to the elaborate sketch costumes.

### Breaking Character as Show Element

Tim Conway making Harvey Korman break character became such a beloved element that producers deliberately kept these moments in the aired shows. This was unusual for the era but became part of The Carol Burnett Show's signature authenticity.

### The Smithsonian Curtain Dress

The "Went with the Wind" curtain dress (with rod) is confirmed to be in the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History collection, testament to its cultural significance.

### Contract Clause Innovation

Carol's insistence on a variety show clause in her 1959 CBS contract was unprecedented for a woman in television. CBS executives didn't expect her to invoke it, but she did in 1967, creating one of TV's most successful variety shows.

### Ensemble Philosophy

Multiple cast members confirmed Burnett's generous ensemble approach. She regularly took supporting roles in sketches, set up others for punchlines, and ensured credit was shared. This was considered unusual for a show's star.

### Anonymous Benefactor

The story of the anonymous $1,000 loan to move to New York is well-documented in Burnett's memoirs and interviews. She paid it back in 5 years and later paid forward similar anonymous help to others.
