# Hans Kung: Expertise and Knowledge Base

## Biographical Overview

**Full Name:** Hans Kung
**Born:** March 19, 1928, Sursee, Canton of Lucerne, Switzerland
**Died:** April 6, 2021, Tubingen, Germany
**Nationality:** Swiss
**Religion:** Roman Catholic (remained a priest until death)
**Known For:** Critique of papal infallibility, ecumenical theology, Global Ethic project, interfaith dialogue, Vatican II theological advisor

---

## Intellectual Biography

### Early Formation and Education (1928-1960)

- Born in Sursee, Switzerland to a Catholic family
- Studied philosophy and theology at the Pontifical Gregorian University in Rome (1948-1955)
- Ordained a Catholic priest in 1954
- Continued studies at the Sorbonne and Institut Catholique in Paris
- Doctoral dissertation on justification in Karl Barth's theology (1957) - an ecumenical breakthrough showing Catholic-Protestant agreement possible
- Appointed Professor of Fundamental Theology at the University of Tubingen in 1960, at age 32

### Vatican II Period (1960-1965)

- Appointed by Pope John XXIII as theological advisor (peritus) to the Second Vatican Council
- Influential voice for church reform, decentralization, and ecumenical openness
- Worked alongside other progressive theologians including Joseph Ratzinger, Karl Rahner, and Yves Congar
- Advocated for vernacular liturgy, episcopal collegiality, and dialogue with other Christians and religions
- Published "The Council, Reform and Reunion" (1961) which became influential preparation for the Council

### Reform Theology Period (1965-1979)

- Published "The Church" (1967) - major work on ecclesiology emphasizing service over hierarchy
- Published "Infallible? An Inquiry" (1970) - controversial critique of papal infallibility prompted by Humanae Vitae
- Published "On Being a Christian" (1974) - comprehensive Christology that became an international bestseller
- Published "Does God Exist? An Answer for Today" (1978) - philosophical theology engaging modern atheism
- Vatican investigations into his writings intensified throughout the 1970s

### Censure and Ecumenical Ministry (1979-1996)

- December 18, 1979: Vatican's Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith revoked his license to teach as a Catholic theologian (missio canonica)
- Declaration stated he "can no longer be considered a Catholic theologian, nor function as such in a teaching role"
- Remained at Tubingen as Professor of Ecumenical Theology under secular rather than Catholic auspices
- Sixty American and Canadian theologians publicly protested, affirming him as a Catholic theologian
- Thousand students held candlelight vigil at Tubingen
- Continued prolific writing on world religions and ethics
- Retired from Tubingen in 1996 as Professor Emeritus

### Global Ethic Period (1990-2021)

- Developed concept of "Weltethos" (Global Ethic) in late 1980s
- Published "Global Responsibility: In Search of a New World Ethic" (1990)
- Drafted "Declaration Toward a Global Ethic" adopted by Parliament of World's Religions in Chicago (1993)
- Founded Foundation for a Global Ethic (Stiftung Weltethos) in 1995, serving as President
- Published major works on world religions: Judaism (1992), Christianity (1994), Islam (2004)
- Met with Pope Benedict XVI in 2005 for four-hour conversation
- Continued writing and advocating for global ethic until his death

### Declaration Toward a Global Ethic (1993)

Drafted by Kung, adopted by Parliament of World's Religions, signed by 200+ religious leaders from 40+ traditions:

**Two Fundamental Principles:**
1. Every human being must be treated humanely
2. The Golden Rule: What you wish done to yourself, do to others

**Four Irrevocable Directives:**
1. Commitment to a culture of nonviolence and respect for life
2. Commitment to a culture of solidarity and a just economic order
3. Commitment to a culture of tolerance and a life of truthfulness
4. Commitment to a culture of equal rights and partnership between men and women

**Fifth Directive (added 2018):**
5. Commitment to a culture of sustainability and care for the Earth

## Key Concepts Glossary

| Concept | Definition |
|---------|------------|
| Ecclesia semper reformanda | "The church is always reforming" - principle that reform is continuous, not exceptional |
| Indefectibility | The Church is sustained in truth by the Spirit despite errors, without requiring infallible propositions |
| Paradigm shift | Major transformation in the "entire constellation of beliefs, values, techniques" shared by a community |
| Global Ethic (Weltethos) | Shared ethical principles across religious and cultural traditions |
| Christology from below | Beginning with the historical Jesus of Nazareth rather than dogmatic formulations |
| Missio canonica | License from the Church to teach as a Catholic theologian (revoked from Kung in 1979) |
| Peritus | Expert theological advisor at an ecumenical council |
| Historical-critical method | Reading religious texts in their historical context using scholarly analysis |

## The Six Paradigms of Christianity

Adapted from Thomas Kuhn's concept of scientific paradigms:

1. **Jewish-Christian Apocalyptic Paradigm** - Earliest Christianity awaiting imminent return of Christ
2. **Hellenistic-Byzantine Paradigm** - Greek philosophical synthesis, early councils, Eastern Orthodoxy
3. **Roman Catholic Medieval Paradigm** - Papal monarchy, scholasticism, Western Christendom
4. **Reformation Protestant Paradigm** - Luther, Calvin, scripture alone, faith alone
5. **Enlightenment Modern Paradigm** - Historical criticism, liberal theology, accommodation to modernity
6. **Contemporary Ecumenical Paradigm** - Emerging synthesis, interfaith dialogue, global perspective

## Historical Context

### Vatican II and Its Aftermath

The Second Vatican Council (1962-1965) represented the most significant Catholic reform in centuries:
- Vernacular liturgy replacing Latin Mass
- Emphasis on episcopal collegiality with papal primacy
- Opening to ecumenism and interfaith dialogue
- Religious liberty affirmed
- Church as "People of God" not just hierarchy

Kung and colleagues like Ratzinger were progressive voices at the Council. After the Council:
- Pope Paul VI's Humanae Vitae (1968) on contraception disappointed progressives
- Ratzinger moved toward conservatism; Kung continued pushing reform
- Tensions between Roman centralization and conciliar collegiality persisted
- Kung became the most prominent theological voice for continued reform

### The Infallibility Controversy

The doctrine of papal infallibility was defined at Vatican I in 1870:
- Pope speaking ex cathedra on faith and morals is infallible
- Opposed by many bishops, passed after their departure
- Used rarely (only Assumption of Mary in 1950 definitively)
- Humanae Vitae invoked related teaching authority

Kung's critique argued:
- No biblical or early church support for papal infallibility
- Church has made demonstrable errors (Galileo, Photius, biblical criticism)
- "Indefectibility" better describes how Church is sustained in truth
- Infallibility claims impede ecumenical reunion

## Scholarly Assessment

### Contributions

- Made Catholic reform theology accessible to wide audience
- Pioneered serious Catholic engagement with other world religions
- Created practical framework (Global Ethic) for interfaith cooperation
- Applied paradigm analysis to religious history
- Maintained Catholic identity while enabling ecumenical dialogue
- Embodied prophetic theological witness at personal cost

### Critiques

- Some argue he underestimated theological distinctives
- Orthodox critics see his Christology as insufficiently divine
- Conservative Catholics view him as Protestant in Catholic clothing
- Some scholars find his paradigm analysis oversimplified
- Interfaith critics question whether "Global Ethic" is too Western/Abrahamic

### Legacy Position

- Most influential Catholic reform theologian of post-Vatican II era
- Central architect of Catholic interfaith engagement
- Global Ethic remains framework for religious cooperation
- Demonstrated that loyal criticism and institutional belonging can coexist
- His questions about infallibility remain unresolved in Catholic theology

## Key Relationships

### Joseph Ratzinger (Pope Benedict XVI)

- Met in 1957, became colleagues at Tubingen
- Both served as progressive periti at Vatican II
- Kung recruited Ratzinger to Tubingen faculty
- Split after 1968 student revolts pushed Ratzinger toward conservatism
- Ratzinger as head of CDF oversaw continued Vatican opposition to Kung
- Met privately for four hours after Ratzinger became Pope (2005)
- Maintained "distant respect" despite theological divergence

### Karl Rahner

- Fellow German-speaking progressive Catholic theologian
- Collaborated at Vatican II
- Rahner more acceptable to Vatican, remained within good standing
- Rahner's "anonymous Christianity" related to but distinct from Kung's interfaith approach

### Karl Barth

- Kung's doctoral dissertation on Barth's theology
- Barth endorsed Kung's interpretation as accurate
- Demonstrated Catholic-Protestant dialogue possible at highest level
- Kung's ecumenical method influenced by this early success
