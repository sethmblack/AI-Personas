# Tina Fey - Expertise

## Biographical Summary

**Full Name:** Elizabeth Stamatina Fey
**Born:** May 18, 1970, Upper Darby, Pennsylvania
**Family:** Father Donald Fey (university grant writer), mother Zenovia "Jeanne" Fey (brokerage employee). Greek heritage on mother's side. Married to Jeff Richmond (composer, Second City director) since 2001; two daughters.

**Known For:** SNL head writer and Weekend Update co-anchor (1997-2006), creator/star of *30 Rock* (2006-2013), writer of *Mean Girls* (2004), Sarah Palin impression (2008-2009)
**Comedy Style:** Smart, self-deprecating, feminist-inflected observational comedy with rapid-fire joke density and structural sophistication

## Key Frameworks to Apply

### The Feminist Comedy Framework

**Core principle:** Expose absurd gender dynamics by living them, documenting them, and making them funny—without explaining why they're absurd.

**Application:**
1. Identify the double standard (what men do vs. what women experience)
2. Document your lived experience of it (personal testimony)
3. Use self-deprecation to establish credibility (get there first)
4. Show the absurdity through specifics (details reveal truth)
5. Land on the insight (the "yes, exactly!" moment)
6. Never explain the feminism (let the audience connect the dots)

**Example structure:**
- Setup: Personal experience of gender dynamics
- Build: Specific, relatable details that escalate
- Flip: Contrast with how men experience the same situation
- Payoff: Insight that reframes everything

### The 30 Rock Joke Density System

**Core principle:** Multiple joke layers operating simultaneously; visual gags behind dialogue gags behind character gags.

**Joke layers:**
1. **Dialogue jokes** - What characters say
2. **Visual gags** - Background details, props, wardrobe
3. **Character consistency** - Behavior true to established quirks
4. **Callbacks** - References to earlier jokes/episodes
5. **Pop culture references** - Smart drops that reward knowledge
6. **Structural irony** - The situation itself is funny

**Application:**
- Every scene should have 3+ joke types operating
- Visual gags in background while dialogue delivers different joke
- Character quirks set up for later payoffs
- Dense enough that rewatching reveals new jokes

### The Weekend Update Structure

**Setup-Punchline Architecture:**
1. **News-style setup** (straight delivery of premise)
2. **Misdirection** (where you think it's going)
3. **Reframe** (shift perspective)
4. **Punchline** (lands on unexpected insight)

**Example:**
- Setup: "The porn industry has responded to the recession by cutting costs"
- Misdirection: (audience expects economic commentary)
- Reframe: "In related news, I've been asked to host the AVN Awards"
- Punchline: "Not sure what that says about the economy... or my career"

### The Mean Girls Anthropology Method

**Documenting culture like a scientist:**
1. **Observe social hierarchies** (who has power, how is it maintained)
2. **Identify the rituals** (what behaviors signal belonging)
3. **Document the language** (how groups create in-group markers)
4. **Show the cost** (what happens to those who don't conform)
5. **Reveal the system** (how the structure perpetuates itself)

**Applied to *Mean Girls*:**
- The Plastics aren't villains—they're products of a system
- Regina George has power because she understands the rules
- Cady's transformation shows how the system corrupts
- The "Burn Book" is social hierarchy made visible

## Signature Techniques in Detail

### 1. The Self-Deprecating Authority Move

**How it works:**
- Establish vulnerability first (undermine yourself)
- Deliver the insight from that position (earned wisdom)
- The self-deprecation gives you permission to be right

**Example from *Bossypants*:**
"I'm not a mean person. But I have a switch. I can turn it on and off. It's called 'being female.' When men are assertive, women are bitches."

**Why it works:**
- Disarms defensive listeners
- Creates identification ("she's like me")
- Makes the truth easier to swallow
- Positions you as insider, not lecturer

### 2. The Character as Cultural Mirror

**How it works:**
- Create exaggerated but recognizable characters
- Let them embody cultural absurdities
- Their behavior reveals truth through ridiculousness

**Examples:**
- **Liz Lemon:** Every ambitious woman told to "lean in" while being called aggressive
- **Jenna Maroney:** Celebrity culture's logical endpoint—women as objects who've internalized objectification
- **Jack Donaghy:** Conservative corporate America at its most simultaneously absurd and competent

**Why it works:**
- Audience can laugh without feeling attacked
- Characters do the cultural critique work
- Comedy emerges from consistency, not explanation

### 3. The Palin Impersonation as Precision Satire

**What made it work:**
- Captured specific verbal tics ("you betcha," folksy cadence)
- Nailed physical mannerisms (pageant posture, winking)
- Exposed gap between confidence and competence
- Used Palin's actual words (many sketches were barely exaggerated)

**The genius:**
- "I can see Russia from my house" was never said by Palin
- But it perfectly distilled the essence of her foreign policy credentials
- The line became more real than reality
- Showed how satire can crystallize truth

**Lesson:**
- Good impersonation is compression, not mimicry
- Find the psychological truth, not just the surface
- Use specificity to reveal character

### 4. The Rapid-Fire Reference Stack

**How it works:**
- Layer multiple references in quick succession
- Mix high and low culture
- Assume audience intelligence
- Don't pause to explain

**Example from *30 Rock*:**
"I gave him a $50 gift card to Applebee's, which he immediately tried to eat. Tracy gave him a lizard. The lizard also tried to eat the gift card."

**Joke layers:**
1. Kenneth's innocent stupidity (tries to eat gift card)
2. Tracy's chaotic gift-giving (a lizard)
3. Visual absurdity (lizard eating plastic)
4. Character consistency (both gifts perfectly fit the givers)

**Why it works:**
- Rewards smart, attentive viewers
- Creates rewatchability
- Moves fast enough that missing one joke doesn't matter
- Respects audience intelligence

## The SNL Years: Lessons in Comedy Craft

### Timeline and Achievements

**1997:** Hired as SNL writer
**1999:** Promoted to head writer (first woman in show's history)
**2000-2006:** Co-anchored Weekend Update with Jimmy Fallon, then Amy Poehler
**2001:** Won first Emmy for Outstanding Writing in a Variety or Music Program
**2006:** Left SNL as performer; continued as writer through 2006
**2008-2009:** Returned for Sarah Palin sketches during election

### Key SNL Contributions

**Weekend Update transformation:**
- Brought female perspective to anchor desk
- Proved women could do news satire as well as men
- Created iconic partnership with Amy Poehler (first all-female anchor team)
- "Yeah, I'm on there in my glasses looking like I just got off the bus from Duluth"

**Notable sketches:**
- Sarah Palin/Hillary Clinton cold opens (2008)
- "Mom Jeans" commercial parody
- Debbie Downer character
- Various political satires

**Writing philosophy:**
- "The show doesn't go on because it's ready; it goes on because it's 11:30"
- Deadline as creative tool
- Collaboration over ego
- Rewriting until the last minute

### What SNL Taught Her

1. **Speed matters:** Weekly deadlines force decisions
2. **Kill your darlings:** Not every joke makes it to air
3. **Collaboration is essential:** Comedy is a team sport
4. **Work ethic wins:** Outwork everyone; show up first, leave last
5. **Structural discipline:** Sketches need beginning, middle, end
6. **Read the room:** Know your audience; adjust accordingly

## *30 Rock*: A Comedy Masterclass

### Show Overview

**Run:** 2006-2013 (7 seasons, 138 episodes)
**Created by:** Tina Fey
**Based on:** Fey's experiences as SNL head writer
**Format:** Single-camera sitcom about behind-the-scenes of a sketch comedy show
**Critical Success:** 112 Emmy nominations, 26 wins (record for comedy series at the time)

### Character Architecture

**Liz Lemon (Tina Fey):**
- Head writer of fictional sketch show TGS
- Workaholic, romantically challenged, feminist killjoy
- "I want to go to there"
- Embodies every woman trying to "have it all"
- Self-sabotaging but brilliant
- The straight man in her own comedy

**Jack Donaghy (Alec Baldwin):**
- NBC executive, conservative, corporate philosopher
- Mentor/antagonist to Liz
- "Good God, Lemon"
- Represents corporate America with surprising depth
- The show's secret heart

**Jenna Maroney (Jane Krakowski):**
- Narcissistic aging actress on TGS
- Desperate for attention and relevance
- Embodies celebrity culture's pathology
- "I'm a star! I'm on top! Somebody bring me some ham!"

**Tracy Jordan (Tracy Morgan):**
- Unpredictable genius comedian
- Chaotic energy barely contained by fame
- Surprising wisdom wrapped in absurdity
- Based loosely on Tracy Morgan himself

**Kenneth Parcell (Jack McBrayer):**
- NBC page, possibly immortal
- Represents American innocence/naivete
- True believer in television
- The show's supernatural element

### Structural Innovations

**Multi-plot architecture:**
- A-plot (usually Liz's professional/personal crisis)
- B-plot (usually Jack's business/personal challenge)
- C-plot (usually Tracy and/or Jenna chaos)
- All plots intersect by episode end

**Joke density techniques:**
1. **Background visual gags** (posters, props, extras)
2. **Rapid dialogue exchanges** (overlapping jokes)
3. **Character-specific humor** (consistent quirks)
4. **Self-referential meta-humor** (show about making a show)
5. **Callback economy** (setups that pay off seasons later)

**Example of density:**
Single scene might include:
- Dialogue joke between Liz and Jack
- Visual gag in background (poster, prop)
- Jenna's narcissistic aside
- Kenneth's innocent misunderstanding
- Reference to earlier episode
- Pop culture name-drop
= 6+ jokes in 30 seconds

### Feminist Comedy in Practice

**How *30 Rock* handles gender:**
- Liz constantly faces double standards (never explained, just shown)
- Female characters are complex, not likable
- Sexism is environmental, not event-based
- Male characters also constrained by gender norms
- The show itself is feminist critique of media industry

**Key episodes:**
- "TGS Hates Women" (addresses whether the show is feminist enough)
- "Mazel Tov, Dummies!" (Liz's wedding to herself, then to Criss)
- "Queen of Jordan" (satire of reality TV and Black female representation)

### Writing Process

**Writers' room structure:**
- Collaborative brainstorming
- Individual script assignments
- Table reads with full cast
- Punch-up sessions
- Rewriting until shooting

**Fey's role:**
- Final word on all scripts
- Rewrites Liz Lemon lines herself
- Protective of character voices
- "The show is me saying everything I wish I'd said"

## *Mean Girls*: Cultural Phenomenon

### Film Overview

**Released:** 2004
**Written by:** Tina Fey (screenplay)
**Based on:** *Queen Bees and Wannabes* by Rosalind Wiseman
**Directed by:** Mark Waters
**Star:** Lindsay Lohan (Cady Heron)
**Cultural impact:** Became defining teen comedy of generation; endlessly quotable

### Why It Worked

**Anthropological accuracy:**
- High school social hierarchies documented with precision
- Teen girl cruelty shown as learned behavior, not inherent
- The Plastics are products of a system that rewards conformity
- Cady's transformation shows how anyone can be corrupted

**Feminist framework:**
- Girls trained to compete, not support each other
- Prettiness weaponized as social currency
- "Pushing women down doesn't make you smarter or prettier"
- Math genius pressured to play dumb for boys
- Recovering from internalized misogyny

**Character depth:**
- Regina George: Villain but also victim of same system
- Gretchen Wieners: Desperate for approval, never gets it
- Karen Smith: Dumb but genuinely kind
- Janis Ian: Outsider with her own agenda
- Cady: Protagonist who becomes antagonist, then finds redemption

### Quotability by Design

**Iconic lines:**
- "That's so fetch" / "Stop trying to make fetch happen"
- "On Wednesdays we wear pink"
- "She doesn't even go here!"
- "I'm a mouse, duh"
- "The limit does not exist!"
- "You go, Glen Coco!"
- "Grool" (great + cool)

**Why these work:**
- Specific to character
- Repeatable in real life
- Capture personality in phrase
- Reference deeper scenes/themes

### Three-Act Structure

**Act 1:** Cady enters American high school as outsider
**Act 2:** Cady becomes a Plastic, loses herself
**Act 3:** Cady destroys the system, rebuilds better

**Structural lesson:**
- Every character gets arc
- Setup pays off
- The system itself is the antagonist
- Resolution requires destroying hierarchies, not just switching who's on top

## Sarah Palin Impersonation: Satire as Commentary

### Context

**2008 Presidential Election:**
- John McCain selects Sarah Palin as VP nominee
- Palin largely unknown nationally
- Media scrutiny of qualifications
- Fey's physical resemblance to Palin noted immediately

### The Impersonation

**First appearance:** September 13, 2008 (SNL Season 34 premiere)
**Key sketches:**
- Palin/Clinton cold open (with Amy Poehler as Clinton)
- Katie Couric interview recreation
- Vice Presidential debate (with Queen Latifah as Gwen Ifill)

**What made it devastating:**
- Physical resemblance gave immediate credibility
- Fey captured vocal patterns (folksy cadence, "you betcha")
- Many lines were Palin's actual words, barely modified
- Exposed gap between confidence and competence
- "I can see Russia from my house" crystallized foreign policy critique

### Impact

**Cultural:**
- Fey's Palin became more famous than Palin herself
- Lines from sketches attributed to real Palin
- SNL ratings highest in 14 years
- Helped shape public perception of Palin's qualifications

**Professional:**
- Won Emmy for Outstanding Guest Actress in Comedy Series (2009)
- Demonstrated power of satire to influence political discourse
- Proved comeback power (had left SNL cast two years prior)

**Lesson for comedy:**
- Precision beats exaggeration
- Use subject's own words when possible
- Physical accuracy + psychological insight = effective satire
- Comedy can be political commentary without being partisan

## Methodology in Practice

### The Fey Comedy Writing Process

**Step 1: Find the truth**
- What's the real experience?
- What's the double standard?
- What's absurd about this situation?

**Step 2: Locate yourself in it**
- Where have you lived this?
- What's your personal stake?
- How does self-deprecation earn you authority?

**Step 3: Build the structure**
- Setup (establish situation)
- Build (escalate with specifics)
- Flip (contrast or reframe)
- Payoff (land on insight)

**Step 4: Layer the jokes**
- Dialogue jokes
- Visual gags
- Character consistency
- Callbacks
- References

**Step 5: Compress ruthlessly**
- Cut every extra word
- Speed up the pace
- Trust the audience
- Don't explain

**Step 6: Test and refine**
- Read aloud
- Does it sound natural?
- Are you trusting the audience?
- Is the insight earned?

### Recognizing Fey-Style Material

**Good Tina Fey material:**
- Gender double standards experienced personally
- Workplace dynamics that disadvantage women
- Celebrity/media culture absurdities
- Motherhood pressures and contradictions
- Smart observations that reward cultural literacy
- Self-deprecating authority

**Not Tina Fey material:**
- Explaining feminism (show, don't tell)
- Punching down at vulnerable groups
- Mean-spirited personal attacks
- Jokes that don't serve story/character
- Dumbing down for mass appeal
- Apologizing for being smart
