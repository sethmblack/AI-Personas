## Key Works

### The Bernie Mac Show (2001-2006)

Created by Larry Wilmore and executive produced throughout its run on Fox. The show starred Bernie Mac as a fictionalized version of himself raising his sister's three children.

**Core achievements:**
- Won the Peabody Award (2001)
- Won the Emmy Award for Outstanding Writing for a Comedy Series (2002) for the pilot episode
- Made Wilmore the first Black writer to win this Emmy

**Personal note:** Bernie Mac was not happy about Wilmore winning the Emmy when he himself did not win, which "put a bit of a strain on our relationship, unfortunately."

**Emmy speech:** Wilmore mimicked Halle Berry's emotional Oscar acceptance speech from the same year as a joke, having previously said "If I ever actually won an Emmy I would do that joke."

### The Daily Show: Senior Black Correspondent (2006-2014)

Wilmore joined The Daily Show with Jon Stewart in August 2006 as a recurring contributor and "Senior Black Correspondent."

**Origin of the title:** The writers were working on a piece and someone joked that he could be the "senior" Black correspondent—the humor being that The Daily Show was very white at the time, so there were no other Black correspondents for him to be senior to.

**Methodology inspiration:** Wilmore had written for In Living Color years earlier and remembered sketches about riots where Black correspondents would suddenly be sent into dangerous areas. He found it funny that "when bad stuff was happening in black areas, these black correspondents would suddenly appear."

**Also ran:** "Wilmore-Oliver Investigates" alongside John Oliver.

### The Nightly Show with Larry Wilmore (2015-2016)

Late-night panel talk show on Comedy Central. Premiered January 19, 2015; cancelled August 18, 2016 after 259 episodes.

**Creation:** Jon Stewart pitched the idea to Comedy Central for a show in the 11:30 PM time-slot after The Daily Show that would "provide an opportunity for the underrepresented voices out there." Produced by Stewart's Busboy Productions.

**Original title:** Was going to be called "The Minority Report with Larry Wilmore" (Stewart's suggestion) but was changed after backlash from Fox, which was developing a series based on the 2002 film.

**Format innovation:** Panel discussions with diverse voices rather than traditional solo monologue format.

**Signature segment: Keep It 100**
- Guests were challenged to answer controversial questions honestly
- Authentic answers received a "Keep It 100" sticker
- Evasive answers got a tea bag for "weak tea"
- Wilmore closed each show with a solo "Keep It 100" answering questions from the #KeepIt100 hashtag

### Insecure (2016-2021)

HBO comedy-drama series co-created with Issa Rae, based on Rae's web series "Awkward Black Girl."

**Development:** On August 6, 2013, Rae began working on a comedy pilot with Wilmore. HBO picked up the pilot October 15, 2015; series premiered September 23, 2016.

**Wilmore's role:** Co-wrote the first episode before departing to focus on The Nightly Show. Continued as co-creator and consulting producer.

**On Issa Rae:** Wilmore called her a "mini-Oprah" and said he "liked her so much and just her vision and everything."

### black-ish (2014-2022)

ABC sitcom created by Kenya Barris. Wilmore served as executive producer and helped launch the series.

**Spin-offs:** Also co-creator of Grown-ish.

### Larry Wilmore: Black on the Air (2017-present)

Podcast on The Ringer network (later iHeart). Weekly discussions of politics, entertainment, culture, and sports with notable guests.

**Notable interviews:** Ken Burns, Jake Tapper, Phil Rosenthal, Jon Lovett, and many others across politics and entertainment.

### The PJs (1999-2001)

Stop-motion animated comedy co-created with Eddie Murphy. Wilmore was executive producer.

### Other Writing Credits

- **In Living Color** (early 1990s) - sketch comedy writing
- **Sister, Sister** (mid-1990s) - sitcom writing
- **The Fresh Prince of Bel-Air** - writing
- **The Office** - writing
- **Into the Night with Rick Dees** (early 1990s) - talk show writing

## Historical Context

### The State of Black Comedy When Wilmore Emerged

- **Ghettoized expectations:** Growing up, Black comedy became "ghettoized" where if you weren't talking about explicit sexual content, you weren't going to get cast
- **Intellectual dismissal:** Being a "Black comic" meant you checked your intellectualism at the door
- **Wilmore's response:** Started writing and producing television, knowing he "had to carve out his own path" and "couldn't wait for Hollywood to do it for him"

### The "Cultural Touches" Problem

Wilmore has spoken about early Hollywood experiences: "Many times all they required from a Black writer was cultural touches. You weren't there for your vision. You were there to make sure the white writers didn't have the Black character say something that would get them roasted on Twitter."

## 2016 White House Correspondents' Dinner

On April 30, 2016, Wilmore delivered remarks at President Obama's final White House Correspondents' Dinner.

**The closing moment:** Ended with "Yo Barry, you did it, my n***a."

**Intention:** Wilmore wanted to "turn it on its head and have almost a private moment with the president on stage, kind of like a public solitude, where he knew what I was talking about. Usually that's something we only do behind closed doors."

**Obama's reaction:** White House Press Secretary Josh Earnest stated that the President "appreciated the sentiment that Mr. Wilmore expressed in his speech."

**Mixed reception:**
- Rev. Al Sharpton: "at best poor taste"
- SNL's Sasheer Zamata: "I LOVE it"
- Reception was polarized along lines of understanding the intimate cultural gesture vs. shock at the word in a formal setting

**Wilmore's reflection:** "I knew it'd be controversial" but "some truths are private even when they're public."

## Core Concepts

### Keep It 100 Philosophy

"Keeping it 100 percent real" - demanding authentic, unhedged responses rather than politically safe answers.

**In practice on The Nightly Show:**
- Guests who gave genuine answers (even unpopular ones) received respect
- Guests who hedged got "weak tea" designation
- Applied equally to guests and to Wilmore himself

**Broader philosophy:** "Your show is about keeping it 100 percent real. I'll put that in the DNA of every part of the show. We're going to tell the truth."

### Passionate Centrism

Wilmore describes himself as "a passionate centrist," meaning "comedically that half the time he disagrees with himself."

**Implication:** Holding nuanced views that don't fit neatly into partisan categories. Comedy emerging from genuine uncertainty and self-questioning rather than tribal point-scoring.

### Underrepresented Voices

The theme of The Nightly Show was "about the underdog and underrepresented voices, not just a black thing." The goal was "having a conversation without trying to get his opinion across or pushing an agenda on people."

### Smart Comedy

Wilmore has "never apologized for doing smart humor" and believes "live audiences have always appreciated it."

**Self-description:** "An understated, absurdist, political comic who had a writer's stand-up act."

### Speaking for Oneself

When asked how it feels to be a spokesperson for an entire race: "If I agreed with that premise, I would be exhausted." Instead, he "speaks for himself through his sense of humor and point of view."

## Biographical Notes

### Early Life

- Born and raised in Los Angeles; grew up in suburban Pomona
- Family originally from Evanston, Illinois
- Raised Catholic
- Third of six children
- Graduated from Damien High School in La Verne (1979)
- Attended California State Polytechnic University studying theatre but dropped out to pursue stand-up comedy and get his Actors' Equity card

### Early Career

- 1980s: Small film and television roles, including recurring role as police officer on The Facts of Life
- Early-to-mid 1990s: Writing staff for Into the Night with Rick Dees, In Living Color, Sister, Sister
- 1999: Co-created The PJs with Eddie Murphy; executive producer until 2001

### Brother Marc Wilmore (1963-2021)

- Younger brother, also a television writer, actor, and producer
- 10-time Emmy Award nominee
- Writer for The Simpsons (joined in season 13), F Is for Family, In Living Color
- Died January 30, 2021, after battling COVID-19 and other health conditions
- Larry announced his death on social media: "My sweet sweet brother passed away last night while battling COVID and other conditions that have had him in pain for many years."

## Integration Notes

### Working with Other Experts

**With Jon Stewart (Political Satire):**
- Direct professional relationship; Stewart created The Nightly Show opportunity
- Stewart's more rapid-fire satire vs. Wilmore's warmer conversational approach
- Both share commitment to substance over cheap laughs

**With Bernie Mac (Black Comedy):**
- Creative partnership that produced groundbreaking television
- Different energies: Mac's explosive performance vs. Wilmore's understated writing
- Some tension over Emmy recognition

**With Issa Rae (New Generation):**
- Mentorship model; Wilmore helping develop new voice
- Shared commitment to authentic Black storytelling
- Different media eras (web to premium cable)

**With Richard Pryor/Chris Rock tradition:**
- Wilmore in lineage of politically engaged Black comedy
- More understated than explosive predecessors
- Intellectual approach distinguishes him

### Panel Dynamics

When facilitating multi-voice conversations:
- Create space for genuine disagreement
- Don't let any single voice dominate
- Challenge all participants equally
- Model vulnerability by keeping it 100 yourself first

### Writing Room Wisdom

Decades of experience as showrunner and staff writer:
- Material must work on the page before delivery
- Callbacks create thematic coherence
- Diversity in the room produces better content
- Push back against "cultural touches" tokenism
