# Noam Chomsky - References

This document provides verified sources for understanding Noam Chomsky's political thought, particularly the propaganda model developed with Edward S. Herman in *Manufacturing Consent*.

---

## Primary Political Works

### Manufacturing Consent: The Political Economy of the Mass Media (1988)

The foundational text for the propaganda model, co-authored with Edward S. Herman.

- **Authors:** Edward S. Herman and Noam Chomsky
- **Publisher:** Pantheon Books (1988)
- **ISBN:** 978-0-394-54926-2 (hardcover), 978-0-679-72034-8 (paperback)
- **Updated Edition:** 2002 revision with new introduction addressing the fall of the Soviet Union
- **Recognition:** Received the Orwell Award in 1989 for "outstanding contributions to the critical analysis of public discourse"

The book argues that mass media "are effective and powerful ideological institutions that carry out a system-supportive propaganda function, by reliance on market forces, internalized assumptions, and self-censorship, and without overt coercion." The title derives from Walter Lippmann's phrase "the manufacture of consent" in *Public Opinion* (1922).

**Key Resource:**
- [Penguin Random House - Manufacturing Consent](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/78912/manufacturing-consent-by-edward-s-herman-and-noam-chomsky/)

---

### Necessary Illusions: Thought Control in Democratic Societies (1989)

Extends the propaganda model to additional case studies in Central America, Indochina, and elsewhere.

- **Publisher:** South End Press (1989)
- **Origin:** Based on Chomsky's five 1988 Massey Lectures on Canadian Broadcasting Corporation Radio
- **Title Source:** Borrows the phrase from the writings of Reinhold Niebuhr

Chomsky argues that in systems where the state cannot easily rule by force, ideological control becomes central.

**Key Resource:**
- [Wikipedia - Necessary Illusions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_Illusions)

---

### Deterring Democracy (1991/1992)

Explores the contradictions between humanitarian rhetoric and imperialistic reality in U.S. foreign policy.

- **Publisher:** Verso Books (1991)
- **Updated Edition:** 1992 edition includes post-Gulf War afterword

The San Francisco Chronicle called it "a volatile, serious contribution to the debate over America's role as the globe's sole remaining superpower."

**Key Resource:**
- [Wikipedia - Deterring Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterring_Democracy)

---

### Hegemony or Survival: America's Quest for Global Dominance (2003)

Part of the American Empire Project series, analyzing U.S. foreign policy and the risks of nuclear war.

- **Publisher:** Metropolitan Books/Henry Holt (2003)
- **Series:** American Empire Project

---

### Failed States: The Abuse of Power and the Assault on Democracy (2006)

The follow-up to *Hegemony or Survival*, arguing the United States shares features with "failed states."

- **Publisher:** Metropolitan Books (2006)
- **Series:** American Empire Project
- **Republished:** Haymarket Books (2024)

Chomsky defines failed states as those "that do not protect their citizens from violence and perhaps even destruction, that regard themselves as beyond the reach of domestic or international law, and that suffer from a 'democratic deficit.'"

**Key Resource:**
- [Macmillan Publishers - Failed States](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9780805082845/failedstates/)

---

### Profit Over People: Neoliberalism and Global Order (1999)

A critique of neoliberalism and the "Washington Consensus."

- **Publisher:** Seven Stories Press (1999)
- **Introduction by:** Robert W. McChesney

Examines contradictions between democratic principles proclaimed by those in power and those actually practiced, critiquing the "tyranny of the few" that enacts policies vastly increasing private wealth.

**Key Resource:**
- [Seven Stories Press - Profit Over People](https://sevenstories.com/books/3435-profit-over-people)

---

### Understanding Power: The Indispensable Chomsky (2002)

A collection of transcribed seminars, talks, and Q&A sessions from 1989-1999.

- **Editors:** Peter R. Mitchell and John Schoeffel
- **Publisher:** The New Press (2002)
- **ISBN:** 978-1-56584-703-3
- **Supplementary Materials:** 449 pages of footnotes available at understandingpower.com

Current Affairs editor Nathan J. Robinson described it as "rich with insight" and "the best available introduction" to Chomsky's thinking.

**Key Resource:**
- [The New Press - Understanding Power](https://thenewpress.org/books/9781595585882/)

## Documentaries

### Manufacturing Consent: Noam Chomsky and the Media (1992)

The landmark documentary exploring Chomsky's political life and ideas.

- **Directors:** Mark Achbar and Peter Wintonick
- **Runtime:** 167 minutes
- **Production:** Necessary Illusions Productions / National Film Board of Canada
- **Years in Production:** Five years, with over 100 hours of interviews filmed across 23 cities in seven countries

**Recognition:**
- Special Jury Citation for Best Canadian Feature at the 1992 Toronto International Film Festival
- Gold Hugo for Best Social/Political Documentary at the Chicago International Film Festival
- 22 awards and distinctions total
- Screened theatrically in 300 cities and aired on 30 national TV networks
- Became the top-grossing feature documentary in Canadian history until *The Corporation* (2003)

**Notable Participants:** Bill Moyers, William F. Buckley Jr., Tom Wolfe, Peter Jennings, Jeff Greenfield, Michel Foucault, Sarah McClendon, Karl E. Meyer, Robert Faurisson

**Key Resources:**

## Foundational Source

### Public Opinion by Walter Lippmann (1922)

The source of the phrase "the manufacture of consent" that Chomsky and Herman inverted.

- **Author:** Walter Lippmann
- **Publisher:** Harcourt, Brace and Company (1922)
- **Note:** Lippmann first used the phrase in *Liberty and the News* (1919)

Lippmann argued that "the manufacture of consent is capable of great refinements" and saw it as useful and necessary for democracy. Chomsky and Herman invert this: what Lippmann prescribed, they diagnose and oppose.

**Key Resource:**
- [Project Gutenberg - Public Opinion](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/6456)

## About Edward S. Herman (1925-2017)

Co-author of *Manufacturing Consent* and principal architect of the propaganda model.

- **Position:** Professor of Finance at the Wharton School, University of Pennsylvania
- **Key Works:** *Manufacturing Consent* (with Chomsky), *The Real Terror Network*, *Beyond Hypocrisy*

Herman emphasized that "the propaganda model is about how the media work, not how effective they are" and characterized questions of intent as an "unmeasurable red herring."

## Notes on Methodology

The propaganda model employs institutional analysis rather than conspiracy theory. Key methodological principles:

1. Focus on structural incentives rather than individual intentions
2. Examine the five filters: ownership, advertising, sourcing, flak, and ideology
3. Apply consistent standards across cases (allies and enemies evaluated identically)
4. Build arguments through documentary evidence and empirical case studies
5. Predict media behavior from institutional structure, then compare to actual performance

As Herman and Chomsky demonstrated, the model generates testable predictions about media coverage that have been empirically verified across multiple contexts and countries.
