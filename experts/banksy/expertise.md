## Bristol Origins and Career Evolution

### The Bristol Underground Scene (1990-2000)

Banksy emerged from Bristol's vibrant underground scene in the early 1990s. He began as a freehand graffiti artist in 1990-1994 as part of the DryBreadZ Crew (DBZ), working alongside artists known as Kato and Tes. The Bristol scene was a unique crucible where artists and musicians collaborated, including Nick Walker, Inkie, and most influentially, 3D (Robert Del Naja of Massive Attack).

A pivotal moment: In 1985, at the Graffiti Art exhibition, an 11-year-old observed 3D at work. That child would become Banksy. By 1991, around age 17, he started graffiti writing. The social worker John Nation at Barton Hill Youth Club supported graffiti writers, and this is where Banksy notably emerged.

**Early Name Evolution:** He experimented with names, sometimes signing himself "Robin Banx" before settling on "Banksy"—shorter, more memorable, easier to write on a wall.

**Bristol Footprint:**
- Lived in Easton, Bristol during the late 1990s
- Played goalkeeper for the Easton Cowboys and Cowgirls football team
- Toured with the club to Mexico in 2001
- Met photographer Steve Lazarides, who became his agent and sold his early work
- First "inside" exhibition: 1998, in a flat in Easton—mostly acrylic on canvas, including an early version of *Love Is In The Air*

### The Mild Mild West (1999)

Banksy's first known and preserved large wall mural appeared in 1999 on Stokes Croft in Bristol, covering advertising on a former solicitor's office. It depicts a teddy bear throwing a Molotov cocktail at three riot police—a reference to Bristol's 1980s riots and the 1990s warehouse party scene where police often clashed with attendees.

### The Move to London (c. 2000)

Around 2000, Banksy relocated to London, expanding his reach while maintaining Bristol connections. This period marked his transition from local graffiti writer to nationally recognized artist.

### Identity Speculation

The Mail on Sunday claimed in 2008 that Banksy is Robin Gunningham, born 28 July 1974 in Yate, 12 miles from Bristol. Two cassette sleeves from 1993 for the Bristol band Mother Samosa feature Banksy's artwork with Gunningham's signature. Banksy has never confirmed nor denied this identification.

## Stencil Technique: History and Methodology

### The Switch from Freehand to Stencil

Banksy's defining technical shift occurred around 2000. The origin story, told by Banksy himself: While hiding from police under a rubbish lorry, he noticed the stenciled serial number on the vehicle's bumper. This gave him the idea.

**The Problem:** Freehand graffiti took too long. Someone would come along and he'd have to run away, leaving work unfinished.

**The Solution:** Pre-cut templates that could be sprayed onto a wall in seconds, then walk away.

### Influence: Blek le Rat

The most direct technical influence on Banksy is Blek le Rat (Xavier Prou, born 1952), a French artist who began stenciling rats on Paris streets in 1981. Blek described rats as "the only free animal in the city," one that "spreads the plague everywhere, just like street art."

Banksy has acknowledged this debt directly: "Every time I think I've painted something slightly original, I find out that Blek le Rat has done it as well. Only twenty years earlier."

Blek le Rat responded generously: "I'm the old man, he's the new kid, and if I'm an inspiration to an artist that good, I love it."

### Technical Process

**Materials:**
- Stencils cut from cardboard or plastic sheets
- Spray paint (primarily black, with selective color)
- Marker pens for finishing touches
- Mixed media and installation elements for larger works

**Method:**
1. Design created (some computer-assisted for photographic quality)
2. Intricate cutouts made in template
3. Multi-layer stencils devised for color and depth
4. Template pressed against surface
5. Spray paint applied through cutouts
6. Finishing details added by hand

**Speed as Survival:** What takes hours to paint freehand takes seconds to spray through a stencil. Precision without permanence of presence. The artist is gone before anyone notices.

### Visual Signature

- Sharp, clean lines contrasting with gritty urban surfaces
- High contrast black and white imagery
- Selective color for emotional punch (red balloon, gold flower)
- Photographic precision achieved through careful template cutting
- Striking images combined with sparse slogans
- Recurring subjects: rats, monkeys, police officers, soldiers, children, the elderly

## Core Contributions

### Guerrilla Art as Communication

Banksy demonstrates that powerful messages don't require platforms, galleries, or permission. The methodology:
- Unauthorized placement in public space
- Stencil technique for speed and repeatability
- Strategic location selection (context as content)
- Anonymous authorship (let work speak)

### The Banksy Methodology

| Principle | Practice |
|-----------|----------|
| **Ambush** | Place work where audiences don't expect art |
| **Economy** | Single image, few words, maximum impact |
| **Context** | Location IS meaning—choose walls strategically |
| **Subversion** | Turn familiar symbols against themselves |
| **Anonymity** | Identity is weakness; be ungrabble |
| **Humor** | Make them laugh, then think |

## Major Works: Contexts and Meanings

### Girl with Balloon (2002)

**Image:** A young girl reaching toward a red, heart-shaped balloon floating just beyond her grasp.

**Meaning:** Innocence, vulnerability, and the fragility of hope. The balloon—always out of reach—reflects how hope can be fleeting. The simplicity creates universal resonance: loss, longing, the things we cannot hold.

**Evolution:**
- First appeared on Waterloo Bridge, London, 2002
- Text originally read: "THERE IS ALWAYS HOPE"
- Reproduced worldwide; became Banksy's most recognized image
- In 2017, voted Britain's best-loved artwork

**Method:** Iconic simplicity. Strip away everything until only emotion remains.

### Flower Thrower / Love Is in the Air (2003)

**Image:** A protester in aggressive stance, arm cocked back—but instead of a Molotov cocktail, he throws a bouquet of flowers.

**Location:** Jerusalem, on the Israeli West Bank barrier wall.

**Meaning:** True resistance is nonviolent. Violence can be transformed into peace. The juxtaposition of aggressive posture with gentle act creates cognitive dissonance that forces reflection.

**Method:** Context amplification. Placing the image on a conflict zone wall multiplies its meaning exponentially.

### Napalm (2004)

**Image:** Mickey Mouse and Ronald McDonald holding hands with a naked, crying girl running between them.

**Context:** The girl is from Nick Ut's 1972 photograph of Phan Thi Kim Phuc fleeing a napalm attack during the Vietnam War.

**Meaning:** Corporate icons of American cultural imperialism bookending real human suffering. Fantasy mascots of capitalism complicit in war's horrors. Banksy at his most unforgiving.

**Method:** Collision of symbols. Force viewers to see the relationship between cultural products and political violence.

### Kissing Coppers (2004)

**Image:** Two male police officers in full uniform locked in a passionate kiss.

**Location:** First appeared on a pub wall in Brighton, UK—a city with a large LGBTQ+ population.

**Meaning:** Challenges traditional views of authority, masculinity, and power. Questions societal expectations placed on those in uniform. Promotes inclusivity while mocking homophobia.

**Method:** Placement as message. Brighton's demographics made this location perfect.

### Devolved Parliament (2009)

**Image:** A large oil painting depicting the UK's House of Commons filled with chimpanzees instead of MPs.

**Meaning:** Political dysfunction and disorder. Democracy reduced to primate behavior. More satirical than subtle.

**Auction:** Sold for £9.9 million in 2019, becoming one of Banksy's most valuable works. The elite art market embracing satire about elite dysfunction.

### The Son of a Migrant from Syria (2015)

**Image:** A young Steve Jobs (whose biological father was a Syrian immigrant) holding an early Macintosh computer and a black bin bag.

**Location:** "The Jungle" refugee camp in Calais, France.

**Meaning:** Refugees can transform societies if given opportunity. Jobs, born to Abdulfattah Jandali, a Syrian immigrant to America, went on to revolutionize technology. The image directly challenged anti-refugee sentiment in Europe.

**Method:** Historical fact as argument. Undeniable example defeats abstract prejudice.

### Armoured Dove (2005)

**Image:** A white dove—traditional symbol of peace—wearing a bulletproof vest with a sniper's red target on its chest.

**Location:** West Bank barrier wall, Bethlehem.

**Meaning:** Peace is under attack. Even symbols of hope need protection. The dove's security measures suggest peace is fragile and targeted.

### Game Changer (2020)

**Image:** A young boy playing with action figures. Batman and Spiderman lie discarded in a basket. Instead, he holds aloft a nurse figure wearing a cape and face mask.

**Context:** Donated to Southampton General Hospital during the COVID-19 pandemic's first wave, May 2020.

**Message:** Banksy's note to staff read: "Thanks for all you're doing. I hope this brightens the place up a bit, even if it's only black and white."

**Auction:** Sold at Christie's for £14.4 million in 2021—a record for a Banksy painting. All proceeds went to NHS charities.

**Meaning:** Real heroes don't wear capes from comic books. Healthcare workers are the new superheroes.

### London Animal Series (August 2024)

Nine animal-themed stencils appeared across London over nine consecutive days:

**Day 1 - Goat (Richmond):** A mountain goat balanced on a ledge above a CCTV camera. Comment on surveillance and precarious freedom.

**Day 2 - Elephants (Chelsea):** Two elephants reaching trunks toward each other, echoing Michelangelo's Creation of Adam. Connection and "the elephant in the room."

**Day 9 - Gorilla at London Zoo:** A gorilla lifting the zoo shutters to release a sea lion and birds. Anti-captivity message bringing the series full circle.

**Fate:** Many pieces were damaged, stolen, or removed within hours. The wolf piece was stolen from Peckham. The rhino was defaced with graffiti tags within seven hours. The impermanence proved the point.

## Major Installations

### Dismaland (2015)

**What:** A "bemusement park" in Weston-super-Mare, Somerset—"a sinister twist on Disneyland" that Banksy described as "a family theme park unsuitable for children."

**Location:** The Tropicana, a disused lido in a declining seaside resort.

**Secrecy:** Prepared in total secrecy. Locals were told a Hollywood company called "Atlas Entertainment" was filming a crime thriller called "Grey Fox." Signs with "Grey Fox Productions" were posted around entrances.

**Scale:**
- Banksy created 10 new works and funded the entire construction himself
- 58 artists participated (of 60 invited)
- Featured artists included: Damien Hirst, Jenny Holzer, Jimmy Cauty, David Shrigley, and Escif
- 150,000 visitors in 36 days (21 August - 27 September 2015)
- £20 million boost to local economy
- Tickets: £3 each, 4,000 per day

**Key Works:**
- Crashed Cinderella's coach with dead princess inside, surrounded by paparazzi
- Boat migrants installation
- Banksy's take on Michelangelo's Creation of Adam with hazmat glove
- Jimmy Cauty's *Aftermath Displacement Principle*—a model city after civil unrest
- David Shrigley's impossible fairground games ("topple the anvil with a ping pong ball")
- Mike Ross's *Big Rig Jig* (previously shown at Burning Man)
- Miserable stormtroopers and "dismal" staff trained to be unhelpful

**Entertainment:** Friday night performances by Run the Jewels, De La Soul, Damon Albarn, and Pussy Riot.

**Meaning:** Theme parks as capitalist distraction. The dark reality behind manufactured happiness. Consumer culture's hollow promises.

### Barely Legal (Los Angeles, 2006)

**What:** Banksy's major US exhibition, held in an industrial warehouse over the weekend of 16 September 2006.

**The Elephant in the Room:** The centerpiece was a live 38-year-old Asian elephant named Tai, painted in pink and gold floral wallpaper pattern to match the room's decor. A literal "elephant in the room" representing world poverty—the issue everyone ignores.

**Controversy:** Animal rights groups protested immediately. California officials deemed the painting illegal. The paint was replaced with child-safe face paint, then removed entirely.

**Parallel Action:** While in LA, Banksy placed a replica of a Guantanamo Bay detainee inside Disneyland's Big Thunder Mountain Railroad ride. It was removed after 90 minutes.

**Celebrity Attendance:** The address was revealed only on opening morning, yet the exhibition drew curators, critics, collectors, and celebrities.

**Method:** Use controversy to amplify message. The debate about the elephant made more people discuss world poverty.

### Love Is in the Bin (2018) - The Shredded Painting

**The Setup:** Around 2006, Banksy installed a remote-controlled shredder containing scalpel blades inside the frame of a *Girl with Balloon* painting. He then waited.

**The Auction:** On 5 October 2018, Sotheby's London sold the framed painting for £1,042,000—an artist record at the time.

**The Moment:** Within seconds of the gavel dropping, the canvas began sliding through the frame's base, shredding itself. A siren sounded. The audience gasped. The shredder stopped halfway through—Banksy later said this was unintended; he'd meant to destroy it completely.

**Response:** Sotheby's senior director Alex Branczik: "It appears we just got Banksy-ed."

**Aftermath:**
- The buyer agreed to complete the purchase at full price
- Pest Control renamed the work from *Girl with Balloon* to *Love Is in the Bin*
- The piece was loaned to Staatsgalerie Stuttgart
- In 2021, it sold again for £18.6 million ($25.4 million)—a 16-fold return and a new Banksy record

**Meaning:** The art market commodifies everything—even its own destruction. By shredding the painting, Banksy created something more valuable. The system absorbed the critique and monetized it anyway.

### Museum Infiltrations (2003-2005)

Banksy conducted a series of guerrilla installations in major museums, smuggling his own work into collections.

**Tate Britain (October 2003):** Disguised, Banksy hung *Crimewatch UK Has Ruined the Countryside For All of Us*—a pastoral scene covered in police tape. Removed by security and placed in "lost property."

**The Louvre (October 2004):** Installed an image of the Mona Lisa with a smiley-face sticker.

**New York Museums (March 2005):** Four museums infiltrated in a single day:
- **MoMA:** *Discount Soup Can* lasted 6 days
- **Metropolitan Museum:** Canvas of woman with prosthetic nose and beard—2 hours
- **Brooklyn Museum:** *Graffiti Lord* lasted 8 days
- **American Museum of Natural History:** Glass-encased beetle with fighter jet wings and missiles—12 days

**British Museum (May 2005):** *Peckham Rock*—a piece of concrete depicting a stick figure with a shopping trolley, presented as ancient cave art. Remained undetected for 3 days. Later officially exhibited in an Ian Hislop-curated show.

**Method:** Banksy studied Harry Houdini biographies to learn infiltration techniques. He entered during visitor hours wearing fake beards and "moving with the times."

## Political Interventions

### West Bank Barrier (2005, 2007, 2017)

**First Visit (August 2005):** Banksy and his team painted 9 large murals on the barrier wall that Israel built through Palestinian territories.

**Key Works:**
- *Flower Thrower / Love Is in the Air* (also in Jerusalem)
- *Armoured Dove* (dove in bulletproof vest with target)
- *Flying Balloon Girl* (girl lifted by balloons over the wall)
- Children with bucket and spades sitting by a crack showing a beach
- Girl conducting body search on Israeli soldier

**Banksy's Statement:** The wall "essentially turns Palestine into the world's largest open prison." The UN declared the 425-mile barrier illegal. The International Court of Justice ruled against it in 2004.

**Second Visit (2007):** Banksy returned with other artists to open an exhibition in Bethlehem.

**The Walled Off Hotel (2017):** Banksy opened a boutique hotel in Bethlehem directly against the separation barrier—billing itself as having "the worst view in the world." Opened to mark 100 years since the British took control of Palestine. Part hotel, part protest, part artwork exhibition.

**Controversy:** Some of Banksy's West Bank works have been removed and relocated, including a slingshot-toting rat that resurfaced in Tel Aviv in 2022. The Palestinian Tourism Ministry called it "theft of the property of the Palestinian people."

### Gaza Strip (February 2015)

**The Video:** Banksy published a 2-minute video titled "Make this the year YOU discover a new destination"—a parody travel promotion for Gaza.

**Method of Entry:** Banksy entered through an illegal tunnel.

**Footage:** Shows bombed buildings, destroyed homes, and the aftermath of the 2014 conflict. Using glossy brochure language: "nestled in an exclusive setting" and "watched over by friendly neighbours"—then the reality: "In 2014 Operation Protective Edge destroyed 18,000 homes."

**The Kitten Mural:** A white kitten with a pink bow playing with a ball of rusty wire—painted on the ruins of a house destroyed by an Israeli airstrike.

**Why a kitten?** Banksy explained: "I wanted to highlight the destruction in Gaza by posting photos on my website—but on the internet people only look at pictures of kittens."

**Other Gaza Works:**
- Children swinging from a black watchtower
- Greek goddess cowering against a destroyed concrete slab

**Closing Quote:** The video ends with Paulo Freire's words: "If we wash our hands of the conflict between the powerful and the powerless we side with the powerful—we don't remain neutral."

**Context:** Over 2,100 Palestinians were killed during the 2014 conflict, most civilians, many children. 67 Israeli soldiers and 6 civilians were killed.

## Exit Through the Gift Shop (2010)

### The Film

A documentary directed by Banksy that premiered at Sundance Film Festival on 24 January 2010. Nominated for Best Documentary Feature at the 83rd Academy Awards. 96% on Rotten Tomatoes, 85 on Metacritic.

### The Story

**Thierry Guetta** is a French immigrant in Los Angeles who obsessively films everything. In 1999, he discovers his cousin is the street artist Invader. Fascinated, Guetta begins documenting street artists at work, eventually gaining access to Shepard Fairey and then Banksy.

**The Twist:** When Banksy sees Guetta's assembled footage, he calls it "unwatchable." Instead of letting Guetta make a documentary, Banksy decides to make Guetta the subject.

**Mr. Brainwash is Born:** Banksy encourages Guetta to create his own art show. Guetta adopts the name "Mr. Brainwash," mortgages his business and home, hires a production team to create art under his supervision, and rents a 15,000 square-foot warehouse for his show "Life Is Beautiful."

**The Result:** Almost $1 million in sales the first week. The show extended two months. Guetta's work appeared in galleries worldwide. Both Banksy and Shepard Fairey expressed confusion at Guetta's success, finding his work derivative and unready.

### The Question

Is it real? Or is the entire film an elaborate Banksy prank—with Mr. Brainwash as his greatest artwork?

**Evidence of Hoax Theory:**
- Mr. Brainwash's art mimics Banksy and Warhol
- His overnight success seems too perfect a critique of the art market
- The documentary format itself may be the artwork

**Banksy's Response:** When asked if it's real, he simply said: "Yes."

**Guetta's Response:** "In the end, I became [Banksy's] biggest work of art."

### The Meaning

Whatever its truth, the film is a devastating critique of:
- Art world authenticity
- The relationship between hype and talent
- How easily spectacle creates success
- The commodification of street art
- Whether "real" art even exists

**The Greatest Trick:** The documentary makes you question what is serious and what is spoof. That uncertainty IS the art.

## Pest Control: Authentication and Commerce

### What It Is

Pest Control is the handling service acting on behalf of Banksy, established 2008-2009. It is the only official body that can authenticate Banksy artwork.

### Purpose

- Prevent fraud by authenticating genuine works
- Issue Certificates of Authenticity (COAs)
- Stop trading of public works removed from streets
- Maintain arm's-length relationship with traditional gallery system
- Run by Banksy himself

### What They Authenticate

Only pieces produced for commercial sale: limited editions and originals. Street pieces will not be certified (with rare exceptions), since they were not created to be sold.

### Process

1. Submit online form with details: edition number, dimensions, purchase date, price, provenance, previous owners
2. Pest Control verifies against their database
3. If authenticated, a Certificate of Authentication is issued
4. Costs: £50 for screen-prints, £100 for original artwork

**Key Policy:** Pest Control rarely explains why a piece fails authentication—designed to prevent forgers from reverse-engineering the process.

### The Paradox

Banksy, who critiques the art market, runs an authentication service that reinforces the market's obsession with provenance and legitimacy. The system cannot be escaped, only redirected.

## Signature Approaches

### Stencil Philosophy
Speed is essential. Stencils allow complex images to be applied in seconds. What takes hours to paint takes seconds to spray. You can be gone before anyone notices.

### The Souvenir Effect
Make work that people photograph. In the digital age, documentation is distribution. A piece on one wall becomes an image on a million screens.

### Institutional Judo
Use the art market's obsession with authenticity and rarity against it. Sell expensive pieces, then undermine their value. Make collectors complicit in the critique.

### Temporary Permanence
Street art gets painted over. That's part of its power. It existed. People saw it. Then it was gone. Impermanence is honesty—nothing lasts, not even criticism of power.

## Verified Quotes and Statements

### From Published Books

Banksy has released five official books, which serve as primary sources for authenticated quotes:
- *Banging Your Head Against a Brick Wall* (2001)
- *Existencilism* (2002)
- *Cut It Out* (2004)
- *Wall and Piece* (2005) - contains material from previous books
- *Cut And Run* (2023) - accompanying his Glasgow exhibition

### On Graffiti and Street Art

"A wall is a very big weapon. It's one of the nastiest things you can hit someone with."

"Graffiti is one of the few tools you have if you have almost nothing."

"People say graffiti is ugly, irresponsible and childish... but that's only if it's done properly."

"Imagine a city where graffiti wasn't illegal, a city where everybody could draw whatever they liked. Where every street was awash with a million colours and little phrases. Where standing at a bus stop was never boring. A city that felt like a party where everyone was invited, not just the estate agents and barons of big business. Imagine a city like that and stop leaning against the wall—it's wet."

### On Art and Artists

"Art should comfort the disturbed and disturb the comfortable."

"Think outside the box, collapse the box, and take a fucking sharp knife to it."

"Copyright is for losers."

"Every time I think I've painted something slightly original, I find out that Blek le Rat has done it as well. Only twenty years earlier."

### On Power and Institutions

"The greatest crimes in the world are not committed by people breaking the rules but by people following the rules. It's people who follow orders that drop bombs and massacre villages."

"A lot of people never use their initiative because no-one told them to."

"We can't do anything to change the world until capitalism crumbles. In the meantime we should all go shopping to console ourselves."

### On Anonymity

From a rare interview documented in The Guardian (2003): "I need to stay hidden because the day I go public is the day the graffiti ends."

"Nobody ever listened to me until they didn't know who I was."

- "THERE IS ALWAYS HOPE" (with *Girl with Balloon*)

- "WHAT ARE YOU LOOKING AT?"
