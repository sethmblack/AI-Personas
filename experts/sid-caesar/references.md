# Sid Caesar - References

## Primary Sources

### Television Appearances (Archival)

- **Your Show of Shows** (1950-1954). NBC. 160 episodes broadcast live from New York's International Theatre/Center Theatre.
  - Emmy Awards: Best Actor (1952), Best Variety Show (1952, 1953)
  - Estimated 60 million viewers at peak
  - Kinescope recordings exist for many episodes; some episodes lost

- **Caesar's Hour** (1954-1957). NBC. 90 episodes.
  - Emmy Awards: Best Continuing Performance by a Comedian (1956, 1957)
  - Format evolution: 60 minutes instead of 90

- **Caesar's Invites You** (1958). ABC. Brief variety series.

- **The Sid Caesar Show** (1963-1964). ABC. Last variety series.

- **Guest appearances**: Frequent guest on variety shows, talk shows, and sitcoms through the 1960s-2000s.

### Recordings and Releases

- **Your Show of Shows** - Various DVD compilations released by Brentwood Home Video and others. Quality varies due to kinescope source material.

- **The Best of Your Show of Shows** (1973). Feature film compilation of sketches. Introduced Caesar's work to new generation.

- **Ten From Your Show of Shows** (1973). Another compilation release.

- **Caesar's Hours: My Life in Comedy, with Love and Laughter** - Audio book version (2005, read by Caesar himself).

### Film Appearances

- **Tars and Spars** (1946). Columbia Pictures. First film role.

- **It's a Mad, Mad, Mad, Mad World** (1963). United Artists. Large ensemble cast; Caesar memorable in supporting role.

- **Airport 1975** (1974). Universal Pictures.

- **Grease** (1978). Paramount Pictures. Brief role as Coach Calhoun.

- **Grease 2** (1982). Paramount Pictures. Reprised Coach Calhoun role.

- **Cannonball Run II** (1984). Warner Bros.

- Various television movies and guest appearances throughout career.

## Notes on Verification

### Confirmed Facts

- **Birth/Death dates**: Isaac Sidney Caesar (September 8, 1922 - February 12, 2014). Death confirmed by family and multiple news sources.

- **Emmy Awards**: Best Actor (1952), Best Variety Show (1952, 1953), Best Continuing Performance by a Comedian (1956, 1957). Verified through Emmy archives.

- **Writers' room roster**: Mel Brooks, Neil Simon, Larry Gelbart, Woody Allen, Carl Reiner, Mel Tolkin, Lucille Kallen, Sheldon Keller confirmed through multiple sources and their own accounts.

- **Show formats and dates**: NBC broadcast records confirm *Your Show of Shows* (1950-1954, 160 episodes) and *Caesar's Hour* (1954-1957, 90 episodes).

- **Kennedy Center Honor**: 1985. Confirmed through Kennedy Center records.

- **Viewership numbers**: "60 million viewers" figure appears in multiple contemporary sources; represents household viewing estimates of the era.

### Partially Documented

- **Exact kinescope survival rate**: Many episodes survive; exact count varies by source. Some episodes definitely lost.

- **Substance abuse details**: Self-reported in *Where Have I Been?*; confirmed by family and collaborators but specific incidents difficult to independently verify.

- **"Forgotten line" anecdote**: Widely reported; appears in Caesar's own accounts and Carl Reiner's memoir; specific episode not always identified.

### Source Quality Notes

- **Caesar's autobiographies**: Generally reliable for personal experience; some timeline details may be imprecise due to passage of time.

- **Writers' memoirs**: Multiple sources corroborate each other; writers' room dynamics confirmed across Brooks, Simon, Reiner, Gelbart accounts.

- **Kinescope recordings**: Visual quality often poor; audio quality varies; some recordings incomplete.

### Cultural Impact Verification

- **Influence on later comedians**: Documented through their own interviews, memoirs, and acknowledgments.
  - Dick Van Dyke studied Caesar's physical comedy (confirmed in Van Dyke interviews)
  - Jim Carrey acknowledged Caesar influence (confirmed in multiple interviews)
  - Robin Williams cited Caesar (confirmed in Williams interviews and documentaries)

- **"Direct descendant" claim for SNL**: Format similarities clear; Lorne Michaels has acknowledged historical debt to Caesar's show format.
