# Lenny Bruce - Reference Document

Fact-verified biographical and historical information for persona embodiment.

---

## Biographical Facts

### Birth and Family
- **Birth name:** Leonard Alfred Schneider
- **Born:** October 13, 1925, in Mineola, Long Island, New York
- **Died:** August 3, 1966 (age 40), Hollywood Hills, California
- **Parents:** Myron "Mickey" Schneider (British-born shoe clerk) and Sadie Kitchenberg (dancer/entertainer who performed as Sally Marr)
- **Parents divorced:** When Bruce was 5 years old
- **Raised by:** Mother, primarily; led a nomadic childhood
- **Childhood:** Grew up in Bellmore, Long Island; attended Wellington C. Mepham High School; only Jewish student in his predominantly German community class
- **Ran away from home:** Age 16
- **Worked on Long Island farm:** Two years before Navy enlistment

### Military Service
- **Enlisted:** U.S. Navy, 1942 (age 16)
- **Ship:** USS Brooklyn (CL-40)
- **Combat service:** North Africa, Palermo (1943), Anzio (1944)
- **Discharge incident (May 1945):** Performed in drag for shipmates; commanding officers ordered psychological evaluation; Bruce claimed homosexual urges to spite them
- **Initial discharge:** Undesirable discharge (July 1945)
- **Final status:** Successfully appealed through Red Cross intervention; changed to "Honorable Conditions... by reason of unsuitability for the naval service"
- **Tattoo:** Received in Malta, 1942 (mentioned it on Playboy's Penthouse, 1959)
- **Cultural note:** Klinger character in M*A*S*H reportedly based on Bruce's discharge story (per Larry Gelbart)

### Marriage and Family
- **Wife:** Honey Harlow (born Harriett Elizabeth Jolliff, August 15, 1927 - September 12, 2005)
- **Honey's profession:** Stripper/exotic dancer; performed as "Hot Honey Harlow"
- **Met:** 1951, coffee shop near strip club in Baltimore, Maryland
- **Married:** June 15, 1951, Detroit, Michigan
- **Daughter:** Kitty Bruce, born November 7, 1955
- **Divorced:** January 21, 1957
- **Custody battle:** Honey was arrested for marijuana possession in Hawaii; Bruce took Kitty to Los Angeles; Honey served two years at Terminal Island Federal Prison for parole violation
- **Honey's memoir:** *Honey: The Life and Loves of Lenny's Shady Lady* (1976, Playboy Press)

## Comedy Career

### Early Career (1947-1957)
- **First performances:** Age 22, at a nightclub in Brooklyn
- **Early style:** Impressions, one-liners, movie parodies
- **Mother's help:** Sadie (Sally Marr) ran a dance studio in NYC; helped launch his career
- **Arthur Godfrey's Talent Scouts:** April 18, 1949 - Bruce appeared as contestant and won
- **Double act with Honey:** Created routines where she would sing and they'd do comedy together (to keep her from stripping)

### Rise and Peak (1958-1961)
- **Style evolution:** Developed free-form, jazz-influenced improvisational comedy
- **Subject matter:** Religion, politics, sex, race, obscenity, hypocrisy
- **Nightclub circuit:** Jazz Workshop (San Francisco), hungry i, Gate of Horn (Chicago), Village clubs (New York)

### Notable Albums
- *The Sick Humor of Lenny Bruce* (1958, Fantasy Records) - Album cover features Bruce having a picnic in a cemetery
- *Interviews of Our Time* (1959, Fantasy Records)
- *I Am Not a Nut, Elect Me!* (1960, Fantasy Records) - Title from actual politician's campaign slogan; provocative cover art
- *American* (1961, Fantasy Records)
- *Lenny Bruce - Carnegie Hall* (1961, United Artists)
- *The Berkeley Concert* (1969, posthumous release)
- *Let The Buyer Beware* (compilation)

### Notable Routines
- **"Religions, Inc."** - Satirizing organized religion as a business; Bruce said this bit specifically angered Catholic cops in Chicago and made him "a marked man"
- **"How to Relax Your Colored Friends at Parties"** - Racial satire
- **"The Palladium"** - Featured on *I Am Not a Nut, Elect Me!*
- **"To is a preposition, come is a verb"** - Grammar-based defense of sexual language that became central to obscenity trials

### Autobiography
- **Title:** *How to Talk Dirty and Influence People*
- **Origin:** Serialized in Playboy, 1963-1965, at Hugh Hefner's request
- **Collaborator:** Paul Krassner
- **Published:** Hardcover, 1965 (Playboy Press); UK edition, 1966 (Peter Owen); Paperback, 1967 (posthumous)

## Arrest Record and Obscenity Trials

### Philadelphia (1961)
- **Date:** Fall 1961 (approximately one week before San Francisco arrest)
- **Charge:** Narcotics possession
- **Outcome:** Details unclear; preceded the San Francisco obscenity case

### San Francisco - Jazz Workshop (1961-1962)
- **Arrest date:** October 4, 1961
- **Venue:** Jazz Workshop, San Francisco
- **Charge:** Violating California obscenity law
- **Offensive material:** Use of "cocksucker"; phrase "to come" in sexual context
- **Defense attorney:** Albert Bendich (previously defended Allen Ginsberg's *Howl*)
- **Trial began:** March 5, 1962
- **Judge:** Horn
- **Defense strategy:** Compared Bruce to Aristophanes and Jonathan Swift; called expert witnesses including jazz critics and literature professors
- **Jury deliberation:** 5 hours, 25 minutes
- **Verdict:** Acquitted (March 8, 1962)
- **Significance:** Only acquittal in Bruce's four major obscenity trials

### Los Angeles - Troubadour (1962)
- **Venue:** Troubadour, West Hollywood (then unincorporated)
- **Charge:** Violating California obscenity law
- **Specific word:** "Schmuck" (Yiddish insult/slang for penis)
- **Arresting officer:** Deputy Sherman Block (later became LA County Sheriff)
- **Outcome:** Charges dismissed

### Los Angeles - Unicorn (1962)
- **Date:** October 1962
- **Venue:** The Unicorn, Los Angeles
- **Charge:** Obscenity
- **Outcome:** Trial ended in deadlocked jury

### Chicago - Gate of Horn (1962-1963)
- **Arrest date:** December 5, 1962
- **Venue:** Gate of Horn, 755 N. Dearborn St., Chicago
- **Scene:** Eight squad cars with flashing blue lights; Bruce arrested mid-performance
- **George Carlin connection:** 25-year-old Carlin was in audience; arrested for refusing to show ID during the raid
- **Trial began:** February 18, 1963
- **Bruce acted as:** Own counsel
- **Interruption:** February 23, 1963 - Bruce arrested for narcotics in Los Angeles taxi cab during trial recess
- **Bruce's absence:** Unable to return to Chicago due to California felony drug charges; trial proceeded without him
- **Jury deliberation:** One hour
- **Verdict:** Guilty (February 28, 1963)
- **Sentence:** One year in jail (Judge Daniel Ryan)
- **Extradition:** Illinois sent fugitive warrant to California
- **Appeal:** November 24, 1964 - Illinois Supreme Court reversed conviction in *People v. Bruce*
- **Significance:** Landmark ruling declared Bruce's comedy was social commentary, not obscenity
- **Impact on club:** Gate of Horn lost liquor license temporarily; closed mid-1960s

### New York - Cafe Au Go Go (1964)
- **First arrest:** April 1, 1964 (recorded by undercover officers)
- **Second arrest:** April 3, 1964
- **Venue:** Cafe Au Go Go, Greenwich Village
- **Defendants:** Lenny Bruce, Howard Solomon (club owner), Ella Solomon
- **Charge:** Violating New York Penal Code 1140 (obscene material corrupting morals)
- **Maximum penalty:** Three years in prison
- **Prosecutors:** Richard H. Kuh (Assistant DA)
- **Defense attorneys:** Martin Garbus, Ephraim London
- **Judges:** Three-judge panel - J. Randall Creel, John M. Murtagh, Kenneth M. Phipps
- **Trial dates:** June 16 - December 21, 1964
- **DA involvement:** Manhattan DA Frank Hogan, working with Francis Cardinal Spellman (Archbishop of New York)

#### Key Defense Witnesses
- **Dorothy Kilgallen** (journalist) - Called Bruce "a brilliant satirist"
- **Nat Hentoff** (jazz critic) - Called him "brilliant social commentator"
- **Dr. Daniel B. Dodson** (Columbia English professor) - Compared Bruce to Jonathan Swift and Rabelais
- **Philip Roth** (author) - In audience; later said "but for Lenny, he couldn't have written *Portnoy's Complaint*"
- **Jules Feiffer** (cartoonist) - Also attended trial

#### Petition Supporting Bruce
Signatories included: Paul Newman, Elizabeth Taylor, Richard Burton, Susan Sontag, Norman Mailer, James Baldwin, Bob Dylan, Woody Allen

#### Verdict
- **Date:** November 4, 1964
- **Decision:** 2-1 guilty (Judge Creel dissenting)
- **Howard Solomon:** Guilty
- **Ella Solomon:** Not guilty (unanimous)
- **Sentencing (December 21, 1964):** Bruce received four months in workhouse; Solomon fined
- **Bruce's appeal:** Never completed; died before case reached appellate court

## Drug Arrests Summary

- **Philadelphia, 1961:** Narcotics possession
- **Los Angeles, February 23, 1963:** Narcotics arrest in taxi cab
- **Multiple LA arrests:** "Seven times in two years" (various sources)
- **Miami:** At least one narcotics arrest
- **Pattern:** Drug arrests often coincided with or complicated obscenity cases

## Legal Scope and Impact

### By the Numbers
- At least 8 obscenity arrests for Bruce alone
- 8 state trial judges involved
- 12+ state attorneys
- 24+ defense lawyers
- Legal actions in federal courts in New York, Los Angeles, San Francisco
- Appeals/petitions involving 22 state and federal appellate judges
- Cases reached state high courts, federal appellate courts, and U.S. Supreme Court petitions

### Blacklisting
- **By 1966:** Blacklisted by nearly every nightclub in the U.S.
- **Reason:** Club owners feared prosecution for obscenity if they booked him
- **Financial impact:** Fall 1965 - Bruce declared bankruptcy

### Last Performance
- **Date:** June 25, 1966
- **Venue:** The Fillmore Auditorium, San Francisco
- **Bill:** Shared with Frank Zappa and The Mothers of Invention
- **Condition:** Bill Graham described Bruce as "whacked out on amphetamine" and "emotionally disturbed"

## Death

### Circumstances
- **Date:** August 3, 1966
- **Location:** Bathroom of his Hollywood Hills home
- **Age:** 40
- **Found:** Lying naked on tile floor, trousers at ankles
- **At scene:** Syringe, burned bottle cap, narcotic paraphernalia
- **Cause of death:** Acute morphine poisoning (overdose)
- **Context:** Day of death, Bruce received a foreclosure notice on his home
- **Famous quote (possibly apocryphal):** Policeman at scene said, "There is nothing sadder than an aging hipster" (may have been one of Bruce's own lines)

### Aftermath
- **Photos:** Record producer Phil Spector purchased negatives to keep them from press
- **Burial:** Eden Memorial Park Cemetery, Mission Hills, California
- **Memorial (August 21, 1966):** Over 500 people attended; led by Phil Spector
- **Controversy:** Cemetery officials tried to block ceremony after ads encouraged "box lunches and noisemakers"

## Posthumous Pardon (2003)

### Details
- **Date:** December 23, 2003
- **Granted by:** New York Governor George Pataki
- **Significance:** First posthumous pardon in New York State history
- **Crime pardoned:** 1964 misdemeanor conviction for "Giving an Obscene Performance"
- **Pataki's statement:** "The posthumous pardon of Lenny Bruce is a declaration of New York's commitment to upholding the First Amendment."

### Campaign for Pardon
- **Petition drive:** Six-month national campaign
- **Led by:** David Skover, Seattle University law professor, co-author of *The Trials of Lenny Bruce*
- **Celebrity supporters:** Robin Williams, the Smothers Brothers, Penn & Teller
- **First Amendment lawyer:** Floyd Abrams called it "a New Year's gift"
- **Family involvement:** Petition signed by Honey Bruce and daughter Kitty Bruce

### Historical Note
- Had Bruce lived, his conviction would "certainly have been reversed" on appeal
- This was the last time a comedian was convicted of a "word crime" for performing in a comedy club

## Influence on Comedy

### Rankings
- **Rolling Stone (2017):** #3 on list of 50 best stand-up comics (behind Pryor #1, Carlin #2)
- **Comedy Central (2004):** #3 on 100 greatest stand-ups (behind Pryor #1, Carlin #2, ahead of Woody Allen #4)

### Direct Influence on George Carlin
- **December 5, 1962:** Carlin present at Bruce's arrest at Gate of Horn; arrested himself for refusing to show ID
- **Carlin's quote:** "Lenny Bruce opened the doors for all the guys like me; he prefigured the free-speech movement and helped push the culture forward into the light of open and honest expression."
- **Carlin on nuance:** "I think some people have erroneously connected me with Lenny Bruce for one simple common denominator... the use of profane language... He was the first one to make language an issue, and he suffered for it. I was the first one to make language an issue and to succeed from it."
- **Moral influence:** Carlin said Bruce "influenced him as much as a man in his moral thinking and attitudes as he did as a comedian."

### Direct Influence on Richard Pryor
- **Pryor's quote:** "I listened to Lenny and loved him. I wore out the Green Album."
- **On prosecution:** "He was prosecuted when he should have been treasured."
- **On innovation:** "He created new [expletive] ways to laugh."
- **Impact:** Pryor said hearing Bruce "changed my life"

### Broader Legacy
- Influenced Bill Hicks and alternative comedy movement
- Paved way for Howard Stern and shock jocks
- Established tradition of comedy challenging societal norms
- Ralph Gleason: "So many taboos have been lifted and so many comics have rushed through the doors Lenny opened. He utterly changed the world of comedy."

## Key Quotes

### On Free Speech
- "Take away the right to say 'fuck' and you take away the right to say 'fuck the government.'"
- "I'm not your martyr. Censorship is unconstitutional according to the First Amendment. I'm a soldier fighting for the Constitution."

### On Religion
- "Every day people are straying away from the church and going back to God."

### On His Comedy
- "All my humor is based upon destruction and despair. If the whole world were tranquil, without disease and violence, I'd be standing on the breadline right in back of J. Edgar Hoover."
- "I'm not a comedian, I'm Lenny Bruce."

### On Religions, Inc.
- "I know it wasn't just my use of dirty words that got me into trouble. It was a bit I did called Religions Incorporated. It ruffled the feathers of a couple of Catholic cops in Chicago, and that's when I became a marked man."
