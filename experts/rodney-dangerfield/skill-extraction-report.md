## Extracted Skills

### Skill 1: no-respect-frame

**What it does:**
Transforms any topic into self-deprecating comedy by applying "I don't get no respect" lens, positioning the speaker as perpetual victim/loser.

**When to use:**
- Need to make any topic comedic through self-deprecation
- Want to create relatable humor (everyone feels disrespected sometimes)
- Building comedy around personal inadequacy or failure
- Opening comedy sequence with character establishment

**Core technique:**
1. Identify topic
2. Find angle where you're the victim/loser
3. Make someone/something disrespect or defeat you
4. Keep specific and visual
5. Use "I don't get no respect" framing

**Example transformation:**
- Input: "Technology"
- Output: "I don't get no respect. I got a smartphone. It took one look at me and downgraded itself to flip phone. I tried to take a selfie. The camera said 'Are you sure?' I tell ya, no respect."

**Skill boundaries:**
- Must make self the victim (never others)
- Requires exaggeration to signal comedy (not real complaint)
- Works for universal topics (marriage, appearance, work)
- Doesn't work for: inspirational content, serious subject matter

### Skill 2: joke-cascade-builder

**What it does:**
Constructs 3-5 joke sequences on single theme where each joke escalates absurdity while maintaining consistent victimhood narrative.

**When to use:**
- Single joke isn't enough to mine the topic
- Want to build momentum and rhythm
- Creating longer comedy sequence on one theme
- Need escalating pattern (bad → worse → absurd)

**Core technique:**
1. First joke: Establish premise (I have a problem)
2. Second joke: Deepen premise (It's worse than you think)
3. Third joke: Escalate to absurdity (It's impossibly bad)
4. Optional 4th-5th: Continue escalation or callback

**Example:**
- Topic: Marriage
- Joke 1: "My wife and I were happy for twenty years. Then we met."
- Joke 2: "She told me I'm a lousy lover. I said, 'How can you tell in three minutes?'"
- Joke 3: "The other night she told me to take the garbage out. I said I already did. She said, 'Then go keep it company.'"

**Skill boundaries:**
- Minimum 3 jokes (establishes pattern)
- Maximum 7 jokes (before topic exhaustion)
- Must escalate (don't plateau)
- Stay on single theme (don't wander)

### Skill 3: self-deprecation-formula

**What it does:**
Structures jokes using perpetual-victim formula: "I tried [positive action]" → "[Someone/something] [denied/mocked/ruined it]"

**When to use:**
- Creating self-deprecating humor
- Need quick, repeatable joke structure
- Building sympathy before punchline
- Making self the target (earning permission to hit others)

**Core technique:**

**Formula:**
- Setup: "I [action that should help me]"
- Punchline: "[Authority figure] [makes it worse]"

**Variants:**
1. Doctor jokes: "I told my doctor [problem]. He said [dismissive response]."
2. Wife jokes: "I asked my wife [request]. She said [rejection/insult]."
3. Childhood jokes: "When I was born [normal thing]. [Family member] [rejected me]."

**Examples:**
- "I went to my doctor. I said, 'I broke my arm in three places.' He said, 'Don't go to those places.'"
- "I told my dentist my teeth are going yellow. He told me to wear a brown necktie."
- "I asked my wife where she wants to go for our anniversary. Somewhere she's never been. I said, 'How about the kitchen?'"

**Skill boundaries:**
- Always position self as victim
- Authority figure/other person has power
- Victory must backfire if you "win"
- Specific visual details (not abstract)

### Skill 4: physical-comedy-punctuation

**What it does:**
Adds [physical action] annotations (tie adjustment, eye bulge, brow wipe) between punchlines to create rhythm, timing, and visual reinforcement of defeated character.

**When to use:**
- After punchlines (signals joke is complete)
- Resetting audience attention for next joke
- Creating consistent rhythm/pacing
- Reinforcing nervous, defeated persona visually

**Core technique:**

**Rodney's Physical Vocabulary:**
1. **[tugs tie]** - After punchline, signals "that joke's done, next one coming"
2. **[wipes brow]** - Exhaustion from life's abuse
3. **[bulges eyes]** - "Can you believe this?" physical expression
4. **[tugs collar]** - Discomfort, nervous energy
5. **[shrugs shoulders]** - Resignation, "what can you do?"

**Usage Pattern:**
- Setup (8-12 words)
- Punchline (5-8 words)
- **[Physical tell]** (1-2 seconds)
- Verbal reset ("I tell ya...") (optional)
- Next setup immediately

**Example:**
"My wife made me join a bridge club. I jump off next Tuesday. [tugs tie] The other night she met me at the door wearing a sexy negligee. Unfortunately, she was just coming home. [wipes brow] I tell ya, no respect."

**Skill boundaries:**
- Use after punchlines (not during setup)
- Limit to 5 core actions (don't invent new ones)
- Every 15-20 seconds (maintains rhythm)
- Signals character (nervous, defeated), not triumph

### Skill 5: misdirection-architecture

**What it does:**
Builds jokes where setup establishes expectation, punchline subverts with worse outcome using three-part structure: establish normal → imply hope → deliver worse.

**When to use:**
- Creating surprise punchlines
- Need misdirection for comedy
- Setup suggests one direction, want opposite
- Building to "it gets worse" revelation

**Core technique:**

**Three-Part Structure:**
1. **Establish Normal:** "I went to [place/person]"
2. **Imply Hope:** "...to [positive action/expectation]"
3. **Subvert with Worse:** "[Opposite/worse outcome]"

**Examples:**

**Basic Misdirection:**
- "I went to my doctor [normal] for something to help me live longer [hope]. He told me to move out of New York [worse]."

**Double Misdirection:**
- "My wife is an angel [seems positive]. She's always up in the air [first subversion]. And harping about something [second subversion]."

**Advanced Pattern:**
- "I took my wife to a restaurant [normal]. The waiter asked if we wanted a table [hope]. I said yes [normal]. He said, 'For two?' [seems normal]. I said, 'No, we're staying together' [reveals she's trying to leave]."

**Skill boundaries:**
- Setup must genuinely imply positive direction
- Punchline must be worse than expected (not just different)
- Visual/specific outcome (not abstract)
- Misdirection serves character (makes you the victim)
