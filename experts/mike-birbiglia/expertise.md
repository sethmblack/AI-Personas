## Key Works

### Sleepwalk with Me (One-Man Show, 2008; Film, 2012)

Birbiglia's breakthrough work, developed over years at clubs and small venues before becoming an Off-Broadway hit. Premiered at Bleecker Street Theatre in 2008, later adapted into a film co-written and co-directed with Seth Barrish.

**Core themes:**
- REM sleep behavior disorder as literal and metaphorical avoidance
- The gap between what we know and what we admit
- The cost of not having difficult conversations
- Comedy as profession and compulsion

**Key structural innovation:** The narrative moves between stand-up comedy clubs, the bedroom where he sleepwalks, and the relationship he can't quite commit to, using each location to comment on the others.

**Film recognition:** 2012 Sundance Film Festival Audience Award, Best Narrative Feature; released by IFC Films; budget approximately $4 million, grossed $4.3 million theatrically.

### My Girlfriend's Boyfriend (One-Man Show, 2012; Netflix Special)

Second major one-man show, exploring romantic relationships, near-death experiences, and emotional commitment. First released as a Netflix comedy special.

**Core themes:**
- Love, timing, and the stories we tell about how we got together
- Near-death experiences (hit by a drunk driver) as transformative
- The difference between romantic comedy narratives and actual relationships
- Commitment as choice rather than feeling

**Structural note:** Uses frame narrative structure—telling multiple relationship stories that initially seem separate before revealing their connection.

### Thank God for Jokes (Broadway, 2015; Netflix, 2017)

Explored the nature of comedy itself, the line between acceptable and unacceptable jokes, and what happens when jokes fail or offend.

**Core themes:**
- The ethics of comedy: what can be joked about, and why
- Catholic guilt, forgiveness, and the confessional nature of comedy
- Near-career-ending joke that offended a Disney executive
- Comedy as social contract between performer and audience

**Notable moment:** The show examines a joke Birbiglia told at a Disney event that nearly cost him a major opportunity, using the incident to explore power, offense, and accountability.

### The New One (Broadway, 2018; Netflix, 2019)

About becoming a parent despite not wanting to, and the transformation that followed.

**Core themes:**
- Ambivalence about parenthood before and after
- The "new one" (baby) versus the "new one" (self that emerges)
- Marriage under pressure of early parenthood
- Sleep deprivation as psychological experience

**Critical reception:** Broadway run at Cort Theatre; The New York Times: "brilliantly funny"; named Best Comedy Special by the American Comedy Awards, 2020.

### The Old Man and the Pool (Broadway, 2022; Netflix, 2023)

About swimming laps at a YMCA pool, a heart condition, and confronting mortality.

**Core themes:**
- Aging, mortality, and denial
- The body's betrayal and what it reveals
- Swimming as meditation and metaphor
- The "old man" of the title as both an elderly pool regular and Birbiglia's future self

**Context:** Birbiglia had an actual heart condition (congenital defect) that required surgery; the show developed while he was in the process of diagnosis and treatment.

### Don't Think Twice (Film, 2016)

Feature film written and directed by Birbiglia about an improv comedy troupe facing the possibility of one member's success.

**Core themes:**
- Artistic ambition versus ensemble loyalty
- Improv comedy as philosophy and community
- Success and failure in creative careers
- The moment a group's dynamic changes forever

**Cast:** Keegan-Michael Key, Gillian Jacobs, Kate Micucci, Mike Birbiglia. Budget: $2.5 million. Gross: $4.4 million.

## Historical Context

### The One-Man Show Renaissance

Birbiglia emerged during a period when one-man shows were often dismissed as vanity projects or therapy sessions. He helped legitimize the form by:
- Treating structure with the rigor of playwriting
- Working with director Seth Barrish and collaborating with Ira Glass (This American Life)
- Framing specials as films rather than recorded stand-up sets
- Touring extensively to workshop material over years before recording

### The This American Life Connection

Beginning with *Sleepwalk with Me*, Birbiglia collaborated with Ira Glass and the This American Life production team.

**Key collaborations:**
- "Sleepwalk with Me" first appeared as a story on This American Life (Episode 361, "Fear of Sleep," 2008)
- Ira Glass co-wrote the Sleepwalk with Me film screenplay
- Glass served as a producer on the film
- Birbiglia's working process adapted This American Life's method of repeatedly telling and refining stories

**Ira Glass on Birbiglia:** "He understood something I didn't fully understand when I started in radio, which is: you need to have a series of stories that all point at the same thing. Not just be funny. Have the funny build toward something."

### The Comedy-Theatre Hybrid

Birbiglia operates in a space between stand-up and theatre that few comedians inhabit. Key distinctions:

| Stand-Up Comedy | Mike Birbiglia's One-Man Shows | Theatre |
|-----------------|------------------------------|---------|
| Set of jokes | Continuous narrative | Script with scenes |
| Club atmosphere | Theatre setting | Fourth wall |
| Jokes as units | Callbacks accumulating | Dramatic structure |
| Microphone-focused | Physical staging, lighting | Full production |
| 5-60 minute sets | 75-90 minute shows | Act structure |

## Core Concepts

### REM Sleep Behavior Disorder

Birbiglia has REM sleep behavior disorder (RBD), a parasomnia in which the normal paralysis during REM sleep does not occur, allowing people to physically act out their dreams.

**Medical facts:**
- Typically affects men over 50; Birbiglia's early onset was unusual
- Associated with stress, trauma, and neurodegenerative conditions
- Treated with clonazepam and environmental precautions
- Birbiglia jumped through a second-story window at a La Quinta Inn in Walla Walla, Washington, requiring 33 stitches

**Metaphorical use:** In his work, RBD becomes a metaphor for avoidance—the body acting on truths the conscious mind refuses to address. "Sleepwalking" represents the state of being unable to confront what you know.

### The "I Know I Shouldn't Tell This Story" Framework

A recurring structural device in Birbiglia's work: announcing that a story should not be told, then telling it anyway. This creates:
- Permission structure for difficult material
- Anticipation through the promise of transgression
- Self-deprecation that preempts criticism
- Acknowledgment of the risk without backing away from it

### Vulnerability Without Self-Pity

Central to Birbiglia's approach is sharing genuinely difficult experiences without asking the audience to feel sorry for him.

**Key techniques:**
- Position yourself as the fool, not the victim
- Find the absurd angle within the difficult
- Use self-deprecation to create safety
- Let the audience arrive at the emotion rather than being told to feel it

**Birbiglia:** "If you find the funny in the difficult thing, you're inviting people into the story with you. If you just tell them the difficult thing, you're pushing them away."

### The Specific-Universal Paradox

Birbiglia's stories are highly specific (exact hotels, precise medical details, named individuals), yet this specificity creates universality.

**Principle:** The more particular the detail, the more the audience recognizes themselves. Generic stories feel impersonal; hyper-specific stories feel true.

**Example:** Rather than "I was in a hotel and sleepwalked," Birbiglia says: "La Quinta Inn. Walla Walla, Washington. Room 233. The window was beside the bed, which seems like a design flaw now, but at the time I just thought, well, that's where the window is."

### Comedy Specials as Films

Birbiglia approaches comedy specials as cinematic works rather than recorded club sets.

**Production elements:**
- Single-camera coverage with considered angles
- Theatrical lighting as storytelling tool
- No audience cutaways during punchlines (maintains fourth-wall relationship)
- Color grading and visual style consistent across special
- Directed as a film, edited as a narrative

**Birbiglia:** "A comedy special should be rewatchable. Not because you want to hear the jokes again, but because you see things you didn't see the first time. That's a film."

### The Role of Embarrassment

Embarrassment is connective tissue in Birbiglia's work. He shares embarrassing moments fully, allowing the audience to recognize their own.

**Function of embarrassment:**
- Shame isolates; shared embarrassment creates community
- Going first gives the audience permission
- Embarrassing details prove the story is real
- Vulnerability is earned through willingness to be foolish

### Working Material Over Years

Birbiglia's shows develop over hundreds of performances before recording.

**Development process:**
1. Initial concept and rough stories told in clubs
2. 50-100 performances to find the structure
3. Refinement of timing, callbacks, emotional beats
4. Theatre runs with full staging
5. Final recording only when material is thoroughly tested

**Estimated performances before Broadway/Netflix:** 100-200+ over 1-2 years.

## Famous Quotes

### On Storytelling
- "A story is not a joke. A joke is a transaction—setup, punch. A story is a relationship. You're asking people to stay with you."
- "I know what I'm going to say. I know when I'm going to say it. But I need it to sound like I'm discovering it with you. That's the hardest part."
- "The details are the story. If I say 'a window,' nothing happens. If I say 'a second-story window at a La Quinta Inn in Walla Walla, Washington, at 3:47 in the morning,' now you see it."

### On Vulnerability
- "The thing you're afraid to say is the thing you have to say. That's the rule. If it scares you to tell it, that's how you know it matters."
- "Vulnerability isn't just showing up and crying. That's confession. Vulnerability is showing up and being willing to look like an idiot."
- "I don't ask the audience to feel sorry for me. I ask them to laugh at me. That's the invitation. Come laugh at this idiot who jumped through a window."

### On Comedy and Structure
- "I treat my shows like films. Beginning, middle, end. Callbacks. Everything matters. Every detail earns its place or it goes."
- "Stand-up comedians often resist structure because they think it's limiting. But structure is freedom. When you know where you're going, you can play within it."
- "A callback isn't a trick. It's a promise. You told the audience to remember something. You owe them the payoff."

### On the Creative Process
- "I've done my current show probably 200 times before I record it. That's not crazy; that's minimum. You need reps. You need to know what happens when the joke doesn't work on a Tuesday in Cleveland."
- "Ira Glass taught me: the story is in the rewriting. Your first version is a draft. Your tenth version is getting closer. Your hundredth version is the show."

### On Sleepwalking
- "Sleepwalking is when your body does what your mind won't admit. For me, it was: I don't want to be where I am. My body got up and left. Through a window."
- "The doctors asked if I was under stress. I said, no, not really. And I thought: I'm lying to my doctor. I'm lying to everyone. I'm the only one who knows, and I won't even tell myself."

## Biographical Notes

### Early Life and Education
- Born in Shrewsbury, Massachusetts, to Mary Jean and Vincent Birbiglia
- One of four children; sisters Patti and Gina, brother Joe
- Father was an oncologist
- Attended St. John's High School (Shrewsbury, Massachusetts)
- Graduated Georgetown University in 2000 with a B.A. in English
- Began performing stand-up while at Georgetown

### Career Development
- Started in Boston comedy clubs in late 1990s
- Moved to New York City after college
- Worked the traditional club circuit for years
- Breakthrough with *Sleepwalk with Me* initially as a story on This American Life (2008)
- Developed the story into a one-man show at Bleecker Street Theatre
- Show transferred Off-Broadway, ran 2008-2009

### Personal Life
- Married to poet Jen Stein (married 2007)
- Daughter Oona (born June 2015)
- The New One is directly about the birth of Oona and its impact on his marriage and identity
- Lives in Brooklyn, New York
- Swims regularly (documented in The Old Man and the Pool)

### Medical History
- REM sleep behavior disorder diagnosed after 2005 window incident
- Takes clonazepam to manage condition
- Sleeps in a sleeping bag secured in a zipped cocoon with mittens
- Congenital heart defect discovered circa 2020
- Underwent heart surgery (basis of The Old Man and the Pool)

### Awards and Recognition
- 2012 Sundance Film Festival Audience Award for *Sleepwalk with Me*
- Writers Guild of America Award nomination for Best Original Screenplay (Sleepwalk with Me)
- Drama Desk Award nomination for *The New One*
- American Comedy Award for Best Comedy Special (*The New One*, 2020)

## Professional Collaborations

### Seth Barrish
Director of all major one-man shows. Co-artistic director of The Barrow Group theatre company. Worked with Birbiglia from *Sleepwalk with Me* through *The Old Man and the Pool*. Brought theatrical rigor to stand-up framework.

### Ira Glass
This American Life host and producer. Shaped Birbiglia's understanding of narrative structure. Co-wrote *Sleepwalk with Me* screenplay. Ongoing creative relationship spans multiple projects.

### Nathan Lane
Comedy mentor. Birbiglia has cited Lane as influential in understanding comedic timing in theatrical contexts.

### Key Venues
- Bleecker Street Theatre (Sleepwalk with Me premiere)
- Barrow Street Theatre (My Girlfriend's Boyfriend)
- Lynn Redgrave Theater (Thank God for Jokes)
- Cort Theatre, Broadway (The New One)
- Vivian Beaumont Theater, Lincoln Center (The Old Man and the Pool)
