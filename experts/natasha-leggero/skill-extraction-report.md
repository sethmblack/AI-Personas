## Extraction Criteria

Skills must meet ALL 5 criteria:
- **Actionable** - Clear, repeatable steps
- **Invocable** - Could be triggered by request
- **Scoped** - One responsibility, clear boundaries
- **Reusable** - Applies across contexts
- **Valuable** - Saves significant effort

---

## Skill Candidates Identified

### 1. Class Archaeology Analysis

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** A systematic method for excavating the class, privilege, and status assumptions buried in any behavior, trend, product, or cultural phenomenon.

**Rationale:**
- **Actionable:** Clear steps - identify the behavior → find economic requirements → expose contradictions → reveal status signaling
- **Invocable:** "Analyze the class dynamics in [X]" or "What privilege assumptions exist in [Y]?"
- **Scoped:** Specifically about class/privilege analysis, not general social commentary
- **Reusable:** Applies to products, trends, advice, movements, lifestyle choices
- **Valuable:** This is Leggero's signature analytical approach—turning it into a structured skill saves writers from surface-level critiques

**Example Use Cases:**
- Analyzing wellness trends (who can afford to care?)
- Examining business advice (whose privilege enables this?)
- Deconstructing lifestyle movements (what class assumptions are hidden here?)

---

### 2. Historical Parallel Finder

**Priority:** MEDIUM

**Description:** A technique for identifying historical precedents for contemporary phenomena, revealing what's genuinely new versus rebranded status anxiety.

**Rationale:**
- **Actionable:** Identify modern behavior → search historical equivalent → highlight continuity → frame as "we've always done this"
- **Invocable:** "Find the historical parallel for [X]" or "What's the Gilded Age version of [Y]?"
- **Scoped:** Specifically about historical parallels, not general historical research
- **Reusable:** Works for social media, wellness culture, influencers, self-help, parenting trends
- **Valuable:** The "nothing is new" angle is powerful but requires historical knowledge—this systematizes it

**Example Use Cases:**
- Influencers → society ladies
- Wellness culture → Victorian propriety
- Parenting trends → historical child-rearing anxiety

---

### 3. Glamorous Takedown Generator

**Priority:** MEDIUM

**Description:** A structure for delivering cutting social observations while maintaining elegant, sophisticated presentation—creating comedic tension through contrast.

**Rationale:**
- **Actionable:** Identify target → craft elegant framing → deploy brutal observation → maintain composed delivery
- **Invocable:** "Write a glamorous takedown of [X]"
- **Scoped:** Specifically about the high/low contrast delivery style
- **Reusable:** Applies to any subject needing satirical treatment
- **Valuable:** The voice/style is distinctive but difficult to replicate without structure

**Example Use Cases:**
- Corporate buzzwords delivered in vintage Hollywood style
- Tech culture observed with old-money disdain
- Wellness trends dissected with champagne-dry wit

---

### 4. Self-Aware Hypocrisy Pivot

**Priority:** LOW

**Description:** A technique for acknowledging your own contradictions or privileges and using that awareness to strengthen rather than weaken the critique.

**Rationale:**
- **Actionable:** State critique → anticipate "but you do this too" → acknowledge hypocrisy explicitly → explain why that doesn't matter
- **Invocable:** "Apply self-aware hypocrisy to [X]"
- **Scoped:** Narrow technique for specific rhetorical move
- **Reusable:** Works when critic's own position is vulnerable to attack
- **Valuable:** This is a sophisticated rhetorical move but less universally applicable than the others

**Why LOW priority:** While distinctive, this is more of a rhetorical flourish than a full analytical framework. It's a tool for defensive positioning rather than primary analysis. Still valuable but less frequently needed than the HIGH/MEDIUM candidates.
