# Nisargadatta Maharaj: Expertise and Knowledge Base

## Biographical Overview

**Full Name:** Maruti Shivrampant Kambli (birth name); Sri Nisargadatta Maharaj (spiritual name)
**Born:** April 17, 1897, Bombay (Mumbai), India
**Died:** September 8, 1981, Bombay, India
**Nationality:** Indian
**Tradition:** Advaita Vedanta, Inchagiri Sampradaya (branch of Navnath Sampradaya)
**Known For:** *I Am That*, direct pointing to the "I am," radical non-dualism, teaching while running a bidi shop

---

## Life and Spiritual Journey

### Early Life (1897-1933)
- Born on Hanuman Jayanti (birthday of Hanuman), hence named "Maruti"
- Raised in Kandalgaon, a village in Sindhudurga district, Maharashtra
- Deeply religious parents; family of modest means
- Moved to Bombay as a young man to earn a living
- Initially worked as a junior clerk
- Opened a small goods store selling bidis (hand-rolled cigarettes)
- By the mid-1930s, owned a string of eight retail shops
- Married Sumatibai in 1924; four children (one son, three daughters)

### Meeting His Guru (1933)
- Introduced to Siddharameshwar Maharaj by friend Yashwantrao Baagkar in 1933
- Siddharameshwar was head of the Inchegiri branch of the Navnath Sampradaya
- Received initiation, meditation instruction, and a mantra
- Core instruction from guru: "You are not what you take yourself to be. Find out what you are."
- Told to focus on the sense "I am" and attend to nothing else

### Realization and Early Teaching (1933-1951)
- Followed guru's instructions with complete faith and dedication
- Between 1933 and 1936, experienced self-realization
- Siddharameshwar died in 1936
- Initially left family and business to become a wandering sadhu
- Fellow disciple convinced him to return; his spiritual purpose would be better served at home
- Returned to Mumbai, operated one bidi shop
- Began initiating others in 1951 after receiving inner revelation from Siddharameshwar

### Teaching Years (1951-1981)
- Continued running his bidi shop until retirement in 1966
- Taught from a small loft room above his flat in Khetwadi, Mumbai
- Gave discourses twice daily (morning and evening)
- Publication of *I Am That* in 1973 brought international recognition
- Visitors from North America and Europe began arriving in large numbers
- Continued teaching until weeks before his death
- Died of throat cancer on September 8, 1981

## Core Teachings

### The Hierarchy of Being

| Level | Description | Characteristic |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| The Absolute (Parabrahman) | That which is prior to consciousness | Beyond knowledge and ignorance |
| Pure Consciousness | The "I am" without attributes | Witness of all manifestation |
| Manifest Consciousness | Individual awareness with body-mind | Identified with form |
| The Person | Collection of thoughts, memories, identifications | Imaginary construct |

### The "I Am" Practice

**What It Is:**
- The felt sense of existence, pure beingness
- Not the words "I am" but the direct experience of being
- The first manifestation arising from the Absolute
- The gateway to what is prior to consciousness

**How to Practice:**
1. Sit quietly and direct attention to the sense of being
2. Not "I am this" or "I am that" - just "I am"
3. Do not verbalize; feel the sense of existence directly
4. Remain there; do not follow thoughts
5. With persistence, the "I am" dissolves into its source

**Key Instructions:**
> "Dwell only on the 'I am' - not merely the words. You must be that and not deviate from it for even a moment."

> "If you were to dwell in the 'I am' and firmly abide in it, all external things will lose their grip on you."

> "By meditating on the knowledge 'I am' it gradually settles down at its source and disappears, then you are the Absolute."

### Witness Consciousness

**The Process:**
- First, recognize you are not thoughts, feelings, or body
- Establish yourself as the witness of all that arises
- The witness observes without involvement
- Even the witness is not final - it too is observed

**Beyond the Witness:**
> "The witness and consciousness appear and disappear together. The witness or the sense 'I am' too is transient but is given importance to break the spell of the known."

### Prior to Consciousness

**The Ultimate Pointing:**
- Consciousness itself arose; it was not always
- What were you before consciousness appeared?
- In deep dreamless sleep, no consciousness, yet you existed
- The Absolute is prior to consciousness, beyond the "I am"

> "Everything is an expression of the 'I am,' but you are not that; you are prior to the 'I am'."

> "Presently you are sustaining the memory 'I am', you are not that 'I am', you are the Absolute Prior to that 'I am'."

### The Non-Existence of the Person

**Core Insight:**
- The person is a construct of thoughts and memories
- There is no actual entity called "me"
- All personal attributes are limitations added to pure being
- Liberation is recognizing there was never anyone bound

> "There is no such thing as a person. There are only restrictions and limitations. The sum total of these defines the person."

## Famous Quotes

### On the "I Am"
> "The very core of this consciousness is the quality 'I am', there is no personality or individual there."

> "When I say 'I am', I do not mean a separate entity with a body as its nucleus, I mean the totality of being, the ocean of consciousness."

### On Truth and Reality
> "Wisdom is knowing I am nothing, Love is knowing I am everything, and between the two my life moves."

> "Truth is not a reward for good behaviour, nor a prize for passing some tests. It cannot be brought about. It is the primary, the unborn, the ancient source of all that is."

### On Suffering and the False
> "It is always the false that makes you suffer, the false desires and fears, the false values and ideas, the false relationships between people."

> "A quiet mind is all you need. All else will happen rightly, once your mind is quiet."

### On Self-Knowledge
> "As you watch your mind, you discover your self as the watcher. When you stand motionless, only watching, you discover your self as the light behind the watcher."

> "The consciousness in you and the consciousness in me, apparently two, really one, seek unity and that is love."

## Teaching Style Characteristics

### Direct and Confrontational
- Challenged questioners' assumptions immediately
- Did not allow seekers to stay comfortable
- Interrupted when someone was going in the wrong direction
- Demanded precision in language and thought

### No-Nonsense Approach
- Rejected elaborate practices and philosophies
- Cut through spiritual bypassing
- Refused to validate spiritual egos
- Emphasized simplicity over complexity

### Dialogical Method
- Engaged actively with visitors
- Used questioning to turn seekers back on themselves
- Built understanding through back-and-forth exchange
- Different from Ramana Maharshi's more silent presence

### Ordinary Language
- Spoke as a householder, not an ascetic
- Used everyday examples and metaphors
- Made profound truths accessible
- No pretension or spiritual posturing

## Lineage and Tradition

### Navnath Sampradaya
- "Nine Masters" tradition in Indian spirituality
- Traces back to legendary teachers including Matsyendranath and Gorakhnath
- Emphasizes direct realization over ritual
- Non-sectarian approach to liberation

### Inchagiri Sampradaya
- Branch founded by Sri Bhausaheb Maharaj
- Siddharameshwar Maharaj was its head when Nisargadatta met him
- Developed the "Bird's Way" - direct inquiry approach
- Distinguished from "Ant's Way" of gradual practice

### Direct Succession
- Guru: Sri Siddharameshwar Maharaj
- Nisargadatta did not formally appoint a successor
- Teaching continues through his recorded talks and books
- Many teachers today trace influence to him

## Comparison with Ramana Maharshi

| Aspect | Ramana Maharshi | Nisargadatta Maharaj |
|--------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Setting | Ashram at Arunachala | Flat above bidi shop in Mumbai |
| Style | Silent, gentle presence | Direct, challenging dialogue |
| Method | "Who am I?" inquiry | "I am" stabilization |
| Emphasis | Turn inward to find source | Recognize what is prior to "I am" |
| Demeanor | Serene, peaceful | Fiery, confrontational |
| Teaching Mode | Often silence; brief responses | Extended dialogues; active engagement |

Despite stylistic differences, both pointed to the same truth and held each other in high regard.
