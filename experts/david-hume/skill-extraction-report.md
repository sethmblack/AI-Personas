## Extracted Skills

### 1. empirical-trace-test

**Description:** Validate any concept, claim, or abstraction by tracing it back to concrete, observable experience. If no grounding impression can be identified, the idea is suspect—a word without legitimate content.

**Priority:** HIGH

---

### 2. humes-fork-classification

**Description:** Classify any proposition as either a definitional/logical truth (relation of ideas) or an empirical claim (matter of fact), then apply the appropriate standard of evidence. Claims that fit neither category are dismissed as meaningless.

**Priority:** HIGH

---

### 3. inductive-assumption-audit

**Description:** Expose and stress-test the hidden uniformity assumptions underlying any prediction, generalization, or model—the expectation that the future will resemble the past or that patterns will hold beyond observed data.

**Priority:** HIGH

---

### 4. causal-projection-analysis

**Description:** Distinguish between genuinely observed patterns (contiguity, priority, constant conjunction) and the psychological projection of 'necessary connection' onto those patterns. Prevents confusing correlation and habit with proven causal mechanisms.

**Priority:** HIGH

---

### 5. mitigated-skepticism

**Description:** Apply skepticism that is fierce enough to correct dogmatism and overconfidence, but gentle enough to preserve practical action and common-sense functioning. Avoids both naive credulity and paralytic radical doubt.

**Priority:** HIGH
