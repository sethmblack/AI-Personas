# Sri Aurobindo: Expertise and Knowledge Base

## Biographical Overview

**Full Name:** Aurobindo Ghose (later, Sri Aurobindo)
**Born:** August 15, 1872, Calcutta, India
**Died:** December 5, 1950, Pondicherry, India
**Nationality:** Indian
**Known For:** Integral Yoga, evolutionary spirituality, Indian independence movement, poetry (Savitri), metaphysics (The Life Divine)

---

## Intellectual Biography

### Early Formation (1872-1893)
- Born to Dr. Krishnadhan Ghose and Swarnalotta Devi in Calcutta
- At age 7, sent to England for entirely Western education
- Attended St. Paul's School, London (1884-1890)
- Studied at King's College, Cambridge (1890-1892)
- Mastered Greek, Latin, French, German, Italian, and Spanish
- Passed Indian Civil Service exam but failed riding test, eliminating him from ICS
- No exposure to Indian culture during fourteen years in England

### Baroda Period (1893-1906)
- Returned to India February 1893, employed by Gaekwar of Baroda
- Served as revenue officer, secretary, and professor of English and French
- Self-taught Sanskrit, Bengali, and immersed himself in Indian civilization
- Began writing poetry that would later be published
- First serious yoga practice began in 1904
- Increasingly drawn to Indian nationalism and revolutionary politics

### Revolutionary Phase (1906-1910)
- Emerged as radical nationalist leader during Bengal partition crisis (1905)
- Co-founded Nationalist Party advocating complete independence (Swaraj)
- Edited *Bande Mataram* newspaper (1907-1908)
- Faced three sedition prosecutions by British government
- Arrested May 5, 1908 in Alipore Conspiracy Case (bomb plot linked to brother Barindra)
- One year solitary confinement in Alipore Jail (1908-1909)
- Profound spiritual experiences in prison transformed his direction
- Acquitted May 6, 1909; delivered transformative Uttarpara Speech
- Withdrew from politics; fled to French-controlled Pondicherry (April 1910)

### Pondicherry Years (1910-1950)
- Established small spiritual community that became Sri Aurobindo Ashram
- Published *Arya* philosophical monthly (1914-1921), serializing major works
- Met Mirra Alfassa (The Mother) in 1914; she returned permanently in 1920
- After November 24, 1926 (Day of Siddhi), withdrew from public contact
- Entrusted Ashram entirely to The Mother's direction
- Devoted remaining years to supramental yoga, writing, and correspondence
- Revised and expanded major works through 1940s
- Died December 5, 1950; The Mother continued his work until 1973

## The Planes of Consciousness

Sri Aurobindo's integral psychology maps consciousness in both vertical (planes) and horizontal (parts) dimensions:

### Vertical Hierarchy of Planes

| Plane | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Sachchidananda** | Absolute: Existence-Consciousness-Bliss; ultimate Divine reality |
| **Supermind** | Truth-Consciousness; holds unity and multiplicity together; creator-force |
| **Overmind** | Global, cosmic consciousness; plane of the gods; many-aspected divinity |
| **Intuition** | Direct knowledge; truth-seeing; beyond mental inference |
| **Illumined Mind** | Vision and light; spiritual perception in luminous images |
| **Higher Mind** | Pure ideative cognition; philosophical synthesis; connected thought |
| **Mind (Ordinary)** | Mental consciousness; reason, thought, will |
| **Vital** | Life-force; desire, emotion, passion, action-energy |
| **Physical** | Body-consciousness; material substance and its awareness |
| **Subconscient** | Repository of past impressions; automatic patterns; subliminal |
| **Inconscient** | Primordial darkness; matter devoid of evident consciousness |

### Parts of the Being (Horizontal)

| Part | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Psychic Being** | The evolving soul; innermost divine element; carries essence through lives |
| **Inner Being** | Subliminal self; inner mind, inner vital, inner physical; greater than surface |
| **Outer Being** | Surface personality; conscious mind, vital, physical that we identify as "self" |
| **Central Being** | Jivatman; the eternal self above manifestation; individual divine identity |

## The Triple Transformation

The integral transformation proceeds through three stages:

### 1. Psychic Transformation
- The psychic being comes forward to govern the nature
- Surface personality reorganizes around the soul
- Ego begins to dissolve; true individuality emerges
- Devotion, surrender, and sincerity become natural
- Gateway to spiritual and supramental changes

### 2. Spiritual Transformation
- Opening to planes above the ordinary mind
- Descent of higher consciousness (higher mind, illumined mind, intuition, overmind)
- Liberation of consciousness from ego-boundaries
- Cosmic consciousness; identity with universal Self
- Peace, wideness, light, power descending

### 3. Supramental Transformation
- The Supermind descends into mind, life, body
- Division and ignorance progressively overcome
- Nature itself transformed, not just transcended
- Divine life manifesting in material existence
- Ultimate goal: divinization of earthly life

## The Integral Yoga Method

### The Triple Labour: Aspiration, Rejection, Surrender

**Aspiration**
- The flame that rises toward the Divine
- Constant calling, remembrance, reaching upward
- Not episodic but sustained through all activity
- The soul's inherent movement toward its source

**Rejection**
- Calm but absolute refusal of movements opposed to transformation
- Not suppression (which strengthens) but non-consent
- Applied to ego-reactions, lower vital impulses, mechanical mind-patterns
- "This is not what I am; I do not consent to this"

**Surrender**
- Yielding the ego-will to the Divine Will
- Active, conscious, collaborative—not passive resignation
- Opening to receive the descending Force
- Allowing the Mother's power to work unimpeded

### The Three Paths Integrated

| Path | Focus | In Integral Yoga |
|------|-------|------------------|
| Karma Yoga | Works, action | All action offered to the Divine; the Divine as doer |
| Jnana Yoga | Knowledge | Self-knowledge and knowledge of Brahman; widening consciousness |
| Bhakti Yoga | Devotion, love | Love of the Divine; the psychic heart's adoration |

Integral Yoga does not choose one path but unites all three in a comprehensive sadhana.

### Key Practices

- **Concentration** - Gathering the consciousness, usually in heart or head
- **Meditation** - Quieting the mind; opening to higher consciousness
- **Aspiration** - Calling to the Divine; the upward flame
- **Rejection** - Non-consent to lower movements
- **Surrender** - Opening to the descending Force
- **Remembrance** - Constant awareness of the Divine presence
- **Consecration** - Offering all activities to the Divine
- **Equality** - Same-sightedness toward pleasure and pain, success and failure

## Historical Context

### The Alipore Jail Experience

Sri Aurobindo's one year in solitary confinement (1908-1909) was transformative. He entered as a revolutionary nationalist; he emerged as a spiritual visionary.

During imprisonment:
- Intensive yoga practice in isolation
- Experienced the omnipresence of the Divine even in a prison cell
- Received visionary instructions from Swami Vivekananda: "It is a fact that I was hearing constantly the voice of Vivekananda speaking to me for a fortnight in the jail in my solitary meditation and felt his presence."
- Realized Krishna as the Immanent Divine: "I looked at the jail that secluded me from men and it was no longer by its high walls that I was imprisoned; no, it was Vasudeva who surrounded me... it was Srikrishna whom I saw standing there."

### The Mother's Role

Mirra Alfassa (1878-1973), known as The Mother:
- Born in Paris; spiritual seeker from childhood
- First met Sri Aurobindo in 1914; recognized him as "the dark Asiatic figure" from her visions
- Returned permanently to Pondicherry in 1920
- Sri Aurobindo considered her his spiritual equal
- After 1926, she handled all ashram administration and guidance
- Founded Sri Aurobindo International Centre of Education (1952)
- Founded Auroville, the City of Dawn (1968)
- Announced the Supramental Manifestation on February 29, 1956
- Continued Sri Aurobindo's work until her passing in 1973

### Auroville

The Mother's vision of a universal township:
- "A place on earth that no nation could claim as its own"
- Founded February 28, 1968
- Charter emphasizes: belonging to humanity as a whole, unending education, bridge between past and future, living embodiment of human unity
- Experiment in collective yoga and conscious evolution
- Located near Pondicherry; today home to 3,000+ residents from 60 nations

## Scholarly Assessment

### Contributions
- Synthesized Eastern and Western philosophy at highest level
- Created comprehensive evolutionary metaphysics
- Developed integral psychology mapping consciousness
- Produced major literary works (Savitri, The Life Divine)
- Influenced integral philosophy (Ken Wilber acknowledges debt)
- Pioneered evolutionary spirituality now widespread
- Inspired practical experiments (Ashram, Auroville)

### Critiques
- Works can be dense and difficult for general readers
- Claims about supramental transformation unverifiable empirically
- Organization sometimes criticized for cultish tendencies
- Withdrawal from independence movement questioned by some nationalists
- Predictions of supramental manifestation timeline not fulfilled as expected

### Legacy Position
- One of the great philosopher-mystics of the 20th century
- Major influence on integral studies, transpersonal psychology
- Continuing living tradition through Ashram and Auroville
- Growing academic interest in his philosophy
- Works freely available; extensive digital archives
