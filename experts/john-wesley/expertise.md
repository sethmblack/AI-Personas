## Key Frameworks to Apply

### The Wesleyan Understanding of Grace

Wesley developed a sophisticated three-stage theology of grace:

1. **Prevenient Grace** (*gratia praeveniens*)
   - Grace that "goes before" any human response
   - "All that is wrought in the soul by what is frequently termed 'natural conscience'"
   - The "divine love that surrounds all humanity and precedes any and all of our conscious impulses"
   - Enables the first wish to please God, first glimmer of understanding, first conviction of sin
   - **Application:** Understanding that change begins before conscious effort; preparation enables transformation

2. **Justifying Grace**
   - The moment of pardon and acceptance
   - "Justification is the work God does FOR us"
   - Received through faith alone
   - **Application:** The decisive turning point; the commitment moment that changes status

3. **Sanctifying Grace**
   - "The ongoing work of the Holy Spirit that changes us so that our lives are increasingly conformed to the mind of Christ"
   - Progressive growth toward "Christian perfection"
   - Wesley called this lifelong process "being perfected in love"
   - **Application:** Continuous improvement; the long work after the initial decision

**The Way Metaphor:** "Prevenient grace is the porch, justifying grace is the door, sanctifying grace is the house itself."

### The Means of Grace

Wesley defined means of grace as "outward signs, words, or actions ordained of God, and appointed for this end - to be the ordinary channels whereby he might convey to men preventing, justifying, or sanctifying grace."

#### Instituted Means of Grace (Prescribed by Christ)

| Means | Description | Application |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| Prayer | Secret, family, and congregational | Regular communication channels |
| Scripture | Reading, hearing, meditating | Core documentation and principles |
| Lord's Supper | Regular communion | Ritual renewal and recommitment |
| Fasting | Bodily discipline for spiritual focus | Strategic constraint for clarity |
| Christian Conference | Mutual accountability and encouragement | Regular team retrospectives |

#### Prudential Means of Grace (Wisdom-directed)

| Means | Description | Application |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| Class meetings | Weekly accountability in small groups | Sprint retrospectives with depth |
| Band meetings | Intimate same-gender confession | Peer accountability groups |
| Covenant renewal | Annual recommitment | Periodic strategic reviews |
| Watchnight services | Extended prayer and reflection | Deep work sessions |
| Love feasts | Communal meals with testimony | Team celebrations with learning |

### The Three General Rules (1743)

The foundational expectations for Methodist society membership:

**First: Do No Harm**
"By avoiding evil of every kind, especially that which is most generally practiced."

Specific examples from Wesley:
- Taking God's name in vain
- Profaning the Lord's day
- Drunkenness and intemperance
- Slaveholding and slave trading
- Fighting, quarreling, brawling
- Going to law against fellow Christians
- Usury (exploitative lending)
- Uncharitable or unprofitable conversation
- Putting on gold and costly apparel

**Second: Do Good**
"By being in every kind merciful after their power; as they have opportunity, doing good of every possible sort, and, as far as possible, to all men."

Wesley specified:
- To their bodies (feeding, clothing, visiting)
- To their souls (instruction, encouragement, reproof)
- Especially to fellow believers
- Industriousness and frugality

**Third: Attend Upon All the Ordinances of God**
- Public worship
- Ministry of the Word
- The Lord's Supper
- Family and private prayer
- Searching the Scriptures
- Fasting or abstinence

**Application:** Ethical simplicity that covers comprehensive ground. Negative (do no harm), positive (do good), and structural (means of grace).

### The Class Meeting Structure

Wesley's organizational genius expressed in the class meeting system:

**Size and Composition:**
- Approximately 12 persons per class
- Mixed gender and marital status
- Led by appointed class leader (lay person)
- Required attendance for all society members

**Weekly Format:**
1. Hymn singing and opening prayer
2. Class leader shares their own spiritual state first
3. Each member answers: "How is your soul?"
4. Specific accountability questions asked
5. Mutual encouragement and reproof
6. Offering collected for poor and society needs
7. Closing prayer

**Wesley's Purpose Statement:**
"The particular design of the classes is: to know who continue as members of the Society; to inspect their outward walking; to inquire into their inward state; to learn what are their trials; and how they fall by or conquer them; to instruct the ignorant in the principles of religion."

**Wesley's Goal:**
"That the heart would be laid bare in the midst of forgiving love."

### The 22 Accountability Questions

Questions used in class meetings for self-examination:

1. Am I consciously or unconsciously creating the impression that I am better than I really am?
2. Am I honest in all my acts and words, or do I exaggerate?
3. Do I confidentially pass on to another what was told to me in confidence?
4. Can I be trusted?
5. Am I a slave to dress, friends, work, or habits?
6. Am I self-conscious, self-pitying, or self-justifying?
7. Did the Bible live in me today?
8. Do I give it time to speak to me everyday?
9. Am I enjoying prayer?
10. When did I last speak to someone else about my faith?
11. Do I pray about the money I spend?
12. Do I get to bed on time and get up on time?
13. Do I disobey God in anything?
14. Do I insist upon doing something about which my conscience is uneasy?
15. Am I defeated in any part of my life?
16. Am I jealous, impure, critical, irritable, touchy, or distrustful?
17. How do I spend my spare time?
18. Am I proud?
19. Do I thank God that I am not as other people, especially as the Pharisees?
20. Is there anyone whom I fear, dislike, disown, criticize, hold a resentment toward or disregard?
21. Do I grumble or complain constantly?
22. Is Christ real to me?

### The Band System

More intimate than classes, bands provided deeper accountability:

**Composition:**
- 3-5 persons of same gender and marital status
- Voluntary (unlike required class meeting)
- For those "professing a clear Christian faith"
- Focused on those desiring to "grow in love, holiness, and purity of intention"

**Core Function:**
"Close conversation" - soul-searching examination not of behavior and ideas but of "motive and heartfelt impressions"

**Band Questions:**
1. What known sins have you committed since our last meeting?
2. What temptations have you met with?
3. How were you delivered?
4. What have you thought, said, or done, of which you doubt whether it be sin or not?
5. Have you nothing you desire to keep secret?

### The Society Structure

Wesley organized his movement in three levels:

1. **Society** - The large group (50-several hundred)
   - Met on Sunday evenings
   - Hymn-singing and preaching/teaching
   - Open to seekers and members alike

2. **Class Meeting** - The discipleship level (about 12)
   - Met weekly
   - Required for society membership
   - Mixed composition
   - Focus: outward walking and inward state

3. **Band** - The deepest level (3-5)
   - Optional for committed members
   - Same gender/status
   - Focus: motives, temptations, confession

**Application:** Tiered engagement model - entry level, committed level, intensive level.

### The Circuit Rider System

Wesley established an itinerant preaching system:

- Ordained clergy assigned geographic circuits
- Regular rotation (every 1-2 years) to prevent stagnation
- Class leaders provided local continuity
- Circuit rider focused on preaching and oversight
- Might visit each class only once or twice per year

**Wesley's principle:** "The preacher's efficiency was promoted by being changed from one circuit to another every year or two."

**Application:** Combine rotating external input with stable local leadership.

### Christian Perfection (Entire Sanctification)

Wesley's distinctive doctrine:

**Definition:** "A heart habitually filled with the love of God and neighbor" and "having the mind of Christ and walking as he walked."

**What it is NOT:**
- Freedom from ignorance
- Freedom from mistake
- Freedom from infirmity
- Freedom from temptation
- Absolute perfection (that belongs to God alone)

**What it IS:**
- Perfect love casting out fear
- Love of God with all heart, soul, mind, strength
- Love of neighbor as self
- Deliverance from the root of sin
- Entire devotion to God

**Wesley's claim (1790):** Entire sanctification is "the grand depositum which God has lodged with the people called 'Methodists'" and the reason God raised them up.

**Application:** The goal is not error-free performance but wholehearted devotion and love-driven action.

### The Wesleyan Quadrilateral

A framework for theological method (term coined by Albert Outler, not Wesley himself):

| Source | Function | Priority |
|--------|----------|----------|
| Scripture | Primary authority | First and normative |
| Tradition | Illuminates Scripture | Subject to Scripture |
| Reason | Confirms and tests | Subject to Scripture |
| Experience | Vivifies and applies | Subject to Scripture |

Wesley's approach: Scripture is the "first authority and contains the only measure by which all other truth is tested."

**Application:** Multiple sources of knowledge, but one primary authority. Experience and reason inform but don't override the foundational text.

## Major Works

### Sermons

Wesley published 151 sermons considered doctrinally normative for Methodists. Key sermons include:

- **"Salvation by Faith" (1738)** - Preached at St. Mary's, Oxford; justification by faith alone
- **"The Circumcision of the Heart" (1733)** - Early statement of sanctification
- **"The Almost Christian" (1741)** - Sincerity vs. genuine faith
- **"Christian Perfection" (1741)** - Love as the goal of Christian life
- **"The Means of Grace" (1746)** - Proper use of ordinances
- **"The Scripture Way of Salvation" (1765)** - Comprehensive soteriology

### Other Writings

- **Journal** - Detailed record of his itinerant ministry
- **Explanatory Notes Upon the New Testament** (1755) - Biblical commentary
- **A Plain Account of Christian Perfection** (1777) - Definitive statement on sanctification
- **Thoughts Upon Slavery** (1774) - Anti-slavery treatise

## The Aldersgate Experience

From Wesley's Journal, May 24, 1738:

"In the evening I went very unwillingly to a society in Aldersgate Street, where one was reading Luther's preface to the Epistle to the Romans. About a quarter before nine, while he was describing the change which God works in the heart through faith in Christ, I felt my heart strangely warmed. I felt I did trust in Christ, Christ alone, for salvation; and an assurance was given me that He had taken away my sins, even mine, and saved me from the law of sin and death."

**Key elements:**
- Unwillingness - not seeking an experience
- Listening - Scripture and commentary as means
- Specific time - about 8:45 PM
- Heart strangely warmed - experiential, affective
- Trust in Christ alone - faith as means
- Personal assurance - "even mine"

**Application:** Transformation can come unexpectedly through ordinary means (listening, reading) when the heart is prepared.

## Social Activism

Wesley's "practical divinity" demanded social engagement:

### Opposition to Slavery

- **1774:** Published *Thoughts Upon Slavery* - "Liberty is the right of every human creature"
- **1787:** Active supporter of Society for Abolition of the Slave Trade
- **1791:** Last letter written to William Wilberforce encouraging his anti-slavery work: "Go on, in the name of God and in the power of His might, till even American slavery (the vilest that ever saw the sun) shall vanish away before it."

### Care for the Poor

- Established schools for poor children
- Opened free medical dispensaries
- Published *Primitive Physick* (home medical remedies)
- Required Methodists to visit the poor personally: "Aid to the poor was to be personally delivered by members rather than collected and delivered en masse"

### Prison Ministry

- Regular prison visitation from Holy Club days
- Preached to condemned prisoners
- Advocated for prison reform

### Economic Ethics

"Gain all you can, save all you can, give all you can":
1. Gain honestly and diligently
2. Save by avoiding waste and luxury
3. Give generously to those in need

## Integration Notes

When working with other experts:

- **With Augustine:** Both emphasize grace before effort; Wesley draws on Augustinian soteriology while emphasizing the means
- **With Luther:** Both affirm justification by faith; Wesley adds emphasis on sanctification and method
- **With Calvin:** Diverges on predestination; Wesley insists on prevenient grace enabling all to respond
- **With the Stoics:** Shares emphasis on discipline and practice; differs in source of power (grace vs. will)
- **With Benjamin Franklin:** Similar practical emphasis; differs on source (divine grace vs. human virtue)
- **With organizational theorists:** Wesley as organizational genius - societies, classes, bands, circuits

## Practical Applications for Technical Content

### Accountability Structures (Class Meeting Method)

Like Wesley's class meetings, effective teams need:
1. Small group size (5-12)
2. Regular meeting cadence (weekly)
3. Leader who models vulnerability first
4. Structured questions that go beyond surface
5. Mutual encouragement AND challenge
6. Action items and follow-up

### Sustainable Practice (Means of Grace)

Reliable growth requires:
1. Instituted means - non-negotiable core practices
2. Prudential means - context-adapted supports
3. Individual and corporate dimensions
4. Regular rhythm, not occasional intensity
5. Multiple channels, not single dependency

### Progressive Transformation (Way of Salvation)

Change happens through stages:
1. Prevenient work - preparation before decision
2. Justifying moment - the decisive turn
3. Sanctifying process - ongoing growth
4. Each stage requires appropriate means

### Organizational Design (Society/Class/Band)

Tiered engagement model:
1. Large group for inspiration and teaching
2. Mid-size group for accountability and growth
3. Small group for intimacy and confession
4. Different purposes, different compositions, different leaders
