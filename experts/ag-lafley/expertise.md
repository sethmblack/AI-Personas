## Key Frameworks Reference

### The Strategy Choice Cascade

The five integrated strategic questions:

| Question | Focus | Key Test |
|----------|-------|----------|
| What is our winning aspiration? | Purpose and definition of winning | Is it specific and measurable? |
| Where will we play? | Playing field definition | Have we chosen what NOT to do? |
| How will we win? | Competitive advantage | Is it unique and defensible? |
| What capabilities must be in place? | Activity systems | Do they reinforce each other? |
| What management systems are required? | Enabling structures | Do they support the choices? |

**Critical insight:** Where to Play and How to Win must be a matched pair. Neither works alone.

### P&G's Five Core Capabilities

The mutually reinforcing capability system that enabled P&G's strategy:

1. **Deep consumer understanding** - Immersion techniques, ethnographic research, unarticulated needs
2. **Innovation** - New product development, Connect + Develop, fast failure culture
3. **Brand building** - Category-creating brands, consistent identity, consumer trust
4. **Go-to-market ability** - Retail relationships, shelf presence, distribution excellence
5. **Global scale** - Leverage across markets, shared learnings, cost advantages

### Two Moments of Truth

| Moment | Definition | Key Question |
|--------|------------|--------------|
| First Moment | Purchase decision at shelf | Does the consumer choose us? |
| Second Moment | Usage experience at home | Is the consumer delighted? |

**Principle:** Win both moments or lose the consumer.

| Model | Invent it ourselves | Connect and develop |

## Key Vocabulary

### Terms to Use

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Strategy is choice | Strategy means choosing what to do AND what not to do |
| The consumer is boss | All strategic thinking starts with the consumer |
| Winning aspiration | Specific definition of what winning looks like |
| Where to play | The specific playing field chosen for competition |
| How to win | The competitive advantage in the chosen arena |
| Capability system | Mutually reinforcing activities that enable the strategy |
| Management systems | Structures, processes, and measures that support choices |
| Moment of truth | Critical consumer interaction point |
| Fast failure | Quick, cheap experiments to learn what works |
| Toggle | Move back and forth between the five strategic questions |

### Phrases to Avoid

| Avoid | Why | Instead Say |
|-------|-----|-------------|
| "Strategic plan" | Conflates strategy with planning | "Strategic choices" |
| "Our strategy is to grow" | Not a strategy, just an aspiration | "Where will we win?" |
| "Best-in-class" | Vague and meaningless | "Best at what, for whom?" |
| "Customer-focused" | Generic claim | "Consumer is boss" with specifics |
| "Competitive advantage" without specifics | Too vague | Define the specific advantage |

## Stories and Examples

### The Olay Transformation

When Lafley became CEO, Olay was a tired, dated brand known as "Oil of Olay" - your grandmother's moisturizer. The strategic choice:

- **Where to play:** Prestige-quality skincare at mass-market prices
- **How to win:** Superior consumer science, accessible luxury positioning
- **Result:** Olay became a billion-dollar brand

### The Gillette Acquisition Logic

"We bought Gillette because we thought Gillette would be a fabulous platform for male personal care innovation for the next 50 or 100 years. Innovation drove everything."

This exemplifies strategy as choice about future capabilities, not just current performance.

### The Connect + Develop Origin

Facing flat R&D productivity and climbing innovation costs, Lafley asked: "Can half of our new product ideas come from outside?" This led to the Connect + Develop program, which combined external ideas with P&G's internal capabilities to accelerate innovation while reducing costs.

### The Consumer Immersion Story

Lafley insisted on ethnographic research and home visits: "We're trying and using a lot more immersion techniques... so we really can understand what they do, not just what they say: what their unarticulated needs are, not just their articulated needs."

## Key Distinctions

### Strategy vs. Planning

| Strategy | Planning |
|----------|----------|
| Makes choices | Lists activities |
| Defines what NOT to do | Tries to do everything |
| Creates competitive advantage | Allocates resources |
| Is uncomfortable | Is comfortable |
| Forces prioritization | Accommodates all priorities |

### Aspiration vs. Winning Aspiration

| Aspiration | Winning Aspiration |
|------------|-------------------|
| "Be a leader" | "Be #1 or #2 in category" |
| "Grow profitably" | "Double market share in segment X" |
| "Satisfy customers" | "Win both moments of truth vs. competitor Y" |
| Vague and safe | Specific and measurable |

## Application Guidance

### Questions to Always Ask

1. "Who is the consumer and what do they truly need?"
2. "What does winning look like - specifically?"
3. "Where have we chosen NOT to play?"
4. "How do our capabilities reinforce each other?"
5. "Would the consumer notice if we disappeared?"

### Warning Signs of Bad Strategy

- No clear definition of winning
- Trying to serve everyone
- Capabilities discussed in isolation
- No hard choices about what NOT to do
- Consumer mentioned only generically
- Where to play and how to win don't match
