# A.G. Lafley

> "Strategy is not complex. But it is hard. It's hard because it forces people and organizations to make specific choices about their future."

---

**Alan George Lafley** (1947-present) is the business leader who transformed Procter & Gamble by insisting that strategy is choice, not planning, and that the consumer - not the CEO - is the true boss of any organization. As two-time CEO of P&G, he doubled sales, quadrupled profits, and more than doubled the company's portfolio of billion-dollar brands by applying a rigorous, choice-driven approach to strategy that he later codified with Roger Martin in the influential book *Playing to Win*.

---

## The Journey: From Navy Officer to Strategy Architect

### The Formation Years (1947-1977)

Born June 13, 1947, in Keene, New Hampshire, Lafley grew up in a middle-class family where his father worked in human resources at General Electric and Chase Manhattan Bank. After earning his bachelor's degree from Hamilton College in 1969 (including a year studying at the Sorbonne in Paris), he began a doctoral program at the University of Virginia before taking a commission as a U.S. Navy supply officer during the Vietnam War, stationed in Japan.

The Navy experience proved foundational. Managing supply chains in a foreign country taught him about operations, logistics, and the importance of understanding the end user. After his service, he attended Harvard Business School, earning his MBA in 1977 and immediately joining Procter & Gamble.

*Key insight:* Understanding the consumer requires going where they are, not waiting for them to come to you.

### The P&G Ascent (1977-2000)

Lafley spent over two decades rising through P&G's ranks, leading some of the company's most important businesses. He was responsible for innovations like Liquid Tide and Tide with Bleach - products that emerged from deep understanding of how consumers actually did laundry, not just what they said they wanted.

In 1994, he was sent to Japan to turn around P&G's struggling Asian operations. He succeeded dramatically, growing the China business from under $90 million to nearly $1 billion in sales. This experience reinforced his conviction that success comes from understanding local consumers and making clear strategic choices.

*Key insight:* You cannot be all things to all people. Focus on where you can win.

### The First CEO Tenure (2000-2009)

When Lafley became CEO in June 2000, P&G was in crisis - the stock had lost half its value in six months. His response was not a dramatic turnaround plan but a disciplined focus on fundamentals: Who is the consumer? Where will we play? How will we win?

Under his leadership, P&G:
- Doubled revenue from approximately $39 billion to $76 billion
- Quadrupled profits
- Increased market value by more than $100 billion
- Grew billion-dollar brands from 10 to 24
- Acquired Gillette in 2005 for $57 billion, creating the world's largest consumer goods company

He established "The consumer is boss" as the company's guiding principle and introduced the "Two Moments of Truth" framework for understanding consumer decisions.

*Key insight:* Great results come from making clear choices, not from trying to do everything.

### The Playing to Win Era (2009-present)

After stepping down as CEO in 2009 (remaining as Chairman until 2010), Lafley partnered with strategy professor Roger Martin to codify the strategic approach that had driven P&G's transformation. Their 2013 book *Playing to Win: How Strategy Really Works* introduced the Strategy Choice Cascade - five integrated questions that define what strategy really means.

Called back as CEO from 2013-2015 to address performance challenges, he continued to apply the same principles: focus, choice, and consumer obsession. He retired as Executive Chairman in 2016.

*Key insight:* Strategy is a set of integrated choices, not a plan. And choosing means deciding what you will NOT do.

## The Core Frameworks

### The Strategy Choice Cascade

Strategy requires answering five questions as an integrated set:

1. **What is our winning aspiration?** - The specific definition of what winning looks like
2. **Where will we play?** - The playing field: geographies, customers, channels, products
3. **How will we win?** - The competitive advantage in the chosen arena
4. **What capabilities must be in place?** - The activities that enable winning
5. **What management systems are required?** - The structures that support the choices

The heart of strategy is the pairing of Where to Play and How to Win. A where-to-play choice without a how-to-win is useless. A how-to-win without a where-to-play is equally meaningless.

### The Consumer Is Boss

"At P&G, the CEO is not the boss - the consumer is."

Every strategic choice must be grounded in deep understanding of the consumer - not what they say they want, but what they actually do. Not their articulated needs, but their unarticulated needs. Three billion times a day, P&G products touch consumers' lives. Strategy means winning with them.

### Two Moments of Truth

The best brands win at two critical moments:
- **First Moment of Truth:** The purchase decision at the shelf - does the consumer choose you?
- **Second Moment of Truth:** The usage experience at home - is the consumer delighted?

Win both moments or lose the consumer.
