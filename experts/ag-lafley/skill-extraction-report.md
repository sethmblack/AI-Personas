## Evaluation Methodology

Each pattern evaluated against 5 criteria:
- **Actionable** - Has clear, repeatable steps
- **Invocable** - Could be triggered by a request
- **Scoped** - One responsibility, clear boundaries
- **Reusable** - Applies across multiple contexts
- **Valuable** - Saves significant effort or ensures consistency

All 5 criteria must be YES for CANDIDATE status.

## HIGH Priority

### 1. strategy-choice-cascade

**Source Pattern:** The Strategy Choice Cascade
**Purpose:** Walk through the five strategic questions systematically to develop integrated strategy
**Trigger:** "What's our strategy?", "Help me develop strategy", "We need a strategic plan"
**Inputs:** Current business situation, aspirations, competitive context
**Outputs:** Integrated set of five strategic choices with reinforcement mapping
**Reasoning:** Core framework from Playing to Win; highly actionable with clear 5-question structure; used in every strategic planning context; saves weeks of unfocused strategy discussions by providing disciplined structure

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Clear 5-question sequence with defined outputs
- Invocable: YES - Triggered by any strategy development request
- Scoped: YES - Covers strategy development, clear start/end
- Reusable: YES - Applies to corporate, business unit, brand, personal levels
- Valuable: YES - Transforms vague planning into specific choices

**Decision:** CANDIDATE (HIGH)

---

### 2. where-to-play-analysis

**Source Pattern:** Where to Play / How to Win Analysis (Where to Play component)
**Purpose:** Define the specific playing field for competition through systematic scope choices
**Trigger:** "Where should we compete?", "Which markets should we enter?", "What's our scope?"
**Inputs:** Market landscape, customer segments, geographic options, channel alternatives
**Outputs:** Specific where-to-play choices with rationale for what is included AND excluded
**Reasoning:** Heart of strategy per Lafley/Martin; forces the hard "what NOT to do" choices that most strategies avoid; applicable across industries and levels

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Systematic evaluation of playing field dimensions
- Invocable: YES - Clear trigger phrases around market/scope decisions
- Scoped: YES - Focused on scope decisions, pairs with how-to-win
- Reusable: YES - Any organization/individual facing scope choices
- Valuable: YES - Prevents the "be everything to everyone" trap

**Decision:** CANDIDATE (HIGH)

---

### 3. how-to-win-diagnosis

**Source Pattern:** Where to Play / How to Win Analysis (How to Win component)
**Purpose:** Clarify the competitive advantage and value proposition in the chosen arena
**Trigger:** "What's our competitive advantage?", "How do we differentiate?", "Why will customers choose us?"
**Inputs:** Where-to-play choices, competitor analysis, capability assessment
**Outputs:** Specific how-to-win statement with competitive advantage articulation
**Reasoning:** Must be paired with where-to-play; forces clarity on actual differentiation vs. hoped-for differentiation; tests whether advantage is real and defensible

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Structured analysis of competitive positioning
- Invocable: YES - Triggered by differentiation/advantage questions
- Scoped: YES - Focused on competitive positioning, pairs with where-to-play
- Reusable: YES - Any competitive context requires this analysis
- Valuable: YES - Prevents vague "customer focus" claims

**Decision:** CANDIDATE (HIGH)

## MEDIUM Priority

### 4. capability-system-mapping

**Source Pattern:** The Capability System
**Purpose:** Map the mutually reinforcing capabilities required to win and identify gaps
**Trigger:** "What capabilities do we need?", "Do we have the right capabilities?", "What should we build?"
**Inputs:** Where-to-play/how-to-win choices, current capability inventory
**Outputs:** Capability system map showing reinforcing relationships, gap analysis
**Reasoning:** Capabilities must work as a system; most organizations assess capabilities in isolation; provides strategic investment priorities

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Systematic capability assessment with reinforcement mapping
- Invocable: YES - Triggered by capability/competency questions
- Scoped: YES - Focused on capability assessment tied to strategy
- Reusable: YES - Any strategy implementation requires this
- Valuable: YES - Prevents scattered capability investments

**Decision:** CANDIDATE (MEDIUM)

---

### 5. consumer-moment-of-truth

**Source Pattern:** Two Moments of Truth
**Purpose:** Analyze consumer experience at purchase (first moment) and usage (second moment)
**Trigger:** "Why do consumers choose us?", "Do we win at the shelf?", "Consumer experience analysis"
**Inputs:** Consumer journey data, purchase behavior, usage feedback
**Outputs:** Moment-of-truth assessment with win/lose diagnosis at each moment
**Reasoning:** Grounds strategy in consumer reality; applicable beyond CPG to any consumer-facing business; prevents product/brand decisions disconnected from actual consumer experience

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Structured analysis of two specific moments
- Invocable: YES - Clear consumer insight triggers
- Scoped: YES - Focused on consumer moments, clear boundaries
- Reusable: YES - Any consumer-facing product/service
- Valuable: YES - Connects strategy to actual consumer behavior

**Decision:** CANDIDATE (MEDIUM)

---

### 6. winning-aspiration-definition

**Source Pattern:** Winning Aspiration (first question of cascade)
**Purpose:** Define what winning looks like in specific, measurable terms
**Trigger:** "What's our goal?", "What does success look like?", "Define our vision"
**Inputs:** Current state, competitive context, stakeholder expectations
**Outputs:** Specific winning aspiration with measurable criteria
**Reasoning:** Most aspirations are vague; this forces specificity about who you're beating and by what measure; foundational to the cascade but can be used standalone for vision setting

**Criterion Evaluation:**
- Actionable: YES - Structured process to define specific aspiration
- Invocable: YES - Triggered by goal/vision setting requests
- Scoped: YES - Focused on aspiration definition
- Reusable: YES - Any organization needs winning aspiration
- Valuable: YES - Transforms vague goals into actionable targets

**Decision:** CANDIDATE (MEDIUM)
