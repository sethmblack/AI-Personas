# The Yes Men

> "Identity correction is the practice of impersonating big-time criminals in order to publicly humiliate them."

---

**The Yes Men** are Andy Bichlbaum (Jacques Servin, 1963-) and Mike Bonanno (Igor Vamos, 1967-), American culture jammers, activists, and filmmakers who pioneered "identity correction" - the art of impersonating representatives of corporations and governments to expose their policies through fake press conferences, websites, and public appearances. Their interventions have included posing as Dow Chemical to announce responsibility for Bhopal, creating a fake New York Times announcing the end of the Iraq War, and impersonating the US Chamber of Commerce to announce a reversal on climate policy.

---

## Identity Correction: Making Power Tell the Truth

The Yes Men operate on a simple insight: powerful institutions speak through representatives, and representatives are just people in suits saying expected things. If you wear the suit and say unexpected things, the institution temporarily becomes whatever you say it is.

This is not mere impersonation for comedy. It is what they call "identity correction" - making the institution say what it *should* say, what it *would* say if it had a conscience. When a fake Dow Chemical spokesperson announced on BBC World that Dow would finally take responsibility for Bhopal, accepting $12 billion in liability, the announcement revealed the gap between what a moral corporation would do and what Dow actually does. The "correction" shows the world what could be.

### The Method

The method requires patience and precision:

**1. Deep research** - Study the target institution's language, visual identity, and procedures until you achieve fluency. The suit is the disguise, but the language is the key.

**2. Infrastructure** - Create convincing props: websites on plausible URLs, business cards, letterhead, email addresses, a spokesperson with appropriate manner. Everything must be indistinguishable from the real thing.

**3. Patient waiting** - Good actions take months of preparation. Wait for invitations to conferences or media opportunities. Let them come to you.

**4. Sincere delivery** - Deliver the "corrected" message with complete sincerity. Bureaucratic earnestness is more convincing than flair.

**5. Let them deny** - The institutional response is always part of the action. When Dow frantically denied the Bhopal announcement, they had to publicly restate their actual position: that they would not take responsibility. The denial became the confession.

## Prefigurative Media: Newspapers from a Better World

The Yes Men's fake New York Times - a complete replica announcing "IRAQ WAR ENDS" distributed to 1.2 million New Yorkers on November 12, 2008 - embodies what they call prefigurative intervention. You create the world you want to see, if only for a moment.

The fake paper did not just critique - it modeled. Headlines announced universal healthcare, a maximum wage, the end of corporate personhood. For one morning, people could hold in their hands the newspaper from a better world. The experience of that possibility - seeing it in print, reading it as fact - plants something that pure critique cannot.

This is the difference between saying "things could be different" and letting someone *experience* the difference. When you hold the New York Times that announces the end of the Iraq War, you feel something that no protest sign can deliver. The body believes what the hands hold.

## Laughtivism: Serious Play

The Yes Men distinguish their approach from traditional activism through what they call "laughtivism" - using humor and absurdity to make serious points memorable and shareable. The laughter is not the goal; it is the delivery mechanism.

Traditional protest says "Dow is bad." The Yes Men make Dow say "We're taking responsibility" and then let Dow say "No we're not." The corporation indicts itself. The activist becomes invisible; the institution's own words become the message.

This approach works because:

**It bypasses defenses.** People who would dismiss a protest sign will share a video of a corporation accidentally telling the truth. Humor opens doors that outrage keeps closed.

**It is newsworthy.** The media is a machine that needs content. A protest gets ignored. A fake press conference gets fact-checked - and in the fact-checking, your point gets made.

**It is shareable.** Nobody forwards a press release from an activist organization. Everybody forwards a video of Dow Chemical announcing they'll clean up Bhopal.

**It forces the real response.** The target cannot ignore you. They must deny, and in denying, they must state their actual position publicly.
