## Key Frameworks to Apply

### The Harold Form

**Core principle:** Long-form improvisation built on pattern, connection, and group mind—a complete theatrical piece emerging from a single audience suggestion.

**Standard Harold Structure:**
1. **Opening** - Group exploration of the suggestion through games, monologues, or movement (establishes themes, motifs, and connections)
2. **First Beat** - Three separate two-person scenes, each initiated from the opening
3. **Group Game** - Ensemble game or heightening exercise
4. **Second Beat** - Return to first beat scenes, heightened (characters may be different, but the game/relationship continues)
5. **Group Game** - Another ensemble moment
6. **Third Beat** - Final beat of all scenes; characters and worlds may collide; callbacks; resolution/connections revealed

**Key Harold Principles:**
- **The opening seeds everything** - All scenes grow from the opening exploration
- **Three beats per thread** - Each storyline is visited three times, each time heightened
- **Connections emerge organically** - Don't force connections; trust the group mind
- **Time dash forward** - Beats often jump ahead in time ("Six months later...")
- **Callbacks create satisfaction** - Reference earlier moments in later beats
- **The third beat resolves** - By the end, themes are tied together

**Harold Philosophy:**
- The Harold is about discovery, not invention
- Trust the opening; it contains everything you need
- The group creates something no individual could
- Patterns emerge; you don't impose them

### Follow the Fear Methodology

**Core principle:** The impulses you most want to avoid are signals pointing toward truth and discovery.

**Application:**
1. **Notice the impulse** - What choice feels dangerous? Embarrassing? Too honest?
2. **Name the fear** - What specifically are you afraid will happen?
3. **Examine the avoidance** - What "safe" choice would you make instead?
4. **Do the scary thing** - Commit fully to the impulse you wanted to avoid
5. **Observe the result** - The audience responds to vulnerability; the scene deepens

**Common fears to follow:**
- Emotional vulnerability (crying, expressing love, admitting weakness)
- Physical commitment (big choices, stillness, unusual physicality)
- Going dark (exploring difficult themes, mortality, conflict)
- Being "unfunny" (playing it straight instead of going for laughs)
- Being specific (naming the real thing instead of generalizing)

**Why it works:**
- Fear marks the edge of the known—where discovery lives
- Audiences recognize authenticity
- Safe choices produce forgettable work
- Vulnerability creates connection

### Truth in Comedy Framework

**Core principle:** Comedy emerges from honest behavior in heightened circumstances, not from imposed jokes.

**The formula:**
- **Truth + Commitment = Comedy**
- (Not: Cleverness + Jokes = Comedy)

**Application:**
1. **Establish the reality** - What's actually happening? Where are we? Who are these people?
2. **Find the unusual thing** - What's the one thing in this reality that's unusual?
3. **React honestly** - How would a real person actually respond to this unusual thing?
4. **Heighten through behavior** - Don't make jokes; intensify honest reactions
5. **The funny emerges** - Audiences laugh at truth, not at "comedy"

**Anti-patterns:**
- Making jokes at the expense of the scene
- Breaking character to get laughs
- Commenting on how weird the situation is
- Prioritizing cleverness over honesty
- Denying reality for comedy's sake

### Game of the Scene

**Core principle:** Every scene has one unusual thing—the "game." Find it, name it (internally), and heighten it.

**Game identification:**
1. **Watch the first minute** - Something unusual will happen
2. **Name it simply** - "The game is: every time X happens, Y responds by doing Z"
3. **Verify it's unusual** - In normal life, this wouldn't happen
4. **Heighten systematically** - Each beat, the game gets bigger

**Examples of games:**
- "Every time she asks for something, he overexplains why he can't provide it"
- "One character responds to everything with the wrong emotion"
- "Both characters agree on the problem but their solutions escalate destructively"

**Game vs. gimmick:**
- A game emerges from the scene; a gimmick is imposed on it
- A game can be heightened infinitely; a gimmick runs out
- A game is discovered; a gimmick is planned
- A game serves the relationship; a gimmick replaces it

### If This Is True, What Else Is True?

**Core principle:** Every established fact has implications. Mine them ruthlessly.

**Application process:**
1. **Accept the established fact** - "My character is afraid of birds"
2. **Ask the question** - If this fear exists, what else must exist in this person's life?
3. **Follow the implications:**
   - How do they navigate city streets?
   - What happened to make them this way?
   - What relationships are affected?
   - What can't they do that others can?
4. **Layer the reality** - Add these implications to the scene
5. **Trust the process** - Richer characters create richer comedy

**Why this works:**
- Specificity creates reality
- Reality creates investment
- Investment creates stakes
- Stakes create comedy (and drama)

## Signature Techniques in Detail

### 1. The Honest Reaction

**How it works:**
- Something unusual happens in the scene
- Instead of making a joke, you react honestly
- The honesty is what's funny

**Example:**
Setup: Partner enters and says "I've turned myself into a cat."
- Wrong response (joke): "Well, you've always been a pussy!"
- Right response (honest): "...What do you mean you've 'turned yourself into a cat'?" (Then genuinely try to understand.)

**Why it works:**
- The audience relates to the confusion/reaction
- The straight man makes the absurd land
- Reality grounds the comedy

### 2. The Support Move

**How it works:**
- Your job is to make your partner look good
- When they make a choice, support it absolutely
- Their success is your success

**Application:**
1. **Accept their reality** - Whatever they establish is true
2. **Add to it** - "Yes, and..." to build the world
3. **Justify them** - If their choice seems strange, find the logic
4. **Give them gifts** - Endow them with positive traits
5. **Share the light** - Don't steal focus; share it

### 3. The Time Dash

**How it works:**
- Jump forward in time between beats
- The audience fills in the gap
- Relationships develop faster than real-time allows

**Application:**
- First beat: Characters meet
- Second beat: "Six months later..." (now they're married)
- Third beat: "Ten years later..." (now they have kids, or are divorced)

**Why it works:**
- Compresses storytelling
- Heightens through accumulation
- Shows consequences of choices

### 4. The Callback

**How it works:**
- Reference something from earlier in the Harold
- The audience recognizes the connection
- Satisfaction from pattern completion

**Types of callbacks:**
- **Direct callback** - Same line repeated in new context
- **Thematic callback** - Same idea in different form
- **Cross-pollination** - Element from Scene A appears in Scene C
- **Resolution callback** - Third beat ties to opening

**Timing:**
- Too early = forced
- Too late = forgotten
- Perfect timing = when the audience has almost forgotten

### 5. The Slow Burn

**How it works:**
- Resist the urge to rush
- Let moments land before moving on
- Trust silence

**Application:**
- After a big revelation, pause
- After a joke lands, don't step on the laugh
- In emotional moments, stay with the emotion
- When confused (character or performer), take time

**Why improvisers rush:**
- Fear of silence
- Fear of losing audience
- Desire to "fix" moments
- Ego (wanting to contribute)

**Why slowing works:**
- Audiences need processing time
- Silence creates tension
- Presence beats activity
- Truth takes time to land

## iO Theatre (ImprovOlympic)

### Founding and Mission

**Founded:** 1981 (initially as ImprovOlympic)
**Co-founder:** Charna Halpern
**Location:** Chicago (later also Los Angeles)
**Mission:** Long-form improvisation as art form; develop and showcase the Harold
**Renamed:** iO Theatre (to avoid Olympic trademark issues)

### The Harold's Development

**Origin:** Del Close developed the Harold throughout the 1970s-1980s
**Name Origin:** Supposedly named after a member of The Committee suggested it
**Evolution:** Refined through thousands of performances at iO
**Documentation:** Codified in *Truth in Comedy* (1994)

### iO Training System

**Levels of Training:**
1. **Level 1:** Basic scene work, "yes and," support, agreement
2. **Level 2:** Game, heightening, pattern recognition
3. **Level 3:** Group mind, openings, first beats
4. **Level 4:** Harold structure, callbacks, connections
5. **Level 5:** Advanced Harold, directing, performance

**Key Training Principles:**
- "Yes, and..." is the foundation
- Support your partner above all
- Find the game; heighten it
- Trust the Harold structure
- Serve the ensemble, not the self

### Notable iO Alumni

**Comedy Luminaries:**
- Tina Fey
- Amy Poehler
- Chris Farley
- Mike Myers
- Vince Vaughn
- Adam McKay
- Rachel Dratch
- Tim Meadows
- Matt Besser
- Amy Sedaris
- Stephen Colbert

**The influence:**
- iO became pipeline to SNL, Second City mainstage
- Harold became standard long-form structure
- Del Close methodology spread globally

## *Truth in Comedy* (1994)

### Book Overview

**Authors:** Charna Halpern, Del Close, Kim "Howard" Johnson
**Publisher:** Meriwether Publishing
**Format:** Instructional book on long-form improvisation
**Significance:** First comprehensive documentation of Harold form and Del Close philosophy

### Core Concepts from the Book

**Chapter 1: Whose Line Is It, Anyway?**
- Improv is not about jokes
- Agreement is the foundation
- "Yes, and..." explained

**Chapter 2: The Harold**
- Structure defined
- Beats explained
- Callbacks and connections

**Chapter 3: Character and Scene**
- Truth in performance
- Honest reactions
- Game of the scene

**Chapter 4: Advanced Techniques**
- Group mind
- Slow comedies (dramatic improv)
- Following the fear

### Famous Quotes from the Book

- "The audience will tell you when something is funny; you don't have to tell them."
- "Don't try to be funny. Be real, and let the funny happen."
- "Agreement is the one rule that can never be broken."

## Del Close Philosophy & Quotes

### On Fear and Risk

**"Follow the fear."** - The foundational Close teaching; the thing you're scared to do is the thing that will work.

**"Safe choices are death."** - Comfort produces mediocrity; risk produces discovery.

**"If you're not scared, you're not reaching."** - Fear is a compass pointing toward growth.

### On Truth

**"The truth is funny. Funny is not."** - Comedy emerges from honesty, not from trying to be funny.

**"The more specific, the more universal."** - Detailed, particular choices resonate more broadly.

**"Don't invent; discover."** - The scene already contains what you need; find it, don't impose.

### On Improv as Art

**"Improv is not about sketch-generation. It is its own art form."** - Improv deserves to be performed, not just used as rehearsal tool.

**"The Harold is jazz for actors."** - Long-form improv has the structure and improvisation of jazz music.

**"We're creating theater that has never existed before and will never exist again."** - Every performance is unique, ephemeral, unrepeatable.

### On Ego and Ensemble

**"Improv requires the death of ego."** - Individual brilliance matters less than group mind.

**"Make your partner look good."** - The best improvisers are the most generous.

**"There is no 'I' in Harold."** - The ensemble creates what no individual could.

### On Teaching

**"My job is to push you past where you thought you could go."** - Teaching is about expansion, not comfort.

**"I'd rather see you fail spectacularly than succeed safely."** - Ambitious failure teaches more than safe success.

**"The stage is the safest place to take risks."** - Theater is where we can explore danger without consequence.

## The Skull Story

### What Happened

**Del Close's dying wish:** He bequeathed his skull to the Goodman Theatre to be used in productions of *Hamlet* (for the Yorick skull).

**The reality:** Reports vary on whether this actually occurred; the skull displayed may be a replica.

**The symbolism:**
- Complete dedication to theater
- Death as continuation of artistic practice
- Del Close as theater's Yorick—the jester who taught others

### Why It Matters

- Demonstrates total commitment to the art form
- Creates mythology around Del Close
- Shows his dark humor and fearlessness
- Became defining image of his legacy

## Teaching Methodology

### The Del Close Approach

**1. High Expectations**
- Demand excellence from the first day
- Don't coddle; challenge
- Treat students as potential masters

**2. Direct Feedback**
- No sugar-coating
- Identify exactly what's wrong
- Provide specific, actionable correction

**3. Philosophical Grounding**
- Connect technique to larger principles
- Explain why, not just how
- Treat improv as intellectual pursuit

**4. Push Past Comfort**
- Identify where students are holding back
- Create exercises that force risk
- Celebrate spectacular failure

**5. Model Intensity**
- Demonstrate commitment yourself
- Take the work seriously
- Embody the principles you teach

### Common Exercises

**The Harold**
- Full Harold performances as training
- Notes after each beat
- Focus on connections and callbacks

**Organic Opening**
- Group creates opening from single word
- No planning; pure listening and response
- Find themes through movement and association

**Slow Comedy**
- Scenes played completely straight
- No attempts at humor
- Let the comedy emerge from reality

**Fear Mapping**
- Identify personal performance fears
- Create exercises targeting those fears
- Practice running toward terror

## Influence and Legacy

### Direct Influence

**The Harold spread:**
- Now performed at improv theaters worldwide
- Adapted into countless variations (Armando, La Ronde, etc.)
- Became the default long-form structure

**The teaching pipeline:**
- iO → Second City → SNL is common path
- Del Close methodology embedded in training globally
- His students teach his techniques

**The philosophy persists:**
- "Follow the fear" quoted constantly
- "Yes, and" became business cliché (for better or worse)
- Truth-based comedy is now standard approach

### Cultural Impact

**SNL connections:**
- Virtually every SNL cast member from 1980s-present trained in Close techniques
- The show's sensibility shaped by Second City/iO training
- Harold timing and structure influences sketch format

**Film comedy:**
- Judd Apatow films use improvisation
- McKay-Ferrell collaborations rooted in Harold training
- "Groundlings + iO + UCB" became comedy pipeline

**Business appropriation:**
- "Yes, and" imported into corporate culture
- Improv training for executives
- (Mixed results; principles often misapplied)

### The Mourning

**When Del Close died (1999):**
- Chicago comedy world stopped
- Memorial events drew hundreds
- Considered end of an era

**Ongoing tribute:**
- Del Close Marathon (annual long-form festival)
- iO Theatre continues his mission
- His name invoked whenever improv discussed
