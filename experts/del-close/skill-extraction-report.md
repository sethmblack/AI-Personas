## Skill Candidates Identified

### 1. harold-form-construction

**Priority:** HIGH

**Rationale:**
- **Actionable:** Clear 6-part structure (opening, three beats, two group games, resolution) with specific principles for each phase
- **Invocable:** Natural triggers: "How do I build a Harold?", "Teach me long-form structure", "What's the Harold format?", "Long-form improv structure"
- **Scoped:** Single responsibility - constructing and teaching the Harold long-form improvisation structure
- **Reusable:** Applies to improv performance, improv teaching, understanding collaborative creation, even structuring presentations or collaborative projects
- **Valuable:** The Harold is foundational to all long-form improv; understanding it enables everything else

**Source Framework:** "The Harold Form" (expertise.md)

**Core Process:**
1. Opening - Group exploration of suggestion establishing themes and motifs
2. First Beat - Three separate two-person scenes initiated from opening
3. Group Game - Ensemble heightening exercise
4. Second Beat - Return to first beat scenes, heightened
5. Group Game - Another ensemble moment
6. Third Beat - Final beat; worlds may collide; callbacks; resolution

**Key Principles:**
- Opening seeds everything
- Three beats per thread, each heightened
- Connections emerge organically (don't force)
- Time dash forward between beats
- Callbacks create satisfaction
- Third beat resolves themes

### 2. follow-the-fear-audit

**Priority:** HIGH

**Rationale:**
- **Actionable:** Clear 5-step process (notice impulse → name fear → examine avoidance → do scary thing → observe result)
- **Invocable:** Triggers: "I'm playing it safe", "What am I avoiding?", "Where's the risk?", "Push me harder", "This feels too comfortable"
- **Scoped:** Single responsibility - identifying what someone is avoiding and pushing them toward it
- **Reusable:** Applies to improv, writing, public speaking, creative work, even personal decisions
- **Valuable:** Unlocks breakthrough work by identifying and pushing past comfort zones

**Source Framework:** "Follow the Fear Methodology" (expertise.md)

**Core Process:**
1. Notice the impulse - What choice feels dangerous?
2. Name the fear - What specifically are you afraid will happen?
3. Examine the avoidance - What "safe" choice would you make instead?
4. Do the scary thing - Commit fully to the impulse you wanted to avoid
5. Observe the result - The audience responds to vulnerability

**Common fear categories:**
- Emotional vulnerability
- Physical commitment
- Going dark (difficult themes)
- Being "unfunny" (playing it straight)
- Being specific (naming the real thing)

### 3. game-of-the-scene-extraction

**Priority:** HIGH

**Rationale:**
- **Actionable:** Clear process for identifying and heightening the unusual element in any scene or premise
- **Invocable:** Triggers: "What's the game here?", "What's unusual?", "How do I heighten this?", "Find the pattern", "What's the one thing?"
- **Scoped:** Focused on identifying and naming the game, then heightening systematically
- **Reusable:** Works for improv scenes, sketch comedy, sitcom writing, any comedy premise
- **Valuable:** Game identification is the core skill of scene-based improv; enables all heightening

**Source Framework:** "Game of the Scene" (expertise.md)

**Core Process:**
1. Watch the first minute - Something unusual will happen
2. Name it simply - "The game is: every time X happens, Y responds by doing Z"
3. Verify it's unusual - In normal life, this wouldn't happen
4. Heighten systematically - Each beat, the game gets bigger

**Game vs. gimmick distinction:**
- Game emerges from scene; gimmick is imposed
- Game can heighten infinitely; gimmick runs out
- Game is discovered; gimmick is planned
- Game serves relationship; gimmick replaces it

### 4. truth-in-comedy-test

**Priority:** HIGH

**Rationale:**
- **Actionable:** Clear framework for evaluating whether comedy emerges from truth or from imposed jokes
- **Invocable:** Triggers: "Is this honest?", "Why isn't this landing?", "Something feels fake", "Too jokey", "Not grounded"
- **Scoped:** Focused on diagnosis - is the comedy truthful? - and redirection toward honesty
- **Reusable:** Works for any comedy evaluation (improv, sketch, stand-up, sitcom, film)
- **Valuable:** Identifies the root cause of "why isn't this funny" - usually it's lack of truth

**Source Framework:** "Truth in Comedy Framework" (expertise.md)

**Core Process:**
1. Establish the reality - What's actually happening? Where are we?
2. Find the unusual thing - What's the one thing in this reality that's unusual?
3. React honestly - How would a real person actually respond?
4. Heighten through behavior - Don't make jokes; intensify honest reactions
5. The funny emerges - Audiences laugh at truth, not at "comedy"

**Anti-patterns to identify:**
- Making jokes at expense of scene
- Breaking character to get laughs
- Commenting on weirdness instead of inhabiting it
- Prioritizing cleverness over honesty
- Denying reality for comedy's sake

### 5. if-this-is-true-what-else-is-true

**Priority:** MEDIUM

**Rationale:**
- **Actionable:** Clear question-based framework for mining implications from established facts
- **Invocable:** Triggers: "Develop this premise", "What are the implications?", "This feels thin", "Add depth to this character"
- **Scoped:** Focused on extrapolating implications from established premises
- **Reusable:** Works for improv, character development, world-building, story development
- **Valuable:** Creates richer, more layered work by following logical implications

**Source Framework:** "If This Is True, What Else Is True?" (expertise.md)

**Core Process:**
1. Accept the established fact - "My character is afraid of birds"
2. Ask the question - If this fear exists, what else must exist?
3. Follow the implications:
   - How do they navigate daily life?
   - What happened to create this?
   - What relationships are affected?
   - What can't they do?
4. Layer the reality - Add these implications to the scene
5. Trust the process - Richer characters create richer comedy

**Note:** This technique is powerful but more narrow than the top four skills. Consider whether it's distinct enough for standalone skill or better integrated as technique within truth-in-comedy-test.

### 6. group-mind-facilitation

**Priority:** MEDIUM

**Rationale:**
- **Actionable:** Principles for surrendering ego and creating ensemble intelligence
- **Invocable:** Triggers: "How do we think together?", "Ensemble work", "Collaborative creation", "Support the group"
- **Scoped:** Focused on ensemble collaboration and ego-death
- **Reusable:** Works for improv, writers' rooms, any collaborative creative work
- **Valuable:** Enables group creativity beyond what individuals could achieve

**Source:** Multiple sections of expertise.md (group mind, ensemble, ego-death)

**Core Principles:**
- Make your partner look good
- Accept all offers
- Build on what's given
- Stop planning; start listening
- The group thinks together
- Individual brilliance matters less than collective flow

**Note:** This is more philosophical than procedural. May be better as contextual knowledge supporting other skills rather than standalone skill.

### 7. callback-construction

**Priority:** MEDIUM

**Rationale:**
- **Actionable:** Types of callbacks (direct, thematic, cross-pollination, resolution) and timing principles
- **Invocable:** Triggers: "How do I use callbacks?", "When should I callback?", "Tie this together"
- **Scoped:** Focused on referencing earlier material for comedic/structural payoff
- **Reusable:** Works for improv, sketch, sitcom, stand-up specials
- **Valuable:** Callbacks create satisfaction and reward audience attention

**Source Framework:** "The Callback" technique (expertise.md)

**Types:**
- Direct callback - Same line in new context
- Thematic callback - Same idea in different form
- Cross-pollination - Element from Scene A in Scene C
- Resolution callback - Third beat ties to opening

**Timing:**
- Too early = forced
- Too late = forgotten
- Perfect = when audience has almost forgotten

**Note:** This is important technique but may be too narrow for standalone skill. Consider integrating into harold-form-construction.

**Create 4 HIGH-priority skills:**

1. **harold-form-construction** - Build and teach the Harold long-form improv structure
2. **follow-the-fear-audit** - Identify avoidance and push toward vulnerable, truthful choices
3. **game-of-the-scene-extraction** - Find the unusual thing and heighten it systematically
4. **truth-in-comedy-test** - Evaluate whether comedy emerges from truth or imposed jokes

These four skills capture Del Close's core innovations:
- **harold-form-construction** = STRUCTURE (how to build long-form)

- **game-of-the-scene-extraction** = TECHNIQUE (how to find and play the game)
- **truth-in-comedy-test** = EVALUATION (how to know if it's working)

## Usage Scenarios

**Use harold-form-construction when:**
- Building or teaching Harold structure
- Understanding long-form improv architecture
- Designing collaborative performance
- Creating content with multiple threads that resolve

**Use follow-the-fear-audit when:**
- Work feels safe or predictable
- Performer/creator is holding back
- Need to identify what's being avoided
- Pushing for breakthrough over safety

**Use game-of-the-scene-extraction when:**
- Scene has started but lacks direction
- Need to identify what to heighten
- Premise exists but no "engine" for comedy
- Evaluating what makes existing scene work

**Use truth-in-comedy-test when:**
- Comedy isn't landing
- Work feels "jokey" but not funny
- Evaluating honesty of material
- Redirecting from cleverness to truth

**Use MULTIPLE skills when:**
- Building complete Harold (structure + game + truth + fear)
- Teaching comprehensive improv methodology
- Diagnosing complex performance issues
- Creating or evaluating long-form comedy
