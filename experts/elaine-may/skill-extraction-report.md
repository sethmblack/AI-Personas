## Extraction Criteria

Each skill candidate must meet ALL 5 criteria:
- **Actionable** - Clear, repeatable steps
- **Invocable** - Could be triggered by request
- **Scoped** - One responsibility, clear boundaries
- **Reusable** - Applies across contexts
- **Valuable** - Saves significant effort

## Skill Candidates

### 1. Scene Tension Construction

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Build scenes where two or more characters pursue incompatible goals through verbal maneuvering, creating tension without overt conflict.

**Trigger Conditions:**
- "How do I create conflict without shouting?"
- "My scene feels flat"
- "The characters are just talking"
- When dialogue exists but lacks tension
- When scenes need subtext

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Clear framework: establish wants, make them incompatible, let characters maneuver |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered when scenes lack energy or tension |
| Scoped | Yes | Only builds tension structure; does not resolve scenes |
| Reusable | Yes | Applies to any dialogue scene |
| Valuable | Yes | Transforms flat scenes into engaging exchanges |

**Output:** Scene analysis identifying each character's want, the incompatibility, and specific tension points

### 2. Character Want Discovery

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Uncover the driving motivation beneath surface dialogue and behavior, revealing what a character actually wants versus what they say they want.

**Trigger Conditions:**
- "What does my character actually want?"
- "My character feels unmotivated"
- "I don't know why my character does this"
- When character behavior seems arbitrary
- When dialogue feels purposeless

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Clear process: identify surface want, probe for deeper want, test against behavior |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered when examining character motivation |
| Scoped | Yes | Only identifies wants; does not determine how character pursues them |
| Reusable | Yes | Works for any character in any medium |
| Valuable | Yes | Provides foundation for all character work |

**Output:** Hierarchy of character wants from surface to core, with behavioral evidence

### 3. Uncomfortable Moment Extension

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Determine when to stay in an awkward or uncomfortable scene moment rather than cutting away, and what that extension reveals.

**Trigger Conditions:**
- "Should I cut this awkward part?"
- "This scene is too uncomfortable"
- "How long should I stay on this moment?"
- When impulse is to escape discomfort
- When scene feels like it should end but hasn't earned ending

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Clear decision framework: identify discomfort, assess what staying reveals, determine payoff |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered by editing/pacing decisions around uncomfortable content |
| Scoped | Yes | Only addresses duration of uncomfortable moments; does not determine content |
| Reusable | Yes | Applies to any scene with emotional tension |
| Valuable | Yes | Distinguishes meaningful discomfort from gratuitous discomfort |

**Output:** Recommendation to stay or cut, with rationale based on what extension reveals

### 4. Dialogue as Strategy

**Priority:** HIGH

**Description:** Transform dialogue from information delivery into active strategy, where each line is a move in a character's attempt to get what they want.

**Trigger Conditions:**
- "How do I write dialogue that sounds real?"
- "My dialogue is too on-the-nose"
- "Characters are just stating their feelings"
- When dialogue serves exposition rather than action
- When exchanges feel like information dumps

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Clear test: cover dialogue, read actions, determine who is winning |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered when evaluating dialogue quality |
| Scoped | Yes | Only transforms dialogue function; does not rewrite content |
| Reusable | Yes | Works for any dialogue in any medium |
| Valuable | Yes | Eliminates the most common dialogue weakness |

**Output:** Analysis of dialogue as strategic moves, with identification of information dumps to convert

**Trigger Conditions:**
- "My villain is too one-dimensional"
- "My protagonist seems too perfect"
- "The moral of my story is too obvious"
- When characters are clearly good or clearly bad
- When audience knows exactly whom to root for

**Criteria Assessment:**
| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Actionable | Yes | Clear steps: identify apparent alignment, find villain's reasons, find victim's flaws |
| Invocable | Yes | Triggered when examining moral simplicity |
| Scoped | Yes | Only complicates sympathy; does not determine story outcome |
| Reusable | Yes | Applies to any narrative with moral dimension |
| Valuable | Partial | More specialized; not all stories benefit from moral ambiguity |

**Output:** Specific complications for each character that shift audience sympathy

## Prioritization Summary

### HIGH Priority (Implement First)
1. **scene-tension-construction** - Core technique for building engaging scenes
2. **character-want-discovery** - Foundation for all character work
3. **uncomfortable-moment-extension** - Distinctive May technique for emotional truth
4. **dialogue-as-strategy** - Addresses most common dialogue problem

### MEDIUM Priority (Consider for Implementation)
5. **sympathy-reversal-construction** - Valuable but more specialized
6. **improvisers-commitment-test** - More philosophical, harder to operationalize
7. **telephone-dynamic-construction** - Specific technique for specific effect

### LOW Priority (Not Extracted as Standalone)
- Improv game facilitation (requires live participants)
- Script doctoring diagnosis (too broad, requires full script analysis)
- Dark comedy tonal balance (too subjective for clear steps)
