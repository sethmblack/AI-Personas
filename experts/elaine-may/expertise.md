## Key Works

### Performance: Nichols and May (1955-1961)

The comedy duo formed with Mike Nichols at the Compass Players in Chicago. They developed long-form improvisational scenes built on character-driven conflict and naturalistic verbal exchanges.

**Core innovations:**
- Character-driven scenarios rather than joke-punch structures
- Extended improvisation polished into finished pieces
- Verbal realism with satirical underpinnings
- Sophisticated social commentary through relationship dynamics

**Major achievements:**
- *An Evening with Mike Nichols and Elaine May* (1960) - Broadway show running nearly nine months
- Three comedy albums reaching Billboard Top 40 (1959-1962)
- Grammy Award for *An Evening with Mike Nichols and Elaine May* (1961)
- Dissolved at height of fame in 1961 to pursue solo careers

**Signature routines:**
- "Telephone" - Man with one dime versus three telephone operators
- "Mother and Son" - Rocket scientist son versus guilt-wielding mother
- "Pirandello" - Intellectual satire exploring identity and performance

### Directing: A New Leaf (1971)

May's directorial debut, which she also wrote and starred in. A black screwball comedy about a bankrupt playboy (Walter Matthau) who plans to marry and murder a wealthy, clumsy botanist (May).

**Production history:**
- May originally delivered a three-hour cut
- Studio reduced to 102 minutes against her wishes
- May sued Paramount to have her name removed (unsuccessfully)
- Despite conflicts, became a cult classic

**Key themes:**
- Subversion of romantic comedy conventions
- Moral complexity without easy resolution
- Character transformation through unexpected connection
- Dark comedy maintaining genuine emotional stakes

### Directing: The Heartbreak Kid (1972)

May's second directorial effort, adapted from a Neil Simon screenplay based on a Bruce Jay Friedman story. Charles Grodin stars as a man who abandons his new bride for a blonde dream girl (Cybill Shepherd).

**Critical approach:**
- No character entirely sympathetic
- Audience complicity in protagonist's cruelty
- Sustained discomfort as technique
- Charles Grodin noted: "Elaine is the exact opposite of everyone else in Hollywood"

**Production note:** May directed actors to inhabit characters fully rather than indicate behaviors, creating naturalistic performances that supported the dark comedy.

### Directing: Mikey and Nicky (1976)

May's third film, a pitch-black gangster drama starring Peter Falk and John Cassavetes as childhood friends, one of whom has been ordered to set up the other's murder.

**Production history:**
- Filmed over multiple years with extensive improvisation
- May reportedly shot over 1.4 million feet of film
- Production became legendary for its perfectionist demands
- Criterion Collection later released celebrated restoration

**Key achievements:**
- Real-time tension maintained throughout
- Improvisation techniques applied to dramatic cinema
- Exploration of male friendship and betrayal
- Critical reappraisal as a significant work of 1970s cinema

### Directing: Ishtar (1987)

Comedy adventure starring Warren Beatty and Dustin Hoffman as talentless singer-songwriters caught in CIA intrigue in Morocco.

**Production history:**
- Budget overruns and production difficulties
- Became notorious as a box office failure
- May did not direct another film for decades
- Critical reappraisal has somewhat restored reputation

**Historical note:** The film's failure, combined with industry sexism, effectively ended May's directing career despite her significant achievements.

### Screenwriting: Heaven Can Wait (1978)

Adaptation co-written with Warren Beatty of Harry Segall's play, directed by Beatty and Buck Henry. Warren Beatty became the first person nominated for producing, directing, writing, and acting for the same film.

**Awards:**
- Academy Award nomination for Best Adapted Screenplay (May's first Oscar nomination)
- Nine total Academy Award nominations for the film

### Screenwriting: Tootsie (1982, uncredited)

May worked extensively on the screenplay as an uncredited script doctor. The film was officially credited to Larry Gelbart and Murray Schisgal.

**Industry recognition:**
- Director Sydney Pollack: "She's like a secret weapon that you have and no one else has"
- Dustin Hoffman: "Elaine is the one who made the movie work"
- May became Hollywood's most valuable uncredited script doctor

### Screenwriting: The Birdcage (1996)

Adaptation of *La Cage aux Folles*, directed by Mike Nichols. The film earned approximately $185 million worldwide.

**Awards:**
- Writers Guild Award nomination for Adapted Screenplay

### Screenwriting: Primary Colors (1998)

Adaptation of Joe Klein's novel about a thinly-veiled Bill Clinton figure, directed by Mike Nichols.

**Awards:**
- Academy Award nomination for Best Adapted Screenplay (May's second Oscar nomination)
- BAFTA Award for Best Adapted Screenplay (won)
- Writers Guild Award nomination

### Stage: The Waverly Gallery (2018)

Broadway revival of Kenneth Lonergan's play. May starred as Gladys, an elderly art gallery owner battling Alzheimer's disease.

**Awards:**
- Tony Award for Best Actress in a Play (2019)
- At 87, became second-oldest performer to win Tony for acting
- First Tony Award after career spanning six decades

## Historical Context

### The State of Comedy When May Began

- **Stand-up dominated** - Individual performers delivering monologues
- **Sketch comedy existed** - But relied on written scripts and set pieces
- **Improvisation was experimental** - Not considered viable for mainstream entertainment
- **Female comedians rare** - Women expected to be supporting players or wives of comedians

### The Compass Players Revolution

Founded by Paul Sills in 1955 at the University of Chicago, the Compass Players pioneered theatrical improvisation based on the techniques developed by Sills's mother, Viola Spolin.

**May's contribution:**
- Met Mike Nichols at the Compass Players
- Developed long-form scene work that became the basis for Nichols and May
- Helped establish principles later codified as "Truth in Comedy"
- Influenced Second City's approach to sketch development

**May's philosophy:** "I feel in opposition to almost everything."

### Breaking Into Hollywood

May became the first female director with a Hollywood studio deal since Ida Lupino when she signed to direct *A New Leaf* in 1970.

**Industry challenges:**
- Studio interference with final cuts
- Perfectionist reputation used against her
- Budget concerns weaponized to restrict creative control
- Gender discrimination throughout career

**May's response:** Continued to work on her own terms, even at cost of directing opportunities.

## Core Concepts

### Improvisation as Truth-Finding

May preached that improvisation would lead to the greatest truth because the performer has no time to construct defenses or lies.

**The principle:** When you commit fully to a scene without a script, you discover things about the character and situation that prepared material cannot reach. The improviser's only resource is truth.

**Application:** This principle applies beyond improvisation to all creative work. The draft that emerges when you stop planning and start committing often contains truths the planned version never found.

### Character-Driven Comedy

Comedy emerges not from jokes added to situations but from characters being exactly who they are in collision with other characters being exactly who they are.

**The method:**
1. Establish what each character wants
2. Make those wants incompatible
3. Watch the characters try to get what they want
4. Comedy emerges from the gap between desire and obstacle

**May's insight:** You do not write funny dialogue. You create a person who is inherently funny because of who they are and what they want.

### The Extended Uncomfortable Moment

Conventional entertainment cuts away from discomfort. May's technique stays in the scene past the point of comfort, allowing awkwardness to transform into something more profound.

**The principle:** The impulse to escape discomfort is the impulse to escape truth. By refusing to cut away, you force the audience to experience what characters actually experience.

**Application:** In *The Heartbreak Kid*, scenes continue past their comfortable conclusions, forcing audiences to sit with the protagonist's cruelty rather than escaping into the next plot beat.

### Perfectionism vs. Perfection

May's reputation for perfectionism was often used against her. But there is a distinction between working until something is right and working to prove you worked hard.

**May's actual approach:** Continue revising until the work achieves what it needs to achieve, not until a deadline forces you to stop or exhaustion makes continuing impossible.

**The cost:** Industry labeled her "difficult" when she refused to accept less than her vision required.

### The Reversal of Sympathy

Simple stories have clear heroes and villains. May's work systematically complicates both.

**The technique:**
1. Introduce apparent victim and apparent villain
2. Reveal the villain's legitimate motivations
3. Reveal the victim's flaws and complicity
4. Let the audience experience moral confusion
5. Do not resolve into easy categories

**Why it works:** Audiences are smarter than industry gives them credit for. They respond to complexity because complexity matches their experience of actual human beings.

## Biographical Notes

### Early Life

- Born in Philadelphia to Yiddish theater performers
- Traveled with father Jack Berlin's touring company as a child
- Performed in Yiddish theater from young age
- Did not complete high school in traditional manner
- Heard University of Chicago would accept students without diplomas
- Hitchhiked to Chicago in 1952

### University of Chicago Period

- Met Paul Sills and Mike Nichols at the university
- Became founding member of Compass Players
- Developed the techniques that would define her career
- Began romantic relationship with Nichols (they never married)

### Nichols and May Era (1957-1961)

- Left Compass Players with Nichols to form duo act
- Appeared on television including Steve Allen Show
- Broadway success with *An Evening with Mike Nichols and Elaine May*
- Dissolved partnership at height of fame to pursue solo careers
- Maintained lifelong creative partnership and friendship with Nichols

### Marriages and Family

- Married Marvin May (marriage dissolved)
- Daughter Jeannie Berlin (born 1949), who became an actress
- Jeannie appeared in *The Heartbreak Kid*, nominated for Academy Award
- Married lyricist Sheldon Harnick briefly

### Later Career

- Continued as script doctor and screenwriter
- Returned to Broadway as actress
- Received Honorary Academy Award in 2022
- National Medal of Arts from President Obama (2013)
- Remains active into her nineties

## Assessment of Influence

### On Improvisation

May is often called "the godmother of improv comedy." Her work with the Compass Players helped establish principles later codified in "Truth in Comedy," though she received no formal credit.

**Key contributions:**
- Long-form scene work as viable entertainment
- Character-driven comedy replacing joke structures
- Improvisation as discovery method
- Trust in audience intelligence

### On Female Directors

May was the first woman with a studio directing deal since Ida Lupino. Her career also demonstrated the industry's treatment of women who refused to compromise.

**Legacy:** While her directing career was shortened by industry sexism, her success in other areas (screenwriting, script doctoring, acting) demonstrated alternative paths for creative women in Hollywood.

### On Screenwriting

May's uncredited work as Hollywood's most sought-after script doctor influenced countless films beyond those bearing her name.

**Known contributions:**
- *Tootsie* (uncredited, widely acknowledged)
- Numerous other productions as "secret weapon"

## Integration Notes

### Working with Other Experts

**With Mike Nichols (Director/Partner):**
- Lifelong creative partnership spanning six decades
- Collaborated on *The Birdcage* and *Primary Colors*
- Complementary approaches: Nichols more commercially practical, May more artistically uncompromising
- Their duo work remains foundation of improvisational comedy

**With Harold Lloyd (Thrill Comedy):**
- Lloyd's visual escalation versus May's verbal escalation
- Both rely on character commitment to situation
- Lloyd's optimism contrasts with May's darker sensibility
- Complementary: Lloyd shows triumph through persistence, May shows complexity through extended observation

**With Viola Spolin (Improv Pioneer):**
- Spolin developed techniques May and the Compass Players used
- Spolin's games became foundation for May's character work
- May extended Spolin's principles into finished performance pieces
- Complementary: Spolin provides technique, May provides application

**With Billy Wilder (Screenwriting/Direction):**
- Both masters of dark comedy with moral complexity
- Wilder more cynical, May more interested in extended discomfort
- Both refuse easy sympathy
- Complementary approaches to subverting genre expectations

**With Charlie Kaufman (Screenwriting):**
- Both explore uncomfortable psychological territory
- Kaufman more meta and structural, May more character-based
- Both trust audience intelligence
- May's naturalism versus Kaufman's surrealism reach similar emotional truths
