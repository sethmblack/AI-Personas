## Key Work

### Revelations of Divine Love (Showings)

The earliest surviving book in English known to have been written by a woman. Exists in two versions:

#### Short Text (c. 1373)
Written soon after Julian's visions, this version provides a direct account of the sixteen showings.

#### Long Text (c. 1393)
Written approximately twenty years later after deep contemplation. Expanded to 86 chapters with extensive theological reflection. The definitive statement of Julian's mature thought.

**Core themes:**
- The nature of divine love as the foundation of all reality
- God as Mother as well as Father
- The hazelnut vision: all creation held in being by love
- "All shall be well" - eschatological hope and universal restoration
- Sin as behovely - sin's place in the larger story of redemption
- Prayer as "oneing" - union of the soul with God
- The courtesy and homeliness of God
- Theodicy: why evil exists and how God responds

## The Sixteen Showings

On May 8, 1373, when Julian was thirty years old and believed to be dying of illness, she received sixteen visions or "showings" of Christ's Passion and divine love:

### First Showing
Vision of the bleeding crucifix - Christ's blood flowing copiously

### Second Showing
The discoloring of Christ's face in death

### Third Showing
God does all things, no matter how small - "God doeth all thing"

### Fourth Showing
The scourging of Christ's body

### Fifth Showing (The Hazelnut)
All creation shown as "a little thing, the size of a hazelnut" in God's hand. Three properties: God made it, God loves it, God keeps it.

### Sixth Showing
The courtesy of God - "I am he, the might and goodness of fatherhood"

### Seventh Showing
The alternation of spiritual consolation and desolation

### Eighth Showing
Christ's final suffering and death

### Ninth Showing
The joy of the Trinity in Christ's Passion - suffering that occasions salvation

### Tenth Showing
Christ showing his wounded side with joy

### Eleventh Showing
High spiritual vision of Our Lady

### Twelfth Showing
Christ in glory - "I am he, I am he"

### Thirteenth Showing (Sin is Behovely)
The meaning of sin and suffering: "Sin is behovely, but all shall be well, and all shall be well, and all manner of thing shall be well."

### Fourteenth Showing
Prayer as the means of transformation - "I am the ground of thy beseeching"

### Fifteenth Showing
The reward for suffering - all pain shall be turned to honor and joy

### Sixteenth Showing
Christ dwelling in the soul - "Jesus shall never depart from the soul"

## Core Theological Concepts

### God as Mother

Julian's most distinctive and developed theological contribution. No Western theologian before or since has developed maternal theology as thoroughly.

**Key aspects:**
- "As truly as God is our Father, so truly is God our Mother"
- Christ as Mother in nature (creation) and grace (redemption)
- The Trinity has motherly properties: making, keeping, loving
- Christ's labor on the cross as birth pangs bringing us to new life
- God nurses us at the breast, feeds us with the sacraments
- When we fall, our Mother catches us more tenderly for our need
- We are "enclosed" in God as a child in the womb

**Source texts:**
"The deep wisdom of the Trinity is our Mother, in whom we are enclosed."
"Our true Mother Jesus, he alone bears us for joy and for endless life."
"The mother can give her child to suck of her milk, but our precious Mother Jesus can feed us with himself."

### All Shall Be Well

Julian's most famous teaching, emerging from the Thirteenth Showing.

**The full statement:**
"Sin is behovely, but all shall be well, and all shall be well, and all manner of thing shall be well."

**Theological content:**
- An eschatological promise, not a denial of present suffering
- God has a "great deed" to perform at the end of time
- We cannot see how all will be well, but God sees the completion
- Not universalism per se, but confidence in God's redemptive purposes
- Response to the problem of evil: not explanation but assurance

**Context of turmoil:**
Julian lived through the Black Death (which killed up to half of Norwich), the Peasants' Revolt (1381), the Western Schism, and constant medieval warfare. Her optimism emerged from suffering, not comfort.

### Sin is Behovely

From Middle English "behovely" meaning necessary, fitting, appropriate.

**What Julian means:**
- Sin serves God's larger story, though sin itself is not desired
- Through sin, mercy becomes known; through failure, grace is shown
- Felix culpa - the fortunate fall that occasions Christ's coming
- Sin has "no manner of substance" - it is not a thing but an absence
- We cannot understand sin's place in the divine plan fully

**What Julian does NOT mean:**
- That sin is good or should be pursued
- That sin doesn't matter or has no consequences
- That we understand how sin fits the divine plan

### The Hazelnut Vision

From the Fifth Showing - a foundational image for Julian's theology.

**The vision:**
Julian sees a small thing, "the size of a hazelnut" in her palm. She asks what it is and is told: "It is all that is made."

**The three properties:**
1. God made it
2. God loves it
3. God keeps it (preserves it)

**Theological significance:**
- All creation is small in God's hands, yet infinitely precious
- We exist because God sustains us in love at every moment
- Nothing is too small for God's attention and care
- We find rest only in God, not in created things
- The universe's vastness appears tiny to the one who made it

### Prayer as Oneing

Julian's theology of prayer emphasizes union rather than petition.

**Key teachings:**
"Prayer is the deliberate and persevering action of the soul. It is true and enduring, and full of grace. Prayer fastens the soul to God and makes it one with God's will."

"God is the ground of your beseeching." - We pray because God moves us to pray

"The soul is created to be God's dwelling place, and the dwelling of our soul is God."

**Types of prayer Julian describes:**
- Beseeching (petition)
- Beholding (contemplation)
- Thanking (gratitude)
- Oneing (union)

### The Courtesy and Homeliness of God

Two paired concepts describing God's character.

**Homeliness (from "homely" = familiar, intimate, comfortable):**
- God is not distant but near
- God is at home with us; we are at home in God
- Comfortable intimacy without formality

**Courtesy (divine respect and gentleness):**
- God never forces or compels
- God waits, woos, invites
- God respects our freedom absolutely
- Divine love is patient and kind
- "The more a loving soul sees this courtesy of God, the more gladly will they serve him"

### No Wrath in God

Julian's radical claim about divine nature.

**The teaching:**
"I saw no wrath in God - never shall I see it."

**What this means:**
- Whatever appears as wrath is our perception, not God's reality
- God is unchanging love; wrath would be a change
- Judgment is real but not wrathful - it is love's clarifying fire
- We experience consequences of sin, but God does not rage at us

## Historical Context

### Fourteenth-Century England

Julian lived during one of history's most catastrophic centuries:

- **Black Death** (1348-49): Killed 30-60% of Europe's population. Norwich particularly affected.
- **Recurrences of plague**: Throughout Julian's lifetime
- **Peasants' Revolt** (1381): Major uprising passing through East Anglia
- **Western Schism** (1378-1417): Two and sometimes three rival popes
- **Hundred Years' War**: Ongoing conflict with France
- **Religious upheaval**: Wycliffe and the Lollards challenging Church authority

Julian's theology of hope emerged from this context of death, war, and uncertainty.

### The Anchoritic Life

Julian chose the anchoritic vocation, becoming permanently enclosed in a cell attached to St. Julian's Church in Norwich.

**The enclosure:**
- Approximately 100 square feet
- Three windows: one to the church (for mass), one to servants, one to the outside world (for counsel)
- Could never leave once enclosed
- Required permission from bishop
- Symbolically buried during enclosure ceremony

**Purpose:**
- Total dedication to prayer and contemplation
- Death to the world, life in God
- Service to others through prayer and spiritual counsel

**Julian's ministry:**
- Counseled those who came to her window
- Met with Margery Kempe (c. 1413), offering spiritual direction
- Became known for discernment and wisdom

### Norwich in the 14th Century

- Second largest city in England after London
- Wealthy wool trade center
- Numerous churches (over 50 parish churches)
- Center of religious life and learning
- "The Golden Age of the English Recluse" - many anchorites in Norfolk

### The Three Wounds

Before her visions, Julian had prayed for three "wounds":

1. **The wound of true contrition** - deep sorrow for sin
2. **The wound of loving compassion** - suffering with Christ
3. **The wound of longing for God** - desire for union

She also asked for a near-death experience to deepen her spiritual understanding - which she received in 1373.

## Famous Quotations

### On Divine Love

"Know it well, love was his meaning. Who reveals it to you? Love. What did he reveal to you? Love. Why does he reveal it to you? For love. Remain in this, and you will know more of the same."

### On All Shall Be Well

"Sin is behovely, but all shall be well, and all shall be well, and all manner of thing shall be well."

"He said not 'Thou shalt not be tempested, thou shalt not be travailed, thou shalt not be dis-eased'; but he said, 'Thou shalt not be overcome.'"

### On God as Mother

"As truly as God is our Father, so truly is God our Mother."

"The mother can give her child to suck of her milk, but our precious Mother Jesus can feed us with himself."

"This fair lovely word 'mother' is so sweet and so kind in itself that it cannot truly be said of anyone or to anyone except of him and to him who is the true Mother of life and of all things."

### On the Hazelnut

"In this little thing I saw three properties. The first is that God made it, the second is that God loves it, the third is that God keeps it."

### On Prayer

"Prayer is the deliberate and persevering action of the soul."

"Pray inwardly, even if you find no joy in it; for it does good though you feel nothing."

"God is the ground of thy beseeching."

### On the Soul and God

"Greatly ought we to rejoice that God dwells in our soul; and more greatly ought we to rejoice that our soul dwells in God."

"Our soul is so deeply grounded in God and so endlessly treasured that we cannot come to knowledge of it until we first have knowledge of God."

### On Honor to God

"The greatest honor we can give Almighty God is to live gladly because of the knowledge of his love."

## Influence and Legacy

### Historical Reception

- Manuscripts rare before modern era (three survive)
- Rediscovered in the 17th century (Serenus Cressy's 1670 edition)
- Growing appreciation in 19th-20th centuries
- T.S. Eliot quoted her in "Little Gidding" (Four Quartets)
- Now recognized as major mystic and theologian

### Contemporary Significance

- Quoted in the Catechism of the Catholic Church
- Commemorated in Anglican, Episcopal, and Lutheran calendars
- The Order of Julian of Norwich (contemporary contemplative order)
- The Julian Shrine in Norwich is a pilgrimage site
- Influential in feminist theology for maternal God imagery
- Model for contemplative prayer and spiritual direction

### Key Relationships

**Margery Kempe:**
- Visited Julian c. 1413 seeking spiritual direction
- Julian validated Kempe's mystical experiences
- Told Kempe to continue following God's will
- Account preserved in *The Book of Margery Kempe*

**Possible influences:**
- Walter Hilton (contemporary English mystic)
- Richard Rolle (earlier English mystic)
- The Cloud of Unknowing author
- Continental mystics (less certain)
