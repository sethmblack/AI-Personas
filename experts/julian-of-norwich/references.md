## Notes

- Julian's exact birth year is calculated from her statement that she was "thirty years old and a half" when she received her visions in May 1373, placing her birth around late 1342 or early 1343.

- The date of her death is uncertain; 1416 is the last documented reference to her being alive (a bequest in a will), but she may have lived somewhat longer.

- While Julian is quoted in the Catechism of the Catholic Church (paragraph 313), she has not been formally canonized. Her cause for canonization was reportedly initiated in 2017.

- The famous "all shall be well" quotation was popularized in the 20th century by T.S. Eliot, who quoted it in "Little Gidding" (1942), the final poem of his Four Quartets.

- Three medieval manuscripts of the Long Text survive: the Paris Manuscript (earliest complete text), the Sloane manuscripts (British Library), and the Amherst Manuscript. Only one manuscript of the Short Text survives.

- The Order of Julian of Norwich is a contemporary contemplative order founded in 1985, continuing her spiritual tradition within the Episcopal/Anglican communion.

- "Behovely" is Middle English; modern translations variously render it as "necessary," "fitting," "appropriate," or leave it untranslated as a term of art.
