# Ai Weiwei - References

This document compiles verified sources for the Ai Weiwei expert persona, covering his writings, major works, activism, detention, and scholarly analysis.

## Primary Sources

### Books by Ai Weiwei

**1000 Years of Joys and Sorrows: A Memoir** (2021)
- Publisher: Crown (US), Bodley Head (UK)
- Translator: Allan H. Barr
- Ai Weiwei's autobiography interweaving his own life with that of his father, poet Ai Qing
- Documents his childhood exile during the Cultural Revolution, his years in New York, and his rise as an artist and activist
- Title derives from his father's poetry: "Of a thousand years of joys and sorrows / Not a trace can be found"
- ISBN: 978-0553419467

**Ai Weiwei's Blog: Writings, Interviews, and Digital Rants, 2006-2009** (2011)
- Publisher: MIT Press
- Editor: Lee Ambrozy
- Collection of writings from Ai's blog before Chinese authorities shut it down on June 1, 2009
- The most complete documentation of the original Chinese blog available in any language
- Includes social commentary, criticism of government policy, thoughts on art and architecture, and autobiographical writings
- ISBN: 978-0262015219

**Weiwei-isms** (2012)
- Publisher: Princeton University Press
- Editor: Larry Warsh
- Collection of quotations from articles, tweets, and interviews
- Organized into six categories: freedom of expression; art and activism; government, power, and moral choices; the digital world; history and the future; and personal reflections
- ISBN: 978-0691157665

### Early Editorial Work (The "Cover Books")

**Black Cover Book** (1994)
- Co-edited with Xu Bing and Zeng Xiaojun
- Underground publication featuring experimental Chinese artists

**White Cover Book** (1995)
- Continuation of the experimental art documentation series

**Gray Cover Book** (1997)
- Final volume in the trilogy
- Included interviews with Western artists including Marcel Duchamp, Andy Warhol, and Jeff Koons

### Social Media Archives

**Blog (2006-2009)**
- Active from 2006 until forcibly shut down by Chinese authorities on June 1, 2009
- Platform for citizen journalism, political commentary, and documentation of the Sichuan earthquake investigation

**Twitter (@aikitweet)**
- Began using Twitter after his blog was censored
- Used VPN to circumvent China's Great Firewall
- Documented artist protests in Beijing, organized activism, and maintained international visibility

**Instagram (@aiww)**
- Continued visual documentation and political commentary
- Platform for sharing work and refugee advocacy

## Major Works and Installations

### Foundational Works

**Dropping a Han Dynasty Urn** (1995)
- Three black-and-white photographs (148 x 121 cm each)
- Documents Ai holding, dropping, and standing over the remains of a 2,000-year-old Han dynasty urn
- Began his ongoing use of antique readymade objects
- Ai's response to criticism: "Chairman Mao used to tell us that we can only build a new world if we destroy the old one"

**Study of Perspective** (1995-2011)
- Photographic series showing Ai's middle finger raised at symbolic locations
- Targets include Tiananmen Square, the White House, and the Eiffel Tower
- Ongoing documentation of dissent and challenge to institutional power

### Sichuan Earthquake Works

**Citizens' Investigation** (2008-2009)
- Grassroots investigation documenting names of children killed in the May 12, 2008 Sichuan earthquake
- Compiled 5,385 names when the Chinese government refused to release official records
- Investigated shoddy school construction ("tofu-dreg schoolhouses") as cause of deaths
- Published findings through blog and YouTube documentaries

**Remembering** (2009)
- Installation: 9,000 children's backpacks on the facade of Haus der Kunst, Munich
- 1,000 square meters in scale
- Backpacks spell out: "She lived happily for seven years in this world" (quote from a grieving mother)
- Part of exhibition "So Sorry"
- Colors matched the Toys 'R' Us logo palette

**Names of the Student Earthquake Victims Found in the Investigation** (2009)
- Audio work: Ai reading the names of 5,385 children for hours
- Transforms statistics into individual human presence

### Monumental Installations

**Sunflower Seeds (Kui Hua Zi)** (2010-2011)
- Location: Tate Modern Turbine Hall, London
- 100 million individually hand-crafted porcelain sunflower seeds
- Total weight: approximately 150 tons
- Each seed went through a 30-step procedure, hand-painted and fired at 1,300C
- Produced by 1,600 artisans over 2.5 years in Jingdezhen, China's "Porcelain Capital"
- Reference to Mao's propaganda (Mao as sun, people as sunflowers)
- Initially visitors could walk on the seeds; suspended due to dust concerns
- Tate acquired 8 million seeds (10 tonnes) in 2012

**Circle of Animals/Zodiac Heads** (2010-2011)
- Twelve bronze animal head sculptures
- Reference to the zodiac fountain heads looted from Beijing's Old Summer Palace by Anglo-French troops in 1860
- Meditation on cultural heritage, nationalism, and repatriation
- Exhibited globally including at Seattle's Olympic Sculpture Park

### Detention Response Works

**S.A.C.R.E.D.** (2013)
- Six-part sculptural installation
- Debuted at Venice Biennale 2013
- Six iron boxes with viewing apertures containing fiberglass dioramas
- Depicts scenes from Ai's 81-day detention in 2011
- Title is acronym: Supper, Accusers, Cleansing, Ritual, Entropy, Doubt
- Figures at three-quarter scale
- Documents surveillance during eating, bathing, sleeping, and interrogation
- Evokes Stations of the Cross iconography

### Refugee Crisis Works

**Life Jackets on Konzerthaus Berlin** (2016)
- 14,000 refugee life jackets wrapped around columns of Berlin's Konzerthaus
- Jackets collected from beaches on Lesbos, Greece
- Included lifeboat with "#safepassage" message
- Installed for Cinema for Peace gala, February 2016
- Location historically significant: Gendarmenmarkt was center of Huguenot refugee population in 17th century

**Law of the Journey** (2017)
- 70-meter (230-foot) inflatable boat
- 258 oversized refugee figures
- Ai's largest installation to date
- Debuted at National Gallery, Prague
- Made in Chinese factory that produces actual refugee dinghies
- Subsequently exhibited at Sydney Biennale on Cockatoo Island

**Good Fences Make Good Neighbors** (2017-2018)
- City-wide public art exhibition across New York City
- Presented by Public Art Fund for its 40th anniversary
- 300+ sites across all five boroughs
- October 12, 2017 - February 11, 2018
- Major installations:
  - "Gilded Cage" at southeast corner of Central Park
  - "Arch" under Washington Square Arch
  - "Circle Fence" around Unisphere in Flushing Meadows, Queens
- Installation at 48 East 7th Street (where Ai lived in 1980s)
- 100 posters with refugee statistics and poetry
- Title references Robert Frost's poem "Mending Wall" (1914)

### Architectural Work

**Beijing National Stadium (Bird's Nest)** (2003-2008)
- Artistic consultant with Herzog & de Meuron
- Main venue for 2008 Summer Olympics and 2022 Winter Olympics ceremonies
- 80,000 capacity (91,000 with temporary seating)
- Design inspired by Chinese ceramics
- Ai later distanced himself from the project
- Stated he felt "used by the government to spread their patriotic education"
- Called the Olympics "propaganda for the ruling party"

## Detention and Activism

### 2011 Detention

**Arrest**
- Date: April 3, 2011
- Location: Beijing Capital International Airport
- Detained without charge for 81 days
- Initial stated reason: "economic crimes" (tax evasion)
- Actual interrogation charge: "inciting subversion of state power"

**Conditions**
- Held in 12' x 24' room
- Two military police guards present at all times, never more than a few feet away
- Required to ask permission before any movement, including scratching
- Psychological abuse through constant surveillance
- No physical abuse reported

**Context**
- Part of broader crackdown on activists, bloggers, and writers
- Related to "Jasmine Revolution" fears following Arab Spring
- International protests from US, EU, and human rights organizations

**Release and Aftermath**
- Released June 22, 2011
- Charged with tax evasion
- Passport confiscated for four years
- Restricted from leaving Beijing
- Supporters crowdfunded to pay $2.4 million tax bill
- Passport returned July 2015

### Key Activism

**Sichuan Earthquake Investigation (2008-2009)**
- Citizen journalism project documenting student deaths
- Exposed "tofu-dreg" construction corruption
- Beat by police in Chengdu while investigating (required brain surgery)

**Shanghai Studio Demolition (2011)**
- Government demolished his newly built Shanghai studio
- Ai documented the destruction
- Held "river crab feast" (pun on "harmonious" - censorship term)
- Turned demolition into art event

**Refugee Advocacy (2015-present)**
- Extensive documentation on Lesbos, Greece
- Produced Human Flow documentary
- Created installations worldwide highlighting refugee crisis
- Declared himself a refugee after leaving China

## Secondary Analysis

### Scholarly Articles and Papers

**"Twitter Bodhisattva: Ai Weiwei's Media Politics"**
- Publication: Asian Studies Review, Vol. 39, No. 1
- Examines Ai's use of social media as activist tool and art form

**"Visuality and Parrhesia: Ai Weiwei's countervisual re-enactment of Alan Kurdi's image"**
- Publication: Taylor & Francis / Journal of Visual Culture
- Author: Ana Cristina Mendes (University of Lisbon)
- Analyzes Ai's 2016 re-enactment of drowned Syrian child's image
- Applies theories of visuality and countervisuality

**"Anti-Authoritarian Gestures in the Political Art of Ai Weiwei"**
- Repository: OhioLINK Electronic Theses and Dissertations
- Applies Jacques Ranciere's theories of politics and art
- Analyzes "Study of Perspective" series

**"The Power of Quantity in Ai Weiwei's Sunflower Seeds"**
- Presented at ACAH (Asian Conference on Arts & Humanities) 2015
- Argues quantity is Ai's key visual strategy for political intervention

**"Art as a political choreography through social media"**
- Available on Academia.edu
- Investigates social media as tool and space for art to drive social narratives
- References Guy Debord, Paolo Gerbaudo, Giorgio Agamben

### Exhibition Catalogues with Scholarly Essays

**Ai Weiwei** (Royal Academy of Arts, 2015)
- Interview with Tim Marlow
- Contributions from team of scholars
- Analyzes variety of Ai's output and concerns

**Ai Weiwei: Making Sense** (Design Museum, 2023)
- Authors: Justin McGuirk, Eyal Weizman, Tim Marlow, Julia Lovell
- First major publication presenting Ai's work as commentary on design
- 200+ images

**Ai Weiwei: Good Fences Make Good Neighbors** (Yale University Press, 2018)
- In-depth interview with Ai about the project's personal significance
- Documentation of the 300+ site installation

### Critical Sources

**Smarthistory**
- Academic art history resource
- Entries on "Dropping a Han Dynasty Urn" and "Sunflower Seeds"
- Contextual analysis of works within Chinese and global art history

**The Art Story**
- Comprehensive biography and critical analysis
- Timeline of major works and their significance

**Khan Academy: "Ai Weiwei, 'Remembering' and the Politics of Dissent"**
- Educational resource on the Sichuan earthquake works
- Contextualizes within People's Republic of China art history

## Documentaries

### About Ai Weiwei

**Ai Weiwei: Never Sorry** (2012)
- Director: Alison Klayman
- Runtime: 91 minutes
- Sundance Film Festival Special Jury Prize
- 98% rating on Rotten Tomatoes
- Documents:
  - Installation of "Remembering" at Haus der Kunst, Munich (2009)
  - Sunflower Seeds installation at Tate Modern (2010)
  - Beating by police in Chengdu
  - Shanghai studio demolition
  - 2011 detention
- Available: Netflix, Amazon Prime Video

**Ai Weiwei: The Fake Case** (2013)
- Director: Andreas Johnsen
- Documents aftermath of Ai's detention
- Follows his attempt to appeal tax evasion charges

**#aiww: The Arrest of Ai Weiwei** (2013)
- Theatrical play adapted for television
- Recreates his 81-day detention
- Premiered at Hampstead Theatre, London

**Bird's Nest: Herzog & de Meuron in China** (2008)
- Directors: Christoph Schaub, Michael Schindhelm
- Documents design and construction of Beijing National Stadium
- Features Ai Weiwei's role as artistic consultant

### By Ai Weiwei

**Human Flow** (2017)
- Director: Ai Weiwei
- Runtime: 140 minutes
- Documented over one year in 23 countries and 40 refugee camps
- Countries include: Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Greece, Iraq, Israel, Kenya, Turkey
- Explores global refugee crisis
- Official website: humanflow.com

**Disturbing the Peace (Lao Ma Ti Hua)** (2009)
- Documents Ai's investigation of Sichuan earthquake
- Includes footage of police confrontation in Chengdu

**So Sorry** (2010)
- Compilation of citizen investigation materials
- Distributed via YouTube and Twitter

**Coronation** (2020)
- Documents China's COVID-19 lockdown in Wuhan
- Shot remotely with citizen journalists
