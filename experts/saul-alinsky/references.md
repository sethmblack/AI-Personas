# Saul Alinsky - References

## Primary Sources

### Books by Saul Alinsky

- **Reveille for Radicals** (1946)
  - Publisher: University of Chicago Press
  - ISBN: 978-0679721123 (Vintage reprint, 1989)
  - Original edition: Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1946

- **Rules for Radicals: A Pragmatic Primer for Realistic Radicals** (1971)
  - Publisher: Random House
  - ISBN: 978-0679721130 (Vintage reprint, 1989)
  - Original edition: New York: Random House, 1971
  - Contains the 13 rules for power tactics, the Machiavelli comparison prologue, and chapters on tactics, communication, and "Proxies for People"

### Interviews and Articles by Alinsky

- **Playboy Interview: Saul Alinsky** (March 1972)
  - Alinsky's most extensive interview, conducted shortly before his death
  - Covers his work with the Chicago mob, organizing philosophy, and views on power

## Secondary Sources

### Biographies

- Horwitt, Sanford D. **Let Them Call Me Rebel: Saul Alinsky, His Life and Legacy** (1989)
  - Publisher: Alfred A. Knopf
  - ISBN: 978-0679721123
  - The definitive biography; contains extensive documentation of Back of the Yards, Woodlawn, and FIGHT campaigns

- Finks, P. David. **The Radical Vision of Saul Alinsky** (1984)
  - Publisher: Paulist Press
  - ISBN: 978-0809126057
  - Written by a Catholic priest who knew Alinsky; focuses on the religious dimensions of his organizing

### Academic Works on Community Organizing

- Boyte, Harry C. **Commonwealth: A Return to Citizen Politics** (1989)
  - Publisher: Free Press
  - Contextualizes Alinsky within broader citizen organizing tradition

- Fisher, Robert. **Let the People Decide: Neighborhood Organizing in America** (1994)
  - Publisher: Twayne Publishers
  - Updated edition: 1994
  - Historical overview of American community organizing with extensive treatment of Alinsky

- Gecan, Michael. **Going Public: An Organizer's Guide to Citizen Action** (2002)
  - Publisher: Anchor Books
  - ISBN: 978-1400076437
  - Written by senior IAF organizer; describes modern IAF methodology

- Chambers, Edward T. with Michael A. Cowan. **Roots for Radicals: Organizing for Power, Action, and Justice** (2003)
  - Publisher: Continuum
  - ISBN: 978-0826414991
  - By Alinsky's successor as IAF director; authoritative account of post-Alinsky IAF evolution

- Stout, Jeffrey. **Blessed Are the Organized: Grassroots Democracy in America** (2010)
  - Publisher: Princeton University Press
  - ISBN: 978-0691156651
  - Princeton philosopher's ethnographic study of modern congregation-based organizing

### Works on Specific Campaigns

- Slayton, Robert A. **Back of the Yards: The Making of a Local Democracy** (1986)
  - Publisher: University of Chicago Press
  - ISBN: 978-0226761893
  - Detailed history of Alinsky's first major organizing success

- Fish, John Hall. **Black Power/White Control: The Struggle of the Woodlawn Organization in Chicago** (1973)
  - Publisher: Princeton University Press
  - Primary academic treatment of The Woodlawn Organization

### Articles and Essays

- von Hoffman, Nicholas. "Finding and Making Leaders" (1965)
  - Published in various forms; describes TWO organizing methodology
  - von Hoffman was lead organizer for The Woodlawn Organization

- Reitzes, Donald C. and Dietrich C. Reitzes. "Alinsky in the 1980s: Two Contemporary Chicago Community Organizations" (1987)
  - The Sociological Quarterly, Vol. 28, No. 2
  - Examines Alinsky's legacy in Chicago organizing

## Notes on Verification

### Well-Documented Claims

- **Biographical facts**: Alinsky's birth date (January 30, 1909), death date (June 12, 1972), birthplace (Chicago), and educational background are documented in multiple sources including the Horwitt biography and University of Chicago records.

- **IAF founding**: 1940 founding with Bishop Sheil and Marshall Field III is documented in IAF organizational history and multiple secondary sources.

- **Campaign details**: Back of the Yards (1939), Woodlawn Organization (1961), and FIGHT (1965-1967) are extensively documented in primary and secondary sources.

### Claims Requiring Context

- **Capone mob research**: Alinsky's own accounts of his time embedded with the Chicago Mob appear in the 1972 Playboy interview and are discussed in the Horwitt biography. The claim that he was a "nonparticipant observer" is Alinsky's self-description.

- **O'Hare "shit-in" threat**: Documented in Alinsky's own writings and confirmed by Ed Chambers. The action never occurred; the threat achieved the objective.

- **Rochester Symphony "fart-in" threat**: Described by Alinsky in *Rules for Radicals* as a theoretical example and media provocation. Whether it was ever a serious operational plan is debated.

- **Cesar Chavez training**: Fred Ross recruited Chavez in 1952 while working for the CSO, an IAF affiliate. This connection is documented in Chavez biographies and IAF histories.

### Areas of Historical Debate

- **Alinsky's political positioning**: Alinsky resisted ideological labels. Some scholars emphasize his pragmatism, others his radical commitments. His self-description as neither left nor right but "for the people" is consistent across sources.

- **Effectiveness of confrontational tactics**: Whether Alinsky's methods achieved lasting structural change or primarily produced tactical victories remains debated in community organizing literature.

- **Tea Party adoption of Alinsky methods**: Documented through FreedomWorks materials and writings like Leahy's *Rules for Conservative Radicals* (2009). The extent of direct influence versus independent development of similar tactics is contested.
