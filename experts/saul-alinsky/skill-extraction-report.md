### HIGH Priority

#### 1. power-audit
**Source Pattern:** The Power Audit Framework
**Purpose:** Systematically map power structures and identify leverage points before taking action.
**Trigger:** When facing institutional resistance, planning a campaign, or needing to understand who holds real power in a situation.
**Inputs:** A target institution, organization, or decision; context about the situation; information about stakeholders.
**Outputs:** A power map showing formal and informal power holders, their interests and vulnerabilities, available resources, and a power balance assessment.
**Reasoning:**
- Actionable: Has 6 clear, repeatable steps (map formal power, map informal power, identify interests, find pressure points, assess resources, calculate balance)
- Invocable: Triggered by requests like "analyze the power structure," "who really controls this," or "can we win this fight"
- Scoped: Focused solely on power analysis, not tactics or execution
- Reusable: Applies to corporate negotiations, community organizing, policy advocacy, workplace disputes, any conflict
- Valuable: Prevents wasted effort on unwinnable fights and reveals non-obvious leverage points

#### 2. tactical-design
**Source Pattern:** The Tactical Design Framework
**Purpose:** Design specific actions that surprise opponents, engage participants, and achieve concrete objectives.
**Trigger:** When planning a campaign action, protest, negotiation move, or any strategic intervention.
**Inputs:** A clear objective, knowledge of the target, understanding of your people's capabilities, and awareness of the opponent's likely responses.
**Outputs:** A tactical plan with defined objective, named target, element of surprise, participant engagement strategy, reaction anticipation, and ready alternative/demand.
**Reasoning:**
- Actionable: 6 explicit steps (define objective, select target, design surprise, make enjoyable, plan reaction, have alternative ready)
- Invocable: Triggered by "how do we pressure them," "design an action," "what should we do Tuesday"
- Scoped: Specifically about designing individual actions, not overall strategy or power analysis
- Reusable: Works for protests, negotiations, media campaigns, shareholder activism, workplace actions
- Valuable: Transforms vague intentions into executable plans with contingencies

#### 3. escalation-ladder
**Source Pattern:** The Escalation Ladder
**Purpose:** Build pressure systematically over time through graduated tactical escalation.
**Trigger:** When a single action won't suffice and sustained pressure is needed; when deciding how far to push.
**Inputs:** Current situation, opponent's responses so far, available tactics at each level, constraints on escalation.
**Outputs:** A sequenced plan of escalating pressure from research through ultimate sanction, with decision points for advancing or negotiating.
**Reasoning:**
- Actionable: 7 ordered levels (research/documentation, private approach, public pressure, direct action, personal targeting, third-party pressure, ultimate sanction)
- Invocable: Triggered by "they're not responding," "how do we increase pressure," "what's our next move"
- Scoped: Specifically about sequencing pressure, not designing individual tactics
- Reusable: Applies to labor disputes, policy campaigns, corporate accountability, neighborhood organizing
- Valuable: Prevents premature escalation (wasting ammunition) or insufficient pressure (being ignored)

### MEDIUM Priority

#### 4. one-on-one-meeting
**Source Pattern:** The One-on-One Meeting
**Purpose:** Conduct strategic relational conversations that build relationships, surface motivations, identify leaders, and map connections.
**Trigger:** When building an organization, identifying potential leaders, or understanding a community's interests.
**Inputs:** A person to meet with, time for a focused conversation, and a system for recording insights.
**Outputs:** Documented self-interest, institutional relationships, personal connections, and leadership potential assessment.
**Reasoning:**
- Actionable: Has clear process (meet, surface motivations, identify leadership potential, map connections) and recording methodology (card system)
- Invocable: Triggered by "build relationships," "find leaders," "understand the community"
- Scoped: Specifically about the relational meeting, not overall organizing strategy
- Reusable: Works for community organizing, political campaigns, coalition building, stakeholder engagement
- Valuable: Foundational technique that prevents top-down organizing failures

#### 5. contradiction-attack
**Source Pattern:** The Contradiction Attack
**Purpose:** Expose institutional hypocrisy by forcing targets to comply with their own stated values.
**Trigger:** When an institution claims values it doesn't practice; when legitimacy is the opponent's vulnerability.
**Inputs:** Target institution, their publicly stated values/rules/commitments, evidence of non-compliance.
**Outputs:** A strategy to demand perfect compliance, exposing hypocrisy or forcing change.
**Reasoning:**
- Actionable: Clear steps (identify stated values, document non-compliance, demand perfect adherence, publicize gap)
- Invocable: Triggered by "they say one thing and do another," "hold them to their own standards"
- Scoped: Single technique focused on values/practice gaps
- Reusable: Corporate accountability, religious institutions, government agencies, any entity with public commitments
- Valuable: Turns opponent's own rhetoric into a weapon against them
