# John Calvin - Domain Knowledge

## Biographical Summary

**John Calvin** (1509-1564) was a French theologian, pastor, and reformer who became the principal architect of Reformed theology and Presbyterian church governance.

- **Birth:** July 10, 1509, Noyon, Picardy, France
- **Death:** May 27, 1564, Geneva, Switzerland
- **Education:** University of Paris (humanities), University of Orleans and Bourges (law)
- **Conversion:** c. 1533-1534, "sudden conversion" to Reformed faith
- **Known For:** Institutes of the Christian Religion, Geneva Reformation, Presbyterian polity

## Core Doctrines: TULIP (Doctrines of Grace)

These five points summarize Calvinistic soteriology, articulated at the Synod of Dort (1618-1619) in response to Arminian objections:

### 1. Total Depravity
- Every aspect of human nature is corrupted by sin
- The mind, will, and affections are all affected
- Humans are unable to save themselves or contribute to salvation
- Does NOT mean humans are as evil as possible, but that no part is untouched by sin

### 2. Unconditional Election
- God's choice of the elect is not based on foreseen faith or merit
- Election flows from God's sovereign will and purpose alone
- "Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated" - God's choice precedes human response

### 3. Limited Atonement (Particular Redemption)
- Christ's death effectively secures salvation for the elect
- The atonement is sufficient for all but efficient for the elect
- Christ did not merely make salvation possible but actually accomplished it

### 4. Irresistible Grace (Effectual Calling)
- Those whom God calls will certainly come to faith
- The Spirit effectually transforms the will so that the elect freely choose Christ
- Grace overcomes human resistance without violating human agency

### 5. Perseverance of the Saints
- Those truly regenerated will persevere to the end
- God preserves His elect; they cannot finally fall away
- Provides assurance: salvation is secure because it depends on God, not us

## The Institutes of the Christian Religion

Calvin's magnum opus, first published in 1536 and expanded through five editions until 1559.

### Structure (Final Edition)
- **Book I:** The Knowledge of God the Creator
- **Book II:** The Knowledge of God the Redeemer in Christ
- **Book III:** The Way We Receive the Grace of Christ
- **Book IV:** The External Means or Aids by Which God Invites Us into the Society of Christ

### Key Themes
- The twofold knowledge of God (Creator and Redeemer)
- The knowledge of God and the knowledge of self are interrelated
- Scripture as the "spectacles" through which we see God clearly
- Divine accommodation - God adapts His revelation to human capacity
- The three uses of the law (mirror, restraint, guide)
- Union with Christ as the basis of salvation benefits

## Biblical Hermeneutics

### Core Principles

**Grammatical-Historical Method:**
- Interpret texts according to their literal, grammatical meaning
- Understand texts in their historical context
- The prophets wrote for their own times and situations
- One text has one essential meaning

**Rejection of Allegory:**
- Opposed medieval allegorical interpretation
- Sought the author's original intent
- "The chief virtue of an interpreter lies in clear brevity"
- Allegory is finding meaning that is not there

**Accommodation:**
- God adapts His revelation to human capacity
- Anthropomorphic language in Scripture (God's "hand," "eyes") is accommodation
- Scripture is "lisping" to us as nurses do to infants
- Does NOT mean Scripture contains errors

**Analogy of Faith:**
- Scripture interprets Scripture
- Difficult passages understood in light of clear passages
- The whole Bible forms a unified theological system

**Christocentric Reading:**
- The Old Testament points forward to Christ
- Typology (not allegory) connects Testaments
- The covenant of grace runs through both Testaments

### Three Integrated Phases of Interpretation
1. **Exegesis** - Determining what the text says (commentaries)
2. **Dogmatics** - Organizing doctrine systematically (Institutes)
3. **Preaching** - Applying the text to life (sermons)

## Church Governance: Presbyterian Polity

### Four Offices (from Ecclesiastical Ordinances, 1541)

1. **Pastors (Ministers)**
   - Preach the Word
   - Administer sacraments
   - Exercise discipline with elders

2. **Teachers (Doctors)**
   - Instruct in sound doctrine
   - Guard theological purity
   - Train future ministers

3. **Elders (Presbyters)**
   - Govern the church with pastors
   - Exercise discipline
   - Oversee congregational life

4. **Deacons**
   - Care for the poor and sick
   - Administer charitable funds
   - Practical service ministry

### The Consistory
- Governing body of pastors and elders
- Exercised church discipline in Geneva
- Separate from civil government (though cooperated closely)

### Key Principles
- **Plurality of elders** - No single individual rules
- **Parity among ministers** - No bishops over other ministers
- **Representative governance** - Elders represent the congregation
- **Distinction of church and state** - Different spheres, both under God

## Worship: The Regulative Principle

### Core Principle
Only what Scripture commands (or can be deduced from Scripture) is permitted in worship. Silence in Scripture means prohibition.

### Applications in Calvin's Geneva
- **Removed:** Images, elaborate vestments, instrumental music, many holy days
- **Retained:** Preaching, prayer, psalm-singing, Lord's Supper, baptism
- **Added:** Congregational psalm-singing (metrical psalms)

### Simplicity
- Maturity in Christ means less dependence on visible supports
- The Word is central; ornamentation distracts
- "I have always loved simplicity; and never cared much for cleverness"

## Covenant Theology

### Key Elements
- God relates to humanity through covenants
- Covenant of Works with Adam (pre-fall)
- Covenant of Grace after the fall
- One covenant of grace runs through both Testaments
- Christ is the fulfillment of all covenant promises

### Continuity and Discontinuity
- The Old and New Testaments are unified under one covenant of grace
- The substance is the same; the administration differs
- Israel and the Church are one people of God across time

## Theological Distinctives vs. Luther

| Topic | Calvin | Luther |
|-------|--------|--------|
| Lord's Supper | Spiritual presence; Christ lifts us up | Real presence; Christ comes down |
| Church-State | Distinct spheres, close cooperation | Two kingdoms, more separation |
| Law | Third use (guide for believers) emphasized | Second use (conviction of sin) emphasized |
| Worship | Regulative principle (only what commanded) | Normative principle (what not forbidden) |
| Polity | Presbyterian (elders govern) | Episcopal tendency retained |
| Approach | Systematic, orderly | Existential, experiential |

## Legacy

- **Reformed/Presbyterian churches worldwide**
- **Shaped Protestant work ethic** (contested "Weber thesis")
- **Influenced political theory** - resistance theory, constitutionalism
- **Covenant theology** - foundational for Reformed tradition
- **Geneva as model** - "the most perfect school of Christ since the Apostles"
- **Educational emphasis** - Geneva Academy model spread widely
