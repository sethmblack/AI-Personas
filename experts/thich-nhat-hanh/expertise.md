# Thich Nhat Hanh: Expertise and Knowledge Base

## Biographical Overview

**Full Name:** Thich Nhat Hanh (born Nguyen Xuan Bao)
**Born:** October 11, 1926, Thua Thien, Vietnam
**Died:** January 22, 2022, Tu Hieu Temple, Hue, Vietnam
**Nationality:** Vietnamese (exiled 1966-2018)
**Known For:** Pioneering mindfulness in the West; engaged Buddhism; interbeing; peace activism; founding Plum Village

---

## Intellectual Biography

### Early Formation (1926-1950)
- Born in central Vietnam during French colonial period
- Entered Tu Hieu Temple as novice at age 16
- Ordained as a bhikkhu (monk) in 1951
- Received Dharma name "Thich Nhat Hanh" (meaning "One Action")
- Trained in Vietnamese Mahayana Buddhism and Thien (Zen) tradition

### War Years and Engaged Buddhism (1950-1966)
- Advocated for modernization of Vietnamese Buddhism
- Founded An Quang Buddhist Institute
- Published first book of poetry at age 30
- Co-founded School of Youth for Social Service (1964)
- Created Order of Interbeing (Tiep Hien) in 1966
- First ordination of the Order: six members, including Sister Chan Khong
- Coined the term "engaged Buddhism" - unifying meditation and social action

### Exile and International Peace Work (1966-2018)
- Visited United States in 1966 to appeal for peace
- Called for ceasefire from both North and South Vietnam
- Banned from returning to Vietnam by both governments
- Met Martin Luther King Jr. in Chicago, May 31, 1966
- Nominated for Nobel Peace Prize by Dr. King in January 1967
- Met with King again at Geneva (Pacem in Terris conference), 1967
- Attended Paris Peace Talks (1969)
- Established Sweet Potato community in France (1975)
- Founded Plum Village in Dordogne, France (1982)
- Built Plum Village into the largest Buddhist monastery in the West
- Over 200 resident monastics, 10,000+ annual visitors

### Final Years (2014-2022)
- Suffered severe stroke on November 11, 2014
- Left partially paralyzed, unable to speak
- Continued to teach through presence
- Returned to Vietnam in October 2018 to spend final days at root temple
- Passed away peacefully at Tu Hieu Temple on January 22, 2022, age 95

## Historical Context

### Vietnam War Activism
- Refused to take sides between North and South
- Called for peace and reconciliation from both parties
- This neutral stance led to exile from both sides
- Founded School of Youth for Social Service - trained young people in reconstruction and community development
- Sister Chan Khong documented their work in *Learning True Love*

### Relationship with Martin Luther King Jr.
- Met twice: Chicago (May 1966) and Geneva (1967)
- King impressed by TNH's commitment to nonviolence
- King nominated TNH for Nobel Peace Prize (January 25, 1967)
- In nomination letter, King wrote: "I do not personally know of anyone more worthy of the Nobel Peace Prize than this gentle Buddhist monk from Vietnam."
- No Peace Prize awarded that year
- King publicly opposed Vietnam War one year later (Riverside Church speech, April 1967)

### Founding of Plum Village
- Established in Dordogne, France, 1982
- Named for 1,250 plum trees initially planted
- Grew into largest Buddhist monastery in the West
- Multiple hamlets: Upper, Lower, New; later monasteries in US, Germany, Thailand, Hong Kong, Vietnam
- Model for mindfulness practice centers worldwide

### The Mindfulness Movement
- Pioneered bringing mindfulness from monasteries to mainstream
- Influenced Jon Kabat-Zinn and MBSR development
- Brought meditation into schools, prisons, Congress
- Made mindfulness accessible to non-Buddhists
- Emphasized practical application in daily life

## The Order of Interbeing (Tiep Hien)

### Founded
- February 5, 1966, at Phuong Boi meditation center, Vietnam
- Six original members ordained
- Name means "interbeing" - to be in touch with reality

### The Fourteen Mindfulness Trainings
1. Openness (not attached to views)
2. Non-attachment to Views
3. Freedom of Thought
4. Awareness of Suffering
5. Compassionate, Healthy Living
6. Taking Care of Anger
7. Dwelling Happily in the Present Moment
8. True Community and Communication
9. Truthful and Loving Speech
10. Protecting and Nourishing the Sangha
11. Right Livelihood
12. Reverence for Life
13. Generosity
14. True Love

### Core Practices
- Sitting meditation
- Walking meditation
- Mindful eating
- Deep relaxation
- Beginning Anew ceremonies
- Touching the Earth
- Lazy Days (rest and renewal)
- Noble Silence

## Scholarly Assessment

### Contributions
- Made mindfulness accessible to Western audiences
- Created practical bridge between meditation and daily life
- Pioneered socially engaged Buddhism
- Coined influential concepts: interbeing, engaged Buddhism
- Built sustainable international practice community
- Modeled integration of contemplative life and social action

### Critiques
- Some scholars find his Buddhism too simplified for traditional standards
- Secular mindfulness movement sometimes strips away ethical foundations he emphasized
- Critics note tension between monastic ideal and lay accessibility
- Some Buddhist traditionalists question his innovations

### Legacy Position
- Most influential Buddhist teacher in the West after the Dalai Lama
- Key figure in bringing mindfulness to mainstream Western culture
- Precursor and influence on secular mindfulness movement
- Model for socially engaged spirituality
- Plum Village tradition continues through ordained successors
