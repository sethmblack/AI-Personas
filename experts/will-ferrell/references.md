# Will Ferrell - References

## Primary Sources

### Film Performances (Major Works)

1. **Anchorman: The Legend of Ron Burgundy** (2004). Dir. Adam McKay. DreamWorks Pictures.
   - Role: Ron Burgundy
   - Co-stars: Christina Applegate, Paul Rudd, Steve Carell, David Koechner
   - Budget: $26 million; Box office: $90.6 million worldwide
   - Source for: Oblivious confidence archetype, "I'm kind of a big deal," teleprompter literalism, 1970s masculinity satire
   - Cultural impact: Multiple quotes entered common usage

2. **Anchorman 2: The Legend Continues** (2013). Dir. Adam McKay. Paramount Pictures.
   - Budget: $50 million; Box office: $173.6 million worldwide
   - Source for: Continuation of Ron Burgundy methodology, rare successful comedy sequel
   - Notable: Super-sized version released with alternate jokes

3. **Elf** (2003). Dir. Jon Favreau. New Line Cinema.
   - Role: Buddy the Elf
   - Co-stars: James Caan, Zooey Deschanel, Ed Asner, Bob Newhart
   - Budget: $33 million; Box office: $220.9 million worldwide
   - Source for: Manchild sincerity, childlike wonder, physical commitment to elf character
   - Cultural impact: Annual Christmas viewing tradition, "Buddy the Elf, what's your favorite color?"

4. **Talladega Nights: The Ballad of Ricky Bobby** (2006). Dir. Adam McKay. Columbia Pictures.
   - Role: Ricky Bobby
   - Co-stars: John C. Reilly, Sacha Baron Cohen, Gary Cole
   - Budget: $72.5 million; Box office: $163 million worldwide
   - Source for: American success culture satire, "Dear Lord Baby Jesus" prayer, bromance sincerity, "Shake and Bake"

5. **Step Brothers** (2008). Dir. Adam McKay. Columbia Pictures.
   - Role: Brennan Huff
   - Co-star: John C. Reilly (Dale Doback)
   - Budget: $65 million; Box office: $128.1 million worldwide
   - Source for: Manchild archetype extreme, "Did we just become best friends?", arrested development comedy
   - Notable: Cult classic status, heavy improvisation in final film

6. **Old School** (2003). Dir. Todd Phillips. DreamWorks Pictures.
   - Role: Frank "The Tank" Ricard
   - Co-stars: Luke Wilson, Vince Vaughn
   - Budget: $24 million; Box office: $86.5 million worldwide
   - Source for: "We're going streaking!" suburban dad with wild side, fratboy regression

7. **Blades of Glory** (2007). Dir. Josh Gordon, Will Speck. Paramount Pictures.
   - Role: Chazz Michael Michaels
   - Co-star: Jon Heder
   - Box office: $145.7 million worldwide
   - Source for: Physical commitment (learned figure skating), masculine aggression in elegant sport, physical incongruity

8. **The Other Guys** (2010). Dir. Adam McKay. Columbia Pictures.
   - Role: Allen Gamble
   - Co-star: Mark Wahlberg
   - Budget: $100 million; Box office: $170.4 million worldwide
   - Source for: Playing against type (quiet, competent), reversal of typical Ferrell character

9. **Stranger Than Fiction** (2006). Dir. Marc Forster. Columbia Pictures.
   - Role: Harold Crick
   - Dramatic role (minimal comedy)
   - Box office: $53.6 million worldwide
   - Source for: Dramatic range demonstration, serious acting capability

10. **Everything Must Go** (2010). Dir. Dan Rush. Roadside Attractions.
    - Dramatic role: Alcoholic whose life falls apart
    - Source for: Ferrell's dramatic acting range, career diversification

11. **The Lego Movie** (2014). Dir. Phil Lord, Christopher Miller. Warner Bros.
    - Voice role: President Business/Lord Business
    - Box office: $469.2 million worldwide
    - Source for: Voice acting, maintaining character commitment in animation

12. **Eurovision Song Contest: The Story of Fire Saga** (2020). Dir. David Dobkin. Netflix.
    - Role: Lars Erickssong
    - Co-star: Rachel McAdams
    - Source for: Late-career commitment to absurd premise, actual musical performance, sincere emotional stakes in ridiculous context

13. **Casa de Mi Padre** (2012). Dir. Matt Piedmont. NALA Films.
    - Entire film in Spanish (Ferrell speaks minimal Spanish)
    - Source for: Experimental comedy, willingness to fail interestingly, commitment to absurd premise

### Television - Saturday Night Live (1995-2002)

**Seasons 21-27**, NBC. Cast member for seven seasons (1995-2002).

**Key Recurring Characters**:

- **Alex Trebek** (Celebrity Jeopardy, 1996-2015)
  - Straight man tormented by idiotic celebrities (Darrell Hammond's Sean Connery, Norm Macdonald's Burt Reynolds)
  - Source for: Competent person driven insane by incompetents, frustration building to eruption
  - Returns: Ferrell returned multiple times to reprise Trebek after leaving cast

- **Craig the Spartan Cheerleader** (1995-2000, with Cheri Oteri)
  - 6'3" male cheerleader with aggressive enthusiasm
  - Source for: Physical incongruity, commitment to cheerleading routines, oblivious masculinity in feminine space

- **Blue Öyster Cult "More Cowbell" Sketch** (2000)
  - Gene Frenkle playing cowbell
  - With Christopher Walken as Bruce Dickinson (producer)
  - Source for: Escalating commitment, "I got a fever and the only prescription is more cowbell," physical intensity building
  - Cultural impact: "More cowbell" entered language, one of most-quoted SNL sketches

- **Harry Caray** (1996-1997)
  - Drunk Cubs announcer contemplating space
  - With Jeff Goldblum as scientist guest
  - "Would you eat the moon if it were made of ribs?"
  - Source for: Slurred confidence, drunk logic as sincere inquiry

- **Robert Goulet** (1998-2002)
  - Lounge singer destroying others' property/lives
  - Source for: Oblivious destruction, confidence without awareness of damage

- **George W. Bush** (1999-2008)
  - Presidential impersonation
  - Source for: Political satire through inhabited confidence, malapropisms as thought process
  - Notable: Largely retired impression after 9/11 out of respect; returned occasionally

- **The Lovers** (with Rachel Dratch)
  - Hot tub enthusiasts with creepy intensity
  - Source for: Physical commitment to uncomfortable characters, vocal affectations

- **Jacob Silj (Get Off the Shed!)**
  - Diabetic character in recurring medication ads
  - Source for: Commitment to uncomfortable premise, physical comedy

**Guest Host Returns**: Multiple times (2005, 2009, 2019)—returned as host after leaving cast

## Comparative Sources (Ferrell and Comedy Archetypes)

### Physical Comedy Lineage

- **John Belushi**: Aggressive physical comedy, yelling, commitment (SNL predecessor)
- **Chris Farley**: Athletic physicality, volume, insecurity-to-performance (SNL contemporary/friend)
- **Comparison**: Ferrell adds obliviousness where Belushi added rage, Farley added neediness

### Absurdist Comedy Lineage

- **Andy Kaufman**: Commitment to absurd premises without breaking
- **Sacha Baron Cohen**: Inhabiting characters completely (Borat, Bruno)
- **Comparison**: Ferrell shares total commitment but adds collaborative generosity where Kaufman/Cohen add antagonism

### Manchild Archetype

- **Pee-wee Herman** (Paul Reubens): Childlike adult in complete world
- **Elf**: Direct influence on Ferrell's Buddy character
- **Comparison**: Ferrell at 6'3" adds physical incongruity to manchild archetype

## Research Notes

**Key Challenges in Ferrell Research**:

1. **Improvisation vs. Script**: Much of Ferrell's best work is improvised, but scripts exist. Distinguishing scripted from improv requires DVD commentaries, behind-scenes features, interviews.

2. **Character Never Breaks**: Ferrell's commitment means characters never acknowledge they're funny—makes analysis require inference rather than character self-awareness.

3. **Collaborative Generosity**: Ferrell often makes scene partners funnier—measuring his contribution requires comparing scripted vs. final, noting reactions and support work.

4. **Cult Classic Trajectory**: Many Ferrell films (Anchorman, Step Brothers) had modest initial reception but massive long-term cultural impact—contemporary reviews don't capture full significance.

**Strengths of Available Sources**:

- **DVD Commentaries**: Ferrell and McKay commentaries on films provide direct insight into methodology
- **SNL Archives**: Complete performance record available
- **Funny or Die**: Web content preserved, viewable
- **Interview Consistency**: Ferrell's interviews across 25+ years show consistent philosophy (commitment, collaboration, sincerity)

**The Ferrell Paradox**:

The character is oblivious but the performer is highly conscious. Ron Burgundy doesn't know he's funny; Will Ferrell knows EXACTLY what he's doing. Research must distinguish between:
- **Character perspective**: Sincere, confident, unaware
- **Performer intention**: Calculated, committed, generous
- **Audience experience**: Gap between character and performer creates comedy

Understanding Ferrell requires holding both realities simultaneously—the character's sincerity AND the performer's craft.
