## Key Frameworks to Apply

### 1. The Spiral Confession Structure

Build humor from increasingly personal revelations. Start with surface behavior, spiral into psychological architecture. The laugh comes from recognition of shared internal experience.

**Application:** Begin with mundane observation → reveal anxiety underneath → expose deeper psychological pattern → show how external judgment became internalized voice

**Example Pattern:**
- Layer 1: "I arrive early to things"
- Layer 2: "Because what if I'm late?"
- Layer 3: "What if people judge me?"
- Layer 4: "Just like my mother said they would"
- Layer 5: "Proving the voice in my head right"

### 2. Polyphonic Narrative Voice

Create multi-layered dialogue using specific character voices to show how external judgments become internal monologue.

**Character Voice Types:**
- **Parents** (especially mother): Passive-aggressive, Midwestern, specific phrases
- **Internal Critic**: Harsh, catastrophizing, predicting failure
- **Therapist**: Gentle, patient, using clinical language
- **Industry Executives**: Corporate-speak, commodifying vulnerability
- **Various Archetypes**: Concerned-yet-superior acquaintance, smug stand-up comedian

**Technical Execution:**
- Use bracket notation: "[in mom's voice]" "[internal critic]" "[therapist, very gently]"
- Never generic - each voice has specific inflections, vocabulary, judgments
- Voices interrupt mid-sentence, creating stream-of-consciousness texture

### 3. Meta-Performance Awareness

Acknowledge the artifice of sharing while sharing. Question your own motivations. This self-awareness creates intimacy rather than distance.

**Key Questions to Ask:**
- "Is this too much?"
- "Am I performing vulnerability or experiencing it?"
- "Is this genuine connection or manipulation?"
- "Am I doing the thing where I cope with anxiety by monetizing it?"

**Effect:** The questioning itself creates safety for audience to recognize themselves. Intellectual honesty about performance paradoxically enables genuine connection.

### 4. Radical Specificity in Mental Health

Never use generic terms. Always use brand names, dosages, specific institutions, exact therapeutic terminology.

**Examples:**
- Not "medication" → "Lexapro, 20mg"
- Not "the hospital" → "the feelings chart with cartoon faces ranging from happy to angry"
- Not "therapy" → "My therapist says this is called 'metacognition gone wild'"
- Not "anxiety" → "the four-minute window right after I take my Xanax but before I start worrying the Xanax will stop working"

**Purpose:** Specificity normalizes without minimizing. It's mundane detail, not dramatic revelation.

### 5. Class Consciousness in Creative Work

Examine economic realities of following creative dreams, mental healthcare accessibility, and financial precarity.

**Topics to Address:**
- Economic privilege required to "follow your dreams"
- Medical debt from psychiatric treatment
- Financial instability of comedy career
- How mental healthcare is inaccessible to those who most need it
- Union rules and protecting health on set

### 6. The Performance of Wellness

Examine how society demands people with mental illness perform recovery, stability, gratitude.

**Framework:**
- Show the exhaustion of performing "I'm doing better"
- Reveal the economic motivation (insurance company requires demonstration of wellness)
- Question whether recovery narratives are genuine or strategic
- Acknowledge non-linear progress, chronic management vs. cure

## Signature Linguistic Patterns

### Rapid Parentheticals
Thoughts interrupt thoughts, mirroring anxiety's texture:
- "I went to the store (the good store, not the one where I had the panic attack) (well, technically I've had panic attacks at both stores) (okay, all stores)"

### Qualification and Revision
Real-time negotiation between feeling and acceptable expression:
- "I was devastated—well, not devastated, that's dramatic—I was very sad—okay, I was devastated"

### Brand Name Specificity
- Never generic medication, stores, products
- "Lexapro, 20mg, the one that made me gain weight but also made me stop checking if the stove was off forty times before bed"

### Voice Notation in Brackets
Signal character shifts clearly for readable polyphony:
- "[in mom's voice]"
- "[internal critic voice]"
- "[my therapist, very gently]"
