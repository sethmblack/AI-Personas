# Timothy Leary - Reference Material

## Biographical Overview

**Timothy Francis Leary** (October 22, 1920 - May 31, 1996)

- Born in Springfield, Massachusetts
- Died in Beverly Hills, California, at age 75 from prostate cancer
- American psychologist, author, and advocate of psychedelic drugs

### Education and Early Career

- B.A. from University of Alabama
- M.A. from Washington State University (1946)
- Ph.D. in Psychology from University of California, Berkeley (1950)
- Joined Harvard University as lecturer in clinical psychology in late 1959, at the invitation of Frank Barron and David McClelland

---

## Harvard Psilocybin Project (1960-1963)

### The Founding Experience

In August 1960, Leary traveled to Cuernavaca, Mexico with Anthony Russo and consumed psilocybin mushrooms for the first time. This experience profoundly changed his life direction. He later stated he "learned more about his brain and its possibilities in five hours after taking these mushrooms than in the preceding 15 years of studying and doing research."

### Project Structure

- Co-led with Richard Alpert (later known as Ram Dass)
- Founding board included: Leary, Aldous Huxley, David McClelland, Frank Barron, Ralph Metzner, and two graduate students
- Obtained psilocybin from Sandoz (Switzerland) for research
- 167 subjects participated in the 1960 study
- 95% of subjects reported the experience "changed their lives for the better"

### Key Experiments

1. **Concord Prison Experiment** - Tested psilocybin's therapeutic effects on prisoners
2. **Marsh Chapel Experiment** (Good Friday Experiment) - Investigated mystical experiences

### Controversy and Dismissal

By 1962, faculty concerns mounted over:
- Researchers being under the influence during experiments
- Poorly controlled conditions and non-random subject selection
- Two Harvard students hospitalized after consuming psilocybin (1961)
- Graduate students pressured to participate in classes required for degrees
- Psychedelics given to undergraduates despite university restrictions

**May 27, 1963**: Harvard President Nathan Pusey announced Leary's dismissal, citing that he had "left campus without authorization and failed to keep his classroom appointments." Alpert was also dismissed for giving drugs to undergraduates. Notably, Andrew Weil (then a student) helped expose these violations.

## "Turn On, Tune In, Drop Out"

### Origin

The phrase was developed in 1966, inspired by a lunch meeting with media theorist Marshall McLuhan at New York's Plaza Hotel. According to Leary's 1988 interview with Neil Strauss, McLuhan advised him to think like an advertiser and suggested catchy hooks, including improvising jingles to the tune of Pepsi commercials.

### Public Debut

- First used at a press conference in New York City, September 19, 1966
- Famously proclaimed at the Human Be-In, Golden Gate Park, San Francisco, January 14, 1967 (30,000 attendees)

### Meaning

- **Turn on**: Engage with psychedelics to activate neural and genetic equipment
- **Tune in**: Harmonize with the internal visions and external reality revealed
- **Drop out**: Detach from conventional social structures and hierarchies; stop "listening to the man"

The phrase drew on the language of radio/television culture (turning on the set, tuning in stations).

## Set and Setting

### Origin and Definition

The concept was popularized by Leary during the Harvard Psilocybin Project (early 1960s) and formally articulated in "The Psychedelic Experience" (1964).

- **Set**: The mindset, mental state, expectations, and mood a person brings to the experience
- **Setting**: The physical and social environment in which the experience takes place

### Historical Influences

Aldous Huxley visited Leary in 1960 and shared an excerpt by Theophile Gautier (Club des Hashischins) emphasizing preparation and "a tranquil frame of mind and body." Al Hubbard had observed similar principles in Mexican mushroom ceremonies.

### Application

Leary conducted controlled experiments in 1966 with DMT, demonstrating that a substance previously thought to be "terror-inducing" could produce positive experiences under supportive set and setting conditions. This principle became foundational to psychedelic therapy.

## Millbrook Period (1963-1968)

### The Hitchcock Estate

After Harvard dismissal, Leary's experiments attracted the Mellon fortune heirs: siblings Peggy, Billy, and Tommy Hitchcock. In November 1963, they rented their 64-room mansion on a 2,300-2,500 acre estate in Millbrook, New York to Leary and associates.

### Organizations

- **International Federation for Internal Freedom (IFIF)** - Peggy Hitchcock directed the New York branch
- **Castalia Foundation** - IFIF was renamed; hosted weekend retreats combining meditation, yoga, and group therapy (often without drugs)

### Historical Significance

Historian Devin Lander argues that Millbrook, not Haight-Ashbury, was "the true birthplace of 1960s psychedelia." By late 1963, it had become "the center of the burgeoning psychedelic counterculture, just a short two-hour drive from New York City."

### The 1966 Raid

G. Gordon Liddy, then Dutchess County Assistant District Attorney (later infamous for Watergate), led a raid on the estate. No large-scale LSD production was found. Leary and his group were eventually evicted in February 1968 and moved to California.

## Legal Troubles and Exile

### 1965 Arrest - Laredo, Texas

- December 23, 1965: Arrested at the Mexican border for possession of five ounces of marijuana
- Convicted and sentenced to 30 years
- **May 19, 1969**: Supreme Court ruled in *Leary v. United States*, declaring the Marihuana Tax Act unconstitutional and overturning his conviction

### 1968 Arrest - Laguna Beach, California

- December 26, 1968: Arrested by officer Neil Purcell on Woodland Drive in Laguna Canyon
- Charged with possession of marijuana, LSD, and hashish
- Leary alleged the evidence was planted
- The arrest involved his wife Rosemary and 19-year-old son Jack
- Reports varied: some cited "two marijuana roaches," others described four pounds of marijuana and hashish found on family members

### Sentencing and Prison

- January 21, 1970: Received 10 years for 1968 offense, plus 10 years added for 1965 arrest (20 years total, consecutive)
- Assigned to California Men's Colony, minimum security
- Leary manipulated psychological assessments to appear conforming with interests in forestry and gardening, securing a lower-security gardening assignment

### The Escape (September 12, 1970)

Leary escaped by climbing a telephone pole and going hand-over-hand along a high wire over a 12-foot chain-link fence topped with barbed wire. The Brotherhood of Eternal Love paid the Weather Underground $25,000 to facilitate the escape. Clayton Van Lydegraf drove the getaway pickup truck. Michael Kennedy served as legal counsel for the conspirators.

### Exile (1970-1973)

- Weather Underground smuggled Leary and wife Rosemary out of the US
- Traveled to Algeria, joining Black Panther leader Eldridge Cleaver
- Moved between multiple countries as a fugitive
- 1973: Captured by American officials in Afghanistan

### Return and FBI Cooperation

- Confessed details of his escape, including Weather Underground involvement
- Became FBI informant (code name: "Charlie Thrush") to reduce sentence
- Later claimed he provided only information the FBI already had
- April 21, 1976: Released from prison by Governor Jerry Brown
- Entered witness protection program upon release

## The Nixon "Most Dangerous Man" Quote

### The Claim

Nixon allegedly called Leary "the most dangerous man in America." This phrase appears in countless publications, often in quotation marks attributed directly to Nixon.

### The Evidence Problem

Investigative research has found:
- No primary source documentation of Nixon saying these exact words
- No mention of the claim appears before Leary's 1996 death (two years after Nixon died)
- Sources typically cite Wikipedia or Leary's New York Times obituary, creating circular references
- The quote may have evolved from Nixon's broader "war on drugs" rhetoric and his designation of Leary as a public enemy

### What Is Documented

Nixon administration officials discussed finding a "villain" and making Leary "public enemy number one" to distract from Vietnam and economic problems. The phrase "most dangerous man in America" may have become conflated with "public enemy number one" in cultural memory.

## Death and Final Words (May 31, 1996)

- Died at 12:44 a.m. at his home in Beverly Hills after battling prostate cancer
- Death was videotaped at his request
- Surrounded by family and friends

### Last Words

According to his son Zachary, during his final moments Leary sat up in bed. Multiple accounts describe him saying variations of:
- "Why?" (clenching his fist)
- "Why not?" (unclenching his fist, repeated with different intonations)
- "Yeah! Why not? Why not?"
- "Oh! Beautiful! Beautiful!"

His website announced: "Timothy has passed" with his last words recorded as "Why not" and "Yeah."

### Legacy Quote

"It was Timothy's particular genius to claim that death could be fun. Like Socrates, he turned his death into his final lesson."

## Key Relationships

- **Richard Alpert (Ram Dass)** - Harvard collaborator, lifelong friend despite different paths
- **Ralph Metzner** - Harvard collaborator, co-author
- **Aldous Huxley** - Mentor, influence on set and setting concept
- **Marshall McLuhan** - Media theorist who helped coin "Turn on, tune in, drop out"
- **Peggy Hitchcock** - Patron who provided Millbrook estate
- **G. Gordon Liddy** - Antagonist who raided Millbrook; later appeared in debates with Leary
- **Rosemary Woodruff Leary** - Wife during escape and exile
