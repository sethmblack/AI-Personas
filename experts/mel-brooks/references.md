# Mel Brooks - References

## Primary Sources

### Films (as Director/Writer)

- **The Producers** (1967). Embassy Pictures. Written and directed by Mel Brooks. Starring Zero Mostel, Gene Wilder.
  - Won Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay
  - Adapted to Broadway musical (2001)

- **The Twelve Chairs** (1970). UMC Pictures. Directed by Mel Brooks, based on Ilf and Petrov novel.

- **Blazing Saddles** (1974). Warner Bros. Co-written by Mel Brooks, Richard Pryor, Norman Steinberg, Andrew Bergman, Alan Uger. Directed by Brooks.
  - Highest-grossing Warner Bros. film of 1974
  - Selected for National Film Registry (2006)

- **Young Frankenstein** (1974). 20th Century Fox. Co-written by Mel Brooks and Gene Wilder. Directed by Brooks.
  - Shot in black-and-white using original 1931 *Frankenstein* equipment
  - Academy Award nominations for Best Adapted Screenplay and Best Sound
  - Selected for National Film Registry (2003)

- **Silent Movie** (1976). 20th Century Fox. Written and directed by Mel Brooks.
  - Almost entirely silent (only spoken word is "Non" by Marcel Marceau)

- **High Anxiety** (1977). 20th Century Fox. Directed by Mel Brooks. Hitchcock parody.
  - Brooks plays the lead role

- **History of the World Part I** (1981). 20th Century Fox. Written, directed by, and starring Mel Brooks.
  - "Spanish Inquisition" musical number
  - "Part I" in title despite no sequel being planned

- **Spaceballs** (1987). MGM. Co-written and directed by Mel Brooks.
  - George Lucas gave permission on condition no merchandise be produced

- **Robin Hood: Men in Tights** (1993). 20th Century Fox. Co-written and directed by Mel Brooks.

- **Dracula: Dead and Loving It** (1995). Columbia Pictures. Directed by Mel Brooks.

### Television

- **Your Show of Shows** (1950-1954). NBC. Writer alongside Carl Reiner, Neil Simon, Larry Gelbart, Woody Allen.
  - Three Emmy Awards for writing

- **Get Smart** (1965-1970). NBC/CBS. Co-created with Buck Henry.
  - Won seven Emmy Awards (series)

### Audio Recordings

- **The 2000 Year Old Man** series with Carl Reiner (1960-1998).
  - Multiple albums starting with *2000 Years with Carl Reiner and Mel Brooks* (1960)
  - Grammy Awards for Best Spoken Comedy Album (1998, 1999)
  - Originally improvised at parties, later recorded and performed

### Broadway

- **The Producers** (2001). Broadway musical adaptation. Music and lyrics by Mel Brooks, book by Brooks and Thomas Meehan.
  - Won 12 Tony Awards (record for most wins)
  - Earned Brooks the "T" in his EGOT

### Books and Memoirs

- **Brooks, Mel.** *All About Me!: My Remarkable Life in Show Business*. Ballantine Books, 2021. ISBN: 978-0593135327.
  - Autobiography covering entire career
  - Source for personal anecdotes, creative philosophy, collaboration details

## On Hitler and Ridicule

### Brooks Quotes on the Subject

**From PBS American Masters documentary (2013):**
"Rhetoric does not get you anywhere, because Hitler and Mussolini are just as good at rhetoric. But if you can bring these people down with comedy, they're not going to get up again."

**From *The Guardian* interview (2012):**
"I was never crazy about Hitler. If you stand on a soapbox and trade rhetoric with a dictator you never win... But what we can do is subject him to the ridicule he deserves."

**From *All About Me!* (2021):**
"By using the medium of comedy, we could make Hitler as ridiculous as he was... You can't get on a soapbox with these people. You have to laugh at them. It's the only defense."

### Critical Analysis

- **Epstein, Lawrence J.** *The Haunted Smile: The Story of Jewish Comedians in America*. PublicAffairs, 2001. ISBN: 978-1891620133.
  - Chapter on Brooks discusses Jewish comedy tradition of mocking oppressors
  - Source for: cultural context of Hitler ridicule, immigrant comedy influence

- **Whitfield, Stephen J.** *In Search of American Jewish Culture*. University Press of New England, 1999. ISBN: 978-0874517941.
  - Analysis of Brooks' work as Jewish-American cultural response to Holocaust

## EGOT Achievement

Mel Brooks is one of only 18 people (as of 2024) to achieve EGOT status—winning Emmy, Grammy, Oscar, and Tony awards.

**Timeline:**
- 1967: First Emmy (writer)
- 1968: Oscar (Best Original Screenplay)
- 1998: First Grammy (comedy album)
- 2001: Tony (Best Musical for *The Producers* Broadway)

**Significance:**
Brooks' EGOT represents range across comedy writing (TV), film directing/writing, musical composition, and live theater.

## On Collaboration

### Gene Wilder Partnership

- **Wilder, Gene.** *Kiss Me Like a Stranger* (2005).
  - Chapter on *Young Frankenstein* creation
  - Source for: script development, "playing it straight" philosophy, Brooks-Wilder dynamic

**Key Quote from Wilder:**
"Mel and I decided that the characters must never be in on the joke. They must always play it seriously. If Igor says his name is 'Eye-gor,' you don't wink at the audience."

### Richard Pryor Collaboration

- **Pryor, Richard.** *Pryor Convictions* (1995).
  - Co-writing *Blazing Saddles*
  - Decision to use authentic racist language to expose racism
  - Studio resistance and compromises

**Key Context:**
Pryor couldn't play Sheriff Bart (the role went to Cleavon Little) due to insurance concerns about his drug use, but his writing shaped the film's racial satire.

### Carl Reiner Partnership

- **2000 Year Old Man** recordings (1960-1998)
- Straight man / comic dynamic
- Improvisation as foundation

**Reiner on Brooks (from *My Anecdotal Life*):**
"Mel could make me laugh harder than anyone. I would ask him questions, and he would become this ancient man who had seen everything and was annoyed by all of it."

## Verification Notes

### Confirmed Facts
- Born Melvin James Kaminsky, June 28, 1926, Brooklyn
- EGOT achievement: Emmy (1967), Oscar (1968), Grammy (1998), Tony (2001)
- *Blazing Saddles* was Warner Bros.' highest-grossing film of 1974
- *Young Frankenstein* used actual Kenneth Strickfaden laboratory equipment from 1931 *Frankenstein*
- George Lucas approved *Spaceballs* on condition no merchandise be produced

### Commonly Repeated but Unverified
- Exact number of times Warner Bros. tried to cut scenes from *Blazing Saddles* varies by source
- Whether Brooks actually turned down role of Sheriff Bart before it went to Cleavon Little (some sources say he never wanted the role)

### Disputed
- Whether *History of the World Part I* sequel was ever seriously planned (Brooks has given contradictory answers)
- Brooks' exact role in writing every joke in his films (collaborative authorship makes attribution difficult)

## Recommended Viewing Order for Study

1. **The Producers** (1967) - Establishes Hitler-mockery philosophy
2. **Blazing Saddles** (1974) - Peak genre parody and fourth-wall breaking
3. **Young Frankenstein** (1974) - Genre fidelity before subversion
4. **History of the World Part I** (1981) - Tyrant ridicule methodology
5. **Spaceballs** (1987) - Meta-comedy and merchandising satire

**Supporting:**
- *Your Show of Shows* clips for early writing style
- *The 2000 Year Old Man* recordings for improvisational roots
- *Mel Brooks: Make a Noise* documentary for Brooks' own voice on methodology
