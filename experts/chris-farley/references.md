# Chris Farley - References

## Primary Sources

### Film Performances (Complete Feature Filmography)

1. **Tommy Boy** (1995). Dir. Peter Segal. Paramount Pictures.
   - Role: Thomas R. "Tommy" Callahan III
   - Co-star: David Spade
   - Budget: $32 million; Box office: $109.5 million
   - Source for: "Fat Guy in a Little Coat," deer scene, sales pitch failures, physical comedy methodology, Farley-Spade chemistry

2. **Black Sheep** (1996). Dir. Penelope Spheeris. Paramount Pictures.
   - Role: Mike Donnelly
   - Co-star: David Spade
   - Box office: $32.4 million
   - Source for: Farley-Spade formula continuation, physical comedy techniques

3. **Beverly Hills Ninja** (1997). Dir. Dennis Dugan. TriStar Pictures.
   - Role: Haru
   - Released January 1997 (Farley died December 1997)
   - Box office: $31.5 million
   - Source for: Physical comedy, earnest incompetent character type

4. **Dirty Work** (1998). Dir. Bob Saget. MGM.
   - Supporting role (starred Norm Macdonald)
   - Released June 1998 (posthumously)
   - Source for: Final film appearance

5. **Almost Heroes** (1998). Dir. Christopher Guest. Warner Bros.
   - Role: Bartholomew Hunt
   - Co-star: Matthew Perry
   - Released May 1998 (posthumously)
   - Source for: Last starring role, period comedy attempt

6. **Wayne's World** (1992). Dir. Penelope Spheeris. Paramount Pictures.
   - Small role: Security guard
   - Source for: Early film work, pre-breakout

7. **Coneheads** (1993). Dir. Steve Barron. Paramount Pictures.
   - Role: Ronnie the Mechanic
   - Source for: SNL crossover film, ensemble work

8. **Airheads** (1994). Dir. Michael Lehmann. 20th Century Fox.
   - Role: Officer Wilson
   - Small role
   - Source for: Supporting character work

### Unfinished/Uncompleted Projects

**Shrek** (2001, DreamWorks Animation):
- Farley recorded ~80-90% of voice work for title character
- Died before completion; role recast with Mike Myers
- Some Farley recordings exist in DreamWorks archives
- Released on Shrek Ultimate Edition DVD as bonus feature
- Source for: Voice acting range, what might have been

**Fatty Arbuckle Biopic**:
- Passion project about silent film comedian destroyed by scandal
- Never entered production after Farley's death
- Source for: Farley's self-awareness about parallels to tragic figures

## Secondary Sources

### Biographies

1. **Farley, Tom Jr. and Tanner Colby.** *The Chris Farley Show: A Biography in Three Acts*. Viking, 2008. ISBN: 978-0670019861.
   - Written by Chris Farley's brother (Tom Jr.)
   - Oral history format with 80+ interviews
   - Interviews: Bob Odenkirk, David Spade, Lorne Michaels, Adam Sandler, Dan Aykroyd, Conan O'Brien, Bob Saget, many others
   - Source for: Most comprehensive, family-approved biography; addiction details; career arc; personal relationships
   - Most authoritative source on Farley's life

2. **Ewing, Tom.** *The Second City: Backstage at the World's Greatest Comedy Theater*. Sourcebooks, 2004. ISBN: 978-1402201424.
   - History of Second City with Farley chapter
   - Source for: Training methodology, Chicago improv scene, Del Close influence

### Documentaries

1. **I Am Chris Farley** (2015). Dir. Brent Hodge and Derik Murray. Virgil Films.
   - Comprehensive career documentary
   - Interviews: Dan Aykroyd, David Spade, Adam Sandler, Lorne Michaels, Christina Applegate, Bo Derek, Mike Myers, Molly Shannon, Bob Saget, Tom Arnold, Rob Lowe, Jon Lovitz, Pat Finn
   - Extensive SNL and film footage
   - Source for: Career overview, behind-scenes SNL, archival performances, collaborator perspectives
   - Most thorough visual documentation

2. **Biography: Chris Farley** (1998). A&E Biography.
   - Aired shortly after death
   - Source for: Immediate aftermath perspective, early career coverage

3. **Saturday Night Live: The Best of Chris Farley** (1998). NBC/Broadway Video.
   - Compilation of SNL performances
   - Source for: Matt Foley sketches, Chippendales, Gap Girls, Lunch Lady, other characters

## Notes on Verification

### Confirmed Facts

- **Birth/Death**: February 15, 1964 – December 18, 1997. Death certificate on file.
- **SNL tenure**: 5 seasons (1990-1995), cast member
- **Film releases**: All films documented and released
- **Cause of death**: Morphine and cocaine (speedball); medical examiner report public record
- **Shrek recording**: Confirmed by DreamWorks; partial recordings exist
- **Rehab attempts**: 17 documented by family in biography

### Documented Performance Details

- **Coffee table crash** (Matt Foley): Happened first in dress rehearsal (unscripted), kept for live show; became recurring bit
- **Chippendales choreography**: Matched Swayze move-for-move; rehearsed with choreographer
- **"Fat Guy in a Little Coat"**: Improvised on set; David Spade's actual jacket
- **Physical training**: Stunt coordinators worked with Farley on falls, break-falls

### Disputed or Uncertain

- **Number of rehab stays**: Sources vary between 12-17; "17" is family's number in official biography
- **Interventions**: Various accounts of who participated and when; timeline fuzzy
- **Shrek completion percentage**: Reports vary from "80%" to "nearly complete"; exact percentage uncertain
- **Last hours**: Heidi Hagemeier's account is primary source, no corroborating witness

### Sources Requiring Caution

- **Tabloid coverage** (1997-1998): Death coverage included speculation; verify against medical examiner report
- **Enabler accounts**: Friends who participated in destructive behavior may minimize their role
- **Belushi parallel**: Sometimes overstated; similarities real but each story has unique elements

### Unverifiable Elements

- **Private struggles**: Much of Farley's internal experience (depression, body dysmorphia) inferred from behavior and secondhand accounts
- **What he would have done**: All Shrek speculation is counterfactual; Mike Myers reimagined role completely
- **Intention behind choices**: We have effects but can only infer intent from collaborator testimony

## Research Notes

This reference list prioritizes:
1. **Family-approved sources**: Tom Farley Jr.'s biography is definitive for facts
2. **Primary documents**: Medical examiner report, police investigation over speculation
3. **Collaborator testimony**: People who worked with Farley directly
4. **Contemporary reviews**: Real-time reception over retrospective reassessment
5. **Multiple corroboration**: Controversial claims verified across sources

The Farley story parallels John Belushi's tragically. This reference list attempts to:
- Separate documented facts from legend
- Honor Farley's artistry while acknowledging addiction's role in his death
- Provide context for both the comedy and the tragedy
- Recognize that celebrating the work requires acknowledging the cost

**Key Challenge**: Farley was deeply insecure and performed confidence. Distinguishing "real" Farley from "performed" Farley is difficult because the performance was often the most authentic expression of his real feelings. The vulnerability was real; the neediness was real; the comedy came from his willingness to expose that reality completely.

## Critical Reception Evolution

**1990-1995 (SNL Years)**:
- Critics: Mixed to positive; some dismissed as "fat guy falls down"
- Fans: Immediate embrace; Matt Foley iconic quickly

**1995-1997 (Film Years)**:
- *Tommy Boy*: Critics lukewarm, audiences loved (cult classic status)
- *Black Sheep*: Critics panned as repetitive, box office disappointing
- *Beverly Hills Ninja*: Critics harsh, modest box office

**1998-2008 (Immediate Posthumous)**:
- Nostalgia surge; retrospectives emphasized tragedy
- *I Am Chris Farley* documentary began critical reassessment

**2008-Present (Biography and Beyond)**:
- *The Chris Farley Show* book reframed legacy with depth
- Critical reappraisal: Athletic physicality recognized as skill, not gimmick
- Influence on later physical comedians (Melissa McCarthy, Kevin James) acknowledged
- Ongoing debate about body-shaming in his work vs. celebration of athleticism
