## Linguistic Philosophy: The "Soft Language" Routine

The euphemism routine appeared in **Doin' It Again** (1990), recorded January 12-13, 1990, at the State Theatre, New Brunswick, New Jersey.

### Key Points of the Routine

| Era | Term | Syllables | Carlin's Analysis |
|-----|------|-----------|-------------------|
| World War I | Shell Shock | 2 | "Simple, honest, direct language... almost sounds like the guns themselves" |
| World War II | Battle Fatigue | 4 | "Takes a little longer to say, doesn't seem to hurt as much" |
| Korean War | Operational Exhaustion | 8 | "Sounds like something that might happen to your car" |
| Vietnam War | Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder | 8 | "The pain is completely buried under jargon" |

**Source:** [Scraps from the Loft](https://scrapsfromtheloft.com/comedy/george-carlin-doin-it-again-transcript/), [IMDB Quotes](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0246642/quotes/)

### Core Philosophy on Language

Carlin's thesis: "American English is loaded with euphemisms, because Americans have a lot of trouble dealing with reality. Americans have trouble facing the truth, so they invent the kind of a soft language to protect themselves from it."

**Other Euphemism Targets:**
- "Poor" becoming "economically disadvantaged"
- "Slums" becoming "substandard housing" then "inner city"
- "Used cars" becoming "pre-owned vehicles"
- "Toilet paper" becoming "bathroom tissue"

## Political and Philosophical Evolution

### Early Career (1960s)
- Conventional, suited performer
- Character comedy and impressions
- TV-friendly material

### Counterculture Period (1970s)
- Influenced by Lenny Bruce and anti-war movement
- Drug and Vietnam references
- Language liberation focus

### Mature Period (1980s-1990s)
- Sociocultural criticism
- Religion and patriotism critiques
- Reagan-era political commentary

### Later Period (2000s)
- Increasingly dark and misanthropic
- Philosophical observations on humanity
- "Disappointed idealist" self-identification

### Key Philosophy Quote

"Inside every cynical person, there is a disappointed idealist."

**Source:** Multiple interviews; [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/425528-inside-every-cynical-person-there-is-a-disappointed-idealist)

From 2001 interview with Marc Cooper, *The Progressive*: "The saying goes, if you scratch a cynic, you find a disappointed idealist, and that's what's going on with me. Down deep and underneath, the flame still flickers. I wish for an idealistic, utopic world but the realist in me says it's never gonna happen."

## Notes

### On the Birth Year Discrepancy

Some sources list Carlin's birth year as 1938. The correct year is **1937** (May 12, 1937), as confirmed by his official website, Wikipedia, Kennedy Center, and other authoritative sources.

### On the Mark Twain Prize Timing

The irony of Carlin's posthumous Mark Twain Prize is notable: he died five days after the announcement, having considered it the only legitimate comedy award. At the ceremony, his "Seven Dirty Words" routine was bleeped, prompting Lewis Black to comment on the absurdity of censoring Carlin at his own tribute.

### On Influences

While often compared to Lenny Bruce due to their shared use of profanity, Carlin himself noted the comparison was superficial. His actual influence from Bruce was philosophical: "the approach to comedy" and "taking those positions" as an anti-authoritarian. Carlin succeeded where Bruce suffered; he was "the first one to make language an issue and to succeed from it."
