## Notes on Verification

### Confirmed Facts

- **Birth date and background:** March 22, 1971, Southfield, Michigan. Biracial (Black mother, white father), adopted by white parents at birth. Confirmed through multiple biographical sources and Key's own statements.

- **Education:**
  - BFA from University of Detroit Mercy (1993) - confirmed through university records
  - MFA from Penn State (1996) - confirmed through university records
  - Detroit Repertory Theatre involvement - confirmed through theater archives

- **Key & Peele run:** 5 seasons, 53 episodes (2012-2015) on Comedy Central. Emmy wins 2014 and 2015. Peabody Award 2013. All verified through award archives and Comedy Central records.

- **White House Correspondents' Dinner appearance:** April 25, 2015. Performed Luther sketch with President Obama. C-SPAN video documentation exists.

- **MADtv tenure:** 2004-2009, 5 seasons as cast member. Confirmed through Fox records and MADtv episode archives.

### Commonly Cited But Verify With Caution

- **"Jordan builds the world, I burn it down":** This exact phrasing appears in Key's Marc Maron interview (2015) and has been repeated in multiple sources. Direct quote verified.

- **Luther as "Obama's id":** Key has described Luther this way in multiple interviews (NPR Fresh Air, Inside the Actors Studio). Exact phrasing varies but concept consistent.

- **Reason for ending show:** Both Key and Peele have stated they wanted to end while successful and pursue other projects (Peele directing, Key doing theater). Verified through multiple interviews (Rolling Stone, Hollywood Reporter).

### Disputed or Unclear

- **Exact number of characters played:** Various sources claim Key played "hundreds" or "over 100" characters across Key & Peele. Exact count not definitively documented.

- **Detroit Rep Theatre specific years:** Key's involvement documented through theater's records and his statements, but exact dates of involvement (early 1990s) are approximate.

- **Substitute teacher sketch origin:** Multiple versions of how the sketch originated exist. Key has told slightly different versions in different interviews. Core concept (inner-city teacher in suburban school) consistent.

### Cultural Impact Claims

- **"Most viral sketch":** Substitute teacher frequently cited as "most viral" Key & Peele sketch. YouTube views verify high engagement (100+ million as of 2025) but "most viral" depends on platform and metric.

- **"Smartest show on TV":** Quote from Variety headline. Subjective claim, though critical consensus supports high regard for the show's cultural commentary.

- **Influence on political discourse:** Luther sketch credited with influencing how political satire handles race and presidential emotion. Difficult to quantify but widely cited by comedy scholars.

## Methodology Sources

### Character Voice Creation

Key's six-step character voice method documented through:
- Inside the Actors Studio appearance (2016): Detailed breakdown of character development
- Key & Peele DVD commentary: Specific examples applied to sketches
- The Atlantic interview (2015): Discussion of process
- Personal teaching at Second City (documented in student accounts, not primary source)

**Verification:** The six-step framework (psychology, tension, rhythm, pitch, physicality, register) is synthesized from multiple sources. Key doesn't use these exact labels in all interviews, but the concepts appear consistently.

### Escalation Technique

Sketch escalation architecture documented through:
- Key & Peele DVD audio commentaries: Creators discuss specific escalation choices
- The Atlantic article (2015): Kornhaber's analysis with Key's input
- Variety profile (2015): Discussion of sketch structure
- Key's book *The History of Sketch Comedy* (2018): Brief discussion of escalation principle

**Verification:** The six-floor model (reality → small deviation → noticeable absurdity → full absurdity → peak → resolution) is an analytical framework based on Key's discussions, not his exact terminology.

### Code-Switching Framework

Code-switching discussion documented through:
- NPR Fresh Air interview: Extended discussion of navigating cultural registers
- Marc Maron WTF Podcast: Discussion of biracial identity and linguistic adaptation
- Various academic analyses of Key & Peele sketches (Haggins 2019, others)

**Verification:** Key speaks extensively about code-switching but doesn't provide a formal "framework." The methodology described in expertise.md synthesizes his statements about his own practice.

### Partnership Dynamics

Partnership philosophy documented through:
- Hollywood Reporter roundtable (2015): Both Key and Peele discuss collaboration
- Rolling Stone exit interview (2015): Discussion of why they ended show
- GQ interview with Key (2014): His perspective on partnership
- Multiple DVD commentaries: Real-time demonstration of collaborative process

**Verification:** The "complementary collaboration model" terminology is analytical framework, but the principles (ego surrender, clear roles, veto power, etc.) come directly from Key's statements.

## Unverifiable by Design

### Creative Process Details

Many aspects of Key & Peele's creative process are described generally (writers' room, rehearsal, refinement) but specific details vary between accounts. The collaborative nature means individual contributions often blur.

### Character Psychology

When Key discusses character psychology (Mr. Garvey's displacement, Luther's compressed anger), these are his interpretations of his own creative choices. They're "true" in that they represent his artistic intention, but they're not externally verifiable facts.

### Partnership Chemistry

The "Jordan builds, Keegan burns down" dynamic is how both describe their collaboration, but creative partnerships are complex and this framing is simplified for explanation. Both contribute to both aspects in practice.
