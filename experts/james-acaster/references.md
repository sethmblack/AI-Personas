# James Acaster - References

## Primary Sources

### Stand-Up Specials and Recordings

**Repertoire (2018) - Netflix**
Four-part special representing peak of Acaster's architectural comedy:
- **Part 1: "Recognize"** - Traditional storytelling, anti-callback structure
- **Part 2: "Represent"** - Callback architecture as theme
- **Part 3: "Recap"** - Meta-commentary, fourth wall breaking
- **Part 4: "Reset"** - Unified payoff, emotional revelation
- Available: Netflix streaming
- Runtime: 4 hours total (approximately 1 hour each)

**"Recognise" (2015) - Live Recording**
- Audio recording of Edinburgh show
- Released as album
- Source for: Early versions of "Repertoire" material

**"Represent" (2014) - Live Recording**
- Audio recording of Edinburgh show
- Released as album
- Source for: Development of callback methodology

### Edinburgh Festival Fringe Shows

**The Five-Year Nomination Streak (2012-2016):**
- **"Prompt"** (2012) - First Edinburgh Comedy Award nomination
- **"Recognise"** (2013) - Second consecutive nomination
- **"Represent"** (2014) - Third consecutive nomination
- **"Recognize"** (2015) - Fourth consecutive nomination; show later became "Repertoire" Special 1
- **"Reset"** (2016) - Fifth consecutive nomination; unprecedented in award history

**Earlier Shows:**
- **"Amongst Other Things"** (2010) - Debut Edinburgh hour
- **"Lawnmower"** (2011) - Second Edinburgh show

**Note:** Live show recordings not widely available; primary documentation through reviews, press coverage, and later adaptations (especially "Repertoire").

## Researcher Notes

**Primary vs. Secondary:**
Acaster's primary sources (specials, podcasts, books) are unusually accessible and substantial. "Repertoire" in particular provides complete documentation of his peak structural methodology.

**Best Sources for Specific Aspects:**
- **Callback architecture:** "Repertoire" (especially "Represent")
- **Vulnerability methodology:** "Reset" (Special 4) and "Perfect Sound Whatever" book
- **Musical background:** WTF podcast, various interviews
- **Edinburgh development:** Chortle reviews across 2012-2016
- **Conversational technique:** "Off Menu" podcast episodes

**Gaps in Documentation:**
Pre-2015 material is less accessible. Early Edinburgh shows lack recorded versions. Musical career has limited archival presence. This creates reliance on later interviews and retrospective analysis.

**Reliability Spectrum:**
- **High reliability:** Published books, released specials, official podcast episodes
- **Moderate reliability:** Interview statements (subject to memory, framing)
- **Lower reliability:** Third-party interpretations of technique without direct source citation

**Research Recommendation:**
Begin with "Repertoire" for methodology, supplement with "Off Menu" for personality and conversational style, use book sources for written voice and vulnerability approach.
