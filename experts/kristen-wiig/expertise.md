## Core Contributions

### Character Comedy Through Total Commitment

Wiig revolutionized sketch comedy by refusing to wink at the audience. Her characters aren't "funny people"—they're fully realized human beings whose behavior happens to be absurd. She plays them with absolute sincerity, letting the audience laugh at the situation, never at her performance.

Key innovations:
- Eccentric characters played with dramatic intensity
- Physical transformation as character psychology
- Awkward silence as comedic tool
- Cringe comedy rooted in authentic human behavior
- Seamless transition between comedy and drama

### The Wiig Methodology

| Principle | Practice |
|-----------|----------|
| **Commitment** | No ironic distance; play every moment as real |
| **Physicality** | Body language reveals character psychology |
| **Specificity** | Tiny behavioral details create whole personas |
| **Silence** | Let awkward pauses breathe and expand |
| **Dramedy** | Treat comedy seriously, find humor in drama |

## Famous Works & Methodologies

### Saturday Night Live (2005-2012)

Wiig was an SNL cast member for seven seasons, becoming one of the show's most versatile and celebrated performers. She created dozens of recurring characters and demonstrated range from broad physical comedy to subtle character work.

#### Character Creation Philosophy

Wiig's approach to character creation:
- Start with a specific behavior or vocal quality
- Build outward from one odd detail
- Never comment on the character's weirdness
- Play them as if they're the normal one
- Let the character's obliviousness create the comedy

#### Recurring Characters - Complete Breakdown

**Penelope (The One-Upper)**
- Obsessively one-ups every story with increasingly absurd claims
- Vocal pattern: "I... I do that" followed by elaborate lie
- Physical tells: Nervous hand gestures, shifty eyes
- Psychology: Deep insecurity masked by compulsive lying
- **Method**: Play desperate need for validation completely straight; the comedy is in the transparency of the lies

**Target Lady (Sue)**
- Overly enthusiastic Target employee with boundary issues
- Vocal pattern: High-pitched baby voice, excessive enthusiasm
- Physical signature: Crinkled nose, hands pressed together
- Psychology: Lonely person who uses cheerfulness as social connection
- Catchphrase: "Sooooo GOOOD to seeeee youuuu!"
- **Method**: Genuine joy and loneliness simultaneously; she really IS that happy to see you because she has no one else

**Gilly**
- Disruptive schoolgirl who creates chaos then denies responsibility
- Catchphrase: "Sorry" (sing-song, clearly not sorry)
- Physical signature: Mischievous smile, hands clasped innocently
- Psychology: Anarchist child who understands and exploits social rules
- **Method**: Play pure id without conscience; she knows exactly what she's doing and delights in it

**Dooneese Maharelle**
- Member of "The Lawrence Welk Show" with proportionally tiny hands and slurred speech
- Physical transformation: Prosthetic tiny hands, exaggerated forehead
- Vocal pattern: Slurred, childlike singing voice
- Psychology: Oblivious to her difference, sees herself as normal performer
- **Method**: Complete lack of self-awareness; she believes she belongs and performs with full sincerity

**Judy Grimes**
- Nervous woman who responds to basic questions with defensive, elaborate explanations
- Catchphrase: "Just kidding" (anxious, defensive)
- Physical pattern: Twitchy, over-apologetic body language
- Psychology: Profound social anxiety masked by forced casualness
- **Method**: Play every interaction as potential criticism; she's defending against attacks that don't exist

**Shana**
- Overenthusiastic, boundary-crossing friend who ruins social situations
- Vocal pattern: Loud, braying laugh; interrupts constantly
- Physical signature: Invading personal space, inappropriate touching
- Psychology: Desperate for inclusion, completely unaware of social cues
- **Method**: Total obliviousness to discomfort; she genuinely thinks she's enhancing the experience

**Mindy Grayson (Secret Word)**
- Game show contestant who can't say obvious words, speaks in elaborate circumlocutions
- Format: Describes simple concepts using absurd phrases
- Psychology: Smart person who has inexplicable blind spots
- **Method**: Complete confidence in nonsensical explanations; she believes her descriptions are helpful

**Kat (Surprise Party)**
- Baby-voiced woman who stages elaborate surprise parties
- Physical signature: Childlike gestures, excessive excitement
- Vocal pattern: High-pitched whisper, patronizing tone
- Psychology: Controlling personality masked as whimsy
- **Method**: Play the need to orchestrate as genuine service; she thinks she's being generous

**Aunt Linda (Travel Tips)**
- Unhelpful travel expert with terrible, off-topic advice
- Format: Weekend Update segment giving bizarre recommendations
- Psychology: Confident incompetence; genuinely believes she's helping
- **Method**: Deliver useless information with expert authority; complete sincerity in terrible suggestions

#### Character Work Principles

**Physical Transformation:**
- Wiig uses entire body to create character
- Posture shifts: slouched (insecurity), rigid (control), bouncy (mania)
- Facial expressions: micro-expressions reveal psychology
- Hand gestures: each character has signature movements
- Voice placement: pitch, rhythm, and breathiness vary by psychology

**Behavioral Specificity:**
- Every character has unique laughs, pauses, and tics
- Small details (how they hold a coffee cup, adjust their hair) create reality
- Consistency: once a behavior is established, it repeats predictably
- These details accumulate into full personas

**Dramatic Commitment:**
- Never break character, even when sketch partners corpse (laugh)
- Play emotional reality: if character would be hurt, show real hurt
- No mugging for camera; comedy comes from authenticity
- Trust the character; don't "help" the joke with performance choices

#### Notable Live Performance Moments

**Garth and Kat Improvisations:**
- Paired with Fred Armisen as musical duo who improvise terrible songs
- Format: Weekend Update appearances where they forget lyrics and make up songs
- Method: Real improvisation within character; they actually don't know what they'll sing
- Demonstrates Wiig's comfort with live chaos and genuine spontaneity

**Emotional Final Goodbye (May 19, 2012):**
- Final episode featured elaborate goodbye featuring entire cast and musical performances
- Wiig played it emotionally straight rather than for comedy
- Mick Jagger serenaded her offstage in "She's a Rainbow"
- Demonstrates her ability to shift from comedy to genuine emotion seamlessly

**Method**: SNL taught Wiig to commit fully, work without safety net, create instantly, and trust her instincts

### Bridesmaids (2011) - Writing and Performance

Wiig co-wrote *Bridesmaids* with Annie Mumolo, creating a film that revolutionized female-led comedy by treating women's lives with the same complexity given to male characters in similar films.

#### Writing Philosophy

**Emotional Truth in Comedy:**
- Start with real emotional conflicts (jealousy, insecurity, class anxiety)
- Build comedy from character reactions, not from "jokes"
- Let characters be flawed, selfish, and wrong
- Don't explain or apologize for female complexity

**Structure of Bridesmaids:**
- Annie Walker is failing: business collapsed, bad relationship, losing best friend
- Comedy comes from her sabotaging herself, not from external obstacles
- The bridal shower and wedding aren't the problem; Annie's refusal to accept change is
- Friendship breakup treated with same weight as romantic breakup

#### Annie Walker Character Construction

**Psychologically Specific:**
- Depressed but won't admit it
- Self-sabotages because failure confirms her worldview
- Uses humor as deflection and control
- Jealous of Helen but can't acknowledge it directly
- Wants to be generous but is consumed by her own pain

**Physical Performance:**
- Slumped posture when alone vs. forced energy in social situations
- Fake smiles that don't reach eyes
- Physical stillness in moments of emotional shutdown
- Uses body language to show internal collapse while maintaining external composure

**Awkward Comedy Techniques:**
- The airplane scene: Annie's fear of flying spirals into paranoid rambling
- Playing drunk: slurred speech, loose physicality, emotional vulnerability
- Tennis scene: trying too hard, overcompensating with enthusiasm
- Bridal shower meltdown: slow build from passive-aggression to open breakdown

**Method**: Annie Walker demonstrates Wiig's ability to play deeply flawed characters with empathy, making audiences root for someone actively making bad choices

#### Key Scenes - Performance Breakdown

**The Airplane Scene:**
- Annie takes sedatives and drinks, becomes paranoid and inappropriate
- Wiig plays genuine fear underneath the chemical impairment
- Physical comedy: stumbling, invading space, slurring
- Emotional reality: terror of not being in control, of inadequacy
- Technique: Layer physical comedy over real emotional stakes

**The Bridal Shower Meltdown:**
- Annie destroys the Parisian-themed shower in passive-aggressive rage
- Starts subtle (small digs) and escalates to destructive (destroying decorations)
- Wiig plays it as emotional breakdown, not comic set piece
- We watch her lose control in real time
- Technique: Build slowly; let audience feel discomfort before release

**The Engagement Party Speech:**
- Annie gives toast that becomes veiled attack on Helen
- Surface: complimenting Lillian. Subtext: processing loss and jealousy
- Wiig plays double-level performance: what Annie intends vs. what comes out
- Technique: Play intention (be generous) while action reveals opposite

**The Fight with Rhodes (Chris O'Dowd):**
- Annie pushes away the good relationship she doesn't think she deserves
- Self-sabotage played as self-protection
- Wiig shows Annie convincing herself she's making the right choice
- Technique: Play the lie the character tells themselves

#### Co-Writing Process with Annie Mumolo

**How They Work:**
- Improvise scenes in character
- Record everything, transcribe later
- Find truth first, structure second
- Willing to cut jokes that don't serve character
- Write for actors they know (Melissa McCarthy as Megan written specifically for her gifts)

**Mumolo's Contribution:**
- Scene partner in development phase
- Plays all other characters while improvising with Wiig
- Helps find emotional truth before comedy
- Co-created structure and character arcs

**Script Development:**
- Judd Apatow produced; encouraged longer scenes and improvisation
- Paul Feig directed; trusted actors to find moments
- Final script balanced structure with room for discovery on set
- Many scenes shot with alternative improvised takes

**Themes They Explored:**
- Female friendship as primary relationship
- Jealousy between women without vilification
- Class anxiety and economic insecurity
- Depression and self-sabotage
- Growing up vs. growing apart

**Method**: Treat comedy writing as character study; find universal emotions in specific situations; trust truth over formula

#### Impact and Legacy

*Bridesmaids* grossed over $288 million worldwide and received two Oscar nominations (Best Original Screenplay and Best Supporting Actress for Melissa McCarthy). It proved female-led comedies could succeed commercially while maintaining complexity and edge.

**What Made It Groundbreaking:**
- Women shown as flawed, selfish, sexual, gross, and human
- Comedy came from character, not from "women doing things"
- Economic anxiety (Annie's bakery failure) treated seriously
- Female friendship given weight typically reserved for romance
- Didn't apologize for showing women's full humanity

**Method**: Write women as people, not as "women in comedy"; trust audience to handle complexity; root all comedy in specific character psychology

### Dramatic Range - Film and Television

Wiig's post-SNL career demonstrates intentional range expansion, choosing roles that challenge the "funny person" expectation.

#### Dramatic Roles

**The Skeleton Twins (2014):**
- Plays Maggie, suicidal woman reconnecting with twin brother (Bill Hader)
- Depressed, self-destructive, having affair while married
- Uses Wiig's ability to play discomfort and internal conflict
- Balances dark material with moments of connection
- Technique: Underplay rather than overplay; let silences speak

**Welcome to Me (2014):**
- Plays Alice Klieg, woman with borderline personality disorder who hosts own talk show
- Based on real psychological condition; researched extensively
- Plays character's delusions and grandiosity without condescension
- Finds humanity in behavior others might play as "crazy"
- Technique: Play character's logic, not audience's judgment of it

**Downsizing (2017):**
- Supporting role as ex-wife in Alexander Payne film
- Grounded, realistic performance in high-concept sci-fi premise
- Demonstrates ability to serve story rather than command it

**Where'd You Go, Bernadette (2019):**
- Plays anxious, agoraphobic architect opposite Cate Blanchett
- Supporting role played with empathy and specificity
- Shows range in ensemble work

#### Dramatic Technique

**Stillness:**
- In drama, Wiig uses physical stillness where comedy uses movement
- Allows audience to watch thought processes
- Lets emotion build without telegraphing it

**Understatement:**
- Resists impulse to "show" emotion
- Trusts audience to read subtext
- Uses micro-expressions rather than big choices

**Vulnerability:**
- Willing to be unlikeable, passive, or shut down
- Doesn't apologize for character's behavior
- Commits to emotional truth even when uncomfortable

**Method**: Apply comedy training (commitment, specificity, silence) to dramatic material; trust audience to do the work; play internal states externally minimally

### Physical Comedy and Transformation

Wiig's physical comedy is rooted in observation and psychology, not in slapstick or exaggeration.

#### Body as Character Tool

**Posture:**
- Slumped shoulders = depression, defeat (Annie in *Bridesmaids*)
- Rigid spine = control, repression (uptight characters)
- Bouncy, loose = mania or childlike energy (Target Lady)
- Each character has distinct default posture

**Facial Expressions:**
- Target Lady: crinkled nose, wide eyes, pursed lips
- Penelope: shifty eyes, forced smile
- Gilly: mischievous grin, faux innocence
- Micro-expressions telegraph character's inner life

**Gestural Vocabulary:**
- Each character has signature hand movements
- Repetition creates recognition and rhythm
- Gestures reveal psychology (nervous, controlling, performative)

**Vocal Physicality:**
- Voice placement changes: nasal, breathy, throaty
- Rhythm and pace vary by character psychology
- Uses breath patterns to show anxiety, excitement, exhaustion

#### Physical Transformation Examples

**Dooneese (Tiny Hands):**
- Prosthetic hands change entire physical vocabulary
- Compensates with exaggerated head movements
- Creates uncanny valley effect
- Method: Let physical constraint dictate behavior

**Target Lady:**
- Baby-like physical mannerisms
- High shoulder position, tucked chin
- Hands clasped or waving like child
- Method: Adult woman performing childlike enthusiasm; body reveals psychology

**Awkward Movement:**
- Uses physical awkwardness to show social discomfort
- Stumbling, overstepping, invading space
- Body doing what mind wants to avoid
- Method: Physicality betrays intention

#### Silence and Stillness

**Comedy in Pauses:**
- Wiig masters the uncomfortable pause
- Lets silence expand until audience squirms
- Uses stillness to heighten awkwardness
- Refuses to fill space, forcing others to react

**Physical Stillness:**
- In dramatic roles, uses stillness to convey internal struggle
- In comedy, stillness before explosion creates tension
- Contrast between stillness and sudden movement surprises

**Method**: Body is character psychology made visible; every physical choice should reveal internal state; silence is active, not passive

## Signature Approaches

### The Oblivious Protagonist
Create characters who are unaware of their own weirdness. They see themselves as normal; the audience sees the gap. Play the character's self-perception, not the reality.

### The Behavioral Detail
Start with one odd, specific behavior. Build entire character from that detail. The behavior should reveal psychology, not just be "quirky."

### The Committed Awkwardness
Play socially uncomfortable situations with complete sincerity. Don't signal that you know it's awkward. Let the situation breathe. The more uncomfortable, the longer you hold it.

### The Physical Tell
Give each character a distinctive physical signature—a gesture, posture, or facial expression that instantly communicates who they are.

### The Dramatic Truth
Treat comedy with dramatic seriousness. Play real emotions, real stakes, real consequences. The comedy emerges from truth, not from "being funny."

## Working Method

**Character Development:**
1. Find one specific, odd detail
2. Build psychology around that detail
3. Develop physical vocabulary
4. Practice commitment—no winking
5. Trust the specificity

**On-Set Approach:**
- Arrive with character fully built
- Stay open to improvisation within character
- Trust scene partners
- Play truth, not jokes
- Commit to takes fully, even if it feels too far

**Writing Process:**
- Improvise in character
- Record and transcribe
- Find emotional truth first
- Structure emerges from character
- Cut jokes that don't serve truth

**Method**: Specificity creates reality; commitment creates belief; silence creates space; vulnerability creates connection
