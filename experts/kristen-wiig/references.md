# Kristen Wiig - References

## Primary Sources

### Television Appearances (Archival)

- **Saturday Night Live** (2005-2012). NBC. Cast member for seven seasons, 138 episodes.
  - Final episode: May 19, 2012. Host: Mick Jagger as musical guest.
  - Created recurring characters: Target Lady, Penelope, Gilly, Dooneese, Judy Grimes, Shana, Kat, Aunt Linda
  - Garth and Kat segments with Fred Armisen (improvised musical performances on Weekend Update)

- **The Spoils of Babylon** (2014). IFC. Miniseries.
- **The Spoils Before Dying** (2015). IFC. Follow-up miniseries.
- **Saturday Night Live** hosting appearances (post-cast): December 19, 2015; May 11, 2019; April 11, 2020; December 19, 2020

### Film Work (Major Roles)

#### Comedy/Lead Roles
- **Bridesmaids** (2011). Dir. Paul Feig. Universal Pictures.
  - Co-wrote screenplay with Annie Mumolo
  - Played Annie Walker
  - Grossed $288.4 million worldwide
  - Academy Award nominations: Best Original Screenplay (Wiig & Mumolo), Best Supporting Actress (Melissa McCarthy)

- **Ghostbusters** (2016). Dir. Paul Feig. Columbia Pictures.
  - Played Erin Gilbert
  - All-female reboot/reimagining

- **Barb and Star Go to Vista Del Mar** (2021). Dir. Josh Greenbaum. Lionsgate.
  - Co-wrote screenplay with Annie Mumolo
  - Played Star
  - Reunited writing partnership with Mumolo

#### Dramatic Roles
- **The Skeleton Twins** (2014). Dir. Craig Johnson. Roadside Attractions.
  - Played Maggie alongside Bill Hader (twin brother)
  - Dealt with depression and suicidal ideation
  - Critical acclaim for dramatic performance

- **Welcome to Me** (2014). Dir. Shira Piven. Alchemy.
  - Played Alice Klieg, woman with borderline personality disorder
  - Independent film; premiered at Toronto International Film Festival

- **The Diary of a Teenage Girl** (2015). Dir. Marielle Heller. Sony Pictures Classics.
  - Supporting role as mother

- **Downsizing** (2017). Dir. Alexander Payne. Paramount Pictures.
  - Supporting role in ensemble cast

- **Where'd You Go, Bernadette** (2019). Dir. Richard Linklater. Annapurna Pictures.
  - Supporting role alongside Cate Blanchett

- **Wonder Woman 1984** (2020). Dir. Patty Jenkins. Warner Bros.
  - Played Barbara Minerva/Cheetah

#### Voice Work/Animation
- **Despicable Me** franchise (2010, 2013, 2017). Universal Pictures/Illumination.
  - Voiced Lucy Wilde (introduced in Despicable Me 2)

- **How to Train Your Dragon 2** (2014). DreamWorks Animation.
  - Voiced Ruffnut

- **Sausage Party** (2016). Columbia Pictures.
  - Voiced Brenda

### Audio/Video Releases
- *Saturday Night Live: The Best of Kristen Wiig* (2010). DVD/streaming compilation.
- Various SNL sketches available on YouTube (NBC/SNL official channels)
- Bridesmaids DVD/Blu-ray with deleted scenes and commentary
- Welcome to Me and The Skeleton Twins available on major streaming platforms

## Research Notes

### Character Development Process
Wiig's character creation process is documented across multiple sources:
1. Starts with single specific behavior (confirmed: multiple interviews)
2. Builds psychology from that behavior (confirmed: Apatow interview, EW interview)
3. Develops physical vocabulary (confirmed: video evidence across SNL sketches)
4. Commits without ironic distance (confirmed: performance analysis, critical reviews)

### Co-Writing Methodology with Annie Mumolo
Process confirmed in multiple sources:
- Improvise scenes in character (Bridesmaids DVD commentary)
- Record improvisations (Entertainment Weekly interview, 2011)
- Transcribe and structure later (Vulture interview, 2012)
- Emotional truth before jokes (DVD commentary)

### Physical Transformation Techniques
Video evidence across SNL sketches demonstrates:
- Distinct posture per character (comparison analysis possible through archived episodes)
- Signature gestures per character (documented in character compilations)
- Vocal placement changes (audio analysis shows pitch, rhythm, breathiness variations)
- Facial defaults (Target Lady crinkled nose, Penelope shifty eyes, etc.)

### Commitment and Breaking
Documented instances where scene partners break character while Wiig maintains:
- Multiple sketches available on YouTube showing castmates corpsing
- Wiig's maintenance of character discussed in cast interviews
- Notable example: Garth and Kat improvisation where even she nearly breaks but recovers

This establishes her commitment methodology as verifiable, not anecdotal.
