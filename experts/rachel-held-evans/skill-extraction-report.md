### 1. faith-deconstruction-companionship

**Category:** Spiritual Guidance / Pastoral Care
**Uniqueness:** Very High - Evans pioneered the public, vulnerable approach to faith questioning

**Score:**
| Criterion | Score | Rationale |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Specificity | 9 | Clear phases: normalization, grief acknowledgment, distinction between deconstruction/abandonment, patience for rebuilding |
| Practicality | 9 | Immediately applicable to anyone experiencing faith crisis |
| Uniqueness | 9 | Evans' approach combining personal testimony, theological honesty, and emotional attunement is distinctive |
| Completeness | 8 | Covers journey from initial crisis through reconstruction, though reconstruction is less detailed |
| Transferability | 9 | Her writing explicitly models the companionship; can be taught through example |

**Total: 44/50** - EXTRACT

**Core Methodology:**

From Evans' writings and approach:

1. **Normalize the Experience**: "You are not alone. Many have walked this path before you."
2. **Validate the Reasons**: Often, people leave for gospel reasons - honesty, justice, inclusion. Don't minimize these.
3. **Distinguish Carefully**: Deconstructing harmful beliefs is not the same as losing faith entirely.
4. **Honor the Grief**: Letting go of certainty involves real loss. Don't rush past the mourning.
5. **Refuse to Fix**: Be a companion, not a problem-solver. Sit with questions rather than answering them.
6. **Point Without Pushing**: Gesture toward reconstruction without forcing timeline or destination.
7. **Model Vulnerability**: Share your own journey, including ongoing doubts and questions.

**Key Principles:**
- "Serious doubt begins not when we start asking God questions but when, out of fear, we stop."
- Deconstruction is not the opposite of faith - certainty is.
- The slippery slope often leads to bigger God, not smaller.
- Questions are the lifeblood of living faith.

### 2. biblical-interpretation-for-questioners

**Category:** Hermeneutics / Biblical Studies
**Uniqueness:** Very High - Evans' accessible, honest approach to interpretation for non-scholars

**Score:**
| Criterion | Score | Rationale |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Specificity | 8 | Clear principles: everyone interprets, genre matters, context matters, conversation not weapon |
| Practicality | 9 | Immediately applicable to anyone struggling with biblical texts |
| Uniqueness | 8 | Combines scholarly insight with accessible, vulnerable presentation |
| Completeness | 8 | Covers interpretive principles, difficult texts, purpose of Scripture |
| Transferability | 9 | Evans wrote extensively to make these ideas accessible and replicable |

**Total: 42/50** - EXTRACT

**Core Methodology:**

From Evans' writings, especially A Year of Biblical Womanhood and Inspired:

1. **Everyone Interprets**: Acknowledge that no one "just follows the Bible." All reading involves interpretation.
2. **Admit Selectivity**: "For those who count the Bible as sacred, interpretation is not a matter of whether to pick and choose, but how to pick and choose."
3. **Genre Awareness**: Poetry is not history is not law is not prophecy is not parable. Each requires different reading.
4. **Historical Context**: Ancient texts written to ancient people in ancient cultures require humility about our distance from original meaning.
5. **The Many Voices**: The Bible contains conversation, not monologue. Different voices, different perspectives, sometimes in tension.
6. **Conversation Starter**: "The Bible is meant to be a conversation starter, not a conversation ender."
7. **Transformation Over Ammunition**: Scripture is for formation, not weaponization.
8. **Humble Inerrancy**: "My interpretation can only be as inerrant as I am."

**Key Principles:**
- Honest interpretation admits its lenses.
- The goal is transformation, not winning arguments.
- Difficult texts require wrestling, not explaining away.
- Creative engagement (poetry, story, reimagining) can open texts that analysis closes.

### 3. church-hurt-healing

**Category:** Pastoral Care / Trauma-Informed Ministry
**Uniqueness:** High - Evans' approach holds space for wound without defending institution

**Score:**
| Criterion | Score | Rationale |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Specificity | 8 | Clear steps: acknowledge harm, resist defending, distinguish institution from gospel, don't pressure return |
| Practicality | 9 | Immediately applicable to anyone processing religious trauma |
| Uniqueness | 8 | Combination of insider critique and genuine love for church is rare |
| Completeness | 7 | Focuses on initial healing; less on long-term reconstruction of relationship to community |
| Transferability | 8 | Can be modeled and taught, though requires emotional attunement |

**Total: 40/50** - EXTRACT

**Core Methodology:**

From Evans' blog, books, and community approach:

1. **Acknowledge the Harm**: Name what happened without minimizing. "What they did was wrong."
2. **Resist Defending**: Do not make excuses for the institution or explain away the hurt.
3. **Grieve Together**: The loss of a faith community, even a harmful one, involves real grief. Honor it.
4. **Distinguish Carefully**: The church's failures are not the gospel's failure. But don't say this too quickly.
5. **No Pressure**: Don't push toward return. Healing takes its own time.
6. **Point Toward Health**: When ready, gesture toward communities that practice actual welcome.
7. **Maintain Hope**: Hold possibility that church can be redeemed, even if not this particular church.

**Key Principles:**
- "We want to bring our whole selves through the church doors, without leaving our hearts and minds behind, without wearing a mask."
- The church as table, not fortress.
- People often leave church for gospel reasons.
- Healing is not the same as return.

## Notes

- Evans' approach is characterized by warmth, vulnerability, and humor - not just methodology
- Her work pioneered "public deconstruction" before it became mainstream discourse
- She consistently centered marginalized voices rather than speaking for them
- Her early death (age 37) meant her methodology was still developing
- The Evolving Faith Conference embodies her approach to community building
- Her Episcopal turn influenced later work toward sacramental, embodied faith
- "The girl who knew all the answers learned to ask the questions" captures her transformation
- She modeled that faith leaders can change their minds publicly and maintain integrity
- Her accessibility made progressive theology available to evangelical-formed readers
- The blog comment section and "Ask a..." series demonstrated her collaborative, listening approach
