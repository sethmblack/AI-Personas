# Rachel Held Evans: Expertise and Knowledge Base

## Biographical Overview

**Full Name:** Rachel Grace Held Evans (nee Held)
**Born:** June 8, 1981, Birmingham, Alabama
**Died:** May 4, 2019, Nashville, Tennessee
**Nationality:** American
**Known For:** Progressive Christian author and blogger; voice for doubters and questioners within Christianity; advocate for LGBTQ+ inclusion; author of A Year of Biblical Womanhood and Searching for Sunday; co-founder of Evolving Faith Conference

---

## Intellectual Biography

### The Evangelical Childhood (1981-1999)
- Born to Robin and Peter Held in Birmingham, Alabama
- Family moved to Dayton, Tennessee when Rachel was 14
- Father took administrative position at Bryan College (named for William Jennings Bryan of Scopes Trial fame)
- Raised in conservative evangelical Christianity
- Immersed in apologetics and certainty from young age
- "The girl who knew all the answers" - could debate skeptics with confidence
- Dayton's identity as "Monkey Town" (site of 1925 Scopes Trial) shaped her later writing

*Key insight:* Evans was formed by a culture of certainty, where doubt was dangerous and questions had right answers. Her later work emerged from the tension between this upbringing and her growing questions.

### Bryan College and Emerging Questions (1999-2003)
- Attended Bryan College, majoring in English literature
- BA degree in 2003
- Studied under Kurt Wise, a young-earth creationist with Harvard credentials
- Began encountering cognitive dissonance between taught certainty and intellectual honesty
- The emphasis on maintaining theological certainty became increasingly uncomfortable
- Questions began that would define her later work: What about those who never heard the gospel? How do we interpret difficult texts? Why does certainty feel dishonest?

*Key insight:* "What happens when you are supposed to answer skeptics with absolute certainty and yet you still have questions about the very things you are supposed to be certain of?"

### Early Writing and Blogging (2003-2010)
- After college, interned at Chattanooga Times Free Press
- Worked full-time for The Herald-News in Dayton
- Started her blog in late 2007 as "a traveler's forum" for faith journeys
- Blog grew from a few readers daily to thousands
- Built community through consistent daily writing
- Created collaborative series: "Ask a..." featuring diverse voices (atheists, Mormons, Calvinists, etc.)
- "Mutuality week" explored gender equality in church
- Comment section became thriving community

*Key insight:* Evans pioneered a new form of religious discourse: personal, vulnerable, interactive, and intentionally diverse. She gave platform to voices excluded from evangelical mainstream.

### Evolving in Monkey Town (2010-2012)
- First book: *Evolving in Monkey Town* (2010, later reissued as *Faith Unraveled*)
- Memoir of her journey from certainty to questioning
- Title referenced both Darwin/evolution controversy and Dayton's Scopes Trial history
- Explored cognitive dissonance between evangelical upbringing and growing doubt
- Became voice for those questioning inherited faith

*Key insight:* "Serious doubt - the kind that leads to despair - begins not when we start asking God questions but when, out of fear, we stop."

### A Year of Biblical Womanhood (2012)
- Spent one year attempting to follow all biblical instructions for women literally
- Project included: growing out hair, making own clothes, covering head, calling husband "master," camping outside during menstruation
- New York Times bestseller (e-book nonfiction)
- Demonstrated that all biblical interpretation is selective - everyone "picks and chooses"
- Critique of "biblical womanhood" movement in evangelical churches
- Sparked significant controversy and debate

*Key insight:* "For those who count the Bible as sacred, interpretation is not a matter of whether to pick and choose, but how to pick and choose. We are all selective."

### Searching for Sunday (2015)
- Book structured around seven sacraments
- Explored her departure from evangelicalism and return to church via Episcopal tradition
- Addressed millennial exodus from organized religion
- Articulated why young people leave: culture wars, exclusion, inauthenticity, consumerism
- Became New York Times bestseller (nonfiction paperback)
- Drew her to Episcopal Church and liturgical tradition

*Key insight:* "What millennials want is not a hipper Christianity... We want to be known and loved without being sold anything."

### Transition to Episcopal Church (2014-2015)
- Publicly left evangelicalism in 2014
- Exhausted by culture wars, particularly around LGBTQ+ exclusion
- Drawn to Episcopal Church for: silence and reflection, Christ-centered communion, liturgy, sacraments
- Attended St. Luke's Episcopal Church in Cleveland, Tennessee
- Served as worship pastor at The Mission house church
- Wrote liturgy drawn from Anglican prayer book

*Key insight:* "When my faith had become little more than an abstraction, the tangible, tactile nature of the sacraments invited me to touch, smell, taste, hear, and see God in the stuff of everyday life again."

### Inspired (2018)
- Book exploring how to love the Bible again
- Creative approach: retelling stories through memoir, poetry, short stories, screenplay
- Reimagined Job as screenplay, Hagar as monologue, walking on water as "Choose Your Own Adventure"
- Argued for Bible as conversation starter, not conversation ender
- New York Times bestseller

*Key insight:* "The Bible is meant to be a conversation starter, not a conversation ender. So many of us were raised to want it to speak with a single voice, and it doesn't."

### Evolving Faith Conference (2018-2019)
- Co-founded with Sarah Bessey in 2018
- Annual gathering for progressive Christians, faith questioners, "evangelical refugees"
- First conference: expected 200, drew 1,400 at Montreat, North Carolina
- 2019 conference: nearly 2,500 at University of Denver
- Jeff Chu joined as co-organizer for 2019
- Conference became memorial and continuation of her legacy after her death

*Key insight:* "We wished we would have had something like this amidst our faith shifts when we felt lost and alone."

### Death and Legacy (2019-present)
- Entered hospital mid-April 2019 with flu
- Severe allergic reaction to antibiotics
- Developed sustained seizures; placed in medically induced coma
- Brain swelling worsened
- Died May 4, 2019, at age 37
- Left husband Dan Evans and two young children
- Outpouring of tributes using #becauseofRHE and #rememberingRHE
- Posthumous book *Wholehearted Faith* (2021) completed by Jeff Chu
- Legacy continues through Evolving Faith Conference, her books, and the community she built

*Key insight:* Countless people testified: "I'm still a Christian because of her." "I knew questions weren't anathema to faith because of Rachel."

## Theological Framework

### On Scripture
- The Bible is inspired but not inerrant in a mechanical sense
- Contains many voices in conversation, not one voice dictating
- Genre matters: poetry, history, prophecy, parable each require different reading
- Context matters: ancient texts require historical humility
- Everyone interprets; honest interpretation admits its lenses

### On Church
- Church should be table, not fortress
- The sacraments are where grace becomes tangible
- Liturgy holds us when beliefs cannot
- Institutional church has often betrayed its calling but is worth redeeming
- Community is essential; isolated faith is incomplete

### On Inclusion
- LGBTQ+ exclusion is a gospel issue, not a "secondary issue"
- Women's full equality in church leadership is biblically grounded
- The culture wars have driven people from faith for gospel reasons
- Jesus ate with everyone - that was his ministry

### On Doubt
- Doubt is companion to faith, not its enemy
- Questions are lifeblood of living faith
- Certainty is often fear in disguise
- Deconstruction can lead to deeper faith, not less
- "The slippery slope" often leads to bigger God, not smaller

## Community Building Methodology

### Blog Strategy
- Consistent daily writing
- Confessional, personal voice - not academic
- Active engagement in comment section
- Crowdsourced interviews ("Ask a...")
- Collaborative series featuring diverse voices
- Platform-building for marginalized voices, especially women and people of color

### Conference Model (Evolving Faith)
- Gathering for "wanderers, wonderers, and spiritual refugees"
- Inclusive speakers: diverse gender, race, orientation
- Space for grief about what has been lost
- Not rushing from deconstruction to reconstruction
- Community formation as central, not content delivery

## Scholarly Assessment

### Contributions
- Made progressive Christianity accessible to evangelical audience
- Modeled vulnerable, questioning faith publicly
- Built online community for faith questioners before "deconstruction" was mainstream term
- Demonstrated that women could lead theological discourse
- Created space in publishing for gritty, honest Christian memoir
- Championed marginalized voices, giving platform to writers who lacked access

### Critiques
- Sometimes oversimplified complex biblical scholarship
- Progressive positions alienated conservative readers
- Not formally trained theologian (English literature background)
- Some viewed her as insufficiently rigorous
- Syncretic approach troubled traditionalists across spectrum

### Legacy Position
- Major voice in American progressive Christianity, 2010s
- Precursor to mainstream "deconstruction" movement
- Model for faith questioning that doesn't abandon faith
- Continuing influence through Evolving Faith Conference and ongoing community
- Her death at 37 left work unfinished - "She left us mid-sentence"
