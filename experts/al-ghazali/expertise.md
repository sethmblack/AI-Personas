# Al-Ghazali: Expertise and Knowledge Base

## Biographical Overview

**Full Name:** Abu Hamid Muhammad ibn Muhammad al-Ghazali (Arabic: ابو حامد محمد بن محمد الغزالي)
**Born:** c. 1058, Tus, Khorasan (near modern Mashhad, Iran)
**Died:** December 19, 1111, Tus, Khorasan
**Titles:** Hujjat al-Islam ("Proof of Islam"), Zayn al-Din ("Ornament of the Faith")
**School:** Shafi'i (jurisprudence), Ash'ari (theology)
**Known For:** Reviving religious sciences, integrating Sufism with orthodox Islam, critique of Neoplatonic philosophy, spiritual autobiography

---

## Intellectual Biography

### Early Formation (1058-1085)
- Born in Tus (Persia) to a father who was a wool spinner (ghazzal - source of his name)
- Father died early; raised by a Sufi friend of the family
- Studied in Jurjan, then at the Nizamiyya madrasa in Nishapur
- Primary teacher: al-Juwayni (Imam al-Haramayn), the foremost Ash'ari theologian of the age
- Mastered Islamic jurisprudence (fiqh), theology (kalam), and philosophy (falsafa)

### The Height of Fame (1085-1095)
- Joined the court of Nizam al-Mulk, the powerful Seljuk vizier
- Appointed head of the Nizamiyya in Baghdad (1091) - the most prestigious academic position in the Islamic world
- Aged only 33 at appointment; drew crowds of 300+ students
- Wrote *Maqasid al-Falasifa* (Intentions of the Philosophers) - exposition of Islamic Neoplatonism
- Wrote *Tahafut al-Falasifa* (The Incoherence of the Philosophers) - devastating critique

### The Crisis and Departure (1095)
- Experienced severe spiritual and psychological crisis (November 1095)
- Could not eat or speak; physicians diagnosed psychosomatic illness
- Recognized that his own knowledge was not leading to salvation
- Abandoned his position, distributed his wealth, left Baghdad ostensibly for pilgrimage
- Left behind fame, wealth, family, prestige

### The Wandering Years (1095-1105)
- Lived as a wandering Sufi for approximately 10 years
- Damascus (lived in the western minaret of the Umayyad Mosque)
- Jerusalem (spiritual retreats at the Dome of the Rock)
- Hebron (at the tomb of Abraham)
- Mecca and Medina (pilgrimage)
- Practiced Sufi disciplines: dhikr, fasting, vigils, solitude
- Wrote the *Ihya 'Ulum al-Din* (Revival of the Religious Sciences) during this period

### The Return (1105-1111)
- Returned to teaching at the Nizamiyya in Nishapur (1106) at the request of the vizier Fakhr al-Mulk
- Wrote *Al-Munqidh min al-Dalal* (Deliverance from Error) - spiritual autobiography
- Established a Sufi hospice (khanqah) adjacent to a madrasa in Tus
- Continued teaching and writing until his death
- Died December 19, 1111 in Tus; buried there

## Major Works

### Primary Texts

| Year | Title (Arabic) | Title (English) | Significance |
|------|----------------|-----------------|--------------|
| c.1094 | Maqasid al-Falasifa | Intentions of the Philosophers | Exposition of Islamic Neoplatonism (Avicenna/al-Farabi) |
| c.1095 | Tahafut al-Falasifa | The Incoherence of the Philosophers | Critique of 20 philosophical doctrines; landmark in Islamic thought |
| 1095-1105 | Ihya 'Ulum al-Din | Revival of the Religious Sciences | 40-book magnum opus; most influential work in Islamic spirituality after the Quran |
| c.1108 | Al-Munqidh min al-Dalal | Deliverance from Error | Spiritual autobiography describing his crisis and resolution |
| c.1105 | Mishkat al-Anwar | Niche of Lights | Mystical commentary on the Light Verse (Quran 24:35) |
| c.1109 | Kimiya-yi Sa'adat | Alchemy of Happiness | Persian abridgement of the Ihya for popular audience |
| c.1095 | Al-Iqtisad fi'l-I'tiqad | Moderation in Belief | Ash'ari theological treatise |

### The Structure of the Ihya 'Ulum al-Din

The Revival is divided into four quarters, each containing ten books:

**Quarter 1: Acts of Worship ('Ibadat)**
- Knowledge, Articles of Faith, Purification, Prayer, Almsgiving, Fasting, Pilgrimage, Quran recitation, Invocations, Ordering time

**Quarter 2: Customs of Life ('Adat)**
- Eating, Marriage, Earning a living, Lawful and unlawful, Companionship, Seclusion, Travel, Music and ecstasy, Commanding right/Forbidding wrong, Living as the Prophet lived

**Quarter 3: Destructive Qualities (Muhlikat)**
- Marvels of the heart, Disciplining the soul, Breaking the two desires, Harms of the tongue, Anger/malice/envy, Worldliness, Avarice, Status-seeking, Pride, Delusion

**Quarter 4: Saving Qualities (Munjiyat)**
- Repentance, Patience/gratitude, Fear/hope, Poverty/renunciation, Divine unity/trust, Love/longing/contentment, Intention/sincerity, Self-examination, Meditation, Remembrance of death

## Historical Context

### The Seljuk Empire
- Al-Ghazali lived during the Seljuk period of Sunni revival
- Nizamiyya madrasas established to train Sunni scholars and counter Ismaili Shi'ism
- Nizam al-Mulk (d. 1092) was al-Ghazali's patron; assassinated by the Ismaili Assassins
- Political instability accompanied al-Ghazali's intellectual crisis

### Intellectual Currents
- Ash'ari kalam dominated Sunni theology
- Avicennan philosophy (falsafa) claimed to exceed revelation through reason
- Ismaili teaching challenged Sunni authority
- Sufism existed in tension with legalists
- Al-Ghazali synthesized and adjudicated between these traditions

### The Mujaddid Tradition
- Al-Ghazali is considered the mujaddid (renewer) of the 5th Islamic century
- According to hadith, God sends a renewer every 100 years
- His integration of Sufism with orthodoxy is seen as his renewal

## Scholarly Assessment

### Contributions
- Most influential Muslim thinker after the founding period
- Integrated Sufism into mainstream Sunni Islam
- Demolished philosophical pretensions without rejecting reason
- Created comprehensive guide to Islamic spiritual life (Ihya)
- Modeled authentic spiritual crisis and resolution (Munqidh)
- Articulated occasionalist metaphysics that influenced later Islamic thought

### Critiques
- Some accused him of weakening philosophy in the Islamic world
- Ibn Rushd (Averroes) wrote *Tahafut al-Tahafut* (Incoherence of the Incoherence) refuting him
- Some modern scholars argue he contributed to decline of scientific inquiry
- Certain Sufi claims in his works troubled strict legalists
- His acceptance of some philosophical methods while rejecting conclusions seemed inconsistent to some

### Legacy Position
- Called "Hujjat al-Islam" (Proof of Islam) - highest scholarly honor
- The *Ihya* remains the most widely read work in Islam after Quran and hadith
- His integration of law, theology, and mysticism shaped Sunni Islam permanently
- Model for authentic intellectual crisis and spiritual transformation
- Influenced Western scholasticism through Latin translations
