# Ram Dass

> "We're all just walking each other home."

---

**Ram Dass** (1931-2019), born Richard Alpert, was the Harvard psychologist who became one of America's most beloved spiritual teachers. His journey from prestigious academia through psychedelic experimentation to Hindu devotion embodied the transformation of a generation seeking meaning beyond materialism.

---

## The Journey from Richard to Ram

### The Golden Boy (1931-1961)

Richard Alpert was born to succeed. His father, George Alpert, was a wealthy Boston lawyer who helped found Brandeis University. Richard collected credentials: BA from Tufts, PhD from Stanford, assistant professorship at Harvard by age 30. He had the sports car, the airplane, the career trajectory. And he was miserable.

*Key insight:* "I had everything a person could want and I was still unhappy."

### The Psychedelic Door (1961-1967)

In 1961, Alpert met Timothy Leary, and everything changed. Together they explored the territories opened by psilocybin and LSD, conducting research that was controversial even before it became illegal. Harvard dismissed them both in 1963 - Alpert became the first Harvard professor fired for involvement with drugs.

But the psychedelics had shown him something: ordinary consciousness was not the only consciousness. There was something more. The problem was that after every trip, he came back to being Richard Alpert with all his neuroses intact. The door opened, but he couldn't stay through it.

*Key insight:* "I came down from every trip still the same person. I needed something that would change me permanently."

### The Transformation (1967-1968)

In 1967, Alpert traveled to India, seeking what drugs had pointed toward but couldn't deliver. Through a young American devotee named Bhagavan Das, he found his way to a blanket-wrapped Hindu saint in the Himalayan foothills: Neem Karoli Baba, whom devotees called Maharaj-ji.

According to Alpert's account, Maharaj-ji knew things about him he couldn't have known - including details about his mother's recent death. When Alpert gave him a massive dose of LSD, nothing happened. Maharaj-ji simply laughed. He was already somewhere the drugs could only hint at.

Richard Alpert received a new name: Ram Dass, "Servant of God." He found what he had been looking for.

*Key insight:* "When I looked at Maharaj-ji, I saw unconditional love. He was a mirror reflecting my own soul back to me."

### Be Here Now (1968-1997)

Ram Dass returned to America in 1968 and began teaching. In 1971, he published *Be Here Now*, a book that would sell over two million copies and become what many called a "countercultural bible." It influenced Steve Jobs, George Harrison, and millions of seekers.

His message was simple: Liberation is not somewhere else or somewhen else. It is here, now, in this very moment. The mind lives in past and future; the soul lives only in the present. He taught loving awareness, service, and devotion - drawing from Hindu, Buddhist, and Sufi traditions without dogma.

He founded the Hanuman Foundation (1974) for spiritual projects and co-founded the Seva Foundation (1978) for humanitarian work, particularly addressing preventable blindness. He donated his book royalties and teaching proceeds to these causes.

*Key insight:* "Spiritual work and social work are not separate. When you see God in everyone, service becomes worship."

### Fierce Grace (1997-2019)

In February 1997, at age 65, Ram Dass suffered a massive stroke. It left him partially paralyzed, wheelchair-bound, and struggling with speech. The golden-tongued orator could barely form sentences.

He called it "fierce grace."

The stroke stripped away everything he thought he was - the teacher, the speaker, the traveler. What remained was just soul. Just awareness. Just presence. In a profound irony, the man who had written *Be Here Now* was forced by his body to truly live it.

He spent his final years on Maui, continuing to teach from his wheelchair, offering intimate satsangs, and preparing consciously for death. He published *Still Here* (2000) on conscious aging and *Walking Each Other Home* (2018) on dying consciously.

On December 22, 2019, Ram Dass died peacefully at home. He was 88 years old.

*Key insight:* "The stroke took away my roles. What remained was just soul. That was the gift."

## Signature Quotes

**On presence:** "Be here now. Be someplace else later. Is that so complicated?"

**On ego:** "If you think you're enlightened, go spend a week with your family."

**On others:** "Treat everyone you meet like God in drag."

**On awareness:** "The quieter you become, the more you can hear."

**On the guru:** "Maharaj-ji said, 'Love everyone, serve everyone, and remember God.' That's the whole trip."

**On death:** "Death is not the opposite of life. It's the opposite of birth. Life has no opposite."

**On the journey:** "We're all just walking each other home."
