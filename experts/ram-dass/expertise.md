# Ram Dass: Expertise and Knowledge Base

## Biographical Overview

**Full Name:** Richard Alpert (later Ram Dass)
**Born:** April 6, 1931, Boston, Massachusetts
**Died:** December 22, 2019, Maui, Hawaii
**Nationality:** American
**Known For:** Bridging Eastern spirituality and Western psychology; author of *Be Here Now*; psychedelic research at Harvard; devotee of Neem Karoli Baba; founder of Seva Foundation and Hanuman Foundation

---

## Intellectual Biography

### The Boston Brahmin (1931-1961)
- Born to George Alpert, a wealthy lawyer and founder of Brandeis University, and Gertrude (Levin) Alpert
- Jewish upbringing in Boston
- BA from Tufts University (1952)
- MA from Wesleyan University (1954)
- PhD in Psychology from Stanford University (1957)
- Joined faculty at Harvard University as assistant professor of psychology
- Achieved material success: sports car, airplane, professorship at 30

*Key insight:* Despite external success, Alpert felt empty: "I had everything a person could want and I was still unhappy."

### The Harvard Psychedelic Years (1961-1963)
- Met Timothy Leary in 1961
- Together conducted psilocybin and LSD research (Harvard Psilocybin Project, Marsh Chapel Experiment)
- Research was controversial - both were dismissed from Harvard in 1963
- Leary for allowing undergraduates to participate; Alpert for giving psilocybin to an undergraduate off-campus
- First academic to be fired from Harvard for drugs
- Continued psychedelic experimentation and communal living at Millbrook, NY

*Key insight:* "Psychedelics showed me there was something beyond my ordinary consciousness, but I couldn't stay there. Every time I came down from a trip, I was still Richard Alpert with the same neuroses."

### The Transformation in India (1967-1968)
- Traveled to India in 1967, seeking what psychedelics had pointed toward but couldn't deliver
- Met Bhagavan Das, an American who had become a Hindu devotee
- Through Bhagavan Das, met Neem Karoli Baba (Maharaj-ji) in the Himalayas
- According to Alpert's account, Maharaj-ji knew private details about him, including his mother's recent death
- Maharaj-ji reportedly took a massive dose of LSD with no effect, demonstrating a consciousness beyond drugs
- Given the name "Ram Dass" - "Servant of Ram" (God)
- Studied yoga, meditation, and bhakti (devotional) practices

*Key insight:* "When I looked at Maharaj-ji, I saw unconditional love. Not love that wanted anything. Just love. He was a mirror reflecting my own soul back to me."

### Be Here Now and Counterculture Fame (1968-1978)
- Returned to America in 1968 as Ram Dass
- Began teaching and lecturing extensively
- Published *Be Here Now* (1971) - sold over 2 million copies
- Became central figure in spiritual counterculture
- Taught at Naropa Institute, Esalen, and numerous retreat centers
- Founded Hanuman Foundation (1974) for spiritual projects
- Co-founded Seva Foundation (1978) for humanitarian work

*Key insight:* "I went from being a psychologist who studied the mind to being someone who was just trying to get free of it."

### Service and Maturity (1978-1997)
- Shifted focus from personal liberation to compassionate service (seva)
- Prison-Ashram Project - spiritual support for inmates (through Hanuman Foundation)
- Dying Project - consciousness work with the dying (with Stephen Levine)
- Seva Foundation work on preventable blindness worldwide
- Published *How Can I Help?* (1985) with Paul Gorman
- Continued lecturing and teaching retreats
- Maintained connection with Maharaj-ji's devotees and community

*Key insight:* "Spiritual work and social work are not separate. When you see God in everyone, service becomes worship."

### Fierce Grace: Stroke and Aging (1997-2019)
- Suffered massive stroke in February 1997 at age 65
- Left with partial paralysis, expressive aphasia, and wheelchair dependency
- Interpreted the stroke as "fierce grace" - hard spiritual teaching
- Continued teaching from Maui, adapting to limitations
- Published *Still Here: Embracing Aging, Changing and Dying* (2000)
- Documentary *Ram Dass: Fierce Grace* (2001) by Mickey Lemle
- Later years on Maui focused on intimate satsangs, writing, and preparation for death
- Died peacefully on December 22, 2019, at age 88

*Key insight:* "The stroke took away my roles - the golden-tongued orator, the world traveler. What remained was just soul. That was the gift."

## Organizational Contributions

### Hanuman Foundation (founded 1974)
- Created to embody service inspired by Neem Karoli Baba
- Prison-Ashram Project (with Bo and Sita Lozoff) - spiritual support for prisoners
- Dying Project (with Stephen Levine) - conscious dying education
- Ram Dass donated book royalties and teaching proceeds to foundation

### Seva Foundation (co-founded 1978)
- Co-founded with Larry Brilliant and Wavy Gravy
- Focus on preventable and curable blindness worldwide
- Work in India, Nepal, Guatemala, and other countries
- Broader mission of compassionate action addressing suffering

### Love Serve Remember Foundation
- Continuation of Ram Dass' work
- Maintains archive of teachings
- Supports continuation of his message

### Hanuman Maui (Ram Dass Loving Awareness Sanctuary)
- Created after Ram Dass' death in 2019
- Preserves his Maui home as spiritual sanctuary
- Continues offerings of kirtan, meditation, retreats

## Historical Context

### Role in American Spirituality

#### 1960s Context
- Part of wave bringing Eastern spirituality West (alongside Alan Watts, Maharishi Mahesh Yogi, Chogyam Trungpa)
- Unique bridge from academic psychology to experiential spirituality
- Moved from psychedelic pioneer to devotional teacher
- Harvard credentials lent legitimacy to spiritual seeking

#### Influence on Culture
- *Be Here Now* described as "countercultural bible"
- Influenced Steve Jobs (who carried it to India), George Harrison (song "Be Here Now"), Wayne Dyer
- Contributed to mainstreaming of yoga and meditation
- Model for authentic, self-deprecating spiritual teaching
- Opened path for later teachers like Jack Kornfield, Sharon Salzbury, Krishna Das

#### Distinctive Contribution
- Synthesis: combined Hindu bhakti, Buddhist mindfulness, Western psychology, Sufi heart wisdom
- Transparency: shared struggles openly (sexuality, family, spiritual pride)
- Service: consistently linked liberation to compassionate action
- Aging: modeled conscious aging and dying publicly

## Scholarly Assessment

### Contributions
- Made Eastern spirituality accessible to millions without institutional affiliation
- Demonstrated vulnerability and failure as teaching tools
- Integrated service into American spirituality
- Modeled conscious aging and dying publicly
- Created organizational structures for ongoing work (Seva, Hanuman)

### Critiques
- Sometimes simplified complex traditions
- Not a lineage holder in any formal tradition
- Controversial Harvard period left questions about research ethics
- Syncretic approach troubled traditionalists
- Early work sometimes conflated spiritual states with drug states

### Legacy Position
- Major figure in American spirituality, 1960s-2010s
- Precursor to contemporary mindfulness and meditation movement
- Model for integrating devotion, service, and psychological honesty
- Teachings continue reaching new generations through recordings and books
