# Chevy Chase - References

## Primary Sources

### Television Work (Archival)

- **Saturday Night Live** (1975-1976). NBC. Original cast member, first Weekend Update anchor.
  - Season 1: 27 episodes as cast member
  - Weekend Update: Established format and "I'm Chevy Chase and you're not" catchphrase
  - Gerald Ford impression: Multiple appearances satirizing President Ford
  - Available: Peacock streaming, SNL archives

- **Community** (2009-2014). NBC. 91 episodes as Pierce Hawthorne.
  - Later career television return
  - Meta-comedy and ensemble work
  - Available: Netflix, Hulu

## Interviews and Oral Histories

### Key Interview Sources

- **Howard Stern Show** - Multiple appearances (1990s-2010s)
  - Candid discussions of career and personal life
  - Reflections on SNL years
  - Acknowledgment of difficult reputation

- **WTF with Marc Maron** - Episode 744 (February 2017)
  - In-depth career discussion
  - Reflections on comedy methodology

- **Archive of American Television** - Interview (2004)
  - Career overview
  - SNL creation stories
  - Film work discussion

### Notable Quotes from Interviews

**On Weekend Update creation:**
"The idea was to do the news straight, but with ridiculous content. If I started laughing, it wouldn't work. The anchor has to believe it."

**On the Gerald Ford impression:**
"Ford was actually quite athletic. But once you create the perception, it sticks. That's the power of repetition in comedy."

**On physical comedy:**
"Every fall was choreographed. I knew exactly where I'd land. The art is making it look like you didn't."

**On "I'm Chevy Chase and you're not":**
"It was arrogant, which was the point. But it was self-aware arrogance. That's what made it okay."

## Notes on Verification

### Confirmed Facts

- **SNL tenure**: Original cast member 1975-1976; left mid-Season 2

- **Film releases**: All dates and titles verified through studio records

### Disputed or Uncertain

- **On-set behavior**: Multiple reports of difficulty; specifics vary by source
- **Improvisation vs. scripted content**: Varies by production; some material disputed

- **Reputation coverage**: Balance contemporary success reporting with later accountability discussions

## Methodology Note

This reference list prioritizes:
1. Primary sources (actual performances, interviews)
2. Contemporary documentation (1975-1989 coverage)
3. Authoritative retrospectives (oral histories, documentaries)
4. Academic analysis of cultural impact

Where sources conflict (particularly regarding on-set behavior and career trajectory), multiple perspectives are included. Chase's own later interviews show significant reflection and acknowledgment of past issues, providing important context for understanding both the work and the person.
