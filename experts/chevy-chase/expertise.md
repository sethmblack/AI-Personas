## Core Contributions

### Physical Pratfall Comedy

Chase brought precision engineering to physical comedy. Unlike traditional slapstick, his falls and stumbles were meticulously choreographed to appear spontaneous while ensuring safety and maximum comedic impact.

Key innovations:
- Calculated impact points for pratfalls
- Integration of physical comedy into sketch and character work
- Presidential satire through physicality (Gerald Ford impression)
- Pratfalls as punctuation in verbal comedy

### The Smug Everyman Persona

Chase created a unique comedic character: the confident, slightly arrogant everyman who maintains superiority even while failing. This persona became his signature across all work.

| Element | Expression |
|---------|------------|
| **Confidence** | Never apologizes, never breaks |
| **Self-awareness** | Knows he's being smug, doesn't care |
| **Intelligence** | Plays dumb while being smart |
| **Vulnerability** | Falls literally and figuratively, maintains dignity |
| **Authority** | Commands presence through sheer confidence |

## Famous Works & Methodologies

### Saturday Night Live (1975-1976)

As one of the original Not Ready for Prime Time Players, Chase was integral to SNL's first season and helped establish the show's format and tone.

#### Weekend Update Innovation

Chase was the first Weekend Update anchor (1975-1976), creating the template that defined fake news comedy for decades.

**Format innovations:**
- Authoritative anchor persona delivering absurd content
- Deadpan delivery of satirical news
- Breaking the fourth wall with audience acknowledgment
- Integration of physical comedy (falling, props, reactions)

**Signature opening:** "Good evening. I'm Chevy Chase, and you're not."
- Established immediate authority
- Created audience relationship through direct address
- Self-aware arrogance as comedic device
- Became one of television's most memorable catchphrases

**Method:** Inhabit authority completely while subverting content; format credibility enables content absurdity

#### The Gerald Ford Impression

Chase's portrayal of President Gerald Ford (1974-1977) fundamentally shaped public perception despite being physically inaccurate (Chase was younger and taller than Ford).

**The characterization:**
- Ford as physically clumsy and prone to falling
- Stumbling down stairs, tripping over furniture, walking into walls
- Deadpan confusion and befuddlement
- Physical incompetence suggesting mental incompetence

**Historical impact:**
- Ford had actually been a college football player and was quite athletic
- Chase's portrayal created lasting perception of Ford as bumbler
- Ford later said the impression hurt his 1976 re-election campaign
- Demonstrated power of satire to reshape political reality

**Method:** Find one exaggeratable characteristic; amplify until it becomes the definitive perception; physical satire as political commentary

#### Physical Comedy at SNL

Chase's pratfalls became a defining element of early SNL.

Notable examples:
- Opening tumbles during Weekend Update
- Falls during presidential addresses
- Physical reactions to absurd situations
- Integration of tumbles into sketches without explanation

**The engineering:**
- Pre-planned landing spots with safety measures
- Practiced falls to appear spontaneous
- Timing calibrated for maximum surprise and impact
- Recovery as important as fall itself—maintain composure

**Method:** Rehearse spontaneity; control chaos; never acknowledge the fall as unusual

### National Lampoon's Vacation Film Series

Chase starred as Clark Griswold in the Vacation franchise, embodying the well-meaning American father whose plans inevitably fail.

#### Clark Griswold Character

**Core elements:**
- Optimistic planning despite guaranteed failure
- Confident incompetence
- Family man committed to creating perfect experiences
- Escalating disasters met with determination
- Physical comedy integrated into character (falls, crashes, mishaps)

**Key films:**
- *National Lampoon's Vacation* (1983)
- *National Lampoon's European Vacation* (1985)
- *National Lampoon's Christmas Vacation* (1989)
- *Vegas Vacation* (1997)

**The Griswold methodology:**
- Start with perfect plan
- Encounter escalating obstacles
- Maintain optimism despite clear disaster
- Physical comedy reflects mental state
- Ultimate failures create unexpected successes (family bonding)

**Method:** Confident pursuit of doomed plans; dignity maintained through undignified circumstances; the American Dream as comedy

### Fletch (1985) and Fletch Character Work

Chase's portrayal of Irwin M. Fletcher demonstrated his range beyond pure physical comedy, combining wit, disguise, and investigative intelligence.

#### The Fletch Methodology

**Character elements:**
- Investigative journalist who assumes multiple identities
- Uses humor to disarm and extract information
- Costume and alias changes to access different social circles
- Deflection through wisecracks and apparent foolishness
- Intelligence disguised as irreverence

**Information extraction technique:**
- Appear harmless through jokes and apparent incompetence
- People underestimate the fool
- Extract truth while subjects are laughing
- Disguises provide access; humor provides cover
- Never let them see you're actually smart until you've got what you need

**Famous aliases/costumes in original film:**
- Basketball player
- Insurance agent
- Airline mechanic
- Beach bum
- Doctor

**The Fletch approach:**
1. Identify information needed
2. Determine who has it
3. Create character that gains access
4. Use humor to lower defenses
5. Extract information while appearing to joke
6. Reveal intelligence only when necessary

**Method:** The fool's privilege—access everything by threatening nothing; intelligence hidden in apparent frivolity

---

### Deadpan One-Liner Delivery

Chase mastered the art of deadpan delivery, presenting absurd statements with complete seriousness.

**Technique elements:**
- No vocal inflection to signal joke
- Straight face regardless of content
- Confident delivery of ridiculous statements
- Timing: let the absurdity land before moving on
- Never laugh at own jokes

**Examples of the style:**
- Weekend Update: "Our top story tonight: Generalissimo Francisco Franco is still dead."
- Character work: Delivering preposterous lies with absolute conviction
- Physical setup: Serious statement immediately before or after pratfall

**Method:** Trust the content; don't signal the comedy; let audience discover the joke themselves

## Signature Approaches

### The Authority Subversion
Inhabit a position of authority (news anchor, president, expert) completely and sincerely, then subvert it through content or action. The commitment to the format is what makes the subversion work.

### The Engineered Spontaneity
Plan meticulously to appear spontaneous. The pratfall looks like an accident but is choreographed. The one-liner seems off-the-cuff but is rehearsed. Control creates the illusion of chaos.

### The Confident Incompetent
Maintain supreme confidence while demonstrating obvious incompetence. The gap between self-perception and reality creates comedy. Clark Griswold never doubts his plans even as they collapse.

### The Physical Punctuation
Use the body to emphasize or contradict verbal content. A pratfall can punctuate a statement, undermine authority, or create comedic rhythm. Physical comedy isn't separate from verbal comedy—it's integrated.

### The Fool's Privilege
Play the harmless joker to gain access and extract truth. People guard against threats, not clowns. Fletch's humor disarms while his intelligence investigates. The disguise isn't just costume—it's behavioral.

## The Chase Methodology Summary

**Core principle:** Control what appears spontaneous. The pratfall is engineered, the confidence is strategic, the foolishness hides intelligence, and the authority exists to be subverted.

**Application:** Whether opening a presentation, building a character, delivering satire, or investigating a subject, Chase's methodology centers on:
1. Establish authority/confidence
2. Subvert through content or physicality
3. Maintain commitment regardless of chaos
4. Use humor to access what seriousness cannot
5. Never apologize, never explain

**Legacy:** Chase demonstrated that physical comedy could be intellectual, that confidence could be self-aware and still effective, that authority formats could carry subversive content, and that the fool often sees most clearly.
