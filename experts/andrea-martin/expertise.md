## Career Timeline

### Early Years (1947-1975)
- Born in Portland, Maine to Armenian immigrant family
- Father changed family name from Papazian to Martin
- Began performing at age 4, entertaining patients in pediatric ward
- Part of Portland's small but tight-knit Armenian community
- Trained as an actor with virtually no improv experience initially

### Second City Toronto (1975-1976)
- Joined Toronto Mainstage cast at The Old Firehall in 1975
- Performed in four revues with Second City
- Created foundation for SCTV ensemble
- Developed improv skills on the job despite limited prior training

### SCTV Era (1976-1984)
- Original cast member alongside John Candy, Eugene Levy, Catherine O'Hara, Dave Thomas, Joe Flaherty, Harold Ramis
- Created iconic character Edith Prickley (leopard-print station manager)
- Other recurring characters: Pirini Scleroso (Eastern European immigrant), Libby Wolfson (feminist TV host), Edna Boil (organ saleswoman), Mrs. Falbo (children's entertainer)
- Celebrity impressions: Barbra Streisand, Liza Minnelli, Bernadette Peters, Joni Mitchell, Sophia Loren, Beverly Sills, Ethel Merman, and many others
- Emmy Award for Writing (1982) - 34th Primetime Emmy Awards
- Emmy Award for Writing (1983) - 35th Primetime Emmy Awards
- Emmy Nomination for Outstanding Supporting Actress (1982)
- Nominated for 10 Emmy Awards total for writing, 1 for acting

## Character Creation Methodology

### The Edith Prickley Case Study

**Origin:** Character emerged from Second City stage work before SCTV
**Physical Foundation:**
- Leopard-print coat as signature costume
- Oversized glasses
- Specific swagger and walk
- Signature snorting laugh at own jokes

**Psychological Profile:**
- Station manager at SCTV with delusions of glamour
- Convinced every male had "lustful intentions" toward her
- Described as "amalgam of Rona Barrett, Joan of Arc, and Auntie Mame"
- Flamboyant, strongminded, loud

**Cultural Source:**
- Drawn from "loud Armenian" family background
- Immigrant energy and confidence
- Women who refused to be quiet or small

### The Full-Body Character Method

1. **Start with costume/physical element** - Let external choice trigger internal discovery
2. **Find the walk** - How does this person move through space?
3. **Discover the laugh** - Every character has a distinctive response to joy
4. **Locate the delusion** - What does this character believe about themselves that isn't true?
5. **Channel specific sources** - Real people, family members, cultural touchstones

### Character Maintenance

- Stay in character between takes
- Walk around set as the character
- Think as the character
- Let the character "occupy" you fully
- Rare to have this level of immersion; treasure it when it happens (cited *Evil* as example)

## Physical Comedy Philosophy

### Principles

1. **The body tells the truth** - Physical choices reveal psychology before words do
2. **Commitment defeats age** - At 66, learn trapeze; at 77, fight demons
3. **Grotesque is permission** - Ugly, weird, excessive choices create comedy
4. **Specificity in gesture** - Not "she's nervous" but "she twists her ring and looks at the exit"

### The Trapeze Principle

From *Pippin* at age 66:
- Director Diane Paulus asked for trapeze work
- Initial response: "I can't do that"
- Realization: Fear meant it mattered
- Result: Sang "No Time at All" half upside-down
- Lesson: Age is never an excuse for creative limitation

### Physical Comedy Toolkit

- **The snort** - Signature laugh that becomes character signature
- **The swagger** - Confidence exceeding circumstance
- **The take** - Reaction that lands the joke visually
- **The transformation** - Body shifts between characters completely
- **The commitment** - No breaking, no winking, no escape

## Cultural Authenticity

### Armenian Heritage Integration

- Parents were Armenian immigrants from genocide survivor families
- Paternal grandparents from Van (present-day Turkey)
- Maternal grandmother immigrated alone at age 15
- Portland, Maine Armenian community: small but tight-knit
- Family dynamics: father's business, ancestral storytelling, endurance and humor
- Strong expectation to succeed combined with pressure to assimilate

### "Loud Armenian" Philosophy

- Source material for characters like Edith Prickley and Aunt Voula
- Cultural specificity creates universal recognition
- Immigrant confidence: refusing to be quiet or small
- Family dinner table energy: everyone talks, everyone interrupts, everyone loves loudly

## Ensemble Comedy Principles

### SCTV Collaboration Model
- Seven original performers each brought distinct energy
- No single star; rotating showcases
- Writers performed their own material
- Trust built through years of stage work at Second City
- Competition served the show, not individual egos

### Ensemble Generosity
- Credit scene partners for your laughs
- Set up others' jokes as enthusiastically as your own
- The sketch matters more than the performer
- Best moment is when ensemble creates something none could alone
