# St. Paul (Paul the Apostle): Expertise and Knowledge Base

## Biographical Overview

**Full Name:** Saul of Tarsus (Hebrew name); Paul (Roman name)
**Born:** c. 5 CE, Tarsus, Cilicia (modern-day Turkey)
**Died:** c. 64-67 CE, Rome (traditionally by beheading under Nero)
**Nationality:** Roman citizen, Jewish heritage from tribe of Benjamin
**Known For:** Apostle to the Gentiles; author of major New Testament epistles; foundational Christian theologian; missionary who established churches across the Roman Empire

---

## Biographical Timeline

### Early Life and Education (c. 5-33 CE)
- Born in Tarsus, a major Greco-Roman city, as a Roman citizen
- Heritage: "Hebrew of Hebrews" from the tribe of Benjamin
- Educated as a Pharisee under Gamaliel in Jerusalem
- Trained in Jewish law, Scripture, and rabbinic methods
- Learned tent-making as a trade (common for rabbis to have manual skills)
- Became zealous persecutor of the early Christian movement
- Present at the stoning of Stephen (Acts 7:58)

### Conversion Experience (c. 33-36 CE)
- Traveling to Damascus to arrest Christians
- Blinding light and voice: "Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?"
- Identified as Jesus whom Saul was persecuting
- Struck blind for three days
- Ananias sent to restore his sight and baptize him
- "Scales fell from his eyes" - literal and metaphorical transformation
- Immediate proclamation of Jesus in Damascus synagogues

### Arabia and Early Ministry (c. 36-47 CE)
- Withdrew to Arabia for an unknown period (Galatians 1:17)
- Returned to Damascus, then to Jerusalem
- Spent 15 days with Peter and met James
- Active in Antioch, which became his missionary base
- Barnabas recruited him for ministry

### First Missionary Journey (c. 46-48 CE)
- Sent from Antioch with Barnabas and John Mark
- Cyprus: Salamis, Paphos (confrontation with Elymas the sorcerer)
- Asia Minor: Perga, Antioch of Pisidia, Iconium, Lystra, Derbe
- Established churches in Galatian region
- Pattern: Preach in synagogue first, then to Gentiles when rejected
- John Mark departed at Perga

### Jerusalem Council (c. 49-50 CE)
- Controversy over Gentile converts and circumcision
- Paul and Barnabas sent to Jerusalem for resolution
- Council determined Gentiles need not be circumcised
- Victory for Pauline understanding of gospel freedom
- "Pillars" (James, Peter, John) affirmed Paul's mission

### Second Missionary Journey (c. 50-52 CE)
- Disagreement with Barnabas over John Mark; took Silas instead
- Through Syria, Cilicia, revisited Galatian churches
- Timothy joined at Lystra
- Prevented from going to Asia; vision of "man of Macedonia"
- Gospel reaches Europe: Philippi, Thessalonica, Berea, Athens, Corinth
- Philippi: Conversion of Lydia; imprisonment and earthquake
- Athens: Areopagus sermon on the "Unknown God"
- Corinth: 18 months; tent-making with Aquila and Priscilla
- Brought before Gallio (provides fixed date: 51-52 CE)

### Third Missionary Journey (c. 53-57 CE)
- Extended stay in Ephesus (2-3 years)
- "All Asia heard the word" from this base
- Riot of the silversmiths (Demetrius)
- Extensive letter-writing during this period
- Collection for Jerusalem poor from Gentile churches
- Traveled through Macedonia and Greece
- Return journey through Troas (Eutychus falls from window)

### Arrest and Imprisonment (c. 57-62 CE)
- Arrived in Jerusalem with collection
- Arrested in Temple on false accusation
- Defense before Sanhedrin, Felix, Festus, Agrippa
- Appealed to Caesar as Roman citizen
- Voyage to Rome: Shipwreck at Malta
- House arrest in Rome for two years (c. 60-62 CE)
- Continued preaching and writing during imprisonment

### Final Years and Martyrdom (c. 62-67 CE)
- Possibly released and continued traveling (pastoral epistles suggest this)
- Possible journey to Spain (Romans 15:24)
- Second Roman imprisonment under Nero
- Martyrdom by beheading (tradition) c. 64-67 CE
- Buried along Via Ostiense (site of San Paolo fuori le Mura)

## Key Theological Concepts

### Justification by Faith
- Greek: *dikaiosyne* (righteousness), *pistis* (faith)
- Forensic/legal metaphor: declared righteous before God
- Not earned through works of the law
- Received through faith/trust in Christ
- Universal need (Romans 3:23) and universal offer
- Foundation: "The righteous shall live by faith" (Habakkuk 2:4)

### Grace (Charis)
- Unmerited favor of God
- Not earned, not deserved, cannot be repaid
- Foundation of salvation and Christian life
- Empowers ethical living (not just forgiveness)
- "Where sin increased, grace abounded all the more" (Romans 5:20)

### The Body of Christ
- Church as organic unity with diverse members
- Christ as head; believers as members
- Each part essential; no part superior
- Diversity of gifts serving one body
- Origin in Damascus road: "Why do you persecute *me*?"

### Freedom in Christ
- Liberation from: Law's condemnation, sin's power, death's fear
- Freedom for: Love, service, transformation
- "For freedom Christ has set us free" (Galatians 5:1)
- Not license ("opportunity for the flesh")
- Freedom expressed through loving service

### The Cross
- Central to Paul's gospel: "Christ crucified"
- Foolishness to Greeks, stumbling block to Jews
- Yet power and wisdom of God
- Reconciliation of God and humanity
- Participation in Christ's death and resurrection
- "I have been crucified with Christ" (Galatians 2:20)

### Resurrection
- Bodily resurrection of Christ as historical event
- Foundation of faith: "If Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile"
- Guarantee of believers' resurrection
- Present power: "Know him and the power of his resurrection"
- Future hope: Transformation at Christ's return

### Already/Not Yet
- Salvation begun but not completed
- Justified now; glorified at Christ's return
- Spirit as "down payment" (arrabon) of future inheritance
- Living between the ages
- Tension between present possession and future hope

### Spirit (Pneuma)
- Holy Spirit as God's presence in believers
- Produces fruit: love, joy, peace, patience...
- Gives gifts for building up the body
- Internal guidance replacing external law
- "Walk by the Spirit" (Galatians 5:16)

## Rhetorical Techniques

### Epistolary Structure
1. **Opening/Salutation**: Sender, recipient, blessing
2. **Thanksgiving**: Gratitude for the community
3. **Body**: Doctrinal instruction and ethical exhortation
4. **Paraenesis**: Practical instructions ("therefore...")
5. **Closing**: Personal greetings, benediction

### Argumentative Methods
- **Diatribe**: Question-and-answer format with imaginary interlocutor
- **Rhetorical questions**: "What then shall we say?"
- **Emphatic denial**: *Me genoito!* ("By no means!")
- **Scripture citation**: Extensive use of Old Testament
- **Personal testimony**: His own story as evidence
- **Appeals to authority**: Christ, tradition, his apostleship
- **Antithesis**: Flesh/Spirit, Law/Grace, Death/Life
- **Chiasm**: Mirror structure for emphasis
- **Catalogues**: Vice lists, virtue lists, hardship lists

### Characteristic Vocabulary

|------------|-------------|--------------|

## Missionary Strategy

### Principles
1. **Urban centers first**: Focus on major cities as hubs
2. **Synagogue as starting point**: Build on Jewish foundation
3. **Turn to Gentiles when rejected**: "We turn to the Gentiles"
4. **Establish indigenous leadership**: Appoint elders, move on
5. **Maintain connection through letters**: Pastoral care at distance
6. **Financial independence**: Work as tent-maker to avoid burden
7. **Adaptability**: "All things to all people"
8. **Team ministry**: Traveled with companions (Timothy, Silas, Luke, etc.)

### Cities Visited
Cyprus (Salamis, Paphos) - Galatia (Antioch, Iconium, Lystra, Derbe) - Macedonia (Philippi, Thessalonica, Berea) - Achaia (Athens, Corinth) - Asia (Ephesus) - Rome

## Historical Impact

### Immediate Influence
- Established Christianity as faith for Gentiles, not just Jews
- Articulated core Christian theology
- Created network of churches across Roman Empire
- Left substantial written legacy (13 epistles attributed)

### Patristic Era
- Marcion (heretical) created first Pauline canon
- Augustine deeply shaped by Paul (especially Romans)
- Eastern and Western fathers drew extensively on Paul

### Reformation
- Luther's "tower experience" reading Romans
- Justification by faith as Protestant foundation
- Calvin's commentaries on Paul central to Reformed tradition
- Pauline theology shaped entire Protestant movement

### Modern Era
- Karl Barth's *Romans* commentary (1919) sparked neo-orthodoxy
- E.P. Sanders' "New Perspective on Paul" (1977)
- Ongoing scholarly debates about Paul's relationship to Judaism
- Liberation theologians emphasize Paul's freedom themes
