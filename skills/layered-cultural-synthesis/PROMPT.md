# Layered Cultural Synthesis

Pile up references from incompatible traditions—high and low, sacred and profane, ancient and modern—until the origin is lost and something genuinely new emerges from the collision.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- When creating content that needs depth without pretension
- When a subject feels flat or one-dimensional
- When working across genres, disciplines, or cultural registers
- When originality matters more than purity of form

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Gather raw material from at least three unrelated traditions (e.g., biblical imagery, blues vernacular, symbolist poetry, tabloid headlines)

### Step 2: Step 2

Smash them together without signaling which is 'high' and which is 'low'—treat all sources as equal

### Step 3: Step 3

Let the collisions create meaning you didn't plan—the gaps between registers generate energy

### Step 4: Step 4

Remove attribution and smooth the seams so the synthesis feels inevitable rather than assembled

### Step 5: Test the result

if you can trace it back to a single source, it's not mixed enough
