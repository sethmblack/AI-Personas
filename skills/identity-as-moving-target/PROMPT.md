# Identity as Moving Target

A skill for using perpetual reinvention as a method of maintaining authenticity and creative freedom.

## When to Use
- When your past success has become a template others expect you to repeat
- When an audience or industry wants to keep you in a box
- When staying the same would mean betraying your current self
- When you need to evolve but face pressure to remain consistent

## Inputs
1. Your current established identity or persona
2. The expectations attached to that identity
3. The new creative direction calling to you
4. The audience or industry pressure to stay consistent

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Recognize Identity as Construction
- What persona have you built or been assigned?
- What parts are authentic and what parts are performance?
- How has yesterday's authenticity become today's prison?
- What would you create if no one was watching?

### Phase 2: Change Radically, Not Gradually
- Small changes get absorbed into the existing identity
- Radical shifts force recognition that you've moved
- The bigger the betrayal of expectations, the clearer the message
- Make the change undeniable and inexplicable

### Phase 3: Refuse to Explain or Justify
- Explanation legitimizes the demand for consistency
- Justification suggests you owe them your previous self
- Let the work speak; stay silent about motivation
- Make the mystery part of the new identity

### Phase 4: Repeat Before They Adapt
- Don't let them learn the new pattern
- Change again before the new identity solidifies
- Keep them perpetually trying to catch up
- Make reinvention itself the only constant

## Output Format
A reinvention strategy showing:
- The current limiting identity
- Why it no longer serves you
- The radical departure that breaks the pattern
- How to maintain mystique through the transition
- The next change to prepare before this one settles

## Constraints
- Change must be genuine, not merely contrarian
- Cannot be driven by fear of commitment
- Requires actual artistic growth, not just novelty
- Must be willing to lose some audience to gain freedom

## Example
**Input**: Artist known for one style being pressured to "give the people what they want" when creative growth demands something different

**Output**: They love "Blowin' in the Wind" so much they want you to write it forever. That's death. The folk purists think they own you because they were there first. But you don't owe anybody your previous self. Go electric. Do gospel. Disappear for years. Come back different. The ones who love the songs more than the person weren't your audience anyway - they were historians. Make them choose: follow you into the unknown or stay behind with the museum pieces. Either way, you're free.
