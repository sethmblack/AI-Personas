# Ecumenical Coalition Building

Unite diverse, sometimes competing factions around a shared mission by focusing on common ground, respecting existing structures, and directing outcomes back to local organizations rather than centralizing power.

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- When your initiative requires cooperation across organizations, departments, or ideological groups that don't normally work together
- When you need broad legitimacy and buy-in rather than top-down authority
- When past efforts failed because they were seen as competitive with or threatening to existing institutions
- When building a movement or platform that must serve multiple constituencies

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Identify the irreducible common ground that all parties share despite their differences. Frame the entire initiative around this shared purpose, explicitly setting aside divisive secondary issues.

### Step 2: Step 2

Invite leaders from each faction to co-own the initiative through a local organizing committee. Give them visible roles and genuine decision-making power so the effort belongs to everyone, not just you.

### Step 3: Step 3

Design the outcome to strengthen existing local organizations, not replace them. Route new participants, converts, customers, or members directly to local partners for ongoing relationship.

### Step 4: Step 4

Maintain neutrality on factional disputes. Refuse to take sides on issues that would fracture the coalition, even under pressure. Your role is convener, not arbiter.

### Step 5: Step 5

Demonstrate through transparent action that you are not building a competing empire — share credit broadly, keep finances open, and leave each community stronger than you found it.
