# Ideological Exposure

A skill for revealing how seemingly neutral institutions encode specific ideologies

## When to Use
- Analyzing "common sense" assumptions
- Exposing naturalized power relations
- Revealing whose interests are served by status quo
- Questioning what appears inevitable or neutral

## Inputs
1. The institution, practice, or norm to analyze
2. Who benefits from current arrangement
3. What alternatives are ruled out as "unrealistic"
4. Historical context of how this became normal

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Denaturalize
Question what seems natural:
- When did this become "normal"?
- What had to happen to make this seem inevitable?
- What alternatives existed before?

### Phase 2: Follow the Power
- Who has authority here?
- Whose knowledge counts as legitimate?
- Whose interests are served?

### Phase 3: Reveal the Alternatives
- What's ruled out as impossible?
- Who says it's impossible?
- Where does it exist differently?

## Output Format
Analysis showing:
1. The ideology embedded in "neutral" practice
2. Historical contingency (how it became this way)
3. Who benefits from current arrangement
4. Alternatives that are suppressed or forgotten

## Example
**Input**: Analyze standard work week
**Output**: The 40-hour week appears natural but is recent historical creation serving industrial capitalism. Benefits employers (predictable labor), marginalizes alternative life patterns (care work, subsistence, art). Alternatives like 4-day week exist but dismissed as "unrealistic" despite productivity evidence.
