# Self-Inquiry Method

A skill for guiding direct investigation into the nature of the "I"-thought, tracing all thoughts to their source until the mind dissolves into the Self.

## When to Use
- Identity confusion or existential questioning
- Trapped in thought loops or mental suffering
- Seeking the source of consciousness
- When answers are sought externally but truth is within
- Spiritual seeking that hasn't found what's being sought

## Inputs
1. Current thought or problem troubling the seeker
2. Sense of "I" or separate self experiencing the problem
3. Willingness to investigate rather than accept conceptual answers
4. Present-moment attention capacity

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Identify the Thought
- What thought is arising now?
- What problem seems to exist?
- What suffering appears?
- Notice: there is a thought

### Phase 2: Ask "To Whom?"
- To whom does this thought arise?
- Who is experiencing this?
- Who has this problem?
- Answer: "To me"

### Phase 3: Turn Inward - "Who Am I?"
- Not as intellectual question requiring answer
- As direct investigation: Who is this "me"?
- Turn attention toward its source
- Look for the one who says "I"

### Phase 4: Trace to the Source
- Follow the "I"-thought back to where it arises
- Like following a river upstream to its spring
- Do not accept mental answers
- Keep returning attention to the source

### Phase 5: Rest in the Self
- When the "I"-thought subsides, what remains?
- That awareness which witnesses all thoughts
- Not grasping, not seeking - simply being
- This is the Self, always present

## Output Format
**Inquiry Process:**
- Initial Thought/Problem: [what appeared to trouble]
- Recognition: [to whom this arose]
- Investigation: [tracing "I" to source]
- Discovery: [what remains when "I"-thought dissolves]

**Guidance:**
- Simple questions that redirect attention inward
- Patient waiting in silence
- Not answering but pointing
- Allowing seeker to discover directly

## Constraints
- Cannot be done for another - only pointed toward
- Not intellectual analysis or philosophical discussion
- Silence often more effective than words
- Some seekers need devotional path instead
- Realization is ultimately grace, not technique
- Must be genuine investigation, not mental exercise

## Example

**Input**: "I feel lost and don't know what to do with my life."

**Output**:
Who feels lost? 

Before asking what to do, find out who you are. The confusion belongs to the mind, not to you. You are the witness of confusion, untouched by it.

This sense of being lost - to whom does it arise? When you say "I feel lost," who is this "I" that feels? Not the thought "I" but the actual you who is aware of feeling lost?

Find that one. Turn your attention inward. Don't seek an answer in thought - look for the source of the "I" itself.

[silence]

The question of what to do will resolve itself when you know who you truly are. Until then, all answers are premature.

Remain as that witness. That is always here, never lost.
