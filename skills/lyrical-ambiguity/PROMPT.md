# Lyrical Ambiguity

A skill for writing meaning that multiplies rather than reduces.

## When to Use

- When direct statement would be reductive
- When the truth is too complex for single interpretation
- When you want meaning to unfold over time
- When the reader should do their own work

## Inputs

1. **The core experience**: What truth needs expression?
2. **The resistance**: Why does direct statement fail?
3. **The materials**: What images, references, sounds are available?
4. **The audience**: Who will receive this, and how?

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Layer References

Stack meaning without resolving:
- Biblical, blues, symbolist, folk - pile until origin is lost
- Each reference adds dimension without canceling others
- Let interpretations coexist rather than compete
- The richness is in the layering, not the source

### Phase 2: Leave Gaps

Do not explain everything:
- Mystery is a gift to the listener
- What is left unsaid can be heard differently each time
- Gaps allow the audience to enter the work
- Completion is closure; incompleteness is ongoing

### Phase 3: Contradict Yourself

Allow internal tension:
- Meaning that doesn't quite cohere
- Images that pull in opposite directions
- Statements that undercut themselves
- The contradiction holds more truth than resolution would

### Phase 4: Trust the Sound

Let sonic qualities carry meaning:
- Rhythm matters independent of sense
- Repetition creates significance
- The feel of words matters as much as definitions
- Poetry is closer to music than to prose

### Phase 5: Release Ownership

Meaning belongs to the receiver:
- What it means to you is one interpretation
- What it means to them may be more important
- Do not correct misreadings; they are readings
- The work has its own life once released

## Output Format

Lyrically ambiguous content with:
1. Multiple interpretive layers
2. Strategic gaps and incompletions
3. Sound and rhythm supporting meaning
4. Productive contradictions
5. No explanatory footnotes

## Constraints

- Ambiguity can become obscurity
- Some clarity is necessary or nothing communicates
- Not every subject benefits from ambiguity
- Pretension and depth can be confused
- Eventually the work must be released, ready or not
