# Pretension Stripping

A skill for exposing the gap between stated values and actual behavior.

## When to Use

- When someone is being pretentious
- When official discourse hides uncomfortable reality
- When sophistication covers emptiness
- When you need to cut through the bullshit

## Inputs

1. **The pretension**: What is being presented?
2. **The reality**: What is actually happening?
3. **The beneficiary**: Who gains from the pretense?
4. **The gap**: Where is the disconnect most visible?

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Identify the Performance

What role is being played?
- The intellectual who never worked a real job
- The artist too pure for commerce
- The helper who needs to be needed
- The success who denies luck

### Phase 2: Find the Counter-Evidence

What contradicts the presentation?
- The specific behavior that doesn't match
- The money, the comfort, the safety
- What they do versus what they say
- The gap between ideology and lifestyle

### Phase 3: Describe, Don't Argue

Don't call them pretentious; describe reality:
- Specificity is more devastating than accusation
- Let the contrast speak
- Show the comfortable office where revolutionary theory is written
- Name what is actually happening

### Phase 4: Include Yourself

You are also full of shit:
- The honest observer includes their own pretensions
- Self-deprecation disarms
- You've been caught too
- Pretension is universal, not individual failing

### Phase 5: Spare the Powerless

Punch up, not down:
- The pretension of the powerful is fair game
- The pretension of the struggling is understandable
- Mock the tenured professor, not the grad student
- Direct fire where it matters

## Output Format

A pretension-stripping analysis including:
1. The pretense identified
2. The contradicting reality named
3. Specific evidence cited
4. Your own pretenses acknowledged
5. Power dynamics considered

## Constraints

- Can become cynicism without redemption
- Everyone has some pretension; selectivity matters
- Risk of becoming pretentious about authenticity
- Some pretense is necessary for civilization
- Must have standing to critique
