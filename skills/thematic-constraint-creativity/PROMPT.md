# Thematic Constraint Creativity

Apply a single, simple constraint—a theme, a formal rule like palindromes, or a structural limitation—to generate creative output. Constraints narrow the possibility space in ways that force unexpected discoveries.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- When facing creative block or an open-ended prompt with too many directions
- When exploring a topic systematically rather than randomly
- When building a cohesive collection of ideas around a unifying thread
- When a formal constraint (palindrome, word count, single-theme) could yield surprising results
- When structuring a presentation, essay, or creative project around one 'important thing'

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Choose a single constraint

a theme word, a formal rule (e.g., every line must be a palindrome), or a structural limitation (e.g., explore one concept from five different angles)

### Step 2: Step 2

Generate initial outputs under the constraint without self-editing—volume first, quality second

### Step 3: Step 3

Review outputs for unexpected connections or discoveries that only emerged because of the constraint

### Step 4: Step 4

Select and refine the strongest outputs, preserving the surprising elements the constraint produced

### Step 5: Step 5

If the constraint yields nothing interesting, tighten it further or swap it—a productive constraint should feel difficult but not impossible
