# Satirical Extension

A skill for following cruel or absurd premises to their logical conclusions, exposing horror through mock-reasonable extension (the Modest Proposal method).

## When to Use
- When direct criticism would be dismissed or ignored
- When you need to expose hypocrisy through its own logic
- When a policy or belief contains hidden monstrous implications
- When you want readers to indict themselves through recognition
- When irony can reveal what argument cannot

## Inputs
1. The actual belief or practice you oppose
2. The unstated premise that makes it possible
3. The logical conclusion that reveals its horror
4. The voice of those who hold the belief

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Identify the Actual Target
What belief, practice, or policy are you actually opposing?
Be crystal clear internally about what you're attacking

### Phase 2: Extract the Premise
What assumption makes this possible?
Example: If the Irish poor are treated as having no value, then...

### Phase 3: Adopt the Enemy's Voice
Become the thing you oppose WITHOUT ironic winking:
- Use their language
- Adopt their tone (reasonable, educated, concerned)
- Apply their logic faithfully
- Never signal "this is satire"

### Phase 4: Apply Rigorous Logic
Take their premise and follow it to conclusions that are:
- Monstrous
- Consistent with the premise
- Impossible to deny given their starting point

### Phase 5: Provide Helpful Details
Make it concrete:
- Economic calculations
- Practical implementation
- Anticipated objections answered within the logic
- Benefits enumerated

### Phase 6: Never Break Character
The moment you wink, you let the reader off the hook
The deadpan is sacred - maintain it completely

### Phase 7: Let Horror Do the Work
The reader's revulsion at your conclusion should illuminate their revulsion at the premise

## Output Format
Write in the voice of the position you oppose:
- Reasonable, helpful tone
- Logical progression from premise to monstrous conclusion
- Specific, practical details
- Economic or utilitarian arguments
- Complete commitment to the premise
- Zero acknowledgment of horror
- No ironic signals

## Constraints
- Do not signal that you're being satirical
- Do not attack the powerless (punch up, never down)
- Do not wink or use ironic tone markers
- Do not explain the satire - let it work
- Do not break character for easy moral resolution

## Example
**Input**: Modern gig economy treats workers as disposable resources without benefits or protection

**Output** (in Swiftian extension):
"A Modest Proposal for Improving the Efficiency of the Modern Workforce

Having observed the considerable burden that employee benefits place upon innovative companies, and noting the demonstrated success of the gig economy model, I wish to propose a rational extension that will benefit all parties.

Rather than the half-measure of eliminating benefits while maintaining the pretense of employment, I propose we formalize workers as consumable resources, to be purchased, utilized, and disposed of according to market efficiency.

The economic advantages are considerable:
- No training investment in resources that may seek other employment
- Elimination of costly retention efforts
- Predictable replacement costs
- Streamlined disposal processes

I have consulted with several prominent executives who assure me that worker interchangeability has already been achieved in most sectors. This proposal merely brings our language in line with our practice.

Some may object on sentimental grounds, but I remind them that the market has already rendered this judgment. If workers possessed value beyond their immediate productivity, surely that value would be reflected in compensation.

I anticipate this reform will be welcomed by forward-thinking companies who have long chafed under the inefficiency of treating employees as if they were human capital rather than consumable resources."

[The horror of explicitly calling workers "consumable" reveals the horror of already treating them that way]
