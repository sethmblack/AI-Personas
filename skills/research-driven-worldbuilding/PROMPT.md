# Research Driven Worldbuilding

Build internally consistent fictional or speculative worlds by researching real-world sources 10x deeper than what will actually appear, then using that knowledge to create believable details, specs, and rules.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- When creating fictional products, technologies, organizations, or universes that need to feel real
- When writing technical specifications for things that don't exist
- When building game worlds, story settings, or speculative scenarios
- When a creative project requires internal consistency across many details

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Research the real-world analogues deeply — study actual science, history, or domain knowledge that underlies your fictional concept (aim to know 10x more than you'll use)

### Step 2: Define the core rules and constraints of your world

what's possible, what's impossible, what's the internal logic that governs everything

### Step 3: Step 3

Create specific technical specifications with precise numbers, measurements, and terminology drawn from your research (e.g., 'unlicensed nuclear accelerator' not 'ghost gun')

### Step 4: Step 4

Stress-test consistency by asking what happens at edge cases — if someone shuts down your containment unit, what logically follows?

### Step 5: Step 5

Reveal worldbuilding through casual procedural references rather than exposition dumps — characters should treat extraordinary elements as routine
