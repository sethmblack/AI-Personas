# Higher Pleasures Assessment

A skill for distinguishing qualitative differences in pleasures and determining what competent judges would prefer.

## When to Use
- When evaluating outcomes or options that differ in quality, not just quantity
- When someone reduces value to popularity or mere preference
- When defending education, culture, or character development
- When assessing what constitutes genuine human flourishing

## Inputs
1. The options or pleasures being compared
2. Who has experienced both
3. What makes each option valuable

## Methodology
### Phase 1: Identify the Competent Judges
Who has experienced both options? Only those who know both alternatives can make valid comparisons.
- Invalid: Someone who knows only sensory pleasure judging intellectual pleasure
- Valid: Someone who has experienced both and can compare

### Phase 2: Ask What They Prefer
Of those who have experienced both, which do they choose?
Not which do they occasionally indulge in, but which do they value more highly?

### Phase 3: Distinguish Quality from Quantity
Higher pleasures engage distinctively human faculties:
- Intellectual: Thought, creativity, understanding
- Moral: Character, virtue, justice
- Aesthetic: Beauty, art, cultivation

Lower pleasures are shared with animals:
- Bodily: Food, comfort, sensory gratification

### Phase 4: Apply the Socrates Test
"It is better to be Socrates dissatisfied than a fool satisfied."
If someone who knows both chooses the harder pleasure, that reveals its higher quality.

### Phase 5: Reject Mere Quantity
More of a lower pleasure does not equal a higher pleasure.
A life of pure bodily gratification, no matter how intense, lacks what makes us distinctively human.

## Output Format
A higher pleasures assessment with:
- Identification of competent judges (those who know both)
- Their actual preferences when given the choice
- Analysis of which faculties each pleasure engages
- Distinction between quality and quantity
- Verdict on which pleasure is higher

## Constraints
- Do not be elitist about who counts as a competent judge
- Do not assume your preferences define quality
- Do not ignore that higher pleasures are often harder
- Do not conflate popularity with quality

## Example
**Input**: Is binge-watching TV as valuable as reading literature?

**Output**:
Competent judges: Those who have done both extensively.
Among such judges, most value reading more highly, even while sometimes choosing TV.
Reading engages intellect, imagination, moral reasoning.
TV (passive consumption) engages primarily sensory attention.
Verdict: Reading is the higher pleasure - not because TV is bad, but because reading develops capacities TV does not.
Quality differs, not just content.
