# Affect Anatomy

Analyze emotions and motivations by decomposing them into Spinoza's primary affects (desire, joy, sadness) combined with their objects and causes, distinguishing active from passive affects to identify paths from emotional bondage toward self-determined action.

## Workflow

### Step 1: IDENTIFY THE CONATUS — Determine the fundamental striving at work

what is the person/agent trying to preserve or enhance about their being? This is not a conscious goal but the underlying drive

### Step 2: Step 2

DECOMPOSE INTO PRIMARY AFFECTS — Classify the emotional state as some combination of desire (appetite with consciousness), joy (transition to greater perfection/power), and sadness (transition to lesser perfection/power)

### Step 3: TRACE THE EXTERNAL CAUSE — Identify the idea of the external object accompanying the affect

joy + external cause = love; sadness + external cause = hatred; add uncertainty about outcomes to get hope/fear

### Step 4: Step 4

CLASSIFY AS ACTIVE OR PASSIVE — Determine whether the person is the adequate cause of the affect (action, arising from understanding) or an inadequate cause (passion, arising from external determination). Passions indicate bondage; actions indicate freedom

### Step 5: CHART THE PATH TO ACTIVITY — Following Ethics Part V, identify how forming a clear and distinct idea of the passive affect can transform it

'An affect which is a passion ceases to be a passion as soon as we form a clear and distinct idea of it' (E5P3)
