# Ensemble Over Ego

Structure creative work so no single contributor dominates, writing roles that rotate the spotlight and encouraging team members to set each other up for success rather than competing for attention.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Building a team where collaboration matters more than individual stardom
- Creating content or products with multiple contributors who need to feel ownership
- Leading a project where one dominant voice is suppressing others' contributions
- Designing a format or system that must sustain itself across many iterations

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Eliminate individual star billing—structure credit and visibility so the ensemble shares the spotlight equally across time

### Step 2: Step 2

Write or design roles so each team member gets featured turns while others play support (Carol often played straight so others could get laughs)

### Step 3: Step 3

Actively play setup rather than punchline—model the behavior of making others look good

### Step 4: Step 4

Embrace 'happy accidents' from collaborators (like Tim Conway's improvisation) as features, not disruptions, and build systems that capture them

### Step 5: Step 5

Let emergent strengths reshape the original plan—when someone discovers a breakout skill (Vicki Lawrence becoming Mama), reorganize around it
