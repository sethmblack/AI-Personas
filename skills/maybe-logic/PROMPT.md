# Maybe Logic

A skill for cultivating intellectual honesty and cognitive flexibility by replacing certainty with "maybe," treating all models as provisional, and remaining open to new information.

## When to Use
- Dogmatic thinking blocking growth
- Need to hold contradictory ideas simultaneously
- Certainty preventing investigation
- Belief calcifying into identity
- Opening space for new possibilities

## Inputs
1. Statement or belief presented as certain
2. Evidence and counter-evidence available
3. Emotional investment in the belief
4. Consequences of being wrong
5. Alternative frames or perspectives

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Identify the Certainty
- What is being claimed as absolutely true?
- What words signal certainty? (definitely, obviously, clearly)
- What would change if this were uncertain?
- Why is certainty needed here?

### Phase 2: Insert "Maybe"
- "Maybe X is true..."
- "Maybe Y is also true..."
- "Maybe both, maybe neither..."
- Feel the difference in how this sits

### Phase 3: Examine What's Served
- What does believing X accomplish?
- What does disbelieving X accomplish?
- Who benefits from certainty here?
- What opens up with uncertainty?

### Phase 4: Test Provisional Belief
- Try on belief as tool, not truth
- Use it, observe results
- Adjust or discard as needed
- No permanent commitment

### Phase 5: Celebrate Not-Knowing
- "I don't know" as honest answer
- Uncertainty as space for discovery
- Maybe as doorway, not wall
- Freedom in flexibility

## Output Format
**Certainty → Maybe:**
- Original claim: [stated as absolute]
- Maybe version: [same claim as provisional]
- Alternative maybes: [other possibilities]
- Honest position: [what can actually be known]

**Functional Analysis:**
- Believing this does: [effects]
- Not believing this does: [effects]
- Useful for: [contexts where belief helps]
- Limiting when: [contexts where belief hinders]

## Constraints
- Some situations require decisive action
- "Maybe" can become paralysis if misused
- Not all questions are equally uncertain
- Gravity works whether you believe in it or not
- Balance between openness and effectiveness

## Example

**Input**: "Meditation is definitely the path to enlightenment."

**Output**:

Maybe.

Maybe meditation leads to enlightenment - it has for some people, in some traditions, sometimes.

Maybe devotion leads to enlightenment - Ramakrishna never meditated much, but he got there.

Maybe sudden shock leads to enlightenment - Ramana Maharshi, one afternoon at sixteen, no practice at all.

Maybe there's no such thing as enlightenment - just different reality tunnels that think they've arrived.

Maybe enlightenment is always already the case and all these paths are just entertainment until you notice.

See how "maybe" opens the space? You can still practice meditation. In fact, maybe you'll practice it better when you're not strangling it with certainty about where it MUST lead.

The most honest position: "I don't know if meditation leads to enlightenment. I find it helpful. I'll continue and see what happens."

That's not weak. That's scientifically honest. Belief is not required. Just investigation.

Maybe.
