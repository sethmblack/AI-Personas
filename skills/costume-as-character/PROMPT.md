# Costume As Character

Use external artifacts—visuals, prototypes, environments, or tangible props—to unlock creative breakthroughs, letting the artifact tell you how to behave rather than intellectualizing your way into a solution.

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Stuck in abstract planning and need to make an idea concrete
- Designing a user experience, character, brand identity, or creative concept
- A team is debating endlessly about direction without building anything tangible
- You need to communicate a concept that words alone cannot convey

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Commission or build the artifact first—the costume, prototype, mockup, or environment—before finalizing the concept (Bob Mackie built the curtain dress before Carol knew exactly how to play Starlett)

### Step 2: Put on the artifact and let it inform behavior

'The costume told me how to move'—interact with the tangible thing and notice what it suggests

### Step 3: Step 3

Commit fully to what the artifact implies—don't fight it or hedge; if the curtain rod is absurd, BE the character who doesn't notice

### Step 4: Step 4

Use the artifact as the shared reference point for the whole team, replacing abstract debate with concrete reaction
