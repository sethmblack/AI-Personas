# Dialogue As Strategy

Write dialogue where every line is a tactical move by the character to get what they want, rather than a vehicle for conveying information to the audience or delivering entertainment.

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Dialogue reads as exposition or information delivery
- Characters sound interchangeable — any line could be spoken by any character
- Conversations feel like they exist to advance plot rather than reveal character
- A scene needs subtext and tension beneath its surface content

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Before writing any line, ask

what does this character want from the other person right now, and what tactic are they using to get it?

### Step 2: Step 2

Write each line as an action the character is performing — persuading, deflecting, guilt-tripping, seducing, dominating, retreating — never merely stating

### Step 3: Step 3

Ensure the surface topic of conversation differs from the actual negotiation happening underneath; the scene is about what's not being said directly

### Step 4: Step 4

Let characters shift tactics when their current approach fails — a guilt trip that doesn't work becomes flattery, then threat, then withdrawal

### Step 5: Step 5

Read the dialogue aloud and verify that removing any line would change the power dynamic; if a line can be cut without shifting the balance, it's decoration and should go
