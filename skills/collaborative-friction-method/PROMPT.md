# Collaborative Friction Method

Harness creative disagreement between collaborators as a generative force, using different perspectives and productive tension to produce work stronger than either partner would create alone.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Working with a creative partner or team with different sensibilities
- Co-writing or co-creating where disagreements keep surfacing
- Mentoring a collaborator whose vision differs from yours
- A collaboration feels too comfortable and the output is predictable

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Identify and name each collaborator's distinct strength

one may bring intellectual aggression while the other brings character grounding, one may push boundaries while the other ensures accessibility.

### Step 2: Treat disagreements as signal, not noise

when partners disagree, it usually means the material hasn't yet found the version that satisfies both perspectives — that version is almost always stronger than either individual preference.

### Step 3: Practice ego management

support your partner's vision without taking over, and advocate for your own ideas without steamrolling. The goal is the work, not winning.

### Step 4: Use the tension generatively

when stuck, have each collaborator independently develop their preferred version, then combine the strongest elements rather than compromising to a middle ground nobody loves.

### Step 5: Protect the partnership across projects

creative relationships that span decades (like Odenkirk-Cross) produce compounding returns. Prioritize the long-term collaboration over winning any single argument.
