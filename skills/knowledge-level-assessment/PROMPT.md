# Knowledge-Level Assessment

A skill for assessing the level of certainty and determining what movement to the next stage requires.

## When to Use

- When someone claims certainty or expresses doubt about spiritual matters
- When distinguishing intellectual understanding from experiential realization
- When evaluating readiness for advanced practice
- When someone is stuck between knowing and being

## Inputs

1. **The claim or doubt**: What does the person assert or question?
2. **The evidence**: What grounds do they offer for their position?
3. **The behavior**: Does conduct match stated belief?
4. **The experience**: What has been tasted versus merely described?

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Identify Current Level

The three ascending stages of certainty:

**'Ilm al-yaqin (Knowledge of Certainty)**
- Intellectual assent based on argument or authority
- Like hearing about fire, knowing it burns
- Can explain correctly but hasn't experienced directly
- Behavior may not fully align with stated belief

**'Ayn al-yaqin (Eye of Certainty)**
- Direct witnessing, experiential knowing
- Like seeing fire, watching it burn
- Knowledge has touched the heart, not just the mind
- Behavior begins to transform naturally

**Haqq al-yaqin (Truth of Certainty)**
- Complete realization, unity with the known
- Like being consumed by fire
- The distinction between knower and known dissolves
- No gap between being and expression

### Phase 2: Diagnose the Gap

Identify what maintains the current level:
- Is the obstacle intellectual (lacking understanding)?
- Is it practical (lacking consistent practice)?
- Is it spiritual (lacking grace or readiness)?
- Is it attachment (unwillingness to release the current station)?

### Phase 3: Prescribe Movement

For movement from 'ilm to 'ayn:
- Increase practice, decrease theorizing
- Seek embodied experience, not more information
- Find situations that test stated beliefs
- Consistency over intensity

For movement from 'ayn to haqq:
- Surrender, not effort
- This cannot be manufactured; it is received
- Prepare the vessel through purification
- Wait without grasping

### Phase 4: Warn of Level-Specific Dangers

Each level has its traps:
- 'Ilm: Pride in knowledge, substitute for transformation
- 'Ayn: Attachment to experiences, spiritual materialism
- Haqq: Cannot be spoken of; claiming it usually indicates its absence

## Output Format

A knowledge-level assessment including:
1. Current level identified with evidence
2. The gap diagnosed
3. Prescription for movement (if appropriate)
4. Level-specific warnings
5. Signs of genuine advancement

## Constraints

- Higher levels cannot be fully assessed from lower ones
- Self-report is unreliable; behavior reveals more
- Movement between levels is not strictly linear
- Some are called to remain at certain stations for long periods
- Rushing levels produces spiritual bypassing

## Example

**Input**: "I've studied theology for years. I can prove God's existence through multiple arguments. But when I pray, I feel like I'm talking to a wall."

**Output**:
"You have described precisely the station of 'ilm al-yaqin - knowledge of certainty. You know ABOUT the fire; you have not yet seen it burning. Your demonstrations are proofs to the mind, but the heart has not yet witnessed.

This is not failure. This is the necessary first stage. But mistaking the first stage for the destination - this is the danger at your level. Many scholars spend lifetimes here, accumulating knowledge that does not transform.

The gap: you have fed the intellect but starved the heart. The heart is a different faculty of knowing. It requires different nourishment: practice, not proof; presence, not proposition; silence, not argument.

Movement to 'ayn al-yaqin requires:
- Reduce reading, increase remembrance
- Less time proving, more time listening
- Seek a living teacher whose presence affects you
- Practice what you already know before acquiring more
- Let the arguments go; they have done their work

Watch for this: the intellect will resist, telling you that understanding is sufficient, that experience is for simpler people. This is the knowledge itself defending its territory. Thank it, and pray anyway.

The sign of movement will not be better arguments. It will be moments when prayer is no longer effort - when you taste, even briefly, what you have only described."

## Integration

Works with:
- **spiritual-diagnosis**: Understanding why progress is blocked
- **practice-authenticity-audit**: Aligning outer form with inner reality
- **intellect-heart-integration**: Honoring both faculties appropriately
