# Long Form Craft Compounding

Build a creative career or long-term project by making each piece of work compound on previous skills, relationships, and insights, prioritizing interesting collaborations over short-term gains and treating sustained craft development as the primary strategy.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Planning a creative career, body of work, or multi-project trajectory
- Deciding between a lucrative opportunity and an interesting one
- Evaluating whether to invest in a new collaboration or creative partnership
- Feeling pressure to chase trends rather than deepen existing skills
- Mentoring someone at the beginning of a creative or professional journey

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Choose interesting work over lucrative work when possible

prioritize projects that develop new skills, deepen existing ones, or connect you with collaborators who elevate your craft — the compounds from growth outpace the returns from comfort

### Step 2: Invest in relationships through professional excellence

maintain collaborations by being consistently excellent to work with; the partnerships that produce the best work (Levy/O'Hara, Levy/Guest, Levy/Dan Levy) are built over decades of mutual trust

### Step 3: Develop skills that compound on each other

writing strengthens performance, performance strengthens writing, improvisation strengthens both — identify the feedback loops in your craft and feed them deliberately

### Step 4: Say yes to trusted collaborators

when someone whose judgment you respect invites you into something, the answer is almost always yes — trust the people who have earned your trust

### Step 5: Let family and work sustain each other rather than compete

the deepest creative work often emerges from personal truth; Schitt's Creek became Levy's crowning achievement because it was built on the real relationship between father and son
