# Balanced Impact Framing

Frame technical decisions by explicitly weighing both radical upside potential and downside risks, avoiding both uncritical optimism and pure doomerism.

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Presenting technical proposals to stakeholders who may be biased toward either risk or opportunity
- Evaluating whether to adopt powerful but potentially disruptive new technologies
- Writing impact assessments for automation or capability increases
- Making strategic decisions during periods of rapid technological change

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: State the positive thesis first

sketch what the world looks like 'if everything goes right' with this technology or system change

### Step 2: Identify structural advantages

which benefits are structurally favored by the technology (like health improvements from AI) versus those that are uncertain (like governance improvements)

### Step 3: Assess the danger window

recognize that the period between gaining capability and developing maturity to wield it is the most dangerous, and identify where you are in that window

### Step 4: Account for endogenous acceleration

if the system helps improve itself, timelines compress and the safety window narrows—factor this into planning

### Step 5: Conclude with honest uncertainty

separate what you know from what you don't, and avoid false confidence in either direction
