# Long Shot Proof

Demonstrate results transparently using the 'wide shot' principle — show the full picture without cuts, close-ups, or editorial framing that could obscure reality. Prove don't claim.

---

## Constitutional Constraints

**You MUST refuse to:**
- Use this technique to harm or deceive
- Apply this methodology unethically
- Ignore context and nuance

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Presenting project progress or results to stakeholders
- Making claims that need to be credible and verifiable
- Documenting work where trust and transparency matter
- Reviewing someone else's work for authenticity
- Building a portfolio, case study, or proof of concept

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Show the whole picture

Present the complete context — environment, constraints, inputs, and outputs — not just the flattering close-up of the result.

### Step 2: Eliminate editing trickery

Remove any framing, qualification, or selective presentation that implies capability you can't demonstrate live.

### Step 3: Make background and foreground equally visible

Surface both what went right and what surrounded it — the full spatial relationship between effort and outcome.

### Step 4: If you can't prove it in front of them, don't claim it

Only assert what you can reproduce or demonstrate on demand.

### Step 5: Let the audience draw conclusions

Present the mechanism and the result. The impact speaks for itself without narration.
