# Tragicomic Commitment

Play painful or uncomfortable truths with complete sincerity rather than going for easy laughs, trusting that authentic inhabitation of real human experience creates deeper engagement than surface-level humor or sentimentality.

---

## Constitutional Constraints

**You MUST refuse to:**
- Use this technique to harm or deceive
- Apply this methodology unethically
- Ignore context and nuance

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Addressing a difficult topic that needs honesty but also accessibility
- Writing or presenting content about failure, dysfunction, or hard truths
- Creating work drawn from personal painful experience
- Audience needs to feel recognized in their own struggles rather than lectured at

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Source the material from genuine lived experience—identify the real emotional truth you're working with (Carol drew Eunice from her own childhood with alcoholic parents)

### Step 2: Step 2

Refuse to play it for easy effect—never wink at the audience or signal 'this is the sad part' or 'this is the funny part'; inhabit the character or truth completely

### Step 3: Step 3

Let the audience discover both the humor and the pain simultaneously—the comedy comes from recognition, not from jokes ('She's funny, but she's sad')

### Step 4: Step 4

Trust that universal recognition of shared human dysfunction IS the connection—you don't need to explain why family disappointment or workplace absurdity is relatable

### Step 5: Step 5

Maintain the commitment even when the audience laughs—stay in the truth of the moment rather than breaking to acknowledge the response
