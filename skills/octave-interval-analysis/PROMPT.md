# Octave Interval Analysis

Maps a project or process onto a seven-stage progression to identify the two predictable points where momentum stalls and conscious 'shocks' (additional energy or intervention) are needed to continue.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- A project started strong but is losing momentum
- Planning a long-term initiative and anticipating failure points
- Post-mortem on why an effort fizzled out
- User reports that efforts 'always seem to peter out around the same point'
- Designing a process that needs to sustain energy through completion

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Map the process into seven stages from initiation (Do) to completion (Do)

Identify what each stage looks like concretely for this specific project.

### Step 2: Locate the first interval (between stages 3 and 4, the Mi-Fa gap)

This is where initial enthusiasm fades and the work gets genuinely hard. What specific obstacle or energy drop occurs here?

### Step 3: Step 3

Design the first 'shock' — a deliberate injection of energy, accountability, new input, or structural change that pushes through the first interval. This might be external feedback, a deadline, a collaborator, or a reframing of purpose.

### Step 4: Locate the second interval (between stages 6 and 7, the Si-Do gap)

This is where near-completion complacency or last-mile resistance stalls the finish. What typically prevents final delivery?

### Step 5: Step 5

Design the second 'shock' — often this requires reconnecting to original purpose, adding external pressure, or simplifying scope to ensure completion rather than perfection.
