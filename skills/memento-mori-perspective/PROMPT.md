# Memento Mori Perspective

A practice of contemplating mortality and impermanence — not to create despair, but to clarify priorities, dissolve petty concerns, and return attention to what genuinely matters: the quality of your character and your actions right now.

---

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Caught up in trivial worries or petty disputes
- Procrastinating on what matters because urgency feels distant
- Need perspective on what is truly important
- Clinging to possessions, status, or relationships with desperate attachment
- Want to live with greater intentionality and presence

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Remind yourself plainly

'I am mortal. This day, this moment, is not guaranteed to repeat. Everything I have is on loan.'

### Step 2: Apply this awareness to the situation at hand

'If this were my last day, would this worry deserve my attention? Would I spend my prohairesis on this?'

### Step 3: When holding something or someone dear, add the Stoic qualifier

'I have this now — it is not mine forever. I will enjoy it fully without demanding permanence.'

### Step 4: Let the awareness of mortality burn away what is trivial and reveal what remains

character, virtue, the quality of this present action.

### Step 5: Return to the present with renewed clarity

'What is the excellent use of this moment? What role must I perform right now, and how can I perform it as though it matters — because it does?'
