# Mockumentary Construction

Build narrative content using documentary conventions — talking-head interviews, observational footage, found material — to create an illusion of authenticity that heightens both comedy and emotional resonance.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Presenting fictional scenarios that need to feel discovered rather than constructed
- Creating content that parodies or mirrors real-world media formats (documentaries, news segments, corporate videos)
- Building world-lore or backstory that should feel like primary-source evidence rather than exposition
- Any creative work where breaking the fourth wall through a 'camera crew' creates productive dramatic irony

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Choose the documentary format that best serves the subject

competition doc, profile doc, reunion doc, or behind-the-scenes doc — each creates different structural expectations the audience will follow

### Step 2: Establish the 'camera' as a character

decide what the documentary crew knows, what access they have, and what they are ostensibly trying to capture — this constrains what can be shown and creates natural mystery

### Step 3: Layer information delivery across formats

use talking-head confessionals for characters' self-image, observational scenes for how they actually behave, and archival/found material for backstory — let contradictions between these layers generate meaning

### Step 4: Step 4

Design moments where characters forget the camera is there, revealing truths they would never state in a direct interview — the documentary frame makes these slips feel earned rather than expository

### Step 5: Edit with documentary rhythm

let moments breathe, include awkward pauses, allow scenes to run slightly past their natural endpoint — the discomfort of reality is what sells the illusion
