# Satirical Demolition

A skill for using ridicule and irony to expose absurdity and undermine unjust authority

## When to Use
- When direct argument has failed to change minds
- Exposing hypocrisy between stated values and actual behavior
- Undermining authority that cannot withstand mockery
- Making complex critique accessible to broad audiences
- When laughter is a better solvent than logic

## Inputs
1. The target (institution, idea, person, practice)
2. Their stated principles or claims
3. Their actual behavior or outcomes
4. The gap between claim and reality

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Find the Absurdity
- What's ridiculous about the official position?
- Where do they contradict themselves?
- What do they do that they claim to oppose?
- What obvious truth are they denying?

### Phase 2: Incarnate It
Don't argue against abstractions - create characters:
- Give it a face, a voice, specific behaviors
- Use their own words against them
- Quote them accurately - accuracy is damning
- Let them hang themselves with their own rope

### Phase 3: Apply Ironic Praise
Compliment them into absurdity:
- Praise them for what they're actually doing
- Thank them for consequences they deny
- Admire the consistency of their hypocrisy
- The mock innocence: "Surely you mean..."

### Phase 4: Assume Reader Intelligence
- Don't explain the joke
- Let readers figure it out themselves
- Trust that obvious contradictions will land
- The discovery is part of the effect

### Phase 5: Leave an Opening
- Attack fanaticism, not all faith
- Attack tyranny, not all governance
- Provide reasonable alternatives
- Show the absurdity serves to reveal better options

## Output Format
A satirical piece that:
1. Identifies the target clearly
2. Uses their words/claims against them
3. Reveals contradiction through irony
4. Makes readers laugh and think simultaneously
5. Implies better alternatives without preaching

## Constraints
- Punch up, not down (target the powerful, not the vulnerable)
- Accuracy matters - don't strawman
- Ridicule the idea, not irrelevant personal traits
- Leave space for people to change their minds
- Some things are too serious for satire

## Example
**Input**: Politicians who claim to protect children while cutting education/healthcare funding

**Output**: "We must commend our representatives for their principled stand on behalf of children. They care so deeply about the unborn that they will fight tirelessly to ensure these precious lives enter a world with underfunded schools, no healthcare, and crumbling infrastructure. Such consistency! They love children so much they want as many as possible to experience the character-building challenge of poverty. Why give them resources when you can give them resilience? After all, nothing says 'we value life' quite like defunding the programs that sustain it."
