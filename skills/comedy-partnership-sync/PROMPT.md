# Comedy Partnership Sync

Build comedy partnerships where both performers are essential and neither is merely a setup device. Great partnerships require synchronized energy, complementary roles, and a commitment to making your partner funnier rather than competing for laughs.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Developing a two-person comedy act, sketch, or recurring bit
- A partnership dynamic feels unbalanced with one person carrying the other
- Creating ensemble scenes where multiple characters need to share comedic weight
- Designing dialogue or banter between two characters who need visible chemistry

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Define the power dynamic and energy contrast

decide who leads and who supports, but ensure the supporting role has agency and gets genuine laughs (Wayne leads/Garth supports, but Garth is independently funny)

### Step 2: Choose the synchronization model

either complementary contrast (confident/awkward like Wayne and Garth) or identical energy (perfectly matched intensity like Hans and Franz) — then commit fully to that structure

### Step 3: Build physical synchronization

develop shared physical vocabulary — reactions in unison, mirrored movements, coordinated bits — that signals genuine connection to the audience

### Step 4: Establish the partnership rule

each performer's job is to make the other funnier, feed setup lines cleanly, react authentically, and never get laughs at the partner's expense

### Step 5: Create shared ownership

both characters deliver catchphrases, both participate in signature bits, and the act would be fundamentally incomplete without either performer
