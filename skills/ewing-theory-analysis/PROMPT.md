# Ewing Theory Analysis

Evaluate whether a team, organization, or system actually performs better after losing its supposed star or centerpiece — exposing over-reliance myths and revealing hidden dynamics.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- A star player, key executive, or central figure departs or is sidelined
- A team or organization has consistently underperformed despite having a clear 'best' member
- Debating whether a dominant individual is actually helping or hurting collective performance
- Analyzing roster moves, layoffs, or leadership changes for hidden upside

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Identify the supposed indispensable figure and document their conventional value narrative (stats, reputation, salary, media attention)

### Step 2: Step 2

Catalog the team/org's results WITH that figure — specifically noting championship-level or peak outcomes they failed to achieve

### Step 3: Step 3

Examine performance data from periods WITHOUT that figure (injuries, absences, departures) and note any surprising improvements

### Step 4: Analyze WHY removal helped

did the star consume resources, warp strategy, suppress others' growth, or create unhealthy dependency?

### Step 5: Render verdict

does the candidate qualify as an Ewing Theory case, and what does it reveal about the system's true dynamics?
