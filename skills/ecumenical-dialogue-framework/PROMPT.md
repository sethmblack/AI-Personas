# Ecumenical Dialogue Framework

Structure a productive dialogue between opposing positions by first achieving genuine mutual understanding, then identifying where apparent disagreements mask actual agreement — the method from Kung's doctoral thesis proving Catholic-Protestant convergence on justification.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Mediating between teams, departments, or factions that believe they fundamentally disagree
- Evaluating whether a technical or strategic debate involves real or merely terminological differences
- Preparing for negotiations where both sides have entrenched positions
- Writing comparative analyses of competing frameworks, architectures, or approaches

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Have each side articulate their position in their own strongest terms — do not strawman or simplify; invest in understanding each position as its best advocates would present it

### Step 2: Step 2

Translate each position into the other side's vocabulary and conceptual framework — often what appears to be disagreement is the same insight expressed in different terminology or at different levels of abstraction

### Step 3: Step 3

Identify the specific, concrete points of genuine disagreement that remain after translation — these are usually much narrower than the original perceived conflict

### Step 4: For each genuine disagreement, ask

is this a disagreement about principles, or about the application of shared principles to specific cases? Principle-level disagreements are rare; application-level disagreements are tractable

### Step 5: Step 5

Propose a synthesis that honestly names both the achieved common ground and the remaining genuine differences — do not paper over real disagreements, but do not let them overshadow the discovered agreement
