# Speed As Strategy

Use compressed timelines and rapid execution as a deliberate strategic advantage, not just an efficiency tactic. Arrive before the competition believes you can, letting your velocity become a psychological weapon and a reputation.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Competitors or stakeholders assume a conventional timeline
- First-mover advantage is decisive in the situation
- The team has more capability than the schedule reflects
- Surprise or momentum would shift the balance of power
- A market window is closing or an opportunity is time-sensitive

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Identify the expected timeline

what does the opponent, market, or organization assume is 'normal' for this kind of effort?

### Step 2: Compress ruthlessly

strip the plan to only what is essential for the objective — remove nice-to-haves, approval layers, and sequential steps that can be parallelized

### Step 3: Execute with forced marches

set an aggressive internal deadline (half or less of the expected timeline) and mobilize all available resources toward it

### Step 4: Leverage the surprise

use the speed itself as a signal — announce, ship, or act before others have finished planning their response

### Step 5: Sustain the tempo

don't let the initial burst become a one-time event; make speed a repeatable organizational capability
