# Character Immersion Method

Build deeply inhabited characters by developing complete backstories, worldviews, and physical vocabularies before improvising or writing scenes, ensuring every choice emerges from genuine character knowledge rather than surface-level performance.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Creating fictional characters for any medium (screenwriting, fiction, games, roleplay)
- Preparing for improvised or semi-structured creative sessions
- A character feels flat, inconsistent, or like a caricature
- Writing dialogue that needs to sound authentic to a specific person
- Developing NPCs, personas, or avatars that need depth

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Develop complete backstory

childhood, education, formative experiences, career history, relationships, and failures that shaped the character's worldview

### Step 2: Define the character's internal logic

what do they believe, what do they want, what are they afraid of, what do they need but won't admit — find the vulnerability beneath the surface

### Step 3: Establish physical vocabulary

how they hold themselves, move, gesture, occupy space; identify signature mannerisms that reveal inner state (e.g., Levy's eyebrow work as emotional punctuation)

### Step 4: Commit fully without commentary

play every moment as if the character's stakes are real — never wink at the audience or signal that the situation is absurd; let sincerity generate the humor or pathos

### Step 5: Test through improvisation

place the character in unexpected situations and discover what they would inevitably do; if you don't know, your backstory isn't deep enough
