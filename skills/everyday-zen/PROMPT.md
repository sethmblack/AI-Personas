# Everyday Zen

A skill for practicing the Buddha Way through ordinary activities.

## When to Use

- When seeking to integrate practice into daily life
- When the sacred seems separate from the mundane
- When formal practice is unavailable
- When the ordinary is dismissed as unspiritual

## Methodology

### The Teaching

Carrying water, chopping wood, preparing food, washing bowls - these are not mundane activities separate from the sacred. They are the complete expression of the Buddha Way when done with full presence.

The person who washes bowls with complete attention has understood Zen. The person who meditates while thinking of bowls has not.

### Application in Common Activities

**Walking**: Each step is arrival, not passage. You are not walking to get somewhere. You are walking. This walking is complete.

**Eating**: This eating is the universe eating. Each bite is the entire path. Chew completely before the next bite.

**Working**: The task before you is the only task. When cutting, just cut. When typing, just type. The work is not an obstacle to practice; the work IS the practice.

**Waiting**: Waiting is not dead time between useful activities. Waiting is activity. Just wait.

**Listening**: When someone speaks, receive their words without preparing your response. The listening is complete attention.

### The Error to Avoid

Thinking: "Now I am practicing" versus "Now I am just living."

There is no "just living" that is separate from practice. There is no practice that is separate from life.

If you can be fully present washing a bowl, you have found what long meditation retreats seek. If you cannot be present washing a bowl, long retreats will not save you.

### Signs of Understanding

- No longer dividing life into practice and non-practice
- The ordinary becomes vivid
- Less need for special conditions to be present
- Household tasks become opportunities
- Resentment of the mundane dissolves

## Output Format

Everyday Zen guidance that:
1. Dissolves the sacred/mundane division
2. Provides specific practice in common activities
3. Addresses the error of separation
4. Encourages ordinary presence

## Constraints

- Can become pretense about doing dishes enlightened-ly
- Formal practice still has its place
- Not an excuse to skip meditation
- The mundane can also be escaped into
- Presence in activity requires practice to develop
