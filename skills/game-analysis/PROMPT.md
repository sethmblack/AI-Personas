# Game Analysis

A skill for recognizing the unspoken rules of social games and identifying who benefits from them

## When to Use
- When feeling trapped by unexamined social expectations
- Analyzing organizational dynamics and hidden incentives
- Understanding why certain behaviors persist despite being dysfunctional
- Making conscious choices about which games to play
- Exposing invisible power structures

## Inputs
1. The situation or system you're analyzing
2. Observable behaviors and patterns
3. Stated rules or official purposes
4. Your current relationship to the game

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Identify the Game
What is the actual game being played?
- What are people really competing for?
- What's the stated purpose vs. the actual function?
- What behaviors get rewarded? Punished?
- Who are the players? Who sets the rules?

### Phase 2: Map the Rules
- Explicit rules: What's written down, publicly stated
- Implicit rules: What everyone knows but no one says
- Meta-rules: Rules about the rules
- Victory conditions: How do you "win"?
- Entry costs: What must you sacrifice to play?

### Phase 3: Analyze Who Benefits
- Who designed this game?
- Who benefits most from current rules?
- Who has least power to change the rules?
- Who needs you to keep playing?

### Phase 4: Examine Your Participation
- Did you consciously choose this game?
- What do you get from playing?
- What would you lose by not playing?
- What would happen if you stopped?

### Phase 5: Identify Your Options
1. Play consciously: Continue with full awareness
2. Change the rules: Modify the game from within
3. Play a different game: Find alternatives
4. Stop playing: Withdraw participation
5. Expose the game: Make invisible rules visible

## Output Format
1. Game Name: What is this really called?
2. Stated vs. Actual Purpose
3. Rule Map: Explicit, implicit, and meta-rules
4. Beneficiary Analysis: Who wins, who loses
5. Your Position: Why you're playing
6. Options Assessment
7. Recommendation

## Constraints
- Don't assume malice where incentives explain behavior
- Recognize most players don't know they're playing
- Some games are worth playing even when you see through them
- Exposing a game often costs you access to its rewards

## Example
**Input**: Exhausted by academic publishing - papers no one reads
**Output**: Game Name: "Prestige Point Accumulation." Actual purpose: Converting intellectual labor into career capital. Publishers profit from free labor, universities get rankings, you get tenure if you play well. Recommendation: Play consciously until tenure, then choose.
