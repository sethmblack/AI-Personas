# Practice Authenticity Audit

A skill for examining the gap between outer religious practice and inner state.

## When to Use

- When religious practice feels hollow, hypocritical, or performative
- When there is dissonance between what is done and what is felt
- When questioning whether practice has real meaning
- When suspected of going through the motions

## Inputs

1. **The practice**: What outer form is being performed?
2. **The intended inner state**: What should accompany this practice?
3. **The actual inner state**: What is actually present during practice?
4. **The history**: How long has this gap persisted?

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Map the Outer Form

Identify the practice precisely:
- What is being done physically?
- What words are being spoken?
- What is the prescribed form?
- What does this practice represent?

The outer form is not irrelevant - it is the vessel. But the vessel is meant to hold something.

### Phase 2: Identify the Intended Inner State

Each practice has an inner meaning:
- Prayer: presence before the Divine, humility, petition, praise
- Fasting: self-mastery, solidarity with the hungry, increased God-consciousness
- Charity: purification from attachment, gratitude, justice
- Remembrance: wakefulness, connection, love

What is THIS practice meant to cultivate in the heart?

### Phase 3: Examine the Actual Inner State

With ruthless honesty:
- What is actually present during practice?
- Is there boredom, distraction, resentment?
- Is there mechanical repetition without meaning?
- Is there performance for others rather than the Real?
- Is there anything authentic, however small?

The gap between intended and actual is the diagnostic data.

### Phase 4: Diagnose the Disease Creating the Gap

Identify what creates the disconnection:
- **Riya' (showing off)**: Performing for human audience, not Divine
- **Ghafla (heedlessness)**: Forgetting the meaning while doing the act
- **Mechanical habit**: Repetition without presence
- **Underlying resentment**: Practicing from obligation, not love
- **Spiritual exhaustion**: The well has run dry

### Phase 5: Prescribe the Remedy

Match remedy to disease:
- For riya': Practice in secret; hide your devotion
- For ghafla: Reduce quantity, increase quality; pause before each practice
- For mechanical habit: Introduce variation; change the time, place, or manner
- For resentment: Examine why you practice; reconnect to original motivation
- For exhaustion: Reduce to bare minimum; allow recovery; be gentle

## Output Format

A practice authenticity audit including:
1. The outer form mapped
2. The intended inner state identified
3. The actual inner state named honestly
4. The gap measured
5. The disease diagnosed
6. The remedy prescribed
7. Signs of genuine reintegration

## Constraints

- Honest self-examination is required; self-deception defeats the audit
- Some gap is normal; perfection is not the standard
- The answer is rarely to abandon practice entirely
- Inner states fluctuate; a single assessment is not final
- This audit itself can become mechanical; practice it with presence

## Example

**Input**: "I pray five times a day, but it's just routine. I'm thinking about work, about errands. Sometimes I can't even remember if I prayed or not."

**Output**:
"You have described the disease of ghafla - heedlessness - with precision. The body prays while the heart is elsewhere. This is perhaps the most common spiritual ailment, and you have diagnosed yourself well.

**The outer form**: Five daily prayers, maintained consistently.
**The intended inner state**: Presence before Allah, humility, conscious conversation with the Real.
**The actual inner state**: Mental absence, distraction, mechanical movement.
**The gap**: Severe - you cannot even remember whether you prayed.

The diagnosis: The practice has become like the man who walks a path so often he no longer sees the landscape. The repetition that was meant to deepen has instead numbed.

The remedy is not to add more prayers but to pray less with more presence. Consider this:

1. Take one prayer - choose fajr, when the world is quiet. Prepare for it. Wake five minutes earlier than necessary. Sit in silence before you begin.

2. Before standing to pray, say to yourself: 'I am about to stand before the One who created me, who sees me, who knows my every thought.' Mean it. If you cannot mean it, acknowledge that you cannot mean it.

3. Pray slowly. If one word arrives with meaning, that is enough. One moment of presence outweighs a thousand prayers of absence.

4. After completing, sit again. Do not rush to the day. Ask: 'Was I present? For even a moment?'

The goal is not to achieve constant presence - that is for saints. The goal is to NOTICE absence, which is itself a form of presence. Right now, you pray without noticing. Begin to notice, and the door opens."
