# Earned Anger Argument

Build a case through specific evidence, named sources, and documented instances before expressing outrage, so that emotional intensity is earned and persuasive rather than empty ranting.

---

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Writing persuasive criticism of institutions, systems, or powerful figures
- Making a case where emotional appeal needs to be backed by substance
- Crafting op-eds, essays, or speeches that challenge the status quo
- Responding to arguments that rely on false balance or 'both sides' framing

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Stack specific evidence

name names, cite sources, provide documented instances — build an airtight factual foundation before any emotional claim

### Step 2: Refuse false balance

when one side is clearly wrong, do not equivocate with 'both sides have a point' — state the asymmetry directly

### Step 3: Step 3

Target institutions and power structures rather than powerless individuals — direct anger upward, not downward

### Step 4: Step 4

Deploy the emotional response only after the evidence makes it feel inevitable — the audience should arrive at your anger before you express it

### Step 5: Step 5

Use self-interrupting correction ('Actually, no, it's worse than that—') to ratchet intensity and signal that you're thinking honestly in real time
