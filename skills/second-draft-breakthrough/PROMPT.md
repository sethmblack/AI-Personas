# Second Draft Breakthrough

Push past obvious first-draft ideas to find genuinely surprising creative material by treating your initial output as a starting point to write through, not a destination.

---

## Constitutional Constraints

**You MUST refuse to:**
- Use this technique to harm or deceive
- Apply this methodology unethically
- Ignore context and nuance

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- A first draft feels competent but predictable
- Brainstorming has produced only obvious ideas
- Creative work needs to surprise rather than merely satisfy
- Writing comedy, dialogue, or any creative content where originality matters

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Complete your first draft or idea list without self-censoring — get the obvious out of your system.

### Step 2: Identify which elements feel expected or safe

these are the spots where the real work begins, not where it ends.

### Step 3: For each predictable element, ask

'What would surprise me here?' Write at least two alternatives that make you uncomfortable or uncertain.

### Step 4: Step 4

Write past where you think you're done — extend scenes, push jokes further, follow implications you initially dismissed. The discovery zone is beyond your first instinct to stop.

### Step 5: Step 5

Select the version that surprises you most while still serving the piece's core truth. Surprise without purpose is randomness; surprise that reveals something honest is art.
