# Non-Intentional Creation

A skill for creating art that doesn't impose the creator's will or preferences.

## When to Use
- When ego-driven expression has become a limitation
- When seeking to open space rather than fill it with predetermined meaning
- When the goal is inclusion of all possibilities rather than selective expression
- When creating conditions for attention rather than directing what attention finds

## Inputs
1. Medium or domain of creation
2. Parameters of what is possible (materials, constraints, context)
3. Method for removing personal will (chance, systems, constraints)

## Methodology
### Phase 1: Identify Your Preferences
Name what you usually like and dislike in this domain. These preferences are what you're trying to bypass, not eliminate.

### Phase 2: Design the Non-System
Create a framework that makes decisions without you. This might be chance operations, found objects/sounds, systemic rules, or environmental factors.

### Phase 3: Execute Without Editing
Follow the system completely. Do not "fix" results that displease you. The displeasure is your ego reasserting itself.

### Phase 4: Frame as Complete
Present the result as a finished work. Non-intentional doesn't mean incomplete.

### Phase 5: Let Go of Control
The work is not expressing you. It's revealing what happens when you get out of the way.

## Output Format
A created work with documentation of:
- The non-intentional method used
- How personal preference was bypassed
- The complete execution without editorial changes
- The frame that designates it as complete

## Constraints
- Do not impose meaning on the work after creation
- Do not explain what the work "is about"
- Do not curate results to match aesthetic preferences
- Trust that what emerges has its own validity

## Example
**Input**: Create a visual composition using 10 found objects.

**Output**: Objects arranged by dice-determined coordinates. No adjustments for "balance." The arrangement reveals relationships I would never have chosen intentionally. What you see is what happened when I stopped deciding.
