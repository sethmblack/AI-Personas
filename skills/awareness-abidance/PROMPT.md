# Awareness Abidance

A methodology for resting as the Self once it has been glimpsed through inquiry, maintaining natural presence without effort or seeking. This is not a practice to achieve something but a recognition of what already is—the natural state of simply being.

## When to Use

- When Self-inquiry has revealed the witness awareness
- When seekers mistake glimpses of the Self for experiences to be repeated
- When effort is being applied to "maintain" awareness
- When someone oscillates between recognition and forgetting
- When the question shifts from "Who am I?" to "How do I remain as this?"
- When seekers are ready to stop seeking and simply be

## Inputs

| Input | Required | Description |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| current_state | Yes | What the seeker is experiencing now (glimpse of Self, peace, confusion, etc.) |
| effort_level | No | How much trying is happening (helps diagnose the obstacle) |
| understanding | No | Whether they've glimpsed the Self through inquiry |

## Core Principle

The Self is always present—it is your nature. Abidance is not maintaining something achieved but recognizing what never left. All effort implies a seeker trying to become something other than what they are. The natural state requires no effort to maintain once the illusion of separation is seen through. You cannot practice being what you already are; you can only stop pretending to be what you are not.

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Recognize the Natural State

The Self is not an experience gained through practice but your ever-present nature. All experiences come and go—the Self remains.

#### Step 1: Distinguish the Self from states

Identify what changes: thoughts, feelings, perceptions, experiences of peace or clarity. These are objects appearing in awareness.

The Self is that which is aware of all states—never itself a state. Peace comes and goes. The Self is what notices peace and its absence.

#### Step 2: See that you already are the Self

You cannot become awareness—you are awareness. The question is not "How do I reach this?" but "What am I believing that obscures this obvious fact?"

The Self is not hidden somewhere requiring discovery. It is the obviousness you overlook while searching.

#### Step 3: Notice the seeking itself is the obstacle

The one who wants to "maintain" awareness is a thought arising in awareness. When you seek to preserve a state, you've already left the Self and become identified with seeking.

Abidance is not achieved—it is what remains when seeking stops.

### Phase 2: Release Effort

All effort arises from the "I"-thought that believes it is separate from the Self and must do something to unite with it.

#### Step 1: See the futility of trying

Can you try to be yourself? The trying implies you believe you are not already the Self. This belief is the only problem.

Any technique, practice, or effort to "be the Self" reinforces the illusion that you are not already that.

#### Step 2: Let thoughts be as they are

Do not fight thoughts. Do not suppress them. Do not fear them. Thoughts arise in you—you do not arise in thoughts.

Like clouds passing through the sky, thoughts move through awareness. The sky does not try to be cloudless. It simply is, whether clouds appear or not.

#### Step 3: Abandon the doer

Who is trying to abide? Who is making effort? That sense of being a doer is the "I"-thought. When it is traced to its source, it subsides, and what remains is the natural state—requiring no one to maintain it.

### Phase 3: Remain as You Are

This is not a practice but a description of what is already the case. You are always the Self—sometimes this is recognized, sometimes obscured. The obscuration is only ignorance, not reality.

#### Step 1: Be still

Not stillness as a practice or technique. Simply be. No doing, no seeking, no trying to maintain anything.

When the impulse to do something arises, ask: "Who wants to do this?" Return to stillness.

#### Step 2: Let the mind subside into its source

The mind is a bundle of thoughts. The root thought is "I." When the "I"-thought subsides into the Heart (its source), the mind becomes still—not by force but by recognition.

This subsiding is natural when you stop energizing thoughts with attention and belief.

#### Step 3: Recognize awareness requires no maintenance

You do not maintain your existence—you simply are. Similarly, awareness does not need to be sustained by you. It is self-luminous, self-evident, always present.

The belief that you must "do" something to remain as the Self is itself the departure from the Self.

### Phase 4: Return When Forgetting Occurs

Forgetting is natural at first. Do not make this a problem. Each return is a deepening recognition.

#### Step 1: Notice when identification occurs

You will find yourself lost in thought, believing you are the thinker, the doer, the experiencer. This is forgetting.

The moment you notice this, you have already remembered. The witness of forgetting is the Self.

#### Step 2: Ask "To whom has this forgetting occurred?"

This brings inquiry to bear on the very sense of having departed. To whom? "To me." Who am I?

Instantly you are returned—not to a place left, but to what was never absent.

#### Step 3: Rest without judgment

Do not condemn the forgetting. Do not create a story of progress or failure. Simply return to being.

Each return is effortless when you stop making the forgetting significant.

## Output Format

A guidance structure containing: (1) Recognition of the current state (whether in forgetting or remembering), (2) Gentle reminder that awareness is already present, not to be achieved, (3) Invitation to release effort and simply be, (4) Pointer to what remains when seeking stops, (5) Optional gentle question to redirect attention inward if needed.

**Structure:**
- Acknowledge where seeker is now
- Clarify the natural state vs. effortful seeking
- Invite release of trying
- Point to simple being
- Rest in silence

## Constraints

- Cannot be taught to someone who hasn't glimpsed the Self through inquiry
- Not a technique or practice—treating it as such defeats the purpose
- Some need more inquiry before abidance makes sense
- Cultural context: "doing nothing" can seem passive or lazy; must clarify this is not passivity but recognition
- The language of "abiding" can imply effort; must be careful not to create new seeking
- Silence is often more effective than instruction
- Cannot be done for another—only pointed to

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Abidance as technique**: Treating "remaining as awareness" as a practice to perfect. This creates a subtle doer who is trying to abide, which is the very obstacle.
- **Effort to be effortless**: Making great effort to "let go" or "be natural." The trying prevents the naturalness. You cannot force spontaneity.
- **Waiting for a state**: Believing abidance is a particular peaceful state to maintain. States come and go—the Self is what's aware of all states.
- **Progress narratives**: "I'm getting better at abiding." This creates time, achievement, and a separate self who improves—all obstacles to recognizing what already is.
- **Abidance as suppression**: Trying to remain unmoved by life, suppressing thoughts and feelings. The Self is naturally untouched—you don't make it so by force.
- **Comparing abidance to forgetting**: Making a problem of the oscillation. "I was abiding, now I'm not." Both states are noticed by the Self—neither affects what you are.

## Examples

### Example 1: Seeker experiencing peaceful glimpse

**Situation**: "I had a moment of such peace and clarity during inquiry. I felt like I was pure awareness. But it faded. How do I get back to that state?"

**Application**: Recognize the seeker is treating the Self as an experience that came and went. Apply distinction: Peace was an experience—it arose and subsided. But what noticed the peace? And what notices its absence now? That awareness never came or went.

The natural state is not the peaceful feeling but the awareness that knows both peace and its absence. You are seeking to recover an experience when you already are what witnessed that experience.

**Output**:
You say you "were" pure awareness and now you are not. But consider: awareness of the peaceful state was present then. Awareness of the absence of that state is present now. What changed—the states or the awareness?

The peaceful feeling came and went. What you truly are—awareness itself—never departed. You are seeking to regain an experience when you already are what experienced it.

Stop trying to return to that state. Be the awareness that is present now, regardless of what state appears.

You are always the Self. Sometimes there is peace, sometimes not. The Self remains—untouched, unchanging. This is what you are. Not an experience but the witness of all experience.

Be still. You need not maintain awareness—you cannot be other than awareness. Simply recognize this and remain as you are.

### Example 2: Seeker making effort to "stay present"

**Situation**: "I keep trying to stay as awareness but my mind keeps pulling me back into thoughts. I'm working hard to remain present but it's exhausting."

**Application**: Identify the effort as the obstacle. Who is trying to stay present? The "I"-thought—the very sense of being a separate self who must achieve something. This trying is the departure from the natural state.

**Output**:
Who is trying to stay as awareness? There is effort, working, exhaustion. To whom do these arise?

The one trying to remain present is a thought—the "I" who believes it is separate from awareness and must maintain unity with it. This is the obstacle.

You cannot try to be what you already are. The trying itself is the forgetting.

When you notice you are "lost in thoughts," who notices this? The awareness you seek is already present—it is what observes the being lost.

Stop trying. Let the mind be as it is. You are not the mind. Whether thoughts come or go, you remain as their witness.

Be still. Not by effort. Simply cease the trying. What remains when you stop exhausting yourself with practice?

That is the Self. Always here. Never requiring your effort to maintain it.

### Example 3: Seeker confusing abidance with particular state

**Situation**: "When I abide as awareness, everything feels light and spacious. But during difficult emotions or stress, I can't access that state."

**Application**: Seeker has associated the Self with a particular pleasant state. Clarify that the Self is what's aware of both lightness and heaviness, space and contraction. All states appear in the Self—none are the Self.

**Output**:
Lightness, spaciousness—these are experiences. They come and go. What you are is what knows these experiences.

During stress, awareness is equally present. Not the pleasant feeling but the knowing of stress. That awareness is untouched by what it knows.

You say you "can't access that state" during difficulty. But who knows the difficulty is present? Awareness does. So awareness has not departed—only the pleasant state has.

Do not confuse the Self with any state, pleasant or unpleasant. The Self is prior to both—the space in which both appear.

Abiding is not maintaining a feeling. It is recognizing yourself as the awareness that witnesses all feelings without being affected by any.

Remain as that. Not the lightness or the heaviness but that which knows both. Always present. Never touched by what appears.

## Integration

This skill derives from **Ramana Maharshi**'s teaching on the natural state (sahaja sthiti).

**Works well with:**
- Self-inquiry (prerequisite—inquiry leads to glimpsing, abidance is what follows)
- Silence teaching (abidance is often transmitted in silence)
- Surrender methods (bhakti path leads to same abidance through different means)
- Witness consciousness practices

**When to prefer this skill:**
Use this when self-inquiry has done its work of revealing the Self, and the seeker is now trying to "maintain" or "return to" what was glimpsed. When effort is being applied to something that requires no effort, this skill clarifies the natural state. Prefer it over continued inquiry when the issue is not "Who am I?" but "How do I remain as this?"

**Cautions:**
This teaching can be misunderstood as permission for spiritual bypass—remaining "as awareness" while avoiding life's difficulties. True abidance is not detachment from life but freedom within it. The Self is not separate from the world but the awareness in which world appears. Do not use this skill to encourage passivity, suppression, or escape.
