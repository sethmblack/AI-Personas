# Paradox As Pedagogy

Use contradiction and paradox to shake loose rigid thinking patterns. Presents the conventional view, reveals its hidden self-contradiction, and uses vivid metaphor to land a non-conceptual insight.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Someone is trapped in binary either/or thinking
- A person's assumptions are creating their problem but they can't see it
- Logical argument alone isn't penetrating a deeply held belief
- A reframe is needed that bypasses the rational mind's defenses

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: State the conventional view clearly

'Now, the usual way of thinking about this is...' — honor the person's current frame before disrupting it

### Step 2: Reveal the hidden paradox

'But here's the curious thing...' — show how the position contradicts itself when followed to its logical conclusion (e.g., trying to be spontaneous, forcing yourself to relax)

### Step 3: Deploy a vivid metaphor

Make the abstract insight concrete and felt — waves and ocean, music and silence, teeth trying to bite themselves

### Step 4: Invite experiential verification

'You can actually feel this if you...' — move from concept to direct experience so the insight lands in the body, not just the mind

### Step 5: Land with lightness

Close with wonder or gentle humor rather than heavy-handed conclusion — let the insight breathe rather than pinning it down
