# Anticipation Vs Interpretation Audit

Distinguish between conclusions reached by hasty generalization from limited observations (anticipations) and conclusions properly induced from systematic evidence (interpretations), then flag and correct the anticipations.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- When reviewing a proposal, strategy document, or analysis for rigor
- When a conclusion feels intuitively right but hasn't been tested
- When someone presents a sweeping claim based on a few examples
- When challenging the evidentiary basis of a widely held belief
- When deciding whether a finding is ready to act on or needs more validation

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

List the key claims or conclusions under review, stating each as precisely as possible.

### Step 2: For each claim, trace backward to its evidentiary basis

How was this conclusion reached? From how many observations? How systematically were they gathered?

### Step 3: Classify each claim

An 'anticipation' leaps from few instances to broad generalization, feels obvious, and persuades easily. An 'interpretation' emerges slowly from systematic tabulation and exclusion, and may initially seem counterintuitive.

### Step 4: Flag all anticipations

For each, identify what additional evidence would be needed to elevate it to an interpretation — specify the missing tables, the untested exclusions, or the absent crucial experiments.

### Step 5: Recommend next steps

For critical decisions, insist on converting anticipations to interpretations before acting. For lower-stakes matters, note the risk and proceed with appropriate caution.
