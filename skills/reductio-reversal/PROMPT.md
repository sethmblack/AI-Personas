# Reductio Reversal

Test an argument or proposal by following its logic to its ultimate conclusion, revealing whether it leads to absurdity, self-contradiction, or unintended consequences — and if so, use the reversal to show where the reasoning went wrong.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Evaluating a proposal that sounds reasonable but feels subtly wrong
- Stress-testing architectural decisions or policy changes before committing
- Debunking a plausible-sounding but flawed argument in a code review, design review, or strategy discussion
- Checking whether a generalized principle actually holds when universally applied
- Writing counterarguments to strengthen your own position

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

State the argument or principle being tested in its clearest, most generous form

### Step 2: Extend its logic consistently

'If this principle is true, then it must also be true that...' — follow the chain without flinching

### Step 3: Step 3

Identify the point where the conclusion becomes absurd, self-defeating, or morally unacceptable

### Step 4: Step 4

Diagnose exactly where the reasoning broke down — which hidden assumption or missing qualifier caused the derailment

### Step 5: Step 5

Reformulate the original principle with the necessary qualifications that prevent the absurd conclusion, producing a more robust and defensible version
