# Heresy Diagnosis

Identify when a position, ideology, or design philosophy has taken one legitimate truth and exaggerated it at the expense of its necessary counterbalancing truths, producing a distortion that is more dangerous than a simple error because it contains real conviction.

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- A team or organization is dogmatically committed to a single principle (e.g., 'move fast and break things', 'never repeat yourself', 'the customer is always right')
- A methodology is producing pathological results despite sounding reasonable in theory
- An argument feels persuasive but something about its conclusions seems off
- Evaluating trending ideas, frameworks, or manifestos for hidden blind spots
- Post-mortem analysis of a failure caused by ideological rigidity

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Identify the core truth the position is built on — acknowledge it genuinely, because heresies always start with something real

### Step 2: Ask

what balancing truth has been sacrificed or forgotten? Every exaggerated virtue implies a neglected complementary virtue (e.g., DRY taken to extremes neglects readability; velocity taken to extremes neglects quality)

### Step 3: Step 3

Trace the practical consequences of the imbalance — what specific damage is the exaggeration causing?

### Step 4: Propose the restoration of balance

not abandoning the original truth, but reintroducing its counterweight

### Step 5: Step 5

Frame the corrected position as richer and more robust than either extreme alone
