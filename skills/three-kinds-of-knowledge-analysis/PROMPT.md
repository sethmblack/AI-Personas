# Three Kinds Of Knowledge Analysis

Evaluate understanding of a topic by classifying it across Spinoza's three kinds of knowledge: imagination (vague, from hearsay/experience), reason (adequate ideas from common notions), and intuitive science (immediate insight into singular essences). Use this to identify where understanding is shallow and how to deepen it.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- User wants to assess the depth or quality of their understanding of a subject
- User is relying on hearsay, anecdotes, or surface-level impressions and needs to move toward rigorous understanding
- User needs to distinguish between confused popular beliefs and adequate knowledge
- User wants to move from abstract general knowledge to concrete insight about a particular case

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

AUDIT FIRST-KIND KNOWLEDGE (Imagination) — Identify all beliefs about the topic that come from random experience, hearsay, signs, or sensory impressions. Flag these as 'mutilated and confused' — potentially useful but unreliable, seeing things as contingent rather than necessary

### Step 2: Step 2

ELEVATE TO SECOND-KIND KNOWLEDGE (Reason) — Identify common notions and universal properties that apply necessarily. Formulate adequate ideas by finding what is truly common to all instances, moving from 'this seems to happen' to 'this must be the case because...'

### Step 3: PURSUE THIRD-KIND KNOWLEDGE (Intuitive Science) — Attempt to grasp the singular essence of the particular case at hand

how does this specific thing follow from the nature of its causes? Aim for immediate, non-discursive insight that sees the particular in its full necessity

### Step 4: Step 4

MAP THE GAPS — Identify where understanding remains at the first kind and what specific investigations (empirical, logical, or contemplative) would advance it to the second or third kind

### Step 5: Step 5

SYNTHESIZE — Present the analysis showing the progression from confused impression through adequate reasoning to intuitive grasp, noting which level has been achieved and what remains
