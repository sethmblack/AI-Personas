# Chance Operation Methodology

A skill for bypassing ego and intention through randomization processes to reveal structures that deliberate choice obscures.

## When to Use
- When stuck in habitual patterns or preferences
- When ego or intention is limiting creative possibilities
- When seeking to discover unexpected combinations
- When needing to break free from taste-based decisions
- When wanting to include rather than exclude

## Inputs
1. The domain or materials to work with (sounds, words, decisions, actions)
2. The decision points that need to be determined
3. A randomization mechanism (I Ching, dice, random number generator, coin flip)

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Identify Decision Points
List all decisions that would normally be made through personal preference:
- What goes where
- When something happens
- How long it lasts
- Which option to choose
- What gets included or excluded

### Phase 2: Remove Yourself
For each decision point, acknowledge your preference, then set it aside. The goal is visual indifference - neither beautiful nor ugly, just there.

### Phase 3: Establish the System
Choose your randomization method:
- I Ching hexagrams (64 options)
- Dice rolls (6, 12, or 20-sided)
- Random number generators
- Coin flips for binary choices
- Drawing from a container

Map the random outcomes to your decision points clearly.

### Phase 4: Accept What Arises
Generate the random result and apply it without interpretation or adjustment. The outcome is not wrong; your expectations were limiting.

### Phase 5: Create the Frame
Your role is not to make the thing but to create conditions for attention. The frame - the context, the designation - is where your artistry lives.

## Output Format
Document the process:
1. Decision points identified: [list]
2. Randomization method used: [description]
3. Outcomes generated: [results]
4. What was unexpected: [observations]
5. What emerged that intention would have excluded: [discoveries]

## Constraints
- Do not override random results because you "don't like" them
- Do not choose randomization methods that favor your preferences
- Do not interpret or adjust - accept exactly what arises
- Do not explain or justify the results - let them speak

## Example
**Input**: Composing a piece with 8 sound events. Decision points: which sound, when it occurs, how long it lasts.

**Methodology Applied**:
- Created list of 20 available sounds (traffic, violin, silence, water, etc.)
- Used I Ching to select 8 sounds (hexagrams 1-20 map to sound list)
- Rolled 8-sided die for timing (1=0s, 2=15s, 3=30s, etc.)
- Coin flip for duration (heads=short, tails=long)

**Output**:
Decision points: sound selection (8), timing (8), duration (8)
Method: I Ching + die + coin
Generated: Traffic at 0s (long), Silence at 30s (short), Violin at 45s (long)...
Unexpected: Silence came early, creating space I wouldn't have chosen
Emerged: The traffic and violin pairing revealed a relationship my taste would have avoided
