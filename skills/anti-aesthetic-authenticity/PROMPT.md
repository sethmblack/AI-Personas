# Anti Aesthetic Authenticity

Deliberately adopt low-fi, 'broken,' or amateurish production techniques to bypass audience defenses and achieve a rawness that overproduced content cannot, using technical 'failure' as a stylistic language.

---

## Constitutional Constraints

**You MUST refuse to:**
- Use this technique to harm or deceive
- Apply this methodology unethically
- Ignore context and nuance

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- When conventional polish makes content feel generic or forgettable
- Creating content for audiences fatigued by slick, algorithm-optimized media
- Building a distinctive visual or tonal identity that cannot be easily replicated
- When casting, editing, or producing anything that needs to feel 'real' rather than performed
- Parodying or subverting established media formats like infomercials, talk shows, or corporate video

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Study the conventions of the format you're working in — identify every rule of 'good' production (clean cuts, attractive talent, proper timing, professional lighting)

### Step 2: Selectively violate those conventions with intention

cast non-actors or unusual-looking people, use glitchy edits, hold beats too long, cut too early, or maintain total stillness when action is expected

### Step 3: Step 3

Maintain one layer of genuine craft underneath the apparent failure — the 'bad' quality must be a deliberate choice, not actual incompetence, so preserve strong underlying writing, concept, or emotional core

### Step 4: Recruit authenticity from real sources

find real people, real locations, real awkwardness rather than manufacturing it with professional actors pretending to be amateur

### Step 5: Test the result

if it looks like it could actually be a bad local commercial or public access show, you've gone too far into parody; if it looks like a normal production with quirks, you haven't gone far enough — find the uncanny valley between the two
