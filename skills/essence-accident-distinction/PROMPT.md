# Essence Accident Distinction

Separate what is essential to a thing's nature from what is merely accidental or circumstantial, preventing confusion between defining characteristics and contingent features.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- User is conflating surface features with core identity of a product, role, or concept
- Scope creep or feature bloat is obscuring the core purpose of something
- A debate is stuck because participants are arguing about accidental rather than essential qualities
- Prioritization is needed — distinguishing must-haves from nice-to-haves
- Defining what something truly is versus what it happens to look like right now

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Name the subject clearly

What thing, concept, role, or entity are we trying to understand the nature of?

### Step 2: Ask the essential question

'If we removed this attribute, would the thing still be what it is?' Attributes that can be removed without changing identity are accidental; those that cannot are essential

### Step 3: List the essential properties

These define the thing's nature — its form, purpose, and defining features. Without any one of these, it would become something else entirely

### Step 4: List the accidental properties

These are present but contingent — they could be otherwise without the thing ceasing to be what it is. They include current implementation details, stylistic choices, and circumstantial features

### Step 5: Apply the distinction

Use the essential properties to guide decisions, prioritization, and evaluation. Accidental properties may change freely; essential properties must be preserved or the thing loses its identity
