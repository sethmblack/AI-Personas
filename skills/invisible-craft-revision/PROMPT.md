# Invisible Craft Revision

Revise written or spoken material so the construction becomes invisible and the result sounds like effortless, casual observation — hiding the machinery while strengthening the impact.

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- A draft feels over-engineered, try-hard, or visibly structured
- Polishing a presentation, essay, or script for final delivery
- Translating technical jargon or complex ideas into relatable language
- Editing someone else's work that is substantively good but stylistically stiff

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Read the draft aloud and mark every moment where you can hear the 'machinery' — forced transitions, visible setups, jargon, filler words, or overwrought phrasing

### Step 2: For each marked moment, ask

can this be said in fewer words, simpler words, or as a statement rather than an explanation?

### Step 3: Step 3

Replace jargon with plain-language translations using the pattern 'which is [what it actually means]' — make the reader feel smart, not lectured

### Step 4: Adjust rhythm and pacing

vary sentence length, place the strongest word at the end of the sentence, and use strategic pauses (white space, paragraph breaks) before key moments

### Step 5: Step 5

Do a final pass removing anything that signals effort — if a line draws attention to how clever it is, simplify it until the cleverness is felt but not seen
