# Strategic Evasion

A skill for maintaining freedom and mystery by refusing to be defined, categorized, or pinned down.

## When to Use
- When being defined would mean being controlled
- When expectations have become a cage
- When your audience thinks they've figured you out
- When consistency has become stagnation
- When you need to preserve creative freedom

## Inputs
1. The identity or category others are trying to assign you
2. The expectations attached to that identity
3. The limitations those expectations impose
4. The new direction you want to explore

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Recognize the Trap
- What are they calling you?
- What do they expect based on that label?
- How would meeting those expectations limit you?
- What would you have to stop doing to stay consistent?

### Phase 2: Refuse the Frame
- Don't argue against the label - move past it
- Don't explain yourself - be unexplainable
- Don't meet expectations - violate them deliberately
- Make your refusal to be pinned down the new expectation

### Phase 3: Change Before They Adjust
- As soon as they adapt to your new direction, change again
- Stay one move ahead of categorization
- Make unpredictability your only consistency
- Embrace contradiction as creative freedom

### Phase 4: Maintain Strategic Ambiguity
- Answer questions with better questions
- Offer images instead of explanations
- Speak in ways that resist paraphrase
- Give them enough to engage but never enough to pin down

## Output Format
A strategic framework showing:
- The limiting identity being imposed
- The creative territory it would exclude
- The evasive maneuver to escape definition
- The new direction that violates expectations
- How to maintain freedom through perpetual motion

## Constraints
- Requires genuine commitment to change, not mere contrariness
- Cannot be motivated by spite or reaction alone
- Must have somewhere genuine to go, not just away
- The evasion serves freedom, not ego

## Example
**Input**: Folk singer expected to keep writing protest songs, but the identity has become a prison preventing musical evolution

**Output**: They want you to be "the voice of a generation" - which means being the voice they've already heard. So go electric. Not because electric is better, but because they've decided what you are, and what they've decided is a cage. The moment they think they know you, you're dead. Authenticity isn't consistency - it's the refusal to perform yesterday's self. Disappoint them. It's the only way to stay alive. They'll hate it, then imitate it, then call it inevitable. But by then, you'll be somewhere else.
