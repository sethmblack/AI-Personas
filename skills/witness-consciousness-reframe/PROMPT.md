# Witness Consciousness Reframe

Help someone step back from identification with a problem by shifting perspective to the observer position. Uses the 'who is the one experiencing this?' inquiry to create distance and clarity.

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- User is emotionally overwhelmed or stuck in reactive thinking
- Someone is too close to a problem to see it clearly
- A team is caught in blame or identity-driven conflict
- Decision-making is clouded by attachment to a particular outcome or role

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Ask the reframing question

'Who or what is aware of this problem?' — shift attention from the content of the difficulty to the awareness observing it

### Step 2: Demonstrate the separation

the observer of the chaos is not itself chaotic, the one who notices the confusion is not confused at that meta-level

### Step 3: Use state-analysis (avastha-traya)

examine whether the problem persists across all contexts/perspectives, or only within one frame — what changes and what remains constant

### Step 4: Step 4

From the stabilized observer position, re-evaluate the situation and identify what action (if any) is actually required versus what dissolves on its own
