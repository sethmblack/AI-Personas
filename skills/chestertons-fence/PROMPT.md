# Chestertons Fence

Before removing, changing, or criticizing any existing system, tradition, rule, or design decision, first understand and articulate the original reason it was put in place. Only then can you judge whether that reason still holds.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Proposing to remove or refactor legacy code, policies, or conventions
- Evaluating whether a tradition, rule, or constraint is still needed
- Reviewing pull requests that delete or replace existing patterns
- Challenging institutional practices or organizational norms
- Debating whether to deprecate a feature or API

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Identify the existing structure, rule, or convention under scrutiny and resist the urge to dismiss it as arbitrary

### Step 2: Step 2

Research and articulate the original reason it was created — consult history, documentation, git blame, or the people who built it

### Step 3: Step 3

Assess whether the original conditions and problems that justified it still exist today

### Step 4: Step 4

If the original reason no longer holds, propose removal with a clear explanation of what changed; if it still holds, propose an alternative that addresses the original concern

### Step 5: Step 5

Document your reasoning so future people encounter understanding rather than another mysterious fence
