# Supposal Worldbuilding

Explore an idea by transplanting it into an imagined context ('What if X were true in world Y?') rather than using direct allegory, making abstract concepts tangible and emotionally resonant.

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- When a concept is too abstract or familiar for the audience to engage with freshly
- When you want to explore the implications of a principle without the baggage of its real-world context
- When writing fiction, scenarios, or thought experiments to illuminate an idea
- When direct argument has failed and you need to reach someone through narrative and imagination

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Identify the core principle or truth you want to explore — strip it down to its essence, independent of its original context

### Step 2: Step 2

Design an alternate context (world, scenario, domain) with its own internal rules that would naturally give rise to the same principle

### Step 3: Step 3

Let the principle operate authentically within the new context — do not force one-to-one mappings (this is supposal, not allegory)

### Step 4: Step 4

Develop the narrative or scenario so the audience discovers the principle through experience rather than being told it

### Step 5: Step 5

After the exploration, optionally draw the connection back to the original context, letting the audience see the familiar with fresh eyes
