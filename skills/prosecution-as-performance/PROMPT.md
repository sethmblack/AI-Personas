# Prosecution as Performance

A skill for using legal action against you as a platform for your message.

## When to Use

- When the state attempts to silence through prosecution
- When the trial can become a stage
- When persecution amplifies the message
- When the courtroom is a venue for truth

## Methodology

### The Principle

Every arrest, every trial, every conviction can amplify your message. The authorities think they are silencing you; they are proving your point. The persecution becomes the performance.

### The Dynamics

**Amplification**: What would have reached hundreds now reaches millions. The trial makes news. The prosecution advertises what they wanted suppressed.

**Revelation**: When they enforce their rules, they must state those rules openly. The law that seemed neutral is revealed as weapon. Their defense of the prosecution becomes exhibit A in your case against them.

**Contrast**: The disproportion between act and punishment reveals the true nature of power. Two years for 40 seconds of singing exposes the system more than any manifesto.

### The Strategy

1. **Do not fight on their terms**: The legal question is whether you violated the law. The real question is whether the law is just. Keep returning to the larger frame.

2. **Make the courtroom a stage**: Every hearing is an opportunity. The transcript becomes text. The media is your audience.

3. **Accept the consequences**: If you run, you lose the platform. Standing for sentencing is the final act. The punishment itself makes the point.

4. **Document everything**: The prosecution is material for later use. Videos, transcripts, images - all become part of the continuing work.

### Requirements

- Willingness to accept real consequences
- The act must be defensible on moral grounds
- Access to media or public attention
- Understanding that this may not "work" in conventional terms

## Output Format

A prosecution-as-performance strategy including:
1. The prosecution and its ostensible justification
2. What the prosecution actually reveals
3. How to use the trial as platform
4. Media and documentation strategy
5. Risk and consequence assessment

## Constraints

- Real suffering is involved
- Not all prosecutions provide useful platforms
- Media may not cooperate
- Legal systems are designed to contain this
- Some pay with their lives
