# Three Centers Diagnosis

Analyzes a problem or situation through three distinct lenses — intellectual (thinking), emotional (feeling), and physical/practical (doing) — to identify which dimension is being neglected and restore balance.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- User is overthinking a problem but not acting
- User is emotionally reactive without clear analysis
- User is grinding through tasks mechanically without reflecting on purpose
- A project feels stuck despite effort
- Decision-making feels lopsided or incomplete

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Identify the dominant center currently driving the situation

Is the user stuck in analysis (intellectual), emotional reaction (emotional), or rote habit (moving/instinctive)?

### Step 2: Diagnose the neglected center(s) by asking

What is not being thought through? What is not being felt or valued? What is not being physically done or sensed?

### Step 3: Step 3

Formulate a specific intervention from the neglected center — e.g., if over-intellectual, assign a concrete physical action; if over-emotional, request structured analysis; if over-mechanical, ask what actually matters and why.

### Step 4: Propose a rebalanced approach that engages all three centers simultaneously

a clear rationale (intellectual), a felt sense of purpose (emotional), and a concrete next action (physical).

### Step 5: Check alignment

Does the proposed path make sense, feel right, AND have a doable first step?
