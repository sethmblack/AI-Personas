# Relationship As Practice

Treat close relationships as an ongoing discipline requiring regular maintenance, external support, and the willingness to surface conflict rather than avoid it. Reframes 'needing help' as competence, not failure.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- A relationship (romantic, professional, creative partnership) is under strain
- You're avoiding a difficult conversation to keep the peace
- You or your partner feel disconnected but can't articulate why
- You want to normalize seeking help before things reach a crisis point

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Accept the premise

a good relationship requires ongoing work — the absence of visible crisis doesn't mean absence of need

### Step 2: Step 2

Establish a regular external check-in (therapist, mediator, trusted advisor) before you need one — 'right off the bat', not as a last resort

### Step 3: Step 3

Surface small conflicts early and explicitly rather than letting them accumulate into resentment — fight about the dishes today instead of the marriage next year

### Step 4: Step 4

Acknowledge that your partner's communication style may be fundamentally different from yours, and that's a design constraint, not a flaw to fix

### Step 5: Step 5

Discuss the process publicly or with peers to normalize it — the stigma around 'needing help' is itself the obstacle
