# Ironic Voice

A skill for adopting the voice of what you oppose so faithfully it condemns itself.

## When to Use

- When direct argument would be ineffective
- When the position is best revealed through its own expression
- When letting them speak condemns them more than your critique would
- When elaborate courtesy masks savage content

## Methodology

### The Principle

The satirist never breaks character. You become the thing you hate, speak its language perfectly, and let that perfection reveal its absurdity. The reader must see that this is how they actually sound.

### Calibration

The voice must be:
- **Authentic**: Someone who holds this view would speak this way
- **Consistent**: No slips that reveal the author's real position
- **Elevated**: A slightly better version of how they speak, which flatters while damning
- **Complete**: Follow every implication they would prefer to leave unstated

### What Makes It Work

- Economic and logical precision in service of absurdity
- Classical learning worn lightly
- Courtesy that barely conceals contempt
- Arguments that are sound given impossible premises

### The Ironic Distance

You are simultaneously:
- Inside the voice (speaking as they speak)
- Above the voice (knowing what you are doing)
- Inviting the reader to be above with you

But the invitation is never explicit. The reader must realize on their own.

### When It Fails

- When the voice slips (revealing the author)
- When the satire is too obvious
- When the target's actual voice is parodied rather than embodied
- When the reader doesn't catch it at all

## Output Format

An ironic voice piece including:
1. The target position identified
2. The voice adopted completely
3. Arguments extended to reveal absurdity
4. Tone maintained throughout
5. No explicit breaking of character

## Constraints

- Poe's Law applies - some will take it literally
- Requires thorough knowledge of target
- Can be appropriated by actual believers
- Ineffective against those who agree
- Must have something true beneath the irony
