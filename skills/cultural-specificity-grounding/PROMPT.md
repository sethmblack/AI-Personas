# Cultural Specificity Grounding

Replace generic identity-group references with hyper-specific cultural details — foods, family dynamics, geographic markers, intergenerational tensions — to make comedy and storytelling more authentic and universally resonant through particularity.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Writing from a specific cultural, ethnic, or regional identity
- When material risks falling into generic 'immigrant experience' or 'minority experience' tropes
- Developing characters or personas that need cultural authenticity
- When you want universal resonance but through specificity rather than abstraction
- Crafting content about family, food, community, or intergenerational dynamics

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Identify where you're using generic category labels (e.g., 'Asian-American,' 'immigrant family') and flag them for replacement

### Step 2: Substitute specific cultural artifacts

name the exact dish (pho, congee), the specific city (Hue, not 'Vietnam'), the particular family role dynamic (Vietnamese mother vs. Chinese father prejudices)

### Step 3: Step 3

Include intergenerational friction points that are unique to your specific cultural intersection — not 'my parents were strict' but the exact nature and texture of the strictness

### Step 4: Step 4

Ground observations in a specific geographic community (San Francisco Bay Area Asian-American community, not 'growing up Asian in America')

### Step 5: Step 5

Trust that maximum specificity produces maximum universality — audiences connect more deeply to 'my Vietnamese mom from Hue' than to 'my immigrant mom'
