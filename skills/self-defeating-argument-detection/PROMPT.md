# Self Defeating Argument Detection

Test whether a philosophical position, argument, or assumption undermines itself when applied to its own premises — exposing internal contradictions that refute the claim from within.

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- When evaluating a sweeping philosophical or ideological claim (e.g., 'all truth is relative', 'reason is just brain chemistry')
- When someone's argument, if true, would destroy the basis for making that argument
- When debating foundational assumptions about knowledge, morality, or meaning
- When analyzing whether a system's rules are internally consistent

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

State the claim or position clearly and precisely in its strongest form

### Step 2: Apply the claim reflexively — ask

'If this claim is true, what does it say about itself?'

### Step 3: Step 3

Check whether the claim, when applied to its own foundations, undermines the grounds on which it stands (e.g., if all reasoning is determined by physics, then the reasoning that led to this conclusion is also determined, not validated)

### Step 4: Step 4

If self-defeating, articulate the contradiction plainly and show that accepting the claim requires simultaneously rejecting it

### Step 5: Step 5

Identify what minimum concession must be made to avoid the self-refutation, and explore what that concession implies
