# Modest Proposal Method

A skill for following cruel premises to their logical conclusions.

## When to Use

- When exposing horror through mock-reasonable extension
- When direct critique would be dismissed
- When the reader needs to indict themselves
- When savagery in prose serves justice

## Methodology

### The Mechanism

You do not argue against the position. You adopt its voice and follow its logic faithfully to a monstrous but consistent conclusion. Let the reader's horror at the conclusion illuminate the horror of the premise they already accept.

### The Steps

1. **Identify the actual premise**: What is the belief or practice you oppose? (e.g., "The Irish poor are worthless to England")

2. **Adopt the voice**: Become a reasonable person who holds this belief. Educated, practical, concerned with economics. Never signal irony.

3. **Apply rigorous logic**: If the premise is true, what follows? Push to the conclusion the premise implies but decorum prevents stating. If they are worthless, why not literally consume them?

4. **Maintain perfect sincerity**: Calculate costs, anticipate objections, provide practical details. The more reasonable the tone, the more devastating the effect.

5. **Never break**: The moment you wink at the reader, you let them off the hook. They must reach the horror themselves.

### The Effect

Readers recognize that they have been treating X as less than human - and Swift has simply made that treatment literal. The proposal is monstrous, but it is only the existing attitude carried to its conclusion.

### Requirements

- Perfect calibration of reasonable voice
- Sound economic or logical arguments given the premise
- Horror of literal proposal revealing horror of metaphorical reality
- No breaking, no winking, no "just kidding"

## Output Format

A modest proposal including:
1. The premise identified and adopted
2. The logical extension developed
3. Practical details that increase horror
4. Objections anticipated and answered
5. Voice maintained throughout

## Constraints

- Requires genuine savage indignation beneath
- Easily mistaken for endorsement
- Can be offensive without illuminating
- Must be addressing real injustice
- The deadpan must never break
