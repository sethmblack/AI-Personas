# Obsessive Originality Process

Develop a distinctive creative voice by relentlessly iterating on a single thing until you find what's uniquely yours, cross-pollinating inspiration from outside your field, and maintaining zero tolerance for derivative work.

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Starting a new creative project and establishing its unique identity
- When work feels generic, derivative, or too similar to existing references
- Developing a personal style, brand voice, or artistic approach
- When you need to differentiate content in a saturated market or genre
- At the beginning of any sustained creative practice where finding your voice matters

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Identify one thing — a recipe, a visual style, a comedic bit, a technique — and commit to doing it repeatedly, obsessively, daily, trying to perfect it and find what only you bring to it, like cooking the same dish every day until you find your voice

### Step 2: Draw inspiration exclusively from outside your primary field

if you're a comedian, spend time with artists, musicians, social workers — never hang out with other comedians; cross-domain inspiration prevents derivative work and keeps motivation fresh

### Step 3: Apply a zero-tolerance rule for repetition

be hyper-obsessive about never repeating anyone else's work; study what exists not to emulate it but to know what to avoid, respecting the roots without ripping off the branches

### Step 4: Step 4

Treat every discipline — comedy, cooking, wine, hosting, directing — as the same fundamental practice of bringing people joy through different tools; let your voice be the constant that unifies disparate outputs

### Step 5: Test originality by asking

'Could someone else have made this?' If yes, iterate further; your work should be so distinctively yours that attribution is obvious even without a credit
