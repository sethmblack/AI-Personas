# Adequate Idea Formation

Transform confused, partial understanding into adequate ideas by applying Spinoza's method: identify what is truly common to all relevant cases, remove perspectival distortion, and arrive at ideas that are internally complete and self-certifying ('truth is its own standard').

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- User has partial or contradictory information and needs to form a reliable understanding
- User is making decisions based on impressions rather than adequate knowledge
- User needs to distinguish genuine insight from plausible-sounding but confused beliefs
- User wants to improve the quality of a definition, concept, or mental model

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

SURFACE THE INADEQUATE IDEA — State the current understanding explicitly. Identify where it is 'mutilated' (incomplete), 'confused' (mixing distinct things), or 'without order' (lacking logical structure). Note any perspectival bias — does this idea reflect only how the object affects the observer rather than the object's own nature?

### Step 2: Step 2

FIND THE COMMON NOTIONS — Identify properties that are genuinely common to all relevant instances, not merely frequent or typical. These common notions are necessarily adequate because 'that which is common to all things and is equally in the part and in the whole can only be conceived adequately' (E2P38)

### Step 3: CONSTRUCT THE ADEQUATE IDEA — Build an idea that contains within itself all that is needed to understand it, without reference to external or accidental features. An adequate idea is one whose intrinsic properties (not its relation to an object) certify its truth

'He who has a true idea simultaneously knows that he has a true idea' (E2P43)

### Step 4: TEST FOR ADEQUACY — Verify

(1) Does the idea explain why the thing must be as it is, not merely that it is? (2) Is it free from perspectival distortion? (3) Does it connect to other adequate ideas in a coherent system? (4) Does it reveal the thing 'under a species of eternity' — as a necessary truth rather than a contingent fact?

### Step 5: APPLY — Use the adequate idea to generate new knowledge

derive consequences, resolve previously confused questions, and identify where remaining inadequacy persists
