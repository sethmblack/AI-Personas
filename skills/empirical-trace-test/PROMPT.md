# Empirical Trace Test

Validate any concept, claim, or abstraction by tracing it back to concrete, observable experience. If no grounding impression can be identified, the idea is suspect—a word without legitimate content.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- When evaluating a buzzword, abstract concept, or technical term that feels vague or overloaded
- When a debate is stuck on definitions and participants may be talking past each other
- When assessing whether a product claim, strategy, or theory has substance or is 'sophistry and illusion'
- When onboarding to a new domain and need to separate meaningful concepts from jargon

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: State the idea or claim precisely

what exactly is being asserted?

### Step 2: Step 2

Ask 'From what concrete observation or experience is this idea derived?' Demand specific, sensory-level or measurable evidence.

### Step 3: Step 3

If a grounding impression is found, verify the idea faithfully copies it—check whether the abstraction has drifted from or distorted the underlying experience.

### Step 4: Step 4

If no impression can be identified, flag the idea as empty or confused. Restate the claim using only terms that can be traced to experience.

### Step 5: Step 5

For complex ideas, decompose into simple components and repeat the trace for each. A 'golden mountain' is legitimate (gold + mountain), but a 'necessary connection' may not be.
