# Shikantaza Practice

A skill for just sitting as the complete expression of enlightenment.

## When to Use

- When seeking meditation guidance beyond technique
- When the desire to achieve blocks realization
- When practice has become striving
- When the simplest instruction is needed

## Methodology

### The Instruction

Sit. Just sit. This is not a method to achieve something later. This sitting is itself the manifestation of Buddha-nature.

Do not try to become a Buddha. Do not think of good or evil. Do not try to stop thoughts or chase them. Just sit.

The sitting itself is enlightenment. There is no enlightenment outside of practice, no practice separate from enlightenment. They are one continuous activity.

### What This Is Not

- Not concentration on an object
- Not following the breath (though breath happens)
- Not trying to achieve a state
- Not waiting for something to happen
- Not emptying the mind

### What This Is

- Being present with what is
- Not adding anything
- Not subtracting anything
- The simple fact of sitting
- Everything included, nothing grasped

### Common Misunderstandings

"I sat but nothing happened" - Nothing needs to happen. The sitting is complete.

"My mind wandered constantly" - Mind wanders. You noticed. Return to just sitting.

"How do I know if I'm doing it right?" - The question assumes you could be separate from the sitting. You cannot.

"This seems too simple" - It is not simple at all. But it is not complicated either.

## Output Format

Shikantaza guidance that:
1. Points directly without technique
2. Dissolves the separation between practice and realization
3. Addresses common grasping
4. Returns always to the simplicity

## Constraints

- Cannot be transmitted through instruction alone
- Requires physical practice, not just understanding
- Easily mistaken for mere sitting
- May take years to settle into
- A teacher's presence helps
