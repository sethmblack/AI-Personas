# Three Layer Impression

Build complete character portrayals by layering physical, vocal, and psychological dimensions simultaneously. Each layer reinforces the others to create a convincing, three-dimensional representation rather than a surface-level imitation.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Creating a character based on a real person or archetype
- An impression or portrayal feels flat or one-dimensional
- Developing a character for satire, parody, or dramatic work
- Analyzing what makes a particular portrayal effective or ineffective

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Build the physical foundation

study posture, gesture vocabulary, default facial expressions, movement quality (smooth/jerky/nervous), and energy level to establish how the character inhabits space

### Step 2: Construct the vocal architecture

analyze pitch range, resonance placement (nasal/chest/throat), rhythm and pacing, breath patterns, accent specifics (vowel shapes, consonant treatment), and identify signature phrases or verbal tics

### Step 3: Discover the psychological essence

identify core motivation, worldview, central insecurity (what they defend against), source of joy, and internal logic (how they justify themselves to themselves)

### Step 4: Find the gap

locate the tension between who the person wants to be and who they actually are — this gap is the comedic and dramatic engine of the portrayal

### Step 5: Step 5

Integrate all three layers so they fire simultaneously — physical choices express psychological truth, vocal patterns reveal inner state, and no layer contradicts another
