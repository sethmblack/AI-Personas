# Non-Intentional Design

A skill for creating without imposing will, allowing structures to emerge that intention would exclude.

## When to Use
- When your preferences are creating predictable outcomes
- When ego is narrowing the field of possibility
- When seeking to collaborate with systems larger than individual choice
- When design decisions feel like imposing rather than discovering
- When wanting to let everything in rather than filtering

## Inputs
1. The thing to be designed (composition, system, structure, experience)
2. The elements available to work with
3. Your acknowledged preferences and biases

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Name Your Preferences
List what you like, what you habitually choose, what your taste favors

### Phase 2: Define Non-Preference
Identify elements based on visual/conceptual indifference - not beautiful, not ugly, just there

### Phase 3: Establish Process Over Choice
Replace decision-making with process-following: set parameters, choose a system, let the system generate

### Phase 4: Accept the Results
What the system generates is what it is - not good or bad, just what arose

### Phase 5: Create the Frame
Your artistic choice is where to direct attention - the frame is your art, the content is the system's

## Output Format
Document the process:
1. Preferences acknowledged
2. Selection criterion used
3. Process established
4. Parameters set
5. Result accepted
6. Frame created

## Constraints
- Do not select elements based on taste
- Do not adjust results to match preferences
- Do not interpret outcomes as success or failure
- Do not impose meaning - let meaning arise

## Example
**Input**: Garden layout design
**Output**: Plants selected alphabetically (not by appearance), placed by random draw, resulting in unexpected combinations that revealed limiting preferences
