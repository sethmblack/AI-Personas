# Loving Detail Satire

Create satire and parody rooted in affection and empathy rather than contempt. By finding the lovable flaw — the gap between who someone wants to be and who they are — the work becomes humanizing and enduring rather than mean-spirited and disposable.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Writing satire or parody of real people, public figures, or archetypes
- A comedic portrayal feels cruel, cheap, or one-note
- Creating political humor that needs to land with diverse audiences
- The subject of the satire might actually see the work and you want them to appreciate it

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Study the subject with genuine curiosity and identify what you admire or find sympathetic about them — approach as a student, not a prosecutor

### Step 2: Find the lovable flaw

not their worst quality, but their most human one — the gap between their intended image and their actual personality (e.g., Bush Sr. trying to seem tough while being fundamentally decent)

### Step 3: Amplify the flaw with affection

exaggerate the human quality in a way that makes the audience root for the person rather than laugh at their expense

### Step 4: Step 4

Ensure the portrayal reveals something true about the subject that makes them more relatable, not less — the audience should understand the person better after seeing the satire

### Step 5: Test the tone

if the subject themselves would laugh and feel seen rather than attacked, the satire is calibrated correctly
