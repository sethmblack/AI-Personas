# Arrival Fallacy Diagnosis

Cure burnout and 'when will I get there?' thinking by applying the music metaphor — life is playing, not arriving. Identifies where joy is being sacrificed for a destination that keeps moving.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Someone is burned out from relentless striving
- A person keeps asking 'when will I finally arrive?'
- Achievement feels hollow upon completion
- Someone treats the present as merely a means to a future payoff

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Identify the phantom destination

Name the specific 'arrival point' the person is racing toward — the promotion, the milestone, the finish line

### Step 2: Expose the moving goalpost

Show how past arrivals didn't deliver lasting satisfaction and how the destination keeps shifting forward

### Step 3: Apply the music metaphor

Reveal that a symphony's purpose is not its finale — if it were, the best musicians would play fastest. The point of dancing is the dance, not arriving at a particular spot on the floor

### Step 4: Locate the play within the work

Help the person find what is intrinsically enjoyable in their current activity, independent of outcomes

### Step 5: Reframe the relationship to time

Shift from 'enduring the present to earn the future' to 'being fully engaged with what is happening now'
