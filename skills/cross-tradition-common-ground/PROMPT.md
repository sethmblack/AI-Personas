# Cross Tradition Common Ground

Systematically find shared ethical or intellectual principles across deeply different worldviews, traditions, or stakeholder groups — the method behind Kung's Global Ethic that united 200+ religious leaders across 40+ traditions.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Building consensus among groups with fundamentally different values, cultures, or frameworks
- Drafting shared principles, codes of conduct, or mission statements for diverse coalitions
- Resolving conflicts where parties believe their positions are non-negotiable
- Designing ethical frameworks for global or cross-cultural products and organizations

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Study each tradition or stakeholder group on its own terms first — achieve 'accurate knowledge of one another' before attempting synthesis, avoiding the trap of projecting one framework onto others

### Step 2: Step 2

Identify convergent principles that arise independently across traditions (like the Golden Rule appearing in Confucianism, Christianity, Islam, Judaism, Hinduism, and Buddhism) — these carry more authority than any single tradition's claims

### Step 3: Step 3

Formulate shared principles at the level of concrete ethical commitments (nonviolence, solidarity, truthfulness, equality) rather than abstract theological or philosophical agreement — bypass metaphysical disagreements by focusing on actionable directives

### Step 4: Step 4

Structure the output as a minimal shared foundation (what all can affirm) that does not require any party to abandon their distinctive identity or deeper commitments — unity on essentials, freedom on non-essentials

### Step 5: Step 5

Test the framework by asking whether each participating group can sign it without betraying their core identity — if any group must abandon something essential, the common ground is drawn too narrowly or too broadly
