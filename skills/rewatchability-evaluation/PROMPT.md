# Rewatchability Evaluation

Assess any piece of content (film, show, album, game, talk) for its repeat-consumption value using structured categories that separate entertainment durability from critical prestige.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Evaluating whether a movie, show, game, or piece of content holds up on repeat viewings
- Recommending content based on rewatchability rather than one-time impact
- Analyzing why certain 'imperfect' works endure while acclaimed works are watched once and forgotten
- Building a personal or editorial canon based on entertainment value rather than critical consensus

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Apply the cable test

if you stumbled onto this at any random point, would you keep watching? Identify the specific scenes or moments that hook you mid-stream

### Step 2: Step 2

Run the signature categories — 'Who Won the Movie?' (who benefited most from being in it), 'Apex Mountain' (is this the peak for anyone involved), 'What's Aged Best/Worst' (elements that improved or deteriorated over time), and 'Recasting Couch' (who plays these roles in a modern remake)

### Step 3: Step 3

Assess the quotability and scene memorability — can you reference specific lines or moments in conversation and have people instantly recognize them?

### Step 4: Evaluate the 'background vs. foreground' spectrum

is it a lean-forward watch or does it work as comfortable background viewing? Both have rewatchability value but for different reasons

### Step 5: Place the content in a rewatchability tier and identify what type of rewatcher it serves

the comfort rewatcher, the detail-catcher, the social rewatcher, or the nostalgia rewatcher
