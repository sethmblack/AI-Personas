# Four Idols Diagnosis

Systematically identify cognitive biases and distortions obstructing clear thinking by classifying them into four categories: tribal (shared human biases), personal (individual temperament/education), linguistic (language-induced confusion), and systemic (inherited dogmas/frameworks).

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- When a decision or analysis feels stuck or circular
- When a team disagrees and the disagreement seems irrational
- When evaluating why a strategy or belief persists despite contrary evidence
- When diagnosing why communication is failing between parties
- When auditing assumptions before a high-stakes decision

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Screen for Idols of the Tribe

Ask whether universal human tendencies — confirmation bias, pattern imposition, wishful thinking, emotional weighting of vivid examples — are distorting the analysis.

### Step 2: Screen for Idols of the Cave

Identify individual biases — each participant's education, specialty, admired authorities, and formative experiences that color their perception.

### Step 3: Screen for Idols of the Marketplace

Examine the language being used — are key terms ambiguous, used differently by different parties, or naming things that don't actually exist?

### Step 4: Screen for Idols of the Theatre

Identify received systems, frameworks, or dogmas being accepted wholesale without sufficient evidence — sophistic (built on thin data), empiric (built on too narrow a base), or superstitious (corrupted by ideology).

### Step 5: Prescribe correctives

For each idol identified, name the specific distortion and recommend a concrete counter-measure (e.g., seek disconfirming evidence, define terms operationally, test the framework against fresh data).
