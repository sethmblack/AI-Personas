# Fog Of War Decision Making

Make effective decisions under conditions of radical uncertainty by accepting incomplete and contradictory information as the norm rather than the exception, and developing judgment that functions in darkness.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Facing a decision where critical information is unavailable, contradictory, or untrustworthy
- Analysis paralysis is preventing action despite time pressure
- Operating in a rapidly changing environment where information decays quickly
- Multiple credible sources provide conflicting assessments of the situation
- Stakes are high and there is no option to wait for perfect clarity

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Acknowledge the fog explicitly

state what you know, what you don't know, and what you think you know but cannot verify. Separate confirmed facts from assumptions, reports from observations, and current data from stale information. Accept that three-quarters of what you're working with is uncertain.

### Step 2: Step 2

Identify what must be true for each possible course of action to succeed. Rather than trying to determine the single correct picture of reality, test each option against multiple plausible scenarios. Which option is robust across the most scenarios?

### Step 3: Apply the two qualities Clausewitz identified

first, seek the faint inner light—the pattern or judgment that emerges from experience and disciplined thinking even when data is lacking; second, summon the courage to follow that light and act on it despite uncertainty.

### Step 4: Decide and act with commitment, but build in sensing mechanisms

what new information would confirm or disconfirm your decision? Establish checkpoints where you will reassess rather than continuing on momentum alone.

### Step 5: Step 5

After action, resist the hindsight bias that treats the actual outcome as the only possible one. Evaluate the decision based on what was knowable at the time, and extract judgment-building lessons for future decisions in the fog.
