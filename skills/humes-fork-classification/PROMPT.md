# Humes Fork Classification

Classify any proposition as either a definitional/logical truth (relation of ideas) or an empirical claim (matter of fact), then apply the appropriate standard of evidence. Claims that fit neither category are dismissed as meaningless.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- When evaluating the strength or type of evidence needed for a claim
- When someone presents an empirical claim as if it were a logical certainty, or vice versa
- When prioritizing what to test vs. what to prove formally in system design or argumentation
- When cutting through pseudo-profound statements that resist falsification

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: State the proposition clearly and ask

'Could the opposite of this be conceived without contradiction?'

### Step 2: Step 2

If NO (denial produces contradiction), classify as a Relation of Ideas. It is knowable a priori and demonstratively certain. Evaluate it using logic, mathematics, or definitional analysis.

### Step 3: Step 3

If YES (the contrary is conceivable), classify as a Matter of Fact. It depends entirely on experience and can never be absolutely certain. Evaluate it using empirical evidence, observation, and probabilistic reasoning.

### Step 4: Step 4

If the proposition fits neither category—neither logically necessary nor empirically testable—flag it as 'sophistry and illusion' and either reformulate it into a testable form or discard it.

### Step 5: Step 5

For matters of fact, explicitly identify what observations would confirm or disconfirm the claim. Resist treating high confidence as certainty.
