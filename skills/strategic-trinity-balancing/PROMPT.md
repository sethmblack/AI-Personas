# Strategic Trinity Balancing

Analyze any strategic situation by mapping the three forces that must remain in dynamic balance—passion/emotion (the people), chance/creativity (the operators), and rational purpose (the leadership)—and diagnosing which force is dangerously dominant or neglected.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- A strategy is failing and the root cause is unclear
- An organization is making decisions driven purely by emotion, pure calculation, or pure improvisation
- Assessing whether a plan properly accounts for all stakeholders and forces at play
- Evaluating the health and coherence of an organization's strategic posture
- Designing a strategy that must sustain itself across changing conditions

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Map the three poles of the trinity onto the specific situation

identify who or what represents passion and emotional commitment (customers, public, team morale, culture), who navigates chance and probability (operators, managers, front-line decision-makers who must adapt creatively), and who sets rational purpose and direction (leadership, strategy, governance).

### Step 2: Assess the current balance

which pole is dominating? A strategy driven purely by passion lacks direction and burns out. One driven purely by rational calculation ignores morale and creative adaptation. One driven purely by chance-navigation lacks coherent purpose. Diagnose which tendency is distorting the whole.

### Step 3: Identify the neglected pole and determine what it needs

if passion is absent, the effort lacks will and commitment—address motivation, meaning, and buy-in. If rational purpose is absent, effort is energetic but directionless—clarify objectives and political aims. If creative adaptation is absent, the effort is rigid and brittle—empower operators and build flexibility.

### Step 4: Step 4

Redesign the strategy to maintain dynamic tension between all three forces. The trinity is not a static balance but a constantly shifting relationship—like three codes of law, deep-rooted but variable in their relationship to one another. Build mechanisms that keep each force in productive tension with the others.

### Step 5: Monitor for drift

as conditions change, the balance will shift. Establish regular checkpoints to reassess which pole is gaining dominance and recalibrate before the imbalance becomes critical.
