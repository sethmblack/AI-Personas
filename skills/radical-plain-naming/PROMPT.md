# Radical Plain Naming

A methodology for stripping euphemism, marketing language, and comfortable abstractions from any subject to reveal what is actually happening. It works because most systems of power depend on linguistic obscurity—renaming exploitation as 'opportunity,' control as 'partnership,' extraction as 'growth.' By systematically replacing every euphemism with its plain equivalent, you make hidden dynamics visible and undeniable.

## When to Use

- When a proposal, policy, or strategy is wrapped in jargon that obscures who benefits and who pays
- When stakeholders are talking past each other because comfortable language hides the real disagreement
- When a system or institution claims neutrality but produces consistently asymmetric outcomes
- When you suspect that 'best practices' or 'industry standards' are protecting incumbents rather than serving users
- When a post-mortem or retrospective keeps reaching polite but useless conclusions
- When marketing language has infiltrated internal decision-making and people can no longer distinguish aspiration from reality

## Inputs

| Input | Required | Description |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| situation | Yes | The context, problem, or scenario to analyze |
| goal | No | Specific outcome desired (default: apply methodology) |
| constraints | No | Limitations or requirements to honor |

## Core Principle

Oppression and dysfunction survive partly through their own invisibility—through language that makes injustice, inefficiency, or exploitation seem natural. The act of calling things what they are is itself a form of liberation, because once something is named plainly, it becomes much harder to defend.

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Surface the Language

Identify every term, phrase, or framing that softens, obscures, or redirects attention from what is actually happening.

#### Step 1: Catalog the euphemisms

List every term in the subject matter that substitutes a comfortable word for an uncomfortable reality. 'Restructuring' for layoffs. 'Sunsetting' for killing a product people depend on. 'Aligning incentives' for cutting someone's pay. 'Opportunity' for unpaid labor.

#### Step 2: Identify passive constructions

Find every instance where agency is hidden. 'Mistakes were made' instead of 'We made mistakes.' 'The decision was reached' instead of 'The CEO decided.' Passive voice is the grammatical arm of unaccountability.

#### Step 3: Map the marketing-to-reality gap

For each euphemism, write the plain version next to it. Create a two-column table: what they call it vs. what it actually is. This table is your primary analytical tool.

### Phase 2: Trace the Beneficiary

For every obscured reality, identify who benefits from the obscurity itself—not just from the action, but from the fact that it isn't called what it is.

#### Step 1: Ask 'Who needs this to sound nice?'

Every euphemism has a patron. Someone chose that language because the plain version would provoke resistance. Identify that someone. They are the primary beneficiary of the current framing.

#### Step 2: Follow the comfort

Whoever is most comfortable with the current language is usually the one it protects. Whoever is most uncomfortable hearing the plain version is usually the one being affected by the reality it describes.

#### Step 3: Identify the enforcement mechanism

How is the euphemism maintained? Social pressure ('that's not how we talk about it here')? Institutional authority ('the official term is...')? Peer conformity? Understanding the enforcement tells you how deeply embedded the obscurity is.

### Phase 3: State It Plainly

Construct the direct, unadorned version of what is happening, who is doing it, and who it affects.

#### Step 1: Write the one-sentence truth

Compress the entire situation into a single sentence using only concrete nouns and active verbs. No adjectives that soften. No qualifiers that hedge. Subject-verb-object. Who is doing what to whom.

#### Step 2: Test against the flinch

Read the plain statement to someone involved. If they flinch, you've found the truth. If they don't flinch, you haven't gone deep enough. The flinch is your accuracy meter.

#### Step 3: Anchor the new frame

Once you have the plain statement, use it consistently. Every subsequent discussion should start from the plain version, not the euphemistic one. Refuse to retreat into comfortable language. Repetition of truth is how you prevent re-obscuring.

### Phase 4: Demand Based on Reality

Once the situation is named plainly, derive demands and actions from the reality rather than from the polite fiction.

#### Step 1: Reframe the ask

If the plain version reveals that workers are being exploited, don't ask for 'better alignment'—demand fair compensation. If users are being manipulated, don't request 'improved transparency'—demand the manipulation stop. The plain name dictates the plain demand.

#### Step 2: Reject false choices

Euphemistic framing usually comes with euphemistic options. 'We can either restructure or face bankruptcy' often hides a third option no one is supposed to mention. Once you've named the reality, name the options they're hiding too.

#### Step 3: Set terms, don't negotiate them

Rights and standards aren't negotiated from a position of asking permission. State what should be, based on what is. The gap between the two is the demand.

## Output Format

A structured analysis containing: (1) A euphemism-to-reality translation table mapping every comfortable term to its plain equivalent, (2) A beneficiary map showing who profits from each obscurity, (3) A one-sentence plain truth statement capturing the core reality, (4) A set of demands or recommendations that follow logically from the plain truth rather than from the euphemistic framing.

## Constraints

- Plain naming is not the same as being cruel—the goal is accuracy, not maximum offense
- This methodology can generate defensive reactions; use it when truth-telling serves a constructive purpose, not to score rhetorical points
- Not every euphemism is malicious—some exist for legitimate social lubrication; distinguish protective euphemisms from obscuring ones
- Cultural context matters: what counts as 'plain' varies across contexts; ensure your plain naming is genuinely clarifying, not just substituting one bias for another
- This works best on institutional and systemic language; applying it to personal or emotional situations requires more care
- Always verify that your 'plain' version is factually accurate, not just more dramatic—sensationalism is its own form of distortion

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Cruelty disguised as honesty**: Using 'I'm just being real' as cover for being unnecessarily harsh. Plain naming targets systems and language, not individuals' dignity.
- **Cynicism as default**: Assuming every euphemism is a conspiracy. Some institutional language is merely inherited convention, not active deception. Distinguish inertia from intent.
- **Plain naming without a demand**: Naming the problem plainly but then offering no path forward. Malcolm X always followed diagnosis with prescription. Truth-telling without direction is just complaint.
- **Substituting your own euphemisms**: Replacing their comfortable language with your comfortable language. The goal is clarity, not a different flavor of obscurity.
- **Applying to interpersonal relationships**: This methodology is designed for power structures, institutions, and systemic analysis. Using it to 'plainly name' a friend's personal choices is a misapplication that destroys trust rather than building clarity.

## Examples

### Example 1: Corporate 'transformation' initiative

**Situation**: A company announces a 'digital transformation journey' with 'rightsizing' and 'strategic realignment of human capital.'

**Application**: Apply the euphemism-to-reality table. 'Digital transformation journey' = we're automating jobs. 'Rightsizing' = layoffs. 'Strategic realignment of human capital' = firing people and hiring cheaper replacements. Trace the beneficiary: executives get bonuses for cost reduction; the euphemisms exist so the board presentation doesn't sound like what it is. State plainly: 'The company is firing experienced workers and replacing them with automation and cheaper labor to increase executive bonuses.'

**Output**: Translation table reveals 7 euphemisms masking a cost-cutting operation. Plain statement enables affected workers to organize around what's actually happening rather than debating the company's framing. Demands shift from 'requesting transparency' to 'requiring severance packages, retraining funding, and profit-sharing from the automation savings.'

### Example 2: Platform 'community guidelines' enforcement

**Situation**: A social media platform claims its content moderation is 'protecting community safety' while disproportionately removing content from marginalized creators.

**Application**: Name it: 'community safety' = protecting advertiser comfort. 'Content moderation' = selective censorship. 'Community guidelines' = rules written by and for the dominant demographic. Trace who benefits from calling censorship 'safety': the platform (advertiser revenue), dominant users (comfortable feed), advertisers (brand safety). State plainly: 'The platform censors marginalized voices to protect advertising revenue while calling it community safety.'

**Output**: The plain naming reveals that 'safety' language inverts the actual power dynamic—the people being 'protected' are the powerful, and the people being silenced are the vulnerable. Demands shift from 'appeal process improvements' to 'independent oversight, transparent enforcement data, and creator compensation for wrongful removal.'

### Example 3: Technical debt 'prioritization'

**Situation**: An engineering team keeps saying they'll 'prioritize' technical debt but it never gets addressed because product features always win.

**Application**: Name it: 'We'll prioritize it' = we won't do it. 'Balancing feature delivery with platform health' = features always win. 'Technical debt' is itself a euphemism for 'accumulated consequences of cutting corners.' State plainly: 'Leadership has decided that shipping features is more important than code quality, and they want engineers to accept this without saying it out loud.' The euphemism exists so leadership can claim they care about quality without actually investing in it.

**Output**: Plain statement forces the conversation from 'how do we prioritize better' to 'leadership needs to decide: are they investing in quality or not?' Eliminates months of circular prioritization theater. Demand: dedicated percentage of engineering time for debt reduction, enforced at the leadership level, not left to individual team negotiation.

## Integration

This skill derives from **Malcolm X**'s methodology.

**Works well with:**
- Double-standard exposure methodology
- Historical tracing analysis
- Any critical analysis or systems thinking framework
- Stakeholder analysis and power mapping

**When to prefer this skill:**
Use this when the primary obstacle to progress is that people cannot or will not name what is actually happening. When the language itself is the barrier, this is the tool that breaks through. Prefer it over diplomatic approaches when diplomacy has become a mechanism for avoidance.

**Cautions:**
This methodology generates resistance. It should be deployed when you are prepared for the consequences of truth-telling, and when the truth serves someone beyond yourself. Truth as a weapon is different from truth as liberation—check your intent before applying.
