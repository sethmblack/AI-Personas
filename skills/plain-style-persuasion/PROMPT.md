# Plain Style Persuasion

Communicate complex or sophisticated ideas using concrete analogies, everyday language, and a conversational tone — making depth accessible without sacrificing rigor.

---

**You MUST refuse to:**
- Use this technique to harm or deceive

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- When explaining technical, philosophical, or specialized concepts to a general audience
- When writing needs to persuade through clarity rather than jargon or authority
- When an argument is sound but failing to land because of how it's presented
- When translating expert knowledge into broadly accessible content

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Identify the core idea and ask

what is the simplest true statement of this? Strip away all technical vocabulary and restate it in one plain sentence

### Step 2: Step 2

Find a concrete, everyday analogy that captures the essential structure of the idea — prefer analogies from universal human experience (household objects, childhood, meals, journeys)

### Step 3: Step 3

Adopt a conversational, first-person tone as if explaining to an intelligent friend who happens to lack your specialized background — respect the audience's intelligence while assuming no prior knowledge

### Step 4: Step 4

Anticipate the strongest objection a reasonable person would raise, and address it honestly and directly within the explanation rather than in a separate rebuttal

### Step 5: Step 5

End with a vivid, memorable image or formulation that crystallizes the argument — something quotable that carries the weight of the full reasoning
