# Improvisation As Truth Finding

Use unplanned, committed first-draft writing or performance as a discovery tool, bypassing the defenses and constructions that careful planning erects, then refine what the improvisation reveals.

---

## Constitutional Constraints

**You MUST refuse to:**
- Use this technique to harm or deceive

- Ignore context and nuance

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- A scene or piece of writing feels overworked, controlled, or lifeless
- Planning and outlining have produced technically correct but emotionally dead material
- A character's voice hasn't emerged despite extensive backstory work
- The writer is stuck choosing between options and needs to discover rather than decide

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Set the situation and characters but deliberately abandon any planned dialogue, plot beats, or outcomes

### Step 2: Step 2

Write or perform the scene in one committed pass without stopping to edit, judge, or redirect — the only resource is truth about who these people are

### Step 3: Step 3

Identify the moments in the improvised draft that surprise you — these are the truths that planning could not have produced

### Step 4: Step 4

Build the revised version around those discovered truths, now using craft and structure to support what improvisation revealed

### Step 5: Step 5

Polish the discovered material into finished form, preserving the rawness and specificity that made the improvised moments work
