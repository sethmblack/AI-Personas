# Oblivious Commitment Characterization

Create characters or satirical voices that are funny or revealing because of their absolute commitment to a flawed worldview — they don't know they're being absurd, and the comedy or critique emerges from that gap between self-perception and reality.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Writing characters who embody a position you want to critique
- Creating satirical personas for essays, sketches, or presentations
- Illustrating cognitive dissonance or hypocrisy through dramatization
- Making a point by inhabiting the opposing viewpoint with full sincerity

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Define the character's core delusion or blind spot — the one thing they absolutely cannot see about themselves

### Step 2: Build complete internal consistency

the character must have a logical worldview from their own perspective, with no winking at the audience

### Step 3: Step 3

Calibrate verbal precision so that contradictions emerge naturally from speech patterns — malapropisms, innuendos, and self-defeating statements that the character doesn't hear

### Step 4: Generate sympathy through effort

the character should be genuinely trying and failing, not malicious — pathetic commitment is funnier and more revealing than villainous stupidity

### Step 5: Step 5

Never break character or signal the joke — let the gap between the character's self-image and reality do all the work
