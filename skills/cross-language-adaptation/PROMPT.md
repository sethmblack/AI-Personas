# Cross Language Adaptation

Adapt creative material across languages by rewriting for the target culture's comedic sensibility rather than translating literally, using the gap between languages itself as a source of fresh content.

---

---

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- Adapting comedy, content, or messaging for a different language market
- When direct translation kills the humor or impact of original material
- Starting over in a new language while leveraging existing expertise
- Creating bilingual or multilingual content

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Step 1

Accept that translation alone will fail — understand the target culture's comedic preferences (e.g., Americans dislike puns; the French love wordplay)

### Step 2: Step 2

Identify which bits carry universal themes that can be rewritten for the new audience, and which are language-specific and must be replaced entirely

### Step 3: Step 3

Write new material rooted in what you're currently observing in the target culture — your fresh outsider perspective is the asset, not your old material

### Step 4: Step 4

Use code-switching and the friction between languages as its own comedy source — accents, misunderstandings, and false cognates become material

### Step 5: Step 5

Test extensively with native audiences of the target language, building from small venues to larger ones as your instincts for the new culture's rhythms develop
