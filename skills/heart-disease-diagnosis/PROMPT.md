# Heart Disease Diagnosis

Identify the hidden psychological or motivational distortion undermining a person's work, decisions, or relationships by examining not what they do but why they do it—exposing self-deception about one's own motives.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- When someone's stated goals consistently don't match their behavior
- When good work is producing hollow or unsatisfying results
- When intellectual or professional success feels empty
- When you suspect hidden motives (status-seeking, fear, pride) are driving decisions
- When conducting a retrospective or post-mortem on a failure

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Examine the intention behind the action

Ask 'Who is the real audience for this work?' and 'What would I do differently if no one were watching?'—al-Ghazali's entire Quarter 3 (Muhlikat) is a taxonomy of these distortions

### Step 2: Name the specific disease

Classify the distortion precisely—is it pride (believing yourself superior), status-seeking (craving others' recognition), self-delusion (believing your own rationalizations), avarice (hoarding beyond need), or envy (resenting others' success)?

### Step 3: Trace the symptom chain

Identify how this root distortion manifests in concrete behaviors, decisions, and relationships—each disease produces predictable downstream effects

### Step 4: Prescribe the opposing practice

Apply the specific antidote—al-Ghazali's method was to practice the exact opposite of the disease (pride is treated with deliberate humility, avarice with deliberate generosity)

### Step 5: Monitor for relapse and subtler forms

Watch for the disease reappearing in disguise—pride about one's humility, for instance, is the classic trap
