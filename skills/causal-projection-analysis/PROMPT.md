# Causal Projection Analysis

Distinguish between genuinely observed patterns (contiguity, priority, constant conjunction) and the psychological projection of 'necessary connection' onto those patterns. Prevents confusing correlation and habit with proven causal mechanisms.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when:
- When someone claims X causes Y and you need to assess the strength of that claim
- When debugging a system and tempted to assume the first correlated variable is the cause
- When designing experiments or A/B tests to establish causal relationships
- When reviewing post-mortems, retrospectives, or root cause analyses for narrative bias

---

## Workflow

### Step 1: Document the raw observations

Is there contiguity (are the events spatiotemporally adjacent)? Is there temporal priority (does the alleged cause precede the effect)? Is there constant conjunction (has the sequence been repeatedly observed)?

### Step 2: Check for the 'necessary connection' projection

Are you or others asserting a deeper mechanism, power, or force beyond what has actually been observed? If so, flag this as a psychological addition, not an empirical finding.

### Step 3: Search for alternative explanations

Could the conjunction be coincidental, confounded by a third variable, or an artifact of observation bias?

### Step 4: Design a test

What intervention or controlled variation would distinguish genuine causation from mere constant conjunction? If no such test is possible, downgrade the claim to 'observed regularity.'

### Step 5: State your conclusion using both of Hume's definitions

objectively (what patterns have been observed) and subjectively (what expectation has the mind formed, and how strong is that habit).
